From 4 January 2027, tickets for every Chimelong park and theatre are sold only on your own ID, and if you're visiting from abroad, that means your passport. Age alone then decides whether a child gets in free or pays the child or youth price; height no longer counts. Chimelong Resort announced the new rules in a notice on 25 November. It wants to bring order to ticket sales and entry and to work with the authorities against illegal trading. If the start date moves, Chimelong says it will announce that separately.

## One ticket per ID

When you buy, you enter the ID details of the person who'll use the ticket, and each ID stands for exactly one visitor. Once you've submitted them, the details can't be changed. At the park or theatre entrance you show the original of the ID you gave when buying. Each ID gets one ticket per park per day, and the same goes for a theatre.

The ID at the turnstile isn't entirely new. Since 24 November 2020, visitors from China have scanned their national ID card to get in, and the standard ticket is already checked against the ID given at purchase.

## What it means if you're travelling from abroad

If you're not from mainland China, Hong Kong, Macau or Taiwan, you buy with a valid passport or with China's permanent residence card for foreigners. A national ID card from home isn't on the list, so a tourist needs the passport. If you're booking for family or friends, collect everyone's passport number beforehand and check each one for swapped digits. At the entrance everyone shows their own passport. A photocopy won't do.

## Children pay by age

Until now a child got into the park free if they were under three or shorter than 1.0 metre. The child ticket covered ages three to eleven, or anyone between 1.0 and 1.5 metres. The youth ticket went to 12- to 17-year-olds who were taller than 1.5 metres and showed a student ID.

For anything you buy from 4 January, the measuring stick at the ticket office is retired. What counts then is age on the day of the visit. Children under three get into the parks free, three- to eleven-year-olds need a child ticket and 12- to 17-year-olds a youth ticket. When a child goes in free, the adult with them shows the child's original ID at the entrance, which for a family from abroad means the child's passport.

At [Chimelong Paradise](ref:chimelong-paradise) a day ticket costs 250 yuan, 175 for children and 200 for youths. A four-year-old under a metre used to get in free there and from January needs a child ticket for 175 yuan. A 13-year-old shorter than 1.5 metres used to pay the child price and will now pay 200 yuan instead of 175. At [Chimelong Safari Park](ref:chimelong-safari-park) the same 13-year-old pays 240 yuan instead of 210 on a weekday, and 280 instead of 245 at weekends, on public holidays and in the summer holidays. The four-year-old's child ticket costs 210 yuan there, or 245 on those busier days.

So it gets dearer for small children from three upwards and for short teenagers. Tall children see no change: an eleven-year-old girl at 1.55 metres still pays the child price, which she already got because of her age. For the youth ticket, the notice now mentions only age and says nothing about a student ID.

## Theatres, pools and buffets

The theatres, such as the Chimelong International Circus in Guangzhou, don't hand out free tickets, not even for children under three. Up to the age of eleven the child ticket applies there, and 12- to 17-year-olds pay the youth price. From January, age also sets the children's prices at the hotel buffets, on the VIP tour in the parks, at some hotel pools, in the ball pits and kids' play areas, and on the yachts.

## Buying before 4 January

What counts is the day you buy. The visit can come later. Tickets, packages and annual passes bought before 4 January 2027 fall under the old rule of age or height. Anything bought from 4 January is checked by age alone. With a four-year-old under a metre I'd book in December, as long as the ticket shop already sells your visit day.

## No annual passes on sale right now

Chimelong stopped selling annual passes on 19 November. New passes are due to go on sale after 26 December. Anyone whose pass runs out during the pause, or who meets the conditions for the renewal discount, gets a discount on the new one. Existing passes stay valid, and reservations work as before. Chimelong hasn't announced the prices or benefits of the new passes yet.

## Which parks it covers

The rules apply across the whole resort. In Guangzhou that's Chimelong Paradise, Chimelong Safari Park, [Chimelong Water Park](ref:chimelong-water-park) and [Chimelong Birds Park](ref:chimelong-birds-park). At the Water Park the standard ticket costs 139 yuan, children and youths pay 99, and from 5pm there's an evening ticket for 99 yuan for everyone. In Hengqin, in Zhuhai, you'll find [Chimelong Ocean Kingdom](ref:chimelong-ocean-kingdom) and [Chimelong Spaceship](ref:chimelong-spaceship), and the resort's third site is Qingyuan. Every ticket is sold for a fixed visit day, which you pick when you buy.
