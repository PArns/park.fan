From 4 January 2027, tickets for every Chimelong park and theatre are sold only on your own ID, and if you're visiting from abroad, that means your passport. Age alone then decides whether a child gets in free or pays the child or youth price; height no longer counts. Chimelong Resort announced the new rules in a notice on 25 November. In its own words, the resort wants to bring order to ticket sales and entry and to work with the authorities against illegal trading. The date isn't quite fixed yet. If it moves, Chimelong says it'll announce that separately.

## One ticket per ID

When you buy, you enter for each ticket the ID details of the person who'll walk through the turnstile with it. Once you've submitted them, the details can't be changed. At the park or theatre entrance you show the original of the ID you gave when buying. Each ID gets one ticket per park per day, and the same goes for a theatre.

Visitors from China have been scanning their national ID card at the turnstile since 24 November 2020. The standard ticket is already checked against the ID given at purchase, too.

## What it means if you're travelling from abroad

If you're not from mainland China, Hong Kong, Macau or Taiwan, you buy with a valid passport or with China's permanent residence card for foreigners. A tourist needs the passport. A national ID card from home isn't on the list. If you're booking for family or friends, collect everyone's passport number beforehand and check each one for swapped digits. At the entrance, everyone then needs their own passport in the original, even if you paid for all the tickets. A photo of a passport on your phone won't get the rest of the family through the turnstile.

## Children pay by age

Until now a child got into the park free if they were under three or shorter than 1.0 metre. The child ticket covered ages three to eleven, or anyone between 1.0 and 1.5 metres. The youth ticket went to 12- to 17-year-olds who were taller than 1.5 metres and showed a student ID.

For anything you buy from 4 January, the measuring stick at the ticket office is retired. What counts then is age on the day of the visit. Children under three get into the parks free, three- to eleven-year-olds need a child ticket and 12- to 17-year-olds a youth ticket. When a child goes in free, the adult with them shows the child's original ID at the entrance, which for a family from abroad means the child's passport.

An adult day ticket at [Chimelong Paradise](ref:chimelong-paradise) costs 250 yuan. Children and youths pay these prices there and at [Chimelong Safari Park](ref:chimelong-safari-park):

| Park and day                                                                  | Child ticket | Youth ticket |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -----------: | -----------: |
| Chimelong Paradise                                                            |     175 yuan |     200 yuan |
| Chimelong Safari Park on weekdays                                             |     210 yuan |     240 yuan |
| Chimelong Safari Park at weekends, public holidays and in the summer holidays |     245 yuan |     280 yuan |

It gets dearer for small children from three upwards and for teenagers under 1.5 metres. A four-year-old under a metre used to get in free and from January needs a child ticket. A 13-year-old shorter than 1.5 metres used to pay the child price and will now pay the youth price. Tall children see no change: an eleven-year-old girl at 1.55 metres still pays the child price, which she already got because of her age. The new notice says nothing about the student ID that teenagers used to have to show.

## Theatres, pools and buffets

The theatres, such as the Chimelong International Circus in Guangzhou, don't hand out free tickets, not even for children under three. Up to the age of eleven the child ticket applies there, and 12- to 17-year-olds pay the youth price. From January, age also sets the children's prices at the hotel buffets, on the VIP tour in the parks, at some hotel pools, in the ball pits and kids' play areas, and on the yachts.

## Buying before 4 January

Tickets, packages and annual passes you buy by 3 January 2027 fall under the old rule of age or height, even if the visit comes later. With a four-year-old under a metre I'd book in December. Every ticket is issued for a fixed visit day, though, so that only works if the ticket shop already sells your day.

## No annual passes on sale right now

Chimelong stopped selling annual passes on 19 November. New passes are due to go on sale after 26 December. You get a discount on the new pass if your old one runs out during the pause, or if you'd qualify for the renewal discount during that time anyway. Existing passes stay valid. Reservations work as before. Chimelong hasn't announced the prices or benefits of the new passes yet.

## Which parks it covers

In Guangzhou the rules cover Chimelong Paradise, Chimelong Safari Park, [Chimelong Water Park](ref:chimelong-water-park) and [Chimelong Birds Park](ref:chimelong-birds-park). At the Water Park the standard ticket costs 139 yuan, children and youths pay 99, and from 5pm there's an evening ticket for 99 yuan for everyone. In Hengqin, in Zhuhai, you'll find [Chimelong Ocean Kingdom](ref:chimelong-ocean-kingdom) and [Chimelong Spaceship](ref:chimelong-spaceship). The resort's third site is Qingyuan.
