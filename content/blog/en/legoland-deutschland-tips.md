**Legoland Deutschland** says itself that it's built for children between **two and twelve**. The
highest minimum height in the whole park is **1.25 metres**, on
[Flying NINJAGO](ref:legoland-deutschland/flying-ninjago?bare). And on none of the nine headliner
rides does an early start save enough time for us to give a rope drop tip. Anyone planning a day in
Günzburg needs less tactics than in Rust or Brühl, and more attention to centimetres and ages.

This guide assumes children between three and twelve, from the stroller to the first coaster
without a parent's hand. Our wait times come from measurements since December 2025, which is one
season and 188 measured days. Prices, times and dates are as of **6 October 2026**.

## A park on the site of an ammunition depot

Günzburg lies between Ulm and Augsburg, right on the A8. The Lego Group announced the park on
9 September 1999 as the fourth one after Billund, Windsor and Carlsbad, choosing it over a site in
Tokyo. The land was a Luftwaffe ammunition depot, and according to Wikipedia some 60,000 bombs,
33,800 shells and 2,800 Panzerfaust anti-tank weapons from the Second World War lay in the ground.
Clearing them took two years and cost 34 million Deutschmarks.

The park opened on **17 May 2002**, nine months ahead of schedule, with more than 40 attractions and
costs of around €153 million. In the first year 1.3 million guests came, in 2020 only 750,000, and in
2024 the Themed Entertainment Association's index put it at about **two million**. That makes
Legoland the third most visited theme park in Germany after Europa-Park and Phantasialand. Merlin
Entertainments has run it since 2005. In 2019 Kirkbi, the holding company of the Lego owners,
Blackstone and a Canadian pension fund bought Merlin for €6.6 billion, and Merlin still operates
the park.

By the park's own count it now has **68 attractions in 11 themed areas** and more than 58 million
Lego bricks. Miniland is made of over 23 million of them, built by 140 model designers. The Allianz
Arena there uses more than a million bricks, weighs 1.5 tonnes according to Wikipedia and seats
30,000 minifigures, and the Reichstag building from Berlin takes about 1.07 million. The newest
area is **LEGO Mythica**, which opened on 25 March 2023. At over €15 million it was the most
expensive expansion in the park's history, and its wing coaster is the first new coaster in
Legoland Deutschland in about 20 years.

## Minimum height and age on the rides

Almost everywhere in Legoland two limits apply: a height and an age, set separately for riding with
an adult and for riding alone. A ride that takes a child of 1.10 metres with an adult often asks for
1.20 when they ride alone. The park sets this ride by ride, and at the ride entrance the staff decide
whether someone may board.

Say your child is six and 1.15 metres tall. With you, they can ride the Feuerdrache, Das Große
LEGO Rennen, the Drachenjagd, the Dschungel X-pedition, the Fire & Ice Tower and LEGO NINJAGO The
Ride, but not Maximus and not Flying NINJAGO. They're five and ten centimetres short of those. If
you have two children of different heights, plan two lists and take turns on the big rides.

The limit for riding without an adult matters more. An eight-year-old of 1.30 metres can ride most
of the rides in the table alone, while a six-year-old of 1.20 metres can only do the smaller ones.
Flying NINJAGO, Power Builder and the Wüsten X-kursion also have a **maximum height** of 1.95
metres, Maximus one of 2.00 metres. That mostly affects parents who want to come along.

## The big rides

**[Maximus](ref:legoland-deutschland/maximus-the-guardians-flight)** is the first wing coaster in
any Legoland, built by Bolliger & Mabillard, 17 metres tall, at 15 metres per second, which is
54 km/h. The ride includes a helix and a corkscrew, and your legs dangle in the air. The park
recommends it from primary-school age.


The **[Feuerdrache](ref:legoland-deutschland/fire-dragon)** has been running since May 2002 and is a
family coaster by Zierer in two parts. First the train rolls through the rooms of the castle
residents, past the pantry, kitchen, banquet hall and Merlin's treasure chamber, all built from Lego.
Then the dragon notices the passengers, and the outdoor track takes the curves at up to eight metres
per second, just under 29 km/h. A child can ride it with an adult from 1.10 metres and six years.

**[Das Große LEGO Rennen](ref:legoland-deutschland/the-great-lego-race)** is a wild mouse by Mack
Rides, 18 metres tall, according to Wikipedia 400 metres long and up to 56 km/h fast. It used to run
as Project X and has been themed as a Lego City race track since 2018. With an adult 1.10 metres is
enough, and for a child who has done the Drachenjagd it's the next step.

```glossary-widget slug=wild-mouse

```

**[Flying NINJAGO](ref:legoland-deutschland/flying-ninjago)** was built by Gerstlauer in 2012 for
the park's tenth birthday, and according to Wikipedia it was a world first for the manufacturer. Up
to twelve riders fly 22 metres up at 50 km/h, and by shifting their weight and steering the wings
they can turn the dragon all the way into the 360-degree “Spinjitzu twist”. There's one limit for
everybody, 1.25 metres and seven years.

The **[Dschungel X-pedition](ref:legoland-deutschland/jungle-x-pedition)** is the Intamin log flume
from the opening year, with a twelve-metre drop where, according to the park, 80 litres of water per
second pour into the lagoon. Nobody gets out dry. In October that's the reason to have a change of
clothes in your backpack.

**[LEGO NINJAGO The Ride](ref:legoland-deutschland/lego-ninjago-the-ride)** from 2017 isn't a
coaster but an interactive 4D ride. With hand movements you throw flashes of light, fireballs, ice
fountains and blasts of air at animated opponents, fog and warm air are part of the show, and at the
end you find out who scored the most points. The ride takes about three and a half minutes, and
according to the park you don't need to know the story. Because it's indoors, it comes up again
below, under rain.

For the smaller ones there are the **[Drachenjagd](ref:legoland-deutschland/dragon-hunt)**, a junior
coaster by Gerstlauer from 2003, from one metre with an adult, and the
**[Fire & Ice Tower](ref:legoland-deutschland/fire-and-ice-tower)**, where you spin nine metres up
and then drop, from as young as three. At the Wellenreiter and at Käpt'n Nicks Piratenschlacht,
spectators on shore can join in and splash the boats, and anyone watching there doesn't
necessarily stay dry.

## For the youngest, from two

If you come with a two-year-old, you take the **[LEGOLAND Express](ref:legoland-deutschland/legoland-express)**,
the **[DUPLO Express](ref:legoland-deutschland/duplo-express)** and the
**[Hafenrundfahrt](ref:legoland-deutschland/harbour-cruise)**. On the two trains, children up to
0.80 metres ride from age one on a parent's lap, and the Hafenrundfahrt asks for 0.80 metres and one
year. Add the DUPLO playground, the pirate playground, the adventure playground and the shipyard,
which according to Wikipedia have been in the park since it opened.

The two **driving schools** have been there since 2002. In the
**[Junior driving school](ref:legoland-deutschland/legoland-junior-driving-school-powered-by-hyundai)**
children from three to six drive a simple track, in the
**[driving school](ref:legoland-deutschland/legoland-driving-school-powered-by-hyundai)** for children
from seven to thirteen there's a theory lesson first and then a course with traffic lights and road
signs, and at the end there's a Legoland driving licence. According to Wikipedia both run in
cooperation with Hyundai.

**Miniland** is the quietest part of the park. Most models are built at 1:20 scale. Neuschwanstein
Castle is made of over 300,000 bricks and was the first model the park's workshop designed entirely
by itself, and since 2025 a 2.30-metre Fortnite dino of 73,000 pieces has stood there.

## Rope drop doesn't pay off here

We only give a rope drop tip when the day's peak at a ride is high enough, and the early start saves
enough of it, that the alarm clock is worth it. The thresholds and the reasoning are in the article
[Is 70 Minutes a Long Wait?](/blog/is-70-minutes-a-long-wait). At Legoland none of the nine rides the park lists as
headliners reaches those thresholds, on weekdays or at weekends. So the page of each of these
rides carries no rope drop recommendation.

```glossary-widget slug=rope-drop

```

That changes the day less than you'd think. You don't have to be at the car park at seven. If you
arrive early, start with the two rides whose curve only rises, the Dschungel X-pedition and the
Tret-o-Mobil, then take Das Große LEGO Rennen and the Hafenrundfahrt, where the crowds peak in
the morning, and ride Maximus, the Feuerdrache and Flying NINJAGO later, because their curve hardly
moves. If you'd rather have it quiet, come on a Friday.

## Fastrack: three tiers for 18 rides

**Fastrack** is what used to be called Express Pass. It's a ticket with a fixed number of rides that
gets you into a shorter queue at the ride you choose. After feedback from guests the park dropped the
old system, where you bought a certain time saving, and now counts rides. Fastrack is per person,
includes **no park admission** and is valid only on the booked day.

There are three tiers. **Bronze** costs from €25, **Silver** from €42 and **Gold** from €89. Gold
covers all Fastrack rides of the day, and the park may ask for ID because it can't be passed on. With
Bronze and Silver each ride uses up part of your allowance. A Fastrack package is scanned once every
three minutes, and the daily allowance is limited. Fastrack works at 18 attractions, among them
Maximus, Feuerdrache, Das Große LEGO Rennen, LEGO NINJAGO The Ride, Flying NINJAGO, Dschungel
X-pedition, LEGOLAND Express, the observation tower, Pyramiden Rallye, Wüsten X-kursion, Safari
Tour, Piratenschule and LEGO Studios.

Whether it pays depends on the day. For four people even €25 each is €100 on top of admission, for
a ride where the table above shows no outliers anyway. On a Friday in June I wouldn't buy Fastrack.
On a Saturday in the Bavarian holidays, when Legoland has its busiest days in our measurements, I'd
take Bronze for whichever ride is then at the top of the table.

## Tickets, prices and annual pass

Prices are **dynamic**. According to Wikipedia the park introduced surge pricing at the end of 2024,
and the prices on its site start with “from”. The following apply on 6 October 2026.

A day ticket costs **from €39** online, and **€64** at the box office for visitors from twelve and
**€58** for children between two and eleven. Under two, admission is free. The two-day ticket
costs from €54 online, and the second visit has to fall within six days of the first. The Flexi
ticket costs €54 online and is valid on a day of your choice in the season. The park itself advises
buying online, because day tickets at the box office are only issued subject to availability. There are
no refunds, and tickets can in some cases be passed on to other people in your own travel group.

The **annual pass** costs from €99 and lasts a year, with a discount on stays in the holiday village
and free entry to WinterWonder. The Basic version costs from €79 but has 35 blackout days in the
season. The Merlin Annual Pass costs €139 and includes parking at Legoland and 28 other Merlin
attractions. If you pay the minimum online price every time, the €99 is back after the third visit.
If you're only coming once, you don't need an annual pass.

## Opening hours, closing days and the 2026 season

The season runs from **28 March to 8 November 2026**. The park is closed on **5 and 6 May**, from
**18 to 20 May**, on **8 and 9 June**, and on **15, 16 and from 21 to 23 September**. The park's site
doesn't list dates for 2027 yet.

The normal opening hours are **10 am to 6 pm**, longer at weekends, in the holidays and on public
holidays. In the regular season the rides close **an hour before the park does**. If the park closes
at 6 pm and you want to ride at 5:30 pm, the ride is already shut. The quietest upcoming days are in

## Halloween until 8 November

The Halloween event runs from **3 October to 8 November 2026** and is included in admission. The park
says there are no jump scares, only pumpkins, ghosts and skeletons in the Knights' Kingdom. If you'd
rather skip the decorations, the park has areas without a Halloween theme.

The programme includes the “BOOlevard”, the decorated path from the entrance through the park, the
“Hexen BOOde” as a reward for solving a scavenger hunt, a monster dance at the castle stage on
Saturdays, and the 4D film “Die Monster sind los!” in the LEGO Studios. It runs hourly from 11 am, with
the last film an hour before closing. The **parade** starts at 4 pm on the Halloween Saturdays,
3, 10, 17, 24 and 31 October, and it can be cancelled in bad weather. On those Saturdays the park stays
open until **8 pm**, on 7 November until 7 pm, and otherwise until 6 pm. Come on a Halloween
Saturday and you get two more hours, but also the busiest day of the week.

The show “Battle of the Bricks” at the Affentheater in the Adventure Land plays at noon and 4 pm, not
on Mondays. On the Halloween Saturdays the parade replaces the 4 pm show. Costumes are allowed.

## When it rains

According to the park, the **Tempel X-pedition**, **LEGO NINJAGO The Ride**, the **LEGO Studios**,
**LEGOLAND ATLANTIS by SEA LIFE**, the **LEGO Fabrik** and the maze in the LEGO City police station
are under cover. According to Wikipedia Atlantis has over 2,000 fish, and its glass tunnel is eight
metres long. There's also a **Regen Rallye**, a puzzle round for wet days.

It rains more often than you'd think. Between 24 December 2025 and 5 October 2026, at Legoland
Deutschland at least two millimetres of rain fell on 18 percent of opening days, as the
[rain comparison](/blog/theme-parks-in-the-rain) lists for twelve parks. The park itself recommends an
umbrella, a rain poncho, waterproof shoes and a change of clothes.

## Food, babies and strollers

In the park you'll find, among others, the **STARS Grill House** with a menu, the **City Restaurant**
as a self-service market hall, **Pizza Mania!** with pizza and pasta, the **Schnitzel Depot** and
**Zum Ritterschmaus**, which the park describes as cheap. In the Asian and Oriental area there's the
Asia Chicken House, the Ninja Kitchen and the Fata Morgana Kitchen. According to the park, Café EIN
STEIN suits a first or second breakfast. At three stations you mix Coca-Cola drinks yourself, and
three stands sell ice cream. You can pay by card in all restaurants and at the box office, and only
in cash at the mobile stands. Allergen lists are available in the restaurants.

For babies there's the **Baby Service** in the Imagination area, by the DUPLO playground and Pizza
Mania!, with baby food and a changing and nursing area. All toilets in the park have a changing
room. The park rents out strollers at the entrance for **€6** plus a €4 deposit, in limited numbers
and without a mount for baby seats. You may bring your own wagon, but not tricycles, inline
skates or kick scooters. Lockers cost €5 or €7. Dogs aren't allowed in the park or the holiday
village, apart from assistance dogs.

## Getting there, parking and where to stay

The address for your sat nav is **LEGOLAND Allee 1, 89312 Günzburg**. From Munich and from Stuttgart it's
about an hour and a half by car on the A8 to the Günzburg exit. From Memmingen airport it's around 50
minutes, from Stuttgart airport an hour and from Munich airport an hour and a half.

The car park has over **4,000 spaces**. The parking ticket costs **€12** online and €14 at the box
office, per vehicle and for one entry. It's scanned at the barrier on the way out. If you want to
park closer to the entrance, VIP parking costs €25, in limited numbers per day. By train you ride to
Günzburg and take the bus from there. The park says you have to book your train ticket to “LEGOLAND
Park” or “LEGOLAND Feriendorf”, and only then is the bus transfer included in the price.

The **holiday village** lies east of the park and, according to Wikipedia, has room for 2,836 guests.
You can choose the Pirateninsel Hotel with 142 rooms, three castles, the NINJAGO Quartier with 72
rooms, the Waldabenteuer Lodge with 80 rooms, 39 camping barrels and a campsite. For Halloween the
park offers a package with a night's stay, breakfast, parking and two days' admission from €87 per
person, with a 10 percent discount. If you come with preschoolers, you can add the **Peppa Pig Park**
next door. It opened in May 2024 as a separate park, on 2.2 hectares, with an investment of about €30
million.

## Winter and what's next

**WinterWonder LEGOLAND** runs on selected days in 2026/27: 28 and 29 November, 4 to 6, 11 to 13, 18
to 23 and 26 to 30 December, and 2 to 10 January 2027. There's an ice rink, curling lanes and
shows every half hour, and at many rides it says “open depending on weather”. An early-bird ticket
costs €22 instead of €36 until 15 November. How the park stacks up in winter against the other German
parks is in the [winter overview](/blog/winter-theme-parks-2026).

Legoland has also announced the world's first LEGO Harry Potter Land and the first accommodation with
that theme. The park doesn't give an opening year. What other parks have confirmed for 2027 is in
[New theme park attractions 2027](/blog/new-theme-park-attractions-2027).

## Frequently asked questions about Legoland Deutschland

### When is Legoland Deutschland open in 2026?

From 28 March to 8 November 2026, normally 10 am to 6 pm, longer at weekends, in the holidays and on
public holidays. The park is closed on 5 and 6 May, 18 to 20 May, 8 and 9 June, and 15, 16 and 21 to
23 September. WinterWonder opens on selected days from 28 November to 10 January 2027.

### When is Legoland quietest?

In our measurements since December 2025, on Friday, in June and in September. Saturday and August
were the busiest. The calendar on the [park page](ref:legoland-deutschland?calendar) shows the
quieter upcoming days, and on rainy days wait times were 17 percent shorter than on comparable dry
days.

### What height does my child need to ride?

With an adult from 0.80 metres at Käpt'n Nicks Piratenschlacht and from 1.00 metres at the
Drachenjagd, the Dschungel X-pedition and the Fire & Ice Tower. From 1.10 metres Das Große LEGO
Rennen and the Feuerdrache, from 1.20 metres Maximus and from 1.25 metres Flying NINJAGO. Riding
alone, most rides ask for 1.20 metres, Maximus and Käpt'n Nicks Piratenschlacht for 1.30 metres. Each
also has a minimum age, which is in the table above.

### How much does Legoland Deutschland cost?

A day ticket costs from €39 online and €64 at the box office from age twelve, €58 for children
from two to eleven. Children under two pay nothing. Parking costs €12 online and €14 at the box
office. The annual pass costs from €99, as of 6 October 2026.

### Is Fastrack worth it at Legoland?

Not on a quiet weekday. Fastrack costs from €25 per person, works at 18 rides and doesn't include
admission. On a Saturday in the holidays the Bronze tier can pay for one long queue. The wait times
are on the [park page](ref:legoland-deutschland).

### How many roller coasters does Legoland Deutschland have?

Four: [Maximus](ref:legoland-deutschland/maximus-the-guardians-flight?bare),
[Feuerdrache](ref:legoland-deutschland/fire-dragon?bare),
[Das Große LEGO Rennen](ref:legoland-deutschland/the-great-lego-race?bare) and the
[Drachenjagd](ref:legoland-deutschland/dragon-hunt?bare). Maximus is a wing coaster and the only ride
with upside-down elements.

### Can I take a dog into Legoland?

No, only trained assistance dogs with an ID. The park recommends a dog day-care centre in the area,
about ten minutes away.

### How far is it from Munich and Stuttgart?

About an hour and a half by car each, on the A8 to the Günzburg exit. By train to Günzburg and then
by bus, if the train ticket is booked to “LEGOLAND Park”.

## Where the catch is

The park has four coasters, and only one of them, Maximus, goes upside down. If you come with
children over twelve or without any, Phantasialand or Europa-Park have more rides that suit them. At
Legoland the minimum heights end at 1.25 metres.
