[Pirates of the Caribbean](ref:magic-kingdom-park/pirates-of-the-caribbean) at [Magic Kingdom](ref:magic-kingdom-park) closes for a refurbishment on 11 January 2027 and won't sail again until late 2027. On 19 November the Disney Parks Blog added that the Imagineers will use the break to put in new effects, including in the Dead Man's Cove and Hurricane Lagoon scenes. Disney hasn't named a reopening date yet.

## The last boats leave on 10 January

The ride is still running over Christmas, New Year's Eve and the first week of January. Disney After Hours, the evenings on a separate ticket, starts at Magic Kingdom on 11 January, the pirates' first day off, and runs on selected evenings until 19 July, all of them without Pirates.

## What Disney says about the effects and who misses out

The Parks Blog calls it a routine break, and all it says about the effects is that they'll "build on the ride's story". For a story with cannon fire and a burning town, that leaves plenty of room.

Families lose the most here, because Pirates has no minimum height. The boat ride lasts about eight and a half minutes and goes through 17th-century Caribbean port towns, past a haunted cove, between a fort and a galleon trading cannon fire, and through a town in flames. Captain Jack Sparrow from the films turns up in three scenes. Along the way there's one drop of just over four metres. You queue inside Castillo del Morro, a fort modelled on the fortress in San Juan, Puerto Rico. Along the way you sing along to the pirates' song "Yo Ho, Yo Ho (A Pirate's Life for Me)".

Even for an autumn 2027 trip I wouldn't count on Pirates, because "late 2027" runs right up to New Year's Eve. Spend the time from the pirates' queue on one of the [rides with the longest queues](/blog/magic-kingdom-wait-times-tips). If you're still hoping for the pirates in December 2027, put your Magic Kingdom day at the end of the trip. Universal and the other Disney parks come first, in the order from the [Orlando itinerary for Disney World and Universal](/blog/orlando-itinerary-disney-universal).

## There are still pirates in Adventureland

In Adventureland, the pirate tavern The Beak and Barrel stays open. It opened on 29 August 2025, and the backstory says it was founded by rum smuggler Captain Meridian "Merry" Goldwyn and her first mate Rummy, who looks like the Barker Bird that used to sit at the entrance to Pirates. Children can come in too, two of the drinks are alcohol-free, and you can reserve a table ahead. The shop next door, the Plaza del Sol Caribe Bazaar, sells T-shirts and plush parrots.

## A ride Florida wasn't meant to get

The first Pirates ride opened at Disneyland in California in 1967. It was one of the last attractions whose development Walt Disney still followed himself. It started life as a wax museum idea and ended up being built with Audio-Animatronics.

For Magic Kingdom, which opened in 1971, Disney first planned without pirates, because it thought a Caribbean ride so close to the real Caribbean wouldn't have the same appeal as it did in California. The construction wall read "Opening Christmas 1973", and the first boats set off on 15 December 1973. Disney added Jack Sparrow in 2006, and in 2018 the ride got a new auction scene where the pirates sell off loot rather than a bride.

Disney shows how Pirates was written in the "Storytelling" episode of the video series "We Call It Imagineering" on Walt Disney Imagineering's YouTube channel, and later on Disney+ as well. The same episode also shows Piston Peak National Park, the Cars area going up on part of what used to be Frontierland, and the new Indiana Jones story "Indiana Jones and the Myth of the Jade Serpent".
