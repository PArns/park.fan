On 24 December 2025, park.fan started logging the wait times that theme parks post at their rides and in their apps, every five minutes. Starting on Christmas Eve says a fair bit about our holiday planning, and Europa-Park was closed that day, which was the smarter move. Since then we've logged more than 50 million wait times at 210 parks around the world.

In Europe there's now enough data for 45 parks in ten countries, with 1,806 attractions between them. [Disneyland Park](ref:/parks/europe/france/paris/disneyland-park) near Paris was the only one open every single day, all 302 of them from 24 December 2025 to 21 October 2026. The median European park has 203 days measured, about two thirds of that, because many of them close for the winter. The minutes are in the tables further down, which load the latest figures every time you open the page.

## The very first week was one of the busiest

The week between Christmas and New Year was among the busiest of the whole year at a lot of parks, and we caught it in our first week of measuring. For [Europa-Park](ref:europa-park), [Parc Astérix](ref:parc-asterix) near Paris and [Bellewaerde](ref:bellewaerde) near Ypres, 29 December 2025 is still the day with the longest waits we've ever measured, and [Efteling](ref:efteling) and [Bobbejaanland](ref:bobbejaanland) peaked a day later, on 30 December. Parc Astérix had its entire top five of the year between 26 December and 3 January, right in the middle of its Noël Gaulois, which ran from 20 December to 4 January. If you go to Plailly in May or June, you'll queue a good deal less for the headliners than between Christmas and New Year, and you won't even need a woolly hat.

[PortAventura](ref:portaventura-park) on the Costa Daurada was still in its Christmas season too, which ran there until 6 January, and the park had its busiest day on 2 January 2026. Disneyland Park followed on 3 January. Eleven parks have one of their five busiest days between 26 December and 6 January. Disneyland Park then had its quietest month of the year in January. Efteling's December beats every summer month, even though we only have the eight days from Christmas Eve to New Year's Eve, and all eight fell during Winter Efteling, which ran from 10 November to 1 February.

## Winter half-term and Easter

[Disney Adventure World](ref:disney-adventure-world) had its busiest day in February, on 24 February 2026, and the 23rd was one of its busiest days as well. On both days zones B and C in France were on their winter holidays; only zone A had been back at school since the Monday.

Easter Sunday was on 5 April, and the rush after it showed up mostly in England. [Alton Towers](ref:alton-towers) and [Legoland Windsor](ref:legoland-windsor) had their busiest day of the year on 8 April, [Chessington World of Adventures](ref:chessington-world-of-adventures) on 7 April, and [Thorpe Park](ref:thorpe-park) had one of its busiest days on 7 April too. The Tuesday and Wednesday after Easter both fell in the English Easter holidays, which in Surrey, where Thorpe Park is, ran from 28 March to 12 April. Easter Monday itself isn't on any of these lists.

In Germany the same days were among the busiest at [Legoland Deutschland](ref:legoland-deutschland) in Günzburg, whose busiest day was 9 April, and at [Plopsaland Deutschland](ref:plopsaland-deutschland) in Haßloch. These days fell in the Easter holidays too, which ran from 30 March to 11 April in Baden-Württemberg and until 10 April in Bavaria and Rhineland-Palatinate.

## Bank holidays and long weekends in May

On Friday 1 May, [Plopsaland De Panne](ref:plopsaland-belgium) had its longest waits of the year, and for Bellewaerde and [Parque de Atracciones de Madrid](ref:parque-de-atracciones-de-madrid) it made the year's top five. The Saturday after, 2 May, [Gardaland](ref:gardaland) on Lake Garda was busier than on any other day, and at Europa-Park and Legoland Deutschland that Saturday ranks among the five fullest days as well.

Ascension Day was Thursday 14 May, and French schools were off until Sunday 17 May, the long weekend the French call the pont de l'Ascension. Friday 15 May was among the busiest days at [Futuroscope](ref:futuroscope) near Poitiers and at Bellewaerde.

## Summer holidays from July to September

The first summer peaks came from England, and they came before the holidays had even started. 13 to 16 July were the four busiest days at [Blackpool Pleasure Beach](ref:blackpool-pleasure-beach), and at Thorpe Park 15 July was the busiest. Schools in Surrey had lessons until 17 July, and those in Lancashire, where Blackpool is, until 20 July. Who was in the queue on those school days we can't say, because a posted wait doesn't come with a head count.

On 21 July, a week after Blackpool, [Liseberg](ref:liseberg) in Gothenburg and [Gröna Lund](ref:grona-lund) in Stockholm had their busiest day, and [Legoland Billund](ref:legoland-billund) had its own on 23 July. For seven parks, 21 July was one of the five days with the longest waits, including [Djurs Sommerland](ref:djurs-sommerland) in Denmark and [Heide Park](ref:heide-park) in Soltau.

[Phantasialand](ref:phantasialand) in Brühl peaked on 6 August, and four of its five busiest days fall between 6 and 11 August, right in the middle of the summer holidays in North Rhine-Westphalia, which ran from 20 July to 1 September. On Tuesday 11 August, North Rhine-Westphalia, all three regions of the Netherlands, Flanders and the whole of France were on their summer holidays at the same time. That one Tuesday made the top five at eight parks in seven countries: Phantasialand, Bellewaerde, [Avonturenpark Hellendoorn](ref:avonturenpark-hellendoorn), [Ferrari Land](ref:ferrari-land), [Flamingo Land](ref:flamingo-land), Futuroscope, [Le Pal](ref:le-pal) and [Mirabilandia](ref:mirabilandia) near Ravenna, which we've only measured since August, on 46 days.

On 19 August Ferrari Land in Salou had its busiest day, and so did Le Pal and [Walibi Rhône-Alpes](ref:walibi-rhone-alpes) in France. England got busy once more at the end of August. [Paultons Park](ref:paultons-park) had its busiest day on 24 August, at Alton Towers it was one of the five worst, and Chessington and Legoland Windsor count 1 September among theirs. Last of all came [Fårup Sommerland](ref:farup-sommerland) in Denmark, with its busiest day on 31 August.

Phantasialand's July and August are level with the six December days we measured there in 2025, and in September the typical wait is less than half what it was in August.

## Autumn half-term and Halloween in October

Saturday 17 October 2026 matched 11 August and made the top five at eight parks: [Walibi Holland](ref:walibi-holland), [Walibi Belgium](ref:walibi-belgium), Thorpe Park, Heide Park, [Movie Park Germany](ref:movie-park-germany), Gardaland, [Nigloland](ref:nigloland) and Djurs Sommerland. For Walibi Holland, Nigloland and Djurs Sommerland it was the busiest day of all.

Several holidays started on that Saturday. North Rhine-Westphalia had its autumn holidays from 17 to 31 October, the Midden and Zuid regions of the Netherlands had their herfstvakantie from 17 to 25 October, and in France the All Saints' holidays began for every zone that same day. Lower Saxony was halfway through its autumn holidays, which ran there from 12 to 24 October. On top of that, Halloween was on almost everywhere: at Walibi Holland, 16 to 18 October were Halloween Fright Nights days, Djurs Sommerland ran its Magisk Halloween from 10 to 18 October, and at Heide Park the 17th was one of the long Halloween days.

![An old caravan with algae on its panels between straw bales, a kettle barbecue and tipped wooden crates in front | Nobody lives here any more. | right](/media/halloween-2026/beispiel-wohnwagen.jpg)

That makes October the busiest month at some parks. Fright Nights at Thorpe Park runs on selected dates from 2 October to 1 November, and October there is ahead of every summer month, as it is at Walibi Holland. Parc Astérix, whose Peur sur le Parc lasts from 3 October to 8 November, has October level with August. Walibi Belgium had its busiest day on 10 October, the first day of its Halloween event Ibilaw, and October there is well ahead of July, though so far on only eight days measured. Heide Park peaked as early as Saturday 3 October, German Unity Day, a week before Halloween started.

In Spain the national holiday on 12 October was a Monday, and over the long weekend before it, 10 and 11 October were both among the five worst days of the year at PortAventura, where Halloween had been running since 19 September. 10 October was one of the busiest days at Ferrari Land as well, and the 11th at [Parque Warner Madrid](ref:parque-warner-madrid). Twelve parks have one of their five busiest days in October, and the month isn't over. Phantasialand went the other way, with October so far its quietest month of the year.

## Which weekday is quietest at which park

Saturday was busier than Sunday at 25 of the 38 parks where we can compare all seven days of the week. It went the other way at only three, and all three are in Belgium and the Netherlands: Bellewaerde, Bobbejaanland and Walibi Holland. Monday to Friday, though, sit so close together at 32 of the 38 parks that it hardly matters which one your boss lets you take off.

At Ferrari Land you'll queue least on a Sunday. The two Disney parks near Paris barely differ from one day of the week to the next. For twelve parks in Germany, the Netherlands, Belgium and France we've looked at weekday, time of day and holidays more closely in a [separate comparison](/blog/theme-parks-quiet-days-and-hours).

## What time of day the queues are shortest

We went through the eight rides with the longest peak waits at 39 parks hour by hour. Of the 276 rides that were already running at opening time, 177 have their shortest hour of the day in the first one, or tie for it, and at those rides it pays to be at the turnstiles when the gates open. Legoland Billund's first hour is shorter than every later one on all eight rides. Thorpe Park's is the shortest on every ride that's running at opening, though only just on Hyperia, where the queue stays almost the same length from the first hour to the last.

Six parks work the other way round, and there the evening is the better time: PortAventura, Parc Astérix, Liseberg, Walibi Belgium, Bellewaerde and Futuroscope. PortAventura's big rides have queues almost as long in the first hour measured as at midday, and they get shorter from then until the evening. ## What the rain did

On rainy days people queued less at all twelve parks in our [rain comparison](/blog/theme-parks-in-the-rain) than on dry days in the same month, and at Movie Park Germany by almost a third. A rainy day there means at least two millimetres of rain. So a forecast of rain for Saturday is more a reason to go than to stay at home, especially to Movie Park, as long as no thunderstorm is on the way.

## Where Europe's longest queues are

[Crush's Coaster](ref:disney-adventure-world/crushs-coaster) at Disney Adventure World, named after the turtle from "Finding Nemo", and [Uncharted](ref:portaventura-park/uncharted) at PortAventura are ahead of every other ride in Europe in our ranking. Disney tried out a Fastpass system on Crush's Coaster as early as 2008 to deal with the long queues. The park never brought it in, and a single-rider queue was added later. So Disney has been trying to shorten this queue since 2008, and it still tops our ranking.

Disneyland Park's longest queue is for the dark ride [Peter Pan's Flight](ref:/parks/europe/france/paris/disneyland-park/peter-pans-flight), longer than for any roller coaster, even though what you ride in there is a small pirate ship hanging from a rail under the ceiling.

[Red Force](ref:ferrari-land/red-force) at Ferrari Land and [Hyperia](ref:thorpe-park/hyperia) at Thorpe Park are further ahead of their park's number two than any other ride we measured all year. Hyperia also has the longest queue of any roller coaster in Britain, and at Alton Towers [The Smiler](ref:alton-towers/the-smiler) tops the ranking.

## How the new rides of 2026 did straight away

No new ride of 2026 ranks as high in its park as the family coaster [100% Wolf](ref:plopsaland-deutschland/family-coaster-100percent-wolf) at Plopsaland Deutschland. It opened on 22 March and tops the park's ranking, ahead of Expedition GeForce. It's tight at the top, though, because at Plopsaland Deutschland you don't queue long for anything.

[Frozen Ever After](ref:disney-adventure-world/frozen-ever-after) opened a week later, on 29 March, with the World of Frozen area at Disney Adventure World, and it has the park's second-longest queue, behind Crush's Coaster.

World of PAW Patrol opened at Chessington on 3 May, and since then you've queued longer for its two rides, Chase's Mountain Mission and Zuma's Hovercraft Adventure, than for anything else in the park except Dragon's Fury and Vampire. Two more family rides landed a little further back: the Bluey ride "Here Come the Grannies", which opened at Alton Towers at the end of March, finished seventh in its park, and [Stormvåg](ref:liseberg/stormvag), the new family attraction that opened on 14 May, fifth at Liseberg.

Right at the top of Legoland Windsor's ranking is a play zone: the LEGO Pokémon Play Zone in LEGO Festival, which was only there from 20 July to 31 August. Its first place rests on just 24 days measured, all from those six weeks.

## Where you'll queue longest in Germany

[Taron](ref:phantasialand/taron) at Phantasialand has the longest queue in Germany. Heide Park's [Colossos](ref:heide-park/colossos-kampf-der-giganten), Flug der Dämonen and bobsled are so close that only a photo finish could settle the top spot. Europa-Park has three rides sharing first place: Voltron Nevera, Arthur and Wodan. From 8:15 am the queue for Voltron Nevera is hardly worth mentioning, because in summer only guests of the park's hotels are let in then, 45 minutes before everyone else.

## How our forecast learns from the measurements

[Fancast](/fancast), the model behind our crowd calendar, learns from the same measurements. It's retrained every morning on all the data since 24 December 2025 and works with 75 features. Among them are the school holidays in the park's state or region, the holidays in neighbouring regions, bridge days, long weekends, rain in the last three hours and the wait at the same hour a week earlier. The day after, we compare every forecast with the measured wait and publish the average error in minutes on the Fancast page.

## How we measure

Wherever a park publishes its wait times, we fetch them every five minutes from three public sources at once: [ThemeParks.wiki](https://themeparks.wiki), [Wartezeiten.app](https://www.wartezeiten.app) and [Queue-Times.com](https://queue-times.com). When the sources disagree, the majority wins, then the median, then the mean. What we store is the posted wait rounded to five minutes, so it's the park's estimate. Nobody from park.fan stands next to the ride with a stopwatch.

Only the standby queue counts, and only while the ride is running. Single-rider queues, paid fast lanes and virtual queues are left out, and so are periods when a ride is down or closed. Days on which the park was closed according to its own schedule drop out entirely.

For every ride and every hour we calculate two values from the measured waits. The median, or P50, is the middle value: half of that hour's readings are below it. P90 is the 90th percentile, with nine in ten readings below it. We drop any hour that has only one reading. We use the median rather than the mean because it barely moves when a ride shows a very long wait for a few minutes after a breakdown.

If Taron's row shows a figure under "Typical (median)", that's the average of its hourly medians over every hour measured. "Peak (P90)" is the same for the hourly P90s, and both are rounded to five minutes. A day measured is a day on which the ride was measured for at least an hour. A ride enters a park's ranking after 20 days measured. Where rides from different parks sit side by side, each needs 60 days measured before it gets any minutes there.

We pick each park's headliners by hand. For the park average we average their typical wait per weekday first, then across the seven weekdays, weighted by the number of days measured. "Longest queue" names the ride with the highest median. Only rides with at least 100 days measured qualify, so that a ride with few days measured doesn't decide the park.

A park's busiest days are the days with the longest typical wait at its headliners. For the chapters about the calendar we put the five busiest days of every park side by side and counted how often each date turns up. We left out parks whose wait times keep running overnight or that post the same time every day, among them Energylandia, Toverland, Cinecittà World and Rulantica.

We only name a quietest weekday if it's below the park average and at most one other day ties with it. Every weekday in the comparison has to be measured at least 70 per cent as often as the most-measured one. If fewer than four weekdays make it, we don't name one. That affects parks that open on weekdays only in the holidays, where a Tuesday would almost always be a summer day.

Split by calendar month, "Typical" is the same typical wait at the headliners and "Peak" is the average of the daily peaks at those rides. By hour, it's the eight rides at a park with the highest P90, hour by hour with the average of their hourly medians, and each ride's busiest hour is in bold. An hour only becomes a column if it was measured on at least ten days. It also needs at least 40 per cent as many days measured as the best-measured hour, and at least half of the rides have to be measured in it.

Rides, parks and months cover the last two years, hours the last year. In the first year of measuring, every table therefore goes back to the first day we measured; only after that do the oldest days drop out of the hourly tables. We recalculate the figures once a day. We don't adjust for weather, school holidays or events. To cite a figure, give park.fan as the source, the start of measurement and the day you read the table.

Every park has these tables on a page of its own, for example [average wait times at Disney Adventure World](/parks/europe/france/paris/disney-adventure-world/average-wait-times). Parks that don't publish their wait times are missing. [Hansa-Park](ref:hansa-park) in Sierksdorf, for one, shows them only in its [own app](https://www.hansapark.de/app/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Which days were busiest at Europe's theme parks in 2026?

Saturday 17 October 2026 and Tuesday 11 August 2026. Each was one of the five days with the longest waits of the year at eight of the parks park.fan measures. Then there's the week between Christmas and New Year, when Europa-Park, Efteling and Parc Astérix had their busiest day.

### Is it worth being at the park when it opens?

Usually, yes. The first hour was the shortest of the day, or tied for it, on 177 of 276 rides at 39 European parks. Six parks, among them PortAventura and Parc Astérix, have shorter queues in the evening.

### Is Sunday quieter than Saturday at a theme park?

At most parks, yes: at 25 of 38, Saturday was busier. Bellewaerde, Bobbejaanland and Walibi Holland were busier on Sunday.

### When will Europe's theme parks get busy in 2027?

If 2027 goes the way 2026 did, first around Easter, which falls on 28 March. In North Rhine-Westphalia the Easter holidays start on 22 March, in Staffordshire on 29 March. Ascension Day is on 6 May, and the summer holidays in North Rhine-Westphalia run from 19 July to 31 August. In the autumn, the herfstvakantie in the Noord and Midden regions of the Netherlands starts on Saturday 16 October, and in the Zuid region on 23 October, the same day as the autumn holidays in North Rhine-Westphalia.

### Where are wait times longest in Europe?

At Disney Adventure World near Paris and at PortAventura and Ferrari Land on Spain's Costa Daurada.

### Which roller coaster in Germany has the longest wait?

Taron at Phantasialand in Brühl.

### When is Disneyland Paris least busy?

January 2026 was the quietest month at Disneyland Park. The busiest were June to August, October and the last days of December 2025. Disney Adventure World, the resort's second park, barely changes from month to month, and its average wait is higher than Disneyland Park's.

### Which park in Britain has the longest waits?

Alton Towers and Thorpe Park, closely followed by Chessington World of Adventures and Legoland Windsor.

### Which theme park in Spain has the longest waits?

PortAventura in Salou, just ahead of Ferrari Land in the same resort according to park.fan's measurements. At the two Madrid parks, Parque de Atracciones and Parque Warner, the queues are much shorter.
