## New

- The Orlando Crowd Calendar at park.fan/orlando covers Walt Disney World, Universal with Epic
  Universe, SeaWorld, their water parks and the day trips, 16 parks in November 2026. Each square is
  one park on one day, with the big rides' wait at their busiest in minutes. At the top you see one
  day in Orlando time and the park with the shortest queues at each resort, with a note when a park
  closes early that day for an evening event. Tap a day for its opening hours, Early Entry, evening
  events, seasons, the drive from your hotel area, the five big rides and "Plan this day". The
  calendar is in the More menu, and each of the next eleven months has a page of its own.
- The trip planner on the same page puts each of your parks on the day of your trip with the
  shortest queues, for trips of up to 21 days. You give it your dates and parks; the hotel, the
  hotel area, whether you want to see Magic Kingdom's fireworks, rest days and park hopping wait
  under "Fine-tune". Early closings, party nights, the hotels' early and late hours and, with a Park
  Hopper, two parks a day go into the plan. After a park that closes early there's an idea for the
  evening, in a second park with a hopper ticket or at Disney Springs or CityWalk. The answer opens
  on the minutes per ride you save against an average day of your trip, or says that the order
  barely matters.
- The first question is whether you already have tickets. If you do, you pick them by name for
  Disney, Universal, SeaWorld or LEGOLAND, the UK 14-day tickets included, and your trip dates fill
  in from them. Every park day then stays inside the dates its ticket is valid, and the week view
  marks which ticket day it is, such as "Disney day 2 of 4". If you don't, you get the tickets that
  fit and from when, for example 6 days Disney from 7 February, then 4 days Universal from 16
  February. Under the details you see what a Park Hopper would add on which dates, and where to buy
  each ticket: the cheapest authorized seller we found, Attraction Tickets and the park's own shop,
  with the links in your language first.
- You see the plan as an agenda, a week or a month. In the week view each day sits on the day
  planner's hour axis, so a park that closes early has a shorter block and a party night is hatched
  after it. You can pin a day or swap it with another, and each swap shows its cost in minutes
  before you make it. Any day, or the whole trip, goes into the day planner; you can also save it as
  a calendar file in Orlando time or share a link to this exact plan.
- If your dates are still open, pick "Find the best dates", a trip of 3 to 21 days and a period,
  such as the next 12 months, a year or a season. The three best stretches come back, if you like
  only those inside your region's school holidays, each with its big-ride wait and how far that is
  below the period's average. Heat and the hurricane season show as short notes, and a stretch the
  hurricane season pushed down is marked "ranked lower". Three pages give the best time to visit
  Orlando, Disney World and Universal Orlando in 2027 and move on to the next year each September.
  Each opens on the three quietest 10-day trips with "Plan these dates", followed by a bar for every
  week, a row for every month with the big rides' wait, events, school holidays and the usual
  weather, the best and worst weeks, and links that open the planner inside German, Dutch and
  English school holidays.
- In the chat, Parky plans Orlando trips: which park on which day, the best dates for a trip of a
  given length, events and party nights, and the drive from your hotel to a park. Ask him to file
  the trip in the day planner and every day lands there, two parks on a park-hopping day, and you
  can take back each day on its own; he also opens the trip on the Orlando page with your dates and
  parks filled in. In your day plan he moves, changes and removes rides, adds a show, marks a ride as
  done, clears a day and shares the plan, and he takes back his own change as long as you haven't
  touched that day since. He sets and removes alerts for a ride or a show, saves favourites and knows
  the rides you ticked off as ridden, so "Which headliners am I still missing?" gets an answer. His
  answers can now include a crowd calendar of up to six weeks, one day's crowds and weather, shows
  with their times, restaurants marked where you need a reservation, and up to three guides, news
  items or glossary terms, and a ride in his answer carries the ride page's badges, maintenance and
  out of season included.
- Parky wears a pumpkin in October, a Christmas tree from 1 to 26 December, a party hat on 31
  December and 1 January and a wool hat and scarf until the end of February. On a park page
  he matches that park's weather with an umbrella, a thunder cloud, snowflakes, fog or sun, and "Try
  on an outfit" on the Parky page shows every look. His speech bubbles there follow the live data,
  such as a walk-on of up to 10 minutes, a queue over 60, an outage or a show starting in 15 to 30
  minutes, and in the hour before opening and the last half hour before closing he talks about the
  clock. In the chat window and the homepage photo he only blinks, follows the pointer and sleeps.
- Planned maintenance, until now a badge on the ride page alone, also shows in the wait time
  calendar, the day planner and on ride cards. In the calendar a day with rides in maintenance
  carries a wrench and their number, at Efteling four at once from 4 January 2027; hovering names
  each ride and until when, and the day's panel lists them under "Closed for maintenance". The day
  planner lists the rides in maintenance on the planned day. A ride in maintenance is greyed out in
  the day planner's search and refused on the timeline with the reason, and its plan button on the
  ride page reads "Maintenance until …". A ride card shows "Maintenance until", "Maintenance from" or
  "Maintenance on" with the date, and every coming window on hover or tap. The live status,
  "Refurbishment" in English until now, is called "Maintenance" too.
- A park page now opens with the cover of the park's guide, the whole picture a link, and under it
  the park's news of the last 30 days. "Plan a day at …" is the main button, and the website,
  tickets, Wikipedia and social media sit behind "More". The city and the pin open the route in Apple
  Maps on Apple devices and in Google Maps everywhere else. A ride page opens the same way, with the
  post about the ride, the park's guide or the ride photo and "Add to plan" as its button; what
  matters for your visit (minimum height, single rider, Quick Pass) comes before top speed,
  inversions, ride type, maker and opening year.
- A park that is closed for a week or more says on its page when it opens again. A yellow panel above
  "In the park today" gives the day with its weekday, such as Thursday, 25 March 2027 for
  Hansa-Park, and how many weeks away that is, next to Parky asleep in a picture of the season: on
  his sled in a bobble hat in winter, in a heap of leaves in autumn, among the tulips in spring.
  Every ride page of the park shows the same panel. It appears when the next opening is at least a
  week away, for the winter break and for a break between seasons such as Movie Park's between
  Halloween and Christmas, so a park open only at weekends doesn't show it.

## Improved

- A park's statistics and twelve-month outlook moved from the park page to the bottom of its wait
  time calendar, and the park's guides now come before the nearby parks.
- In the day planner a ride you tick off shows how far its wait was from the estimate (+10, −5,
  ±0), and the day's total now counts walking minutes as well as waiting and riding. A plan for today
  starts from the current time. If "Optimise the day" can't run, a message says your plan is
  unchanged, next to a button to try again.
- On the homepage, the cards of parks near you say how soon a closed park opens, in hours or as
  tomorrow or a weekday, with the exact time on hover. The cards of popular parks show what the
  nearby ones show, the average wait and crowd level while open, where they used to say only "See
  wait times".
- Before you type, search lists up to four of your saved parks after the parks near you or the
  popular ones, on the homepage and in the search window. On the homepage the / key puts the cursor
  in the search field over the photo only while the whole field is in view and opens the search
  window otherwise, so letters no longer land in a field you scrolled past.
- Search finds a glossary term by its other names too, and Parky also by a word from its definition:
  asked in German about a "Blockbremse", he now finds "Block Brake", where he found nothing before.
- In a park, the compass on the homepage names the next show of the day under its rides, with the
  time in park time, the distance and a link to the park's shows.
- On a phone, the share buttons start with "Share via app", which opens the phone's own share menu
  and reaches Instagram, Messenger, Telegram and any other app installed. The footer links park.fan
  on Instagram and Facebook.
- Parky's chats are deleted after 7 days instead of 90. The line under the chat's input field says
  Parky is an AI assistant whose answers may contain errors and come without warranty, and links to
  the Parky section of the privacy policy, which now also lists the day plan, alerts and favourites
  that go with each question.
- The imprint now says on what terms park.fan's pages and forecasts may be reused.

## Fixed

- On a phone, the homepage and park pages with nearby parks, such as Efteling's, could be pushed
  sideways, and the day planner looked cut off.
- In or near a park, the homepage moved its search field once it had found you. The welcome line is
  gone, the park's section sits under the row of cards, and on a phone the compass comes after the
  news, so it no longer pushes the news heading off the first screen.
- The homepage cards of rides near you kept the waits from page load when your location came from
  your connection rather than from GPS. While the tab is open they refresh every 5 minutes. The news
  under the photo no longer stays blank until you scroll.
- On park pages, the twelve-month outlook showed Phantasialand as closed from February to May 2027,
  a guess past the park's published season; such a month now reads "No forecast", and "Closed" only
  when every remaining day is closed. Official weather warnings for hours the park is shut, such as
  frost from 10 pm to 9 am, no longer appear. Sorting by shortest wait now sorts the headliners too,
  where the whole first screen used to stay as it was. The "As of" time above the ride list showed a
  closed park's closing time all night and could run up to 5 minutes ahead of the data; it now gives
  the time the data was read.
- The day planner put a show at midnight before the park's opening, which moved the last show of the
  day at Magic Kingdom and Disneyland Park in Anaheim to the start.
- Saved shows and restaurants in the favourites menu and on the favourites page now lead to their
  park's shows or restaurants.
- Times on Parky's cards followed your device's clock, so a day in Orlando planned from London
  showed London times. They are in the park's time now, like everywhere else on the site. With his
  chat open on a phone, the page behind it no longer scrolls along, also with the keyboard up.
- With reduced motion, Parky's rides and dreams stand still, even when you change the setting after
  the page has loaded.
- A shared link to the day planner or to the photo contribution page showed a preview with an empty
  heading over a count of 0 parks, and an author page's preview was titled "author". They carry
  their page titles now, and an author's preview shows the name and a short bio.
