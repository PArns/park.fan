On 15 April 2027 [Tokyo Disneyland](ref:tokyo-disneyland) opens "Sugar Rush: Sweet Rescue" in Tomorrowland, the first attraction anywhere based on Disney's Wreck-It Ralph. The ride was unveiled at the D23 fan event in August, and on 27 October the Disney Parks Blog published the details of the ride, the shop and the dates leading up to the opening.

## What you do on the ride

You ride with Ralph, Vanellope and their friends through Sugar Rush, the racing game. King Candy wants to take over the world of video games, and he's turned the game's sweets, gummy bears, lollipops, chewing gum and ice cream, into Sugar Bugs that are meant to eat the kingdom. Your job is to turn them back into their "kawaii" form, Japanese for cute.

The vehicles have scanners built in for that. You score points by the type and number of Sugar Bugs you've turned back. Your result shows up on the screens in the vehicle at the end, and for a limited time afterwards in the Tokyo Disney Resort app too, in case anyone wants to argue about a rematch that evening. Photos taken during the ride go straight to "My Photos" in the app, where you can buy them.

Disney hasn't announced a height requirement yet, or whether the ride will be on Premier Access. How Premier Access and the Standby Pass work at the resort is explained in our [Tokyo Disney Resort guide](/blog/tokyo-disney-resort-wait-times-tips).

## What happens before 15 April

- from 12 January 2027: a smartphone mini-game you can play at home, with a special version in selected areas of the park that uses the surroundings
- from 18 February: decorations and photo spots for the ride in Tomorrowland
- from 17 March: the Popping Station popcorn stand with a new Sugar-Lime flavour
- from 18 March: themed projections on Cinderella Castle
- on 15 April: the ride and the Pixel Prism shop

Popping Station looks out over Tomorrowland Plaza. Sugar-Lime is sweet and sour, meant to recall the Sugar Bugs. The popcorn carts across the park sell it as well, but only at Tokyo Disneyland, not at DisneySea. The resort already does soy sauce and butter popcorn, shrimp and garlic, and strawberry napoleon, so sweet and sour is close to the safe choice.

Pixel Prism sits right next to the ride and is designed as a digital marketplace, with fibre-optic touches, a pixel-pattern floor and a large glowing pixel structure at the entrance. It sells plush toys, sweets and toy copies of the scanners from the ride vehicles. Restaurants at Tokyo Disneyland also get their own dishes for the ride when it opens.

## A spot with a birthday

The new ride stands where Buzz Lightyear's Astro Blasters used to run. Buzz opened on 15 April 2004 and ran until 31 October 2024. Its closure was announced in April 2024, together with a Wreck-It Ralph attraction to replace it. That was first planned for 2026 and later pushed back to spring 2027.

Tokyo Disneyland opened on 15 April 1983, so the new ride opens on the park's 44th birthday. For Buzz in 2004 it was the 21st.

## Tomorrowland is being rebuilt

Sugar Rush is one part of a larger rebuild. The old Space Mountain closed on 31 July 2024, and the new one is going up 80 metres away. Since 13 August 2026 it's been called "Space Mountain Earthrise", and it's due to open in 2027, though there's no date yet. It gets a newly developed ride system, and the ride starts with a launch from Earth into space.

Tomorrowland Plaza is taking the old building's place. Its structures are modelled on fire, water, earth and wood, the lighting on the sun and moon, and in the evening light and sound are added. Disney names people and nature in harmony as the idea behind it. The Element Express quick-service restaurant, with snacks and drinks, also opens on the plaza, with no date given.

Tomorrowland already has [The Happy Ride with Baymax](ref:tokyo-disneyland/the-happy-ride-with-baymax), in the park since September 2020, [Monsters, Inc. Ride & Go Seek!](ref:tokyo-disneyland/monsters-inc-ride-and-go-seek) and [Star Tours: The Adventures Continue](ref:tokyo-disneyland/star-tours-the-adventures-continue).
