[Pirates of the Caribbean](ref:magic-kingdom-park/pirates-of-the-caribbean) at [Magic Kingdom](ref:magic-kingdom-park) closes for a refurbishment on 11 January 2027 and won't sail again until late 2027. That's what Disney's page for the attraction says, and on 19 November the Disney Parks Blog added that the Imagineers will use the break to put in new effects, including in the Dead Man's Cove and Hurricane Lagoon scenes. Disney hasn't named a reopening date yet.

## The last boats leave on 10 January

So the ride is still running over Christmas and New Year's Eve, and if you're in Orlando during the first week of January you'll still get on. On the very day the pirates get their time off, Disney After Hours starts at Magic Kingdom: evenings on a separate ticket, on selected dates until 19 July.

## What's changing, and who'll miss it

Disney hasn't shown yet what exactly changes in Dead Man's Cove and Hurricane Lagoon. The Parks Blog calls the closure a routine break and says the new effects will build on the ride's story.

Families lose the most here, because Pirates has no minimum height. You spend about eight and a half minutes in a boat, drifting through 17th-century Caribbean port towns, past a haunted cove, between a fort and a galleon trading cannon fire, and through a town in flames. Captain Jack Sparrow from the films turns up in three scenes, and along the way there's one drop of just over four metres. The queue runs through Castillo del Morro, a fort modelled on the fortress in San Juan, Puerto Rico, and "Yo Ho, Yo Ho (A Pirate's Life for Me)" follows you back to the hotel without needing a ticket of its own.

For an Orlando trip in 2027 I'd plan without Pirates after 10 January and only count on it again once Disney gives a date. A day without the pirates works best when you build it around the rides with the longest queues, and our [guide to Magic Kingdom wait times](/blog/magic-kingdom-wait-times-tips) is there for exactly that. If Universal and the other Disney parks are on the list too, the [Orlando itinerary for Disney World and Universal](/blog/orlando-itinerary-disney-universal) helps you put the days in order.

## There are still pirates in Adventureland

In Adventureland, the pirate tavern The Beak and Barrel stays open. It opened on 29 August 2025, and the backstory says it was founded by rum smuggler Captain Meridian "Merry" Goldwyn and her first mate Rummy, who bears a resemblance to the Barker Bird that used to sit at the entrance to Pirates. Guests of every age are welcome, and two of the drinks are alcohol-free. You can reserve a spot, and the shop next door, the Plaza del Sol Caribe Bazaar, sells the T-shirt and the plush parrot.

## A ride Florida wasn't meant to get

The first Pirates ride opened at Disneyland in California in 1967. It was one of the last attractions whose development Walt Disney followed himself in the 1960s. It started life as a wax museum idea and ended up being built with Audio-Animatronics.

For Magic Kingdom, which opened in 1971, Disney first planned without pirates, reasoning that a Caribbean ride so close to the real Caribbean wouldn't have the same appeal as it did in California. The Western River Expedition was meant to go there instead. Pirates got built after all, the construction wall read "Opening Christmas 1973", and the first boats set off on 15 December 1973. Disney added Jack Sparrow in 2006, and in 2018 the ride got a new auction scene where the pirates sell off loot rather than a bride.

Disney shows how Pirates was written in the "Storytelling" episode of the video series "We Call It Imagineering" on Walt Disney Imagineering's YouTube channel, and later on Disney+ as well. The same episode covers Indiana Jones and the Myth of the Jade Serpent at Animal Kingdom and Piston Peak National Park, the Cars area going up at Magic Kingdom on part of what used to be Frontierland.
