# A blog card is a row on phones (REQUIREMENT)

One standing rule. It is indexed from the repo's [`CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md), which carries the rule in one line and links here for the reasoning, the measurements and the counter-examples.

park, ride and blog cards are one object — a photo under two sheets of frosted glass — and all three drop the photo below `sm`. A park card collapsing onto its panels said everything it had to say, but in 146 px, one per row, so it now makes the same split (see [design system → the park card is a row too](docs/design/design-system.md#the-park-card-is-a-row-too)); a post card says a title and a date in ~200 px, with the cover it was chosen for hidden and the `-mb-4`/`-mt-4` that were meant to lay the panels over the picture overlapping **each other** by 32 px, so the panel below paints over the last line of the excerpt. So below `sm` the card is not rendered at all: `BlogPostRow` is (`sm:hidden` / `hidden sm:grid`), which is the `compact` variant the homepage's lead column already used. It gets **no variants of its own** — the first version had two, a border and a three-line title where the row replaced a card against an `-mx-2` bleed and two lines where it sat in a list, and on the homepage the two meet (below `lg` the lead post is a row and so are the four under it), so the lead came out inset by 8 px, boxed, and carrying a reading time its neighbours did not have. The **gap** is the same decision one level out: the grids holding these cards were spaced for cards (`gap-4`–`gap-6`), which reads as a stack of separate blocks rather than one list, so all of them drop to `gap-2` below `sm` and keep their own spacing above it — including **both** halves of `LatestBlogSection`'s `lead`, whose two gaps have to agree or the lead detaches from its list. Three more things are load-bearing. It is **two markups, not one responsive tree**, because the glass is a block of inline styles and no breakpoint switches an inline style off — the alternative was moving the park and ride panels into CSS with it. Both photo layers of the hidden card claim **`96px`** for the phone segment of their `sizes`, which is what the row beside them actually paints: left at `100vw` a card marked `priority` (the first in `BlogPostGrid`) preloaded a full-width cover for a `display:none` element, and matching the row means both pick the same srcset candidate, one request instead of two. And the thumbnail carries the card's `objectPosition`, so a focal point tuned in the admin holds at 96 × 64. The **article page** pays the same kind of bill one screen down: the banner's `pt-32` is clearance for the floating header and frames nothing (the banner is content-driven at 642 px against a 490 px `min-h`), so it halves below `sm`, and the table of contents is `hidden lg:block` — in a sidebar it is navigation, on a phone it was **998 px** of chapter links between the breadcrumb and the first sentence, on every post. Together with 16 px off the space above the 30 px breadcrumb pill, the first word moves from 782 px to 698 px. The trade is real and worth revisiting: a phone reader can no longer jump between chapters. The **five full-bleed heroes** (`relative isolate -mt-12 flex min-h-[Nvh] items-end overflow-hidden`: the guide at 86vh, Fancast, the best-travel-time hub and the blog index at 78vh through the shared `Hero`, the blog article at 58vh) each spent a phone's whole first screen on one picture and a title. Below `sm` the headline moves to the **top** and the page pulls its own first section up over the lower part of the photo — the image keeps every pixel and usually gets taller. First content at 390 px: guide 782 → 550, Fancast 722 → 533, best time 714 → 553, index 658 → 482, article 594 → 554. The shared `Hero` takes it as a `flowInto` prop (three callers); `GuideHero` and `BlogPostBanner` are one page each and carry it directly. Two things make it hold: the upward tint gets its mirror over the top third, because the headline would otherwise land on the one barely-tinted part of the photo; and the overlap is **safe by construction** — `HERO_FLOW_INTO_PULL` (176 px) pairs with the hero's mobile `pb-48` (192 px) and **the padding must stay larger than the pull**, so a long headline grows the `max(78vh, content + padding)` hero and carries the pulled-up section down with it. Hand-tuned it was wrong at once: at 360 px the German tagline ran 10 px past the first card while French had 117 px spare; by construction the minimum over six locales × four widths is 16 px. See [design system → the blog card is a row on phones](docs/design/design-system.md#the-blog-card-is-a-row-on-phones).
