On Monday, 9 November, the Netzwerk NSG Ententeich handed 5,656 signatures for its petition against the expansion of [Phantasialand](ref:phantasialand) to the town administration in Brühl, RTL West and Radio Erft report. The petition needs 2,536 valid signatures from eligible voters in Brühl, and the network had been collecting since 28 September. The park’s plans for the land and the background to the dispute are in our [post on the start of the petition](/news/phantasialand-expansion-petition).

Inside the park you won’t notice the dispute over the Ententeich, because Phantasialand can only build on the land once a zoning plan is in force, and the town is still working on that.

## What the council has to decide now

First the town checks how many of the signatures are valid. Then the council decides whether the petition is admissible. North Rhine-Westphalia’s municipal code excludes zoning plans from petitions. A petition is only allowed against the decision to start a planning procedure. The network wants to revoke the decisions of 9 July with which the planning committee started the procedure for zoning plan 06.01. Whether a petition may demand that kind of revocation, the network itself leaves open on its website.

Once the council has declared the petition admissible, the town can’t decide or carry out anything that runs against it until the referendum. The council can then adopt the demand itself, and the procedure would be over. If it rejects it, Brühl has to vote within three months. In the planning committee on 9 July, the CDU, SPD and FDP/Volt voted for the procedure, with the Greens and the Left against. The same groups now decide in the council. The town hasn’t announced a date for the session yet.

## What a referendum needs

In the referendum, a yes majority only counts if it adds up to at least 20 percent of all eligible voters. With the 36,227 eligible voters the network works with, that’s 7,246 votes, nearly 1,600 more than the signatures it has now handed in.

If the referendum passes, the procedure for the expansion is over. The result has the effect of a council decision, and for the first two years only a new referendum called by the council can change it. The expert reports Phantasialand is paying for in the procedure haven’t been published yet. They cover seven fields, including species protection, noise and the water balance, and the water report also looks at what would happen to the Ententeich. I’d have liked to see the results before Brühl votes on the land.

## What the park and the supporters say

The park explains the expansion with short-break visitors on its ["Standortsicherung" page](https://www.phantasialand.de/de/rechtliches/standortsicherung/). To keep more guests overnight, it says it needs attractions for longer stays, and there’s no room for them on its roughly 28 hectares. With the roughly 15 hectares the regional plan has set aside for it since 2013, Phantasialand would grow to just over 40 hectares. That would still leave it a little smaller than [Movie Park Germany](ref:movie-park-germany) in Bottrop, which gives its own size as about 45 hectares.

A [counter-petition](/news/phantasialand-expansion-online-petition) on openPetition has been making the case for the expansion since May. It runs until 23 November. By early November, 211 people had signed, 52 of them from Brühl. Only once 740 signatures from Brühl come together does openPetition ask the council members for a statement.
