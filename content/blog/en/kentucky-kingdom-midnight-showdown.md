On November 5, [Kentucky Kingdom](ref:kentucky-kingdom) in Louisville announced Midnight Showdown, a wooden roller coaster that opens inside a building in spring 2027. The park calls it the world's largest indoor wooden coaster. It's a family coaster themed to a horse race, and it'll stand in Bluegrass Downs, a new area. The park hasn't announced an opening day yet.

## What Midnight Showdown is

The wooden track runs 1,130 feet (about 344 meters), and the structure stands more than three stories tall. Two trains run on it, each with five cars that seat two riders side by side, so ten people per train. Top speed is 32 mph, a little over 51 km/h. The layout packs in eleven airtime moments, roughly one every 100 feet, each one a short spell where you and your seat stop agreeing. The ride runs in the dark with light, sound and animated effects, and the race starts to the clatter of hooves and a shout of "And they're off!"

The Gravity Group of Cincinnati designed it. The same company did the [Kentucky Flyer](ref:kentucky-kingdom/kentucky-flyer), a wooden coaster with twelve airtime moments that opened in April 2019. Midnight Showdown is going into the building that used to house the 5D theater. The park's website doesn't list a height requirement for the new ride yet.

## Bluegrass Downs and Happy Valley Farm

Bluegrass Downs gets white fences, turrets and jockey silk colors everywhere. Five rides the park already has move in under new names. Mad Hatter becomes Jolly Juleps, teacups spinning around a giant mint julep cup. It's probably the only mint julep in Kentucky that leaves you dizzy without any bourbon. Scream Extreme turns into Whirlwind. In the story the park tells around the area, that's the name of the winner of a midnight race. Musical Carousel becomes My Old Kentucky Carousel, Zeppelin becomes Soaring Silks, and the bumper cars run as Bumper Derby.

Happy Valley Farm, the second new area, is set on a Kentucky farm with a barn in the middle, sunflowers, red gingham banners and bluegrass music. Eight rides get a new theme there: Jump Around becomes Friendly Frogs, Rowdy Racers becomes Loopy Lambs and Tin Lizzies becomes Country Cruisers. The [Lightning Run](ref:kentucky-kingdom/lightning-run) coaster is renamed Stampede Run, and riders round up animals that have broken loose. [Flying Fox](ref:kentucky-kingdom/flying-fox) stands in Happy Valley Farm too.

## Season Pass and tickets

The 2027 Season Passes are already on sale. Silver costs $69.99, Gold $89.99 and Diamond $134.99, all plus tax. The regular prices are $99.99, $119.99 and $209.99. All three are good right away for Christmas at Kentucky Kingdom and then for the whole 2027 season in the theme park and at the Hurricane Bay water park. Everyone from age six pays the same price, kids three and under get in free, and children who are three to five when they sign up can get a free Pre-K Pass. Online you can pay in eight monthly installments from $8.75, plus a $6 fee per pass.

Gold and Diamond get you 50 percent off a regular one-day ticket at seven other Herschend properties, among them Dollywood in Pigeon Forge and [Silver Dollar City](/news/silver-dollar-city-old-time-christmas-2026) in Branson. Diamond also includes one Skip-the-Line pass for a ride on Flying Fox between May 8 and August 8, 2027.

A one-day ticket starts at $24.99 online if you buy it for a fixed date at least 24 hours ahead, and costs $49.99 at the gate. Two consecutive days start at $34.99 online. If you'd come twice and pay at the gate, the Silver pass already costs less. Guests 15 and under need to be with someone 21 or older to get in.

## Christmas in the park

Christmas at Kentucky Kingdom runs on select days from November 21 to January 2 and is included with a day ticket. Until November 20, a Christmas ticket costs $24.99 online for everyone age four and up. The park puts up more than 1.5 million lights and over 300 Christmas trees. At the Blizzard Bluff snow hill you ride a tube 175 feet (about 53 meters) down real snow, in one of six lanes. The water rides and a few other attractions stay closed over the winter.

## Getting there and parking

The park sits on the grounds of the Kentucky Exposition Center in Louisville, where Interstates 65 and 264 meet, at 937 Phillips Lane. It's about a two-hour drive from Indianapolis and from Cincinnati, two and a half from Nashville. You enter the fairgrounds through Gate 1, 4 or 6. Gate 2 is closed for now. Parking costs $15 per car and is collected by the Exposition Center. With a Season Pass you park free on operating days.

## The park's history

Kentucky Kingdom opened in 1987, ran as Six Flags Kentucky Kingdom from 1998 to 2009 and then sat closed until guests came back in 2014. Herschend took over the park in 2021 and has put almost $50 million into it since. In 2025 it added Discovery Meadow, an area with three new attractions.

Flying Fox opened on May 28, 2026: a $14 million suspended family coaster from Vekoma with 1,380 feet (about 421 meters) of track. Riders need to be 38 inches (97 cm) with a companion or 48 inches (122 cm) to ride alone. It was the park's first new coaster since the Kentucky Flyer. Midnight Showdown will be its seventh.
