[Lake Compounce](ref:lake-compounce) in Bristol, Connecticut, spends the whole 2027 season celebrating the 100th birthday of its Wildcat wooden coaster. The gates open on Saturday, May 8. The park announced the date and its 2027 event lineup on November 16. Crocodile Cove, the water park, follows three weeks later on Saturday, May 29.

## What's planned for the Wildcat's birthday

The anniversary opens with Wildcat Weekends in May; the park will name the exact days later. New this year is the Wildcat Dance Party, where Ryder the Wildcat, the park's mascot, joins in on the dance floor. Season pass holders get a gift on opening day.

> [!QUOTE]
> As Wildcat celebrates 100 years, we're honoring the history and experiences that have made Lake Compounce a part of so many families over the years.
>
> Daniel LLanas, general manager of Lake Compounce, in the [press release of November 16, 2026](https://www.lakecompounce.com/press/2027-opening-day-and-seasonal-event-lineup/)

LLanas has run the park since August 2026. Before that he was director of admissions at Aquaventure World at Atlantis The Palm in Dubai, and he spent more than ten years with SeaWorld Parks & Entertainment.

## The Wildcat and the park's four other coasters

The [Wildcat](ref:lake-compounce/wildcat?bare) opened in 1927. Philadelphia Toboggan Coasters built it as the company's coaster number 61, to a design by Herbert Paul Schmeck. Charlie Dinn and Curtis D. Summers rebuilt it in 1986. The track is 2,746 feet (837 meters) long and 85 feet (26 meters) tall at its highest point. The first drop falls 78 feet (24 meters), and at 48 mph (77 km/h) it's surprisingly quick for a centenarian. There are no inversions, and a ride lasts one minute 15 seconds.

The Wildcat came back for the 2026 season after a renovation that took several years. The park re-tracked the whole ride with Gravity Group's vertically stacked rails to make it run smoother. Since then a second train has been back in service. Both trains come from Great Coasters International, each with seven cars and 14 seats in total. You need to be 48 inches (122 centimeters) tall to ride, and there's no upper limit. The coaster is wheelchair accessible, and Speedy Pass works here too.

[Boulder Dash](ref:lake-compounce/boulder-dash?bare), the park's second wooden coaster, is a terrain coaster that follows the lie of the land, and it has run since May 21, 2000. Custom Coasters International built it to plans by Larry Bill. At 4,725 feet (1,440 meters), its track is more than one and a half times as long as the Wildcat's, and at 60 mph (97 km/h) it's the faster of the two wooden coasters. The [Phobia Phear Coaster](ref:lake-compounce/phobia-phear-coaster?bare), a Premier Rides Sky Rocket II, opened on May 7, 2016. It stands 150 feet (46 meters) tall, launches its train several times by linear motor, reaches 62 mph (100 km/h) and goes upside down once. For Phobia you need to be 54 inches (137 centimeters) tall, or 52 inches (132 centimeters) with an adult, a good hand's breadth more than for the Wildcat. [Zoomerang](ref:lake-compounce/zoomerang?bare) is a 1997 Vekoma Boomerang with three inversions. There's also the Kiddie Coaster for the youngest riders.

## Events from May to October

The first Boulder Dash 5k also runs in May, on a day still to be named, a five-kilometer run the park is organizing with the Hartford Marathon Foundation and Special Olympics Connecticut. The money raised goes to the Special Olympics Connecticut campaign for the organization's first permanent home. It'll be on a new campus in Bristol that opens in 2027.

Kids Fest runs on weekends from June 5 to June 27, with Daniel Tiger and other characters to meet, magic shows and new foam parties. It's included in every 2027 season pass.

Fireworks go up over the lake on the weekend around July 4, followed by new drone and laser shows every weekend in July. Live music plays before each show. Newsletter subscribers get to vote on one of the drone show formations. The Summer Beach Bash follows in August, and it has no dates yet.

Harvest Days are new, on weekends from September 4 to September 26, with music, food, drinks and New England traditions. Phantom Fall Fest, the Halloween event, follows on selected days from September 24 to October 31, with a family program until evening and the haunted attractions after that.

Holiday Lights last ran in winter 2025, and this time the park doesn't open at all over the winter. The 2026 season ended on November 1 with Phantom Fall Fest.

## 2027 season passes and tickets

For Black Friday, Lake Compounce is selling 2027 season passes at a discount. The park hasn't announced an end date for the sale.

The Gold Pass costs $89.99 instead of $169.99. It's also valid at Crocodile Cove and Phantom Fall Fest, and General Parking is included. The season bottle gets you free soft drinks, and you get 15% off food, shopping and Speedy Pass, plus 20% off cabanas. The Silver Pass is also $89.99 in the sale, down from $139.99, and covers the water park and Phantom Fall Fest too, but it only gives 10% off and no parking. Pick Silver in the sale and by your first parking ticket you've already spent $28 more than with Gold.

The Platinum Pass costs $169.99 instead of $209.99. Preferred Parking is free while spaces last, the discount is 20%, or 30% on cabanas. It also gets you into other parks in the US, among them Kennywood, Dutch Wonderland, Story Land and Splish Splash. At [Kennywood near Pittsburgh](/news/kennywood-2027-season), three rides from 1927 also turn 100 in 2027, including the wooden Racer.

Buy Gold or Platinum now and you can bring a friend for free on any day in May 2027 except blackout dates. Every season pass comes with two tickets to the Carousel Museum. Without a pass, the Any Day Ticket costs $59.99 online instead of $65.99. It's good for one day of your choice in the 2027 summer season, Crocodile Cove included. Visitors aged 65 and over get a discount.

Tuesday, December 1, is Giving Tuesday. For every season pass sold that day, Lake Compounce donates a 2027 day ticket to the Boys & Girls Club of Bristol.

## Crocodile Cove from May 29

The park calls Crocodile Cove the largest water park in Connecticut. It has more than 15 water slides, a lazy river and two wave pools. On opening day, Bayou Bay, [Storm Surge](ref:lake-compounce/storm-surge?bare) and [Croc-O-Nile](ref:lake-compounce/croc-o-nile?bare) are among the attractions running, along with the cabanas and the Nathan's food stand.

## Getting there and parking

The park is at 185 Enterprise Drive in Bristol. From I-84, take exit 31, then head north on Route 229 for about two miles (three kilometers). General Parking costs $28, Preferred Parking $45 while spaces last. Lake Compounce doesn't take cash, not even for a coaster from 1927. You pay by card, Apple Pay or Google Pay, and machines swap bills for a payment card. The Lake Compounce Campground, with cabins, RV sites and tent sites, sits on the grounds and opens in early May.

## From the park's history

Lake Compounce started out in 1846 as a picnic park built by Gad Norton, and calls itself "America's First Amusement Park". By the park's own count, 2027 is its 181st season. The carousel is even older than the coaster: built in 1898, it's been at Lake Compounce since Memorial Day 1911. From 1986 to 1987 the park was called Hershey Lake Compounce, and from 1988 to 1995 Lake Compounce Festival Park. Since 2026, Legacy Corner near Down Time has displayed photos and memorabilia from the park's history. Today Lake Compounce belongs to Herschend.
