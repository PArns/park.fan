The train rolls out of the station, swings back a little and stops on the first
launch track. Then a linear motor takes it to 90 km/h, the track tips into a
drop over the park’s pond, and the cars, which spin freely on their axis, make
sure that in the loop and the banana roll you never know which way you’re
facing. The ride has five inversions, two launches and 920 metres of track.

That’s [The Ride to Happiness by Tomorrowland](ref:plopsaland-belgium/the-ride-to-happiness-by-tomorrowland),
and it stands in a park whose main character is a goblin.
[Plopsaland Belgium](ref:plopsaland-belgium) is in De Panne, a few kilometres
from the beach and close to the French border. Most of the rides are built for
children. Three coasters aren’t: The Ride to Happiness, Anubis and Heidi, and
they’re why coaster collectors make the trip.

Half a day is enough for the three big ones, I think. With children up to about
twelve it becomes a full day, and the queues are mostly short either way. The
numbers are further down.

## From honey park to Studio 100 park

The park is older than its name sounds. Alberic-Joseph Florizoone, a honey
merchant from Veurne, bought a piece of polder land in Adinkerke around 1934 and
opened the **Meli Park** there on Easter Sunday 1935. The name was a pun on the
French _miel_, and that the Greek and Hawaiian words for honey are also "meli"
was only noticed later. At first there was an exhibition hall with a shop, a small
playground with a maze, a carousel and a few animals. Florizoone let bees sting
him in front of an audience to show how to handle them.

On 28 June 1952 he presented the extended park, with a bird garden, a fairy-tale
forest and a new main building. It drew guests from the coast and visitors from
northern France. After a large fire in 1979 bigger rides arrived, among them the
dark ride **Apirama** with hundreds of moving bees, the HUSS pirate ship swing
(1981) and the coaster Rollerskater. In 1989 the Belgian prime minister Wilfried
Martens opened the water ride Splash, which according to English Wikipedia was
then the tallest and fastest log flume in Europe. In the seventies and eighties
about 700,000 visitors a year came, in 1994 it was 630,000. According to Dutch
Wikipedia the number fell mainly because large modern parks such as Disneyland
Paris (1992) opened, and there was no money for the rides. Florizoone died in 1992.
His family sold the park to Studio 100 and the media group VMMa at the end of
1999, and the Meli Park closed on 3 October 1999.

Studio 100 is a Belgian company founded in 1996 by Gert Verhulst, Danny Verbiest
and Hans Bourlon. It produces children’s television, first the Flemish show
_Samson & Gert_ and the series _Kabouter Plop_. The name **Plopsa**land comes from
**Plop** and **Sa**(mson). "Pannadine", after De Panne, was also under discussion,
until the new owners noticed that it’s the name of a medical cream. The park
opened under the new name in 2000. The Apirama, which had shown bees, became
**Het Bos van Plop**, a boat ride through a forest full of goblins. A small house at
the entrance of the former fairy-tale forest still has figures from the
fairy-tale forest and bees from the Apirama.

In 2005 Studio 100 also took over the VMMa shares. From 2006 the park was called
Plopsaland De Panne, to tell it apart from the other Plopsa parks, and in summer
2025 the group renamed it **Plopsaland Belgium**. Plopsa is Studio 100’s
theme-park division and has seven parks, which together count more than 3.5
million visitors a year.

The park has grown step by step since 2000. In 2004 the Draak opened, a ride with
which Plopsa wanted to show that the park is more than a children’s park. In 2006
came SuperSplash and a Starflyer, in 2009 Anubis The Ride, in 2011 the indoor hall
Mayaland, in 2013 Wickieland and the Studio 100 Theater with 1,400 seats, in 2015
the water park Plopsaqua, in 2017 Heidi The Ride, in 2021 the first hotel and The
Ride to Happiness, and in 2023 the indoor world Circus Bumba.

Dutch Wikipedia collects the attendance figures from the annual financial
statements. In 2022 it was 1,382,632, in 2023 only
1,278,880, in 2024 1,388,732 and in 2025 1,405,606. The park covers about 30
hectares. The neighbouring parks for comparison:

## A resort on the coast

The park is part of a resort. The entrance, the water park Plopsaqua, the Studio
100 Theater and the **Plopsaland Theater Hotel**, a 4-star superior hotel with 117
rooms, all share one forecourt. Add the **Plopsaland Village** with 55 holiday
villas, the Studio 100 Chalets (there are Plop, Bumba and K3 chalets) and the
campsite **Plopsaland Basecamp**. Plopsa’s head office is on the site too, above
the big shop at the entrance.

If you’re coming from Germany, you probably know only

along the Belgian coast.

## The characters behind the themed areas

The areas follow Studio 100’s series. **Maya the Bee** (Maya here), **Heidi** and
**Vic the Viking** are familiar to German readers, and Mayaland, Heidiland and
Wickieland belong to them. The Flemish characters are less well known abroad: the
goblin **Kabouter Plop**, **Samson & Marie**, the clown **Bumba**, the girl band
**K3**, the youth programme **Nachtwacht** and the series **Het Huis Anubis**.

The assignments have changed several times in recent years. The area with the
castle was called Kasteelplein for a long time and has been "Nachtwacht:
Schemermeer" since 2025, and the ride in it went from De Draak to Draconis at the
start of the season on 29 March 2025. The 1976 Tivoli coaster became Dongo’s Race,
then Viktor’s Race and, since 2022, the #LikeMe Coaster.

## The Ride to Happiness

[The Ride to Happiness](ref:plopsaland-belgium/the-ride-to-happiness-by-tomorrowland)
is a **Mack Xtreme Spinning Coaster**, and when it opened it was the second of its
type in the world, after Time Traveler at Silver Dollar City in Missouri from 2018.
Europe had none until then. The track is 920 metres long, the park gives 35
metres of height, the Roller Coaster DataBase 33. Top speed is 90 km/h, and a ride
lasts 1:20 minutes. A train seats 16 guests, in four cars with two rows of two.
Under each car sits an eddy-current brake that slows its spin and can be adjusted.
According to English Wikipedia the ride holds the record for the most inversions
on a spinning coaster: five, or six if you count the dive loop with two flips twice.

```glossary-widget slug=xtreme-spinning-coaster

```

Between announcement and opening, just under two years passed. In August 2019 media
reported that Plopsaland was planning a large spinning coaster from Mack. On 24
September 2019 park director Steve Van den Kerkhof presented the plans under the
working title "Robo-Spinner", with an investment of 15 million euros. On 15
January 2021 the park announced the name and theme, the electronic music festival
Tomorrowland in Boom. The ride ran for guests for the first time on 1 July 2021,
and the official opening followed on 21 July. According to the park, the music on
the train was produced specially for the ride.

It has won several awards. A jury of international experts gave the **European Star
Award 2021** of the magazine _Kirmes & Park Revue_ to "Europe’s best new coaster".
At the Golden Ticket Awards of the trade magazine _Amusement Today_ it ranked 5th
among the world’s best steel coasters in 2025, behind Fury 325, VelociCoaster,
Steel Vengeance and Iron Gwazi. On the ride’s page the park also writes that it was
voted Europe’s best coaster in 2025 and 2026. The page doesn’t say who voted.

## Anubis, Heidi and the other rides

**[Anubis The Ride](ref:plopsaland-belgium/anubis-the-ride)** is the older of the two
big steel coasters. It opened on 4 April 2009 and was built by Gerstlauer. A linear
motor takes the train to 90 km/h in two seconds, then it goes vertically up a
34-metre top hat, followed by a dive loop, an Immelmann and a heartline roll:
three inversions on 600 metres of track. The layout is almost the same as Dare
Devil Dive at Six Flags Over Georgia. There a vertical lift pulls the train up,
here it’s the launch and the top hat. The station is a two-thirds-scale replica of
the house from the Flemish series _Het Huis Anubis_. Minimum height 125 centimetres.

```glossary-widget slug=top-hat

```

**[Heidi The Ride](ref:plopsaland-belgium/heidi-the-ride)** is a wooden coaster by
Great Coasters International, the same company that built
[Troy at Toverland](ref:attractiepark-toverland/troy). It opened in 2017, is 627
metres long, 22 metres tall and runs at 71 km/h. The layout is an out-and-back with
a chain lift and no inversion, designed by Jeff Pike. According to the Roller
Coaster DataBase it resembles White Lightning at Fun Spot America in Orlando but
isn’t identical. Two trains with twelve seats each run, and capacity is 635 guests
an hour. Minimum height 120 centimetres. The three GCI wooden coasters in the
Benelux compared:

```ride-waits-widget rides=plopsaland-belgium/heidi-the-ride|Heidi The Ride (GCI, 2017);attractiepark-toverland/troy|Troy (GCI, 2007);efteling/joris-en-de-draak|Joris en de Draak (GCI, 2010) columns=park,peak highlight=plopsaland-belgium/heidi-the-ride

```

**[Draconis](ref:plopsaland-belgium/draconis)** is a Mack powered coaster from 2004,
called "De Draak" until the start of the 2025 season on 29 March. The train runs in three spirals through a
castle and crosses the DinoSplash track seven times on the way. It’s 450 metres
long, 14 metres tall and 36 km/h fast. The station is an 80-metre-long castle on
three levels. Minimum height 96 centimetres.

**[SuperSplash](ref:plopsaland-belgium/supersplash)** is a Mack Super Splash from
2006, 30 metres tall and 70 km/h fast, and according to Dutch Wikipedia the first
installation of its kind for Mack with the lift in the tower.
**[DinoSplash](ref:plopsaland-belgium/dinosplash)** is older than it looks: the ride
opened in 1989 as Splash at the Meli Park and got a dinosaur theme in 2019. With two
drops it’s one of the larger log flumes in Europe, according to Dutch Wikipedia. The
park marks both water rides as wet. The minimum height is 96 centimetres at
SuperSplash and 100 at DinoSplash.

The **K3 Roller Skater** is a Vekoma
junior coaster from the Meli Park, 335 metres long and 13 metres tall, themed on
the girl band K3 since 2019. The **#LikeMe Coaster** is a Tivoli coaster by Zierer
that opened in 1976 as Keverbaan at the Meli Park and was replaced by a new copy in 2014. Both have a minimum height of 90 centimetres.

Then there are two dark rides. **[Plop’s Woods](ref:plopsaland-belgium/plops-woods)**
(Het Bos van Plop) is the converted Apirama of 1979 and takes boats through the
goblin forest. **[Bumba’s World Trip](ref:plopsaland-belgium/bumbas-world-trip)** (Op
Reis met Bumba) opened in 2023 in the indoor area Circus Bumba and, according to
Dutch Wikipedia, is the first dark ride the Plopsa group had built itself.

```glossary-widget slug=dark-ride

```

Between the coasters stand the rides you’d also find at a funfair, and a few with a
history. The **Nachtwacht-Flyer** is a 70-metre Starflyer from 2006. In 2021 Plopsa
wanted to retheme it for a TikTok duo and stopped the conversion after a petition
with more than 80,000 signatures; it opened with the Nachtwacht theme on 8 May 2021.
**Wickie The Battle** is a Mack splash battle. **Wickie’s Wervelwind** (Vic’s
Whirlwind on the park’s English site) has been a wild swing by ART Engineering since
12 July 2025, bought originally for Plopsa Indoor Coevorden. The **Plopsa Express**
of 1983 makes a circuit of the park and has stopped at Anubis since 2016, so you can
watch the launch.

The wait times come from our measurements since October 2025, and on the park page
they update every five minutes. The table lists the rides with the longest queues

Even at the top of the table the median is a quarter of an hour or less, and it
covers every operating day of the year, not just busy ones. Several children’s
rides are also among the first eight, and The Ride to Happiness isn’t in front.
The children want Plop.

The highest daily values of the last twelve months fell on Whit Sunday, 1 May,
the Easter holidays, a Monday in early June and days in high summer.

## When to go

The park isn’t open all year. According to the 2026 calendar it was closed from 5
January to 13 February, opened on 14 February for the crocus holidays and ran in
March only on Wednesdays, Saturdays and Sundays, before running almost daily from
April. Over Christmas and New Year the park was open from 24 December 2025 to 4 January 2026.
From April to June and in September the park usually closes at 5 or 6 p.m., in
July and August at 7 p.m. and on a few evenings at 10 p.m., in October 2026 at
5 p.m. on weekdays and 6 p.m. at weekends. It usually opens at 10 a.m. A few days are closed, on 10 October 2026 for a
private event, to which tickets and annual passes don’t apply.

On Wednesdays the park is currently advertising the ticket at €36 instead of €56.
If you have children of school age, you’re going in the holidays anyway, on the
days the calendar marks as busy. Without children, or with pre-school children,
weekdays in September and October and in February and March are worth it according
to the measurements.

In autumn the **Halloween Celebration** runs from 3 October to 8 November, and the
park is open daily during it, except 10 October. You get the Pumpkin King (a scarecrow who leaves Pumpkin Island only at Halloween), the
Nachtwacht Academy, the foxes Fox & Trot from the musical Pinocchio and the 25
Celebration Parade. There are also Halloween fireworks and dishes such as a Pumpkin
Spice Latte, a Halloween waffle and the Spooky Burger. A dated ticket costs from
€42 in this period. What other parks in Belgium and the Netherlands do at
Halloween is in the [Halloween guide 2026](/blog/halloween-theme-parks-2026), including

## A day in the park

The park opens at 10 a.m., and the order follows from the hourly profile above.

Go to **The Ride to Happiness** first, where the queue is shortest in the first
hour. Then comes **Anubis**, before it gets busier in the afternoon, then **Heidi
The Ride** and **Plop’s Woods**, best before 11 a.m., because both peak around midday. Lunch belongs in that
window. After lunch come
**Draconis**, **DinoSplash** and **SuperSplash**, before the queues fill up in the
late afternoon. The **K3 Roller Skater** and the **#LikeMe Coaster** fit into the
gaps.

According to the park’s page, SuperSplash, the Tractors, the Animal Farm and
Willy’s Playground are temporarily closed in autumn 2026, so check before you travel.

The sprint to opening hardly pays off at this park. Our rope-drop analysis compares,
for each ride, the wait at opening with the day’s peak measured on a busy day, and
for no ride is the difference big enough to recommend being at the gate extra early.
The early start saves the most on weekend days at Heidi The Ride, SuperSplash and
Plop’s Woods.

```

With small children the day consists of the play area
**Ploptuin**, the Tractors, Plop’s Woods and the covered world of **Circus Bumba**.
**Mayaland** is covered too, in case it rains. The parade and shows run on
a schedule that the park page and the park’s app display.

## The Express Pass

The park sells the **Express Pass** as an add-on to admission. It gives access through
a separate entrance with a shorter queue. There are three tiers:

| Tier      | Price per person and day | Rides     |
| --------- | ------------------------ | --------- |
| Single    | €7                       | 1         |
| Classic   | €35                      | 9         |
| Unlimited | €95                      | unlimited |

It’s valid on nine rides: The Ride to Happiness, Bumba’s World Trip, Anubis,
Draconis, The Big Wave, DinoSplash, Heidi The Ride, Plop’s Woods and SuperSplash.
At least 15 minutes must pass between two rides, and the park sells only a limited
number of passes. Children under 85 centimetres can use the express entrance for
free if they’re allowed on the ride. You buy it online (with a QR code, also
digitally in the app), at the ticket office at the entrance or in the shops.

Whether it’s worth it depends on the day. On a weekday in September the typical
queue at the most popular rides is a quarter of an hour at most, and €95 for the
Unlimited Pass saves hardly any time then. On a summer holiday weekend, when the
peaks from the table above repeat, it looks different. Even then I’d start with the
Single for €7, for the one ride you don’t want to miss.

The park’s home page says "from €38", the ticket page "from €36". Which price
applies to your day is in the ticket shop’s calendar. The price tier for children
depends on height, which is measured at the ticket desk with shoes on. If you’re in
doubt, have them re-measured at the information desk, and that result stands. If you
buy the ticket online, you skip the ticket office and go straight to the check.

If you want to come several times a year, work it out with the **annual pass**. The
park sells three tiers. The Plopsaland Belgium Pass costs €150 (€125 as a renewal).
The Plopsa Pass Europe for €175 is also valid at the Plopsa parks in Hasselt,
Plopsaland Ardennes, Coevorden and Plopsaland Deutschland. The premium version for
€235 includes both water parks, plus parking and €5 off the Express Pass Classic or
Unlimited. Children 85 to 99 centimetres and seniors 70 and over pay about half.
All three passes include 25 percent off tickets for friends and family (at most two
tickets a year) and the preview of the Flying Cinema.

## Practical information

Dogs aren’t allowed, except assistance dogs with an official vest. Children may
come without an adult only from 12, and there must be at least one adult for every
ten children. Anyone accompanying a child on a ride must be at least 15. Bicycles,
scooters and similar vehicles stay in the parking zone at the entrance. Camping,
barbecuing and picnicking are banned on the park and car park grounds, and nobody
may park there overnight. The park may check bags at the entrance.

The park lends wheelchairs for free and hand carts for a fee. For these you leave
an ID or a €100 deposit. Strollers are allowed in the park but stay in the designated places outside rides
and queues. They aren’t allowed in the theatres.

## Getting there

The park is in De Panne, address **De Pannelaan 68, 8660 De Panne**. By car you take
the E40 towards Ostend. It runs from Cologne via Aachen, Brussels and Ghent, and at
the Jabbeke interchange you keep left towards Veurne-Nieuwpoort and take exit 1
Adinkerke. There you turn right over the bridge across the Duinkerkevaart and the
railway tracks. At the next roundabout the car park for visitors and coaches is on
the left. According to the park there are plenty of spaces right at the park, and a
parking ticket costs €15 if you buy it online beforehand.

Without a car, the **coast tram** goes to the stop "De Panne Plopsaland" right at
the entrance, every 10 minutes in summer, every 15 in spring and autumn and every
20 in winter. De Panne station is within walking distance and reachable by a
footpath of its own. Trains run hourly from Gent-Sint-Pieters, Antwerpen-Centraal
and Brussels-South, among others. If you buy a "Discovery Ticket" in the web shop,
you get 40 percent off the return journey in 2nd class, and you enter the code with
the Belgian railway NMBS.

## Hotels and food

The resort has four places to stay. The **Theater Hotel** (4 stars superior, one to
six people) includes breakfast and parking, and with half board two days of
Plopsaqua as well. In the **Village** (six to twelve people, at least two nights)
and the **Studio 100 Chalets** (one to four people), admission to Plopsaland and
Plopsaqua is part of the package. The **Basecamp** is the campsite for tents,
caravans and motorhomes. According to the park, a package with an overnight stay
costs from €77 per person and night in the hotel, from €58 in the Village and from
€45 in the chalets. You can change or cancel free of charge up to seven days before
arrival and pay in three instalments.

There are many places to eat in the park. The **Pirates’ Grill** is new, a pirate
restaurant with grilled food that opened in summer 2026. There are also
**Mr. Spaghetti** next to Mayaland, the **Plop Hamburger Restaurant**, the
**Prinsessia Restaurant**, the **Viking Grill** in Wickieland and **De Hoeve bij
Big & Betsy**. The hotel has the **Restaurant Le Grand Buffet**. For Maya the Bee’s
50th birthday the park sells a honey cake.

## What’s new and what’s closing

The biggest change comes in 2027. **Plopsaland Plaza** is a new building at the
entrance, which Plopsa puts at 25 million euros, the largest investment in the
park’s history. It has a **Flying Cinema**, a flight simulator by Mack
Rides with projection by Barco. Guests sit for about five minutes in a moving
gondola in front of a dome and fly over Europe with Maya the Bee. The park page
names Iceland, Venice, Paris and Plopsaland for it; the press release of August
2026 has Dutch tulip fields instead of Iceland. It opens for everyone on Saturday
13 March 2027, and from 19 December 2026 on selected days for annual pass holders
and hotel guests. The Plaza also includes a grand café, a bowling alley, arcade
machines and a new station for the Plopsa Express. In summer the Plaza is freely
accessible after the park closes, and the Flying Cinema then costs a ticket of its
own. With the expansion Plopsa wants to reach two million visitors a year. The
other new attractions of the year are in the
[overview of the 2027 theme park news](/blog/new-theme-park-attractions-2027).

September 2025. The Verkeerspark isn’t entirely gone, because as "Rijschool Suikerbuik" it
was rebuilt in 2026. The previous Pirates’ Grill has been replaced by a new
building. From 1 May 2027 a new family show, _Dr. Molino’s Magical Circus_, runs in
the park.

## Frequently asked questions about Plopsaland Belgium

### How long are the queues at Plopsaland Belgium?

Measured over a year, the typical wait at the most popular rides is a quarter of an
hour at most, longer on Saturdays and in high summer. The highest daily values since
October 2025 are well above that and are in the table above.

### How tall do you have to be for The Ride to Happiness?

130 centimetres. Anubis requires 125 centimetres, Heidi The Ride 120, SuperSplash
and Draconis 96 each.

### Is the Express Pass worth it?

On weekdays outside the holidays hardly, on summer holiday weekends more likely. The
Single costs €7 for one ride, Classic €35 for nine, Unlimited €95. There must be 15
minutes between two rides.

### When is Plopsaland Belgium emptiest?

On weekdays in September, October, February and March. Saturday is the busiest day
of the week and August the busiest month.

### How do I get from Germany to De Panne?

By car on the E40 from Cologne via Aachen, Brussels and Ghent to the Jabbeke
interchange, then towards Veurne-Nieuwpoort and exit 1 Adinkerke. Without a car,
take the train to De Panne and walk to the entrance, or take the coast tram to the
stop "De Panne Plopsaland".

### How much does admission cost?

A dated ticket from 1 metre costs from €36, an undated one €56. Children under 85
centimetres pay nothing, children 85 to 99 centimetres and seniors 70 and over pay
€20. The family ticket for three people costs €135.

### What is the Flying Cinema?

A flight simulator by Mack Rides that opens in Plopsaland Plaza in 2027. Guests sit
in a moving gondola in front of a projection dome and fly over places in Europe with
Maya the Bee for about five minutes. It isn’t a coaster.
