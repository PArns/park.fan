At five of the six German parks where we measure wait times, a weekday in the home state's holidays is busier than
an average Saturday or Sunday, and at Heide Park it is busier by almost a quarter. At Europa-Park it comes in
just below. At the weekend it makes little difference whether
it's the holidays.

In Germany each state sets its own school holidays, and which ones count depends on the park, not
always on the state it's in. Heide Park only gets properly full when Lower Saxony and North
Rhine-Westphalia (NRW) are off at the same time, and Plopsaland in Haßloch when
Rhineland-Palatinate and NRW overlap. Phantasialand is full before NRW's holidays have even
started. Legoland is the only park where the Whitsun holidays in Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg
make any difference, and Europa-Park reacts to holidays less than any of the others. At most of the
parks, Whitsun, except in Günzburg, and the end of the summer holidays in the south were barely
busier than school days.

## Where the numbers come from

For every past day, our calendar records how long people queued on average at a park's big
rides. The same calendar lists which states have school holidays that day, and which neighbouring
regions do as well. I put the two side by side for six parks: [Europa-Park](ref:europa-park),
[Phantasialand](ref:phantasialand), [Heide Park](ref:heide-park),
[Movie Park Germany](ref:movie-park-germany), [Plopsaland Deutschland](ref:plopsaland-deutschland)
and [Legoland Deutschland](ref:legoland-deutschland). [Hansa-Park](ref:hansa-park) is missing,
because the park only shows its wait times in its own app and none of them reach us.

The period runs from 24 December 2025 to 3 October 2026, and for Heide Park and Plopsaland from
the start of their season on 22 March. Those are the holiday dates of a single year. I haven't
adjusted for weather, events or construction work.

Mostly I compared weekdays, Monday to Friday, leaving out public holidays. The baseline is a
weekday between 13 April and 30 September on which not one state had school holidays. Depending
on the park there were between 19 and 45 of those. The percentages below are the increase over
that baseline. A figure of +50% means you queue one and a half times as long at the big rides,
and +100% means twice as long.

| Park          | Easter holidays, 30 March to 10 April | Summer, 20 July to 7 August | only Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg off |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------- | --------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Europa-Park   | +13%                                  | +37%                        | +10%                                   |
| Phantasialand | +54%                                  | +96%                        | −6%                                    |
| Heide Park    | +74%                                  | +124%                       | −15%                                   |
| Movie Park    | +52%                                  | +140%                       | too few days                           |
| Plopsaland    | +153%                                 | +63%                        | −14%                                   |
| Legoland      | +129%                                 | +122%                       | +85%                                   |

The middle column covers the three weeks when 14 to 16 states were on holiday at once. The last
column combines Whitsun and September, when only the south was still off. For Movie Park that
comes to four weekdays, too few for a figure, because outside the holidays the park is closed on
a lot of weekdays.

## At the weekend the holidays don't matter

At five of the six parks, a holiday weekend and a term-time weekend are no more than 6% apart,
sometimes one way and sometimes the other. Only at Movie Park are holiday weekends a little
busier, by 13%.

A weekday in the home state's holidays came in 23% above an
average weekend day at Heide Park, 16% at Movie Park, 9% at Plopsaland, 7% at Phantasialand and
5% at Legoland. Only Europa-Park was quieter, by 3%.

On a Saturday outside the holidays, the increase over the baseline ranged from 42% at Plopsaland to 123% at Legoland. The Sunday after
came to 8% at Movie Park, 9% at Europa-Park, 10% at Plopsaland and 15% at Heide Park, which is
barely busier than a weekday. Only Phantasialand, at 36%, and Legoland, at 64%, get noticeably
more crowded on Sundays as well. If it has to be the weekend and you're not heading for Brühl or
Günzburg, go on the Sunday.

## The summer holidays, park by park

The 2026 summer holidays were spread a long way apart. Rhineland-Palatinate, Hesse and Saarland
broke up at the end of June, and Bavaria was the last state to go back, on 14 September. In just
one week, 3 to 7 August, all 16 states were off at the same time.

### Heide Park

Lower Saxony had its holidays from 2 July to 12 August, NRW from 18 July. In the two weeks or so
when only Lower Saxony was off, the increase at [Heide Park](ref:heide-park) was 36%. Then NRW
joined in, and until 12 August you queued at the big rides more than twice as long as on a
weekday without holidays, +123%. The first school day in Lower Saxony put an end to that. With
NRW on its own, 19% was left until 1 September.

My reading is that Soltau needs two catchment areas to fill up. I can't prove that with one
summer, because the weeks when both were off were also the weeks when almost every other state
was on holiday. On
the September weekdays when only the south was still off, the park was 33% below the baseline.

The busiest day of the whole period in Soltau was Tuesday 28 July. Second place went to a day
outside the school holidays, Saturday 3 October, German Unity Day, right in the middle of the
park's Halloween season. Of the 35 days marked very high or extreme in the calendar for Heide
Park, 31 fell in some state's school holidays.

### Phantasialand

Brühl went the other way. From 2 to 17 July, with Lower Saxony, Hesse, Rhineland-Palatinate and
several other states on holiday and NRW still in school, [Phantasialand](ref:phantasialand) was
already 77% above the baseline. Once NRW was off too it reached 102%, and with NRW alone from
mid-August it was still 67%. On the weekdays
when only Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg were off, it was 6% quieter than on an ordinary school
day.

That makes Phantasialand the park that stays full the longest. From the week starting 6 July to
the end of August, not one weekday week there was less than 50% above the baseline. The two
busiest days were Thursday 6 August and Monday 10 August. Next came Friday 2 January, during
Wintertraum.

### Movie Park

[Movie Park](ref:movie-park-germany) in Bottrop looks almost the same as Brühl, with +67% before
NRW's holidays, +132% while NRW and Lower Saxony were off together, and +43% with NRW alone. The
first week of NRW's holidays, 20 to 24 July, was the busiest weekday week of the whole summer. You
queued twice as long at the big rides as in the week before, and the four busiest days of the
entire period all fall in that one week, Tuesday to Friday. By the following week it was over.

### Plopsaland

At [Plopsaland](ref:plopsaland-deutschland) in Haßloch, neither Rhineland-Palatinate nor NRW
filled the park by itself. Rhineland-Palatinate was the first state to break up, on 27 June. In
the three weeks to 17 July, while NRW was still in school, the park sat 2% below the baseline,
despite its own state's holidays. From 20 July to 7 August, with Rhineland-Palatinate and
NRW off at the same time, it was +63%.

On 10 August Rhineland-Palatinate went back to school, but NRW and twelve other states didn't.
That week the weekday average dropped to just over a third of the week before, and until the end
of NRW's holidays the park stayed 23% below a weekday without holidays.

Plopsaland didn't have its busiest days in summer anyway. Four of its ten busiest days fell in
the week of 7 to 10 April, in the Easter holidays. On October evenings the park runs
the [Halloween Fright Nights](/news/plopsaland-deutschland-halloween-fright-nights-2026).

### Europa-Park

[Europa-Park](ref:europa-park) was 37% above the baseline in the three weeks with 14 to 16 states
on holiday, the smallest increase of the six parks. From 20 to 29 July, before Baden-Württemberg's
holidays began and with NRW and the north away, it was also 37%. That's more than in the five
weeks after, with its own state on holiday (31%). At the very end, when only Baden-Württemberg and
Bavaria were still off, 11% was left.

For Rust, our calendar also counts Basel-Stadt, Basel-Landschaft, Aargau and Grand Est as
neighbouring regions. I haven't looked separately at whether their holidays make the difference.
Rust's busiest day of the whole period was Monday 29 December 2025, between Christmas and New
Year, when all 16 states were off.

### Legoland

[Legoland](ref:legoland-deutschland) in Günzburg depends on the southern holidays more than any
other park, though not on them alone. Over Whitsun, which in 2026 only Bavaria and
Baden-Württemberg had off, the increase was 123%, measured on the four weekdays of the second
holiday week. In September, when every state except those two was back at school, it was 69%. Yet
from 20 to 31 July, with Bavaria still in school and NRW and the north on holiday, it also came to
120%.

Nowhere else is the gap between a weekday and the weekend as wide as at Legoland. The park's busiest days came in the
Easter holidays, and the busiest of them all was Thursday 9 April.

## Easter was busy everywhere

In 2026 the Easter holidays fell in the same two weeks in almost every state, 30 March to 10
April. Plopsaland was 153% above the baseline, Legoland 129%,
Heide Park 74%, Phantasialand 54% and Movie Park 52%. Europa-Park came to 13%.

At Plopsaland that figure rests on six weekdays, and Easter fell in the first weeks of its
season. Comparing it with a holiday-free weekday between April and September therefore also
compares the season opening with everyday business. For all six, though, Easter was the only
holiday period in our measurement when no park stayed quiet.

Good Friday and Easter Monday weren't the busiest days. At Europa-Park both were 5% below
the baseline, and at Plopsaland Easter Monday was a perfectly ordinary weekday. The peaks came in
the second week of the holidays. At Legoland the Wednesday and Thursday after Easter were the two
busiest days of the entire period, and at Plopsaland the Wednesday was.

## Public holidays and bridge days

For public holidays each figure rests on a single day per park.

**1 May** fell on a Friday in 2026, and no state had school holidays. At five of the six parks it
was busier than an average Saturday outside the holidays. Plopsaland showed it most clearly, with
64% more, while Movie Park and Legoland sat close together at 31% and 29%. At Europa-Park the day
was as busy as a Saturday.

**Ascension Day and the Friday after.** The holiday itself, Thursday 14 May, was only moderately
busy at most parks, and at Movie Park it was even 24% below the baseline. The bridge day on 15
May, when schools were closed in nine states, was busier than the holiday at all five parks with
figures for both days. Legoland was 136% above the baseline and Heide Park 110%. Even Europa-Park
still reached 75%. At Plopsaland the holiday is missing from the measurement, and the Friday came
in 48% below the baseline.

**Whit Sunday and Whit Monday** were among the ten busiest days of the entire period at Movie
Park. The Monday, 25 May, was 179% above the baseline and 80% above an average Saturday outside
the holidays. At Heide Park and Legoland it was a little over 80% above that Saturday as well. At
Europa-Park and Plopsaland, on the other hand, it was quieter than a normal weekday.

**Corpus Christi** is a public holiday in only some of the states, among them Bavaria,
Baden-Württemberg, NRW and Rhineland-Palatinate, and in 2026 it fell on Thursday 4 June. Legoland
was 136% above the baseline on the holiday and again on the Friday after, because both fell in
the Whitsun holidays there. At Plopsaland the bridge day on 5 June was busier than the holiday,
+80% against +3%. Movie Park stayed as empty as on a normal weekday on both days.

## The days with the biggest crowds

In our calendar every day has a crowd level, from very low to extreme. Across all six parks there
were 143 days rated very high or extreme in the period. Of those, 126 fell in at least one state's
school holidays and 60 on a weekend. Exactly one fell on an ordinary weekday with neither school
holidays nor a public holiday, at Plopsaland.

## Christmas and New Year

In winter 2025/26, only Europa-Park and Phantasialand of the six were open on enough days to work
with. Between 24 December and 6 January you queued a little over two and a half times as long at
Europa-Park as on a weekday in the rest of January, and at Phantasialand it was a little over
twice as long. That rests on six holiday weekdays per park.

The opening days for the coming winter are already in the calendar. Between 19 December 2026 and
12 January 2027, Phantasialand is open on 21 days, Europa-Park on 20, Legoland on 19, Plopsaland
on 13 and Movie Park on 11. Heide Park closes after 31 October. The Christmas holidays start on
23 December in ten states and end between 31 December and 12 January.

## The quiet holiday weeks

**Whitsun.** In Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg the holidays ran until 5 June, and the other states
had a few days around Whitsun at most. At Phantasialand the second Whitsun week was 1% below the
baseline, at Plopsaland 3% above, at Europa-Park 7% and at Heide Park 10%. Those figures rest on
three to five weekdays per park. The one exception is Günzburg.

**The end of the summer holidays in the south.** From 2 to 14 September only Bavaria and
Baden-Württemberg were still off. Heide Park was 33% below the baseline in that stretch,
Plopsaland 20% and Phantasialand 7%. If you live in Munich or Stuttgart, that gives you two
holiday weeks in which Soltau is quieter than on a normal school day. At Europa-Park and Legoland,
right on your own doorstep, the same weeks were busier than a school day, and at Legoland by a
wide margin.

## Start or end of your own holidays

For families who can only go during the holidays, I compared the first and the last ten days of
the summer holidays in each park's own state, six to eight weekdays each.

How far above the baseline a weekday was:

| Park          | Summer holidays in     | first ten days | last ten days |
| ------------- | ---------------------- | -------------- | ------------- |
| Europa-Park   | Baden-Württemberg      | +36%           | +11%          |
| Phantasialand | North Rhine-Westphalia | +78%           | +50%          |
| Legoland      | Bavaria                | +123%          | +62%          |
| Movie Park    | North Rhine-Westphalia | +212%          | +52%          |
| Heide Park    | Lower Saxony           | +27%           | +120%         |
| Plopsaland    | Rhineland-Palatinate   | +16%           | +76%          |

Movie Park has the widest gap, and the first ten days of NRW's holidays, the week of 20 July, are
the highest figure in the whole table. At the first four parks the end of the holidays is clearly
the better choice.

At Heide Park and Plopsaland it's the other way round. Both parks only fill up once NRW joins in,
and in 2026 that happened at the end of their own state's holidays both times.

## The 2026 autumn holidays

The autumn holidays aren't part of this comparison, because our measurements only start on
Christmas Eve 2025. Their dates are already in the calendar, though. Rhineland-Palatinate, Hesse,
Saarland and Schleswig-Holstein start this week, Lower Saxony on 10 October, NRW on 17 October,
Baden-Württemberg on 24 October and Bavaria last of all on 31 October. Lower Saxony and NRW
overlap from 17 to 25 October. If the summer pattern holds, those are the days when Heide Park
will be at its busiest. Its last opening day in the calendar is 31 October.

Heide Park, Movie Park and Plopsaland run Halloween evening events in October (all dates in the
[Halloween guide](/blog/halloween-theme-parks-2026)), and their weekends are full even without
the holidays.

## Holiday weeks up to summer 2027, park by park

Next year's holiday dates are in the calendar already as well. Whether the 2026 pattern fits the
2027 dates we'll only know next autumn. The parks haven't
published their 2027 opening days yet.

**Easter 2027** again falls at the same time in almost every state, from 20 March to 4 April in
NRW, Lower Saxony, Rhineland-Palatinate and Bavaria, and from 25 March in Baden-Württemberg. For
each of the six parks that's the busiest stretch of spring, as soon as it's open.

**At Whitsun 2027**, Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg are off from 15 to 30 May, and NRW and Lower
Saxony from 15 to 18 May. If you're after the quiet
Whitsun week of 2026, you'll find it in 2027 from Wednesday 19 May at the earliest, and at
Legoland not at all.

**In summer 2027** all 16 states again share just one week of holidays, 2 to 6 August. For Heide
Park what counts is Lower Saxony, off from 8 July to 18 August, and NRW, with holidays from 17
July to 31 August. The weeks with both together, 17 July to 18 August, are the ones to avoid in
Soltau. From 1 September only Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg still have holidays, and in 2026
those were the quietest weeks of the summer.

Rhineland-Palatinate is off from 26 June to 8 August, so the overlap with NRW runs from 17 July to
8 August. That's a good three weeks, as in 2026, and by our measurement the time when Plopsaland
is full. Hesse, Rhineland-Palatinate and Saarland break up at the end of
June, Lower Saxony on 8 July. Phantasialand and Movie Park
will probably be full from early July until NRW's holidays end on 31 August.

In the south, Baden-Württemberg is off from 29 July to 12 September and Bavaria from 31 July to
13 September. Going by the 2026 pattern, the weeks in Rust before its own holidays, with NRW and
the north away, are no quieter than the holidays themselves. At Legoland it only gets quiet after
13 September.

## What I'd do in each holiday period

If it has to be Easter, I'd go to Europa-Park, where the increase in 2026 was 13%. I'd avoid
Legoland and Plopsaland in the week after Easter Monday. Good Friday and Easter Monday weren't busier at any of the six parks than the Tuesday to Thursday after them.

At Whitsun any park works except Legoland, as long as the second Whitsun week is running in
Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg.

In summer, coming from NRW, I'd rather go to Brühl, Bottrop or Rust at the end of the holidays
than at the start, and to Heide Park only once Lower Saxony is back at school. From Lower Saxony I'd go to Heide Park in the first
week of the holidays, and from Rhineland-Palatinate to Plopsaland in the first three. From
Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg I'd use the last two holiday weeks for a park in the north rather
than for Legoland.

In autumn, Heide Park is the better bet before 17 October or in the week from the 26th than on the days in between, when Lower Saxony and NRW are both off.

Over Christmas, Europa-Park is open until 9 January, Legoland until the 10th and Phantasialand
until 24 January. After 12 January every state's Christmas holidays are over, and Brühl still has
just under two weeks of Wintertraum to go. By our measurement, the January days after the
holidays there were half as busy as the days between Christmas and New Year.

I avoid the bridge day after Ascension, though not necessarily
the holiday itself, and I plan a 1 May that falls on a weekday as if it were a Saturday.
