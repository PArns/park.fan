On 24 May 1979, Lord Louis Mountbatten opened a park in Surrey that didn't have a single roller coaster. The grounds held replicas of a Stone Age cave, a Norman castle and a Viking camp, plus exhibitions of historic boats and aircraft. The lake all this stood on was a dug-out gravel pit. It was one of Mountbatten's last public appearances, and he died a few months later.

The same site now holds [Hyperia](ref:thorpe-park/hyperia), which the park says is Britain's tallest coaster at 236 feet (72 metres) and its fastest at 81 mph (130 km/h). [Thorpe Park](ref:thorpe-park) belongs to Merlin Entertainments, sits between Chertsey and Staines-upon-Thames about 32 kilometres west of central London, and calls itself the "most thrilling theme park" in the country. According to Wikipedia it had 1.9 million visitors in 2019, which made it the third most visited theme park in Britain after Alton Towers and Legoland Windsor. We've been measuring wait times here since 6 October 2024.

## From gravel pit lake to Merlin park

From 1941 to 1970, Ready Mixed Concrete (RMC) dug gravel out of the ground of the Thorpe Park Estate. It then flooded the pits and set up a subsidiary, Leisure Sport Limited, to use the lake for water sports. The water ski world championships were held there in 1975, and the park opened to visitors in 1979. According to Wikipedia the investment was £3 million.

In the early eighties the heritage site became a theme park with permanent rides. The first coaster, Space Station Zero, a Mack Rides powered coaster in a building, arrived in 1984. From 1983 to 1989 the park also served as a filming location for "The Benny Hill Show". The last big ride of the founding company was X:\ No Way Out in 1996, an indoor coaster that's now called The Walking Dead: The Ride.

The Tussauds Group bought the park in 1998 and expanded it with Tidal Wave in 2000, Vortex in 2001, [Colossus](ref:thorpe-park/colossus) in 2002, [Nemesis Inferno](ref:thorpe-park/nemesis-inferno) in 2003 and [Stealth](ref:thorpe-park/stealth) in 2006. In May 2007 the investment firm Blackstone bought the Tussauds Group for 1.9 billion US dollars and merged it with Merlin. On 17 July 2007 Merlin sold Thorpe Park to the investor Prestbury and leased it back for 35 years with an option to extend. According to Wikipedia the land is now owned by LondonMetric Property, which took over the property company LXi REIT in 2024.

[SAW – The Ride](ref:thorpe-park/saw-the-ride) followed in 2009 and [The Swarm](ref:thorpe-park/the-swarm) in 2012. Between The Swarm in 2012 and Hyperia in 2024 the park built no new major coaster and changed brands and themes instead: Angry Birds Land from 2014, Derren Brown's Ghost Train from 2016, The Walking Dead from 2018. Hyperia stands on the site of the Logger's Leap log flume from 1989, which didn't reopen after the 2015 season.

## Hyperia

The park called the plan "Project Exodus". It put first designs to a public consultation in December 2021, Runnymede Borough Council approved the build on 2 November 2022, and demolition of Logger's Leap began in December 2022. The manufacturer is Mack Rides, and according to Wikipedia it was the maker's first hyper coaster with a custom-designed layout. Hyperia opened on 24 May 2024 and was shut again the next day. The park spoke of "unforeseen circumstances", and the ride didn't run again until 12 June. Further stoppages followed on 15 and 19 June 2024, and it reopened on 22 June.


On its own page the park gives the track length as 995.4 metres, the longest in the park, along with a height of 236 feet, a top speed of 81 mph, a 168-foot (51-metre) loop that the park says is the tallest in Europe, and an element the park lists as a world first, an airtime hill with an outward tilt. It also gives 14.8 seconds of weightlessness. The ride has two inversions, a barrel roll in the downward section and a dive loop with a short hold in mid-air. A chain pulls the train up the lift. The first drop is vertical, and the train turns through 180 degrees as it falls.

The minimum height is **1.30 metres**, the lowest of the park's big coasters. Riders with a prosthesis can't ride, and after a review the park says the ride is no longer suitable for people who can't move independently. Hyperia and SAW both have a single rider queue.

## The five other big coasters

The park calls Hyperia and five further rides its "Top Six". The five are listed here by opening year, with The Walking Dead last.

**[Colossus](ref:thorpe-park/colossus)** opened on 22 March 2002 and was the world's first coaster with ten inversions. Werner Stengel designed it and Intamin built it. The train runs through a loop, a cobra roll, two corkscrews and five heartline rolls, four of them in a row just under three metres above the footpath. Colossus held the record until 2013, when The Smiler opened at Alton Towers with 14. The park gives its height as 98 feet (30 metres) and its speed as 44 mph. Its twin stands in Chimelong Paradise in Guangzhou as the "10 Inversion Roller Coaster".

```glossary-widget slug=heartline-roll

```

**[Nemesis Inferno](ref:thorpe-park/nemesis-inferno)** is the park's only inverted coaster, built by Bolliger & Mabillard and opened on 5 April 2003. The track is 750 metres long, and the train goes through a vertical loop, a zero-g roll and two interlocking corkscrews with the riders' legs dangling. According to Wikipedia it was the first inverted coaster with interlocking corkscrews. The theme is a volcano. For its speed the park gives 47 mph on the ride's page and 50 mph in the running text.

**[Stealth](ref:thorpe-park/stealth)** opened on 15 March 2006 and cost £12 million. It's an Intamin accelerator coaster. A hydraulic launch takes the train to 80 mph (129 km/h) in 1.8 seconds, then it climbs vertically to 62.5 metres and comes back down the other side over a top hat. According to Wikipedia, Stealth has been the world's fastest-accelerating coaster since Do-Dodonpa at Fuji-Q Highland stopped running. In cold or wind the train can miss the top hat and roll back. The brake fins on the launch track catch it, and it's then launched again. Riders with a pacemaker can't ride because of the strong magnetic fields.

```glossary-widget slug=top-hat

```

**[SAW – The Ride](ref:thorpe-park/saw-the-ride)** opened on 14 March 2009 as a Gerstlauer Euro-Fighter and, according to the park, is the first coaster in the world themed on a horror film. The train first runs through a dark building with swinging blades, a strobe pit and a heartline roll above a blood-smeared scene from the first film, then climbs 30 metres into a 100-degree drop, which is past vertical. The ride has three inversions and reaches 55 mph. When it opened, the drop wasn't the world's steepest, because Steel Hawg at Indiana Beach had one of 111 degrees in 2008. The park advertised the steepest "freefall" because, unlike Steel Hawg, SAW has no trim brake on the slope. The park recommends SAW from age 13.

**[The Swarm](ref:thorpe-park/the-swarm)** is Britain's first wing coaster and in March 2012 was only the second ever from Bolliger & Mabillard. It was the first with a wing-over drop, where the train turns on its own axis during the first drop. The ride is 38.6 metres tall and 775 metres long, and the park gives a speed of just under 60 mph. The station is a ruined church on an island the park built up out of the lake. The train passes through a poster, under the wing of a crashed aircraft and past a fire engine. From 2013 to 2015 the last two rows faced backwards. The maximum height is 1.95 metres.

**[The Walking Dead: The Ride](ref:thorpe-park/the-walking-dead-the-ride)** sits in a pyramid-shaped hall in the middle of the park. The ride opened in 1996 as X:\ No Way Out, became X in 2013 and has been Walking Dead since 2018. A tyre lift takes the train up, the ride runs in the dark at around 45 km/h, and the biggest drop is almost ten metres. On busy days there are actors in the exit. The park recommends the ride from age 13.

## What else is in the park

The **[Ghost Train](ref:thorpe-park/ghost-train)** is a dark ride with actors and lasts 15 to 20 minutes. The park opened it in 2016 as Derren Brown's Ghost Train with virtual reality and dropped both after the 2022 season. It opens at 12 every day, the minimum height is 1.30 metres, and the park advises at least age 13.

Among the flat rides the park has mostly taken on second-hand ones over the years. [Samurai](ref:thorpe-park/samurai), a Mondial Top Scan, came from Chessington World of Adventures in 2004, [Zodiac](ref:thorpe-park/zodiac) from Drayton Manor in 2006, where the HUSS ride had run as Cyclone, and [Storm Surge](ref:thorpe-park/storm-surge) in 2011 from Cypress Gardens in Florida, before that site became Legoland Florida. [Vortex](ref:thorpe-park/vortex) in 2001, [Quantum](ref:thorpe-park/quantum) in 2003 and [Rush](ref:thorpe-park/rush) in 2005 were built new. The [Detonator](ref:thorpe-park/detonator) drop tower was meant as a stopgap after a fire and stayed. Among the water rides, [Tidal Wave](ref:thorpe-park/tidal-wave) from 2000 is a shoot the chute with an 85-foot drop, and [Depth Charge](ref:thorpe-park/depth-charge) from 1991 was the first four-lane dinghy slide in Britain.

For children there are [Flying Fish](ref:thorpe-park/flying-fish), the former Space Station Zero from 1984, [High Striker](ref:thorpe-park/high-striker), [Mr Monkey's Banana Ride](ref:thorpe-park/mr-monkeys-banana-ride), [Dobble Tea Party](ref:thorpe-park/dobble-tea-party), the bumper cars [Big Easy Bumpers](ref:thorpe-park/big-easy-bumpers) and the play area The Playground for under-twelves. At Halloween the Sunset Cinema screens The Conjuring in 4D.

The medians are based on 192 measured days between 6 October 2024 and 6 October 2026.

Hyperia is well ahead of everything else. Second place goes to Storm Surge, a water ride with a spinning raft that only fills up in the afternoon. SAW, The Swarm, Nemesis Inferno, Colossus and Stealth sit close together behind it.

Colossus, The Swarm, Nemesis Inferno, SAW and Stealth have their longest queue at eleven or twelve o'clock. It drops after that, and from 4 pm it's clearly shorter than at midday at each of these rides. Storm Surge and Tidal Wave work the other way round: both water rides have their longest queue in the afternoon. Hyperia stays at about the same level from ten to three and already has a long queue at opening.

If you're at the entrance at ten, head for Nemesis Inferno, SAW or Colossus first. In our rope drop analysis, arriving early pays off most there, and you'll find it on each ride's page. Save Hyperia for later, with Fastrack or in the single rider queue.

### Fastrack

```glossary-widget slug=fastrack

```

The park sells Fastrack in four packages, each per person, and the card can be scanned once every three minutes:

- **Bronze** from £9: three rides, spread across nine rides (Detonator, Flying Fish, Ghost Train, Rush, Samurai, Storm Surge, Tidal Wave, Vortex, The Walking Dead). Hyperia isn't included.
- **Silver** from £35: one go each on Colossus, Nemesis Inferno, Stealth, SAW and The Swarm. No Hyperia.
- **Gold** from £115: all day on 14 rides, without Hyperia.
- **Platinum** from £135: all day on 15 rides, with Hyperia.

There's also a **One Shot Fastrack** from £6, which you buy only once you're in the park, through the app or a QR code at the entrance to a coaster. The park sells Fastrack in limited numbers and tells guests to check before buying that admission tickets are available for the day, because the two are sold separately. For the Ghost Train the park contradicts itself. The Fastrack page lists it in three packages, while its own page says there's currently no Fastrack for it.

Guests staying in the Thorpe Shark Cabins get an hour of Fastrack on the second day, at the start or the end of the park day. During Fright Nights it applies to the first hour only.

### The day of the week

Saturday is the busiest day and Thursday the one with the shortest waits. Sunday to Wednesday and Friday sit level in between, and the gap between Thursday and Saturday is small. The weekdays rest on 22 to 30 measured days. That's enough for a rough direction, but I can't prove fine differences with it.

According to its calendar, the park is closed on 12, 13 and 14 October 2026 and open daily otherwise.

### The month

July is the busiest month, well ahead of the rest. August and April follow, April because of the Easter holidays. May, June and September are the lowest. For October we have only four measured days so far, which is too few to say anything.

According to the park's calendar, the 2026 season runs until 1 November.

### Fright Nights 2026

The park calls Fright Nights 2026 "25 Years of Fear". Wikipedia dates the first edition to 2002. Fright Nights runs on 2 to 4 October, 9 to 11 October and from 15 October to 1 November, each day from 10 am to 9 pm.

There are five scare mazes. Tenement is new. In a care home called Nova Lux, visitors play electricians who have to switch the power back on before their shared light goes out. Trailers, DeadBeat, Stitches and Survival Games are back. On top of that there are the scare zones Rebel Ball (with Fanta), The Crows, Lucifer's Lair, Creature Campus and Purgatory Town, and The Conjuring 4D in the Sunset Cinema. The park recommends the scare attractions from age 13, and The Conjuring from 15. Filming is banned in the mazes.

A day ticket with Fright Nights costs from £39 online and £66 at the gate, without mazes. The package with four mazes ("Fearsome Four": DeadBeat, Stitches, Trailers, Survival Games) costs from £71 online and £98 at the gate. Tenement isn't part of the package and is sold separately only. If you already have an admission ticket, you can book the four mazes from £32 per person or each one on its own from £8. The VIP package "Phantom Five", with one priority entry to each of the five mazes, costs from £110 (without admission) in the time slots 3 to 5:30 pm or 6 to 8:30 pm. If you arrive late for a maze, you're turned away after a 15-minute grace period, with no refund.

All coasters keep running in the evening, and the park advertises Hyperia and SAW in the dark. For Halloween at other parks there's the [Halloween overview 2026](/blog/halloween-theme-parks-2026), and for Alton Towers a separate post on [Scarefest 2026](/news/alton-towers-scarefest-2026).

### Weather

```weather-widget slug=thorpe-park

```

In October and the shoulder season, the weather decides which rides run. The park's limits are on its Return Guarantee page: Hyperia runs in winds up to 47 mph, Nemesis Inferno and SAW up to 40 mph, Colossus and The Swarm up to 35 mph, Stealth only up to 31 mph, Zodiac up to 25 and Vortex up to 23 mph. Wind is measured at height, so it can feel calmer on the ground. The coasters run from 5 degrees Celsius, and in heavy rain Nemesis Inferno and SAW stay shut. The Return Guarantee applies on selected days only, retroactively, when the park was hit by closures for at least an hour. In 2026 those were 4 April, 25 May and 13 August, redeemable until 30 September 2026.

## Tickets, passes and prices

The park advertises its day tickets as "from £32" when booked in advance. Prices depend on the date and are listed in the park's price table. During Fright Nights a ticket at the gate costs £66. Students get a discount when they book in advance.

Children under 1.20 metres get in free if they're registered with an online booking. A single booking allows at most four such tickets, and at least one paid ticket has to be part of it. On the day of the visit the children have to be measured at the Ticket Centre, where you collect the ticket.

The Thorpe Park Annual Pass costs £64 a year. The Merlin Essential Pass (£139) is valid on 339 days for more than 20 British Merlin attractions, including Thorpe Park, Chessington World of Adventures, the London Eye and Sea Life. The Gold Pass (£239, renewal £179) is valid on 364 days and includes free parking. The Platinum Pass (£299, renewal £229) has no blackout dates, four friend tickets a year and a One Shot Fastrack per visit. The passes of other parks are compared in the [annual pass comparison](/blog/theme-park-annual-pass-comparison).

Guests with a disability book the Ride Access Pass online before the visit, and places are limited. On the day you show the reservation and ID at the Accessibility Kiosk. The requirements for each ride (walking distances, prostheses, plaster casts) are in the park's "Rider Requirements" PDF.

## Getting there

The park is on Staines Road in Chertsey, postcode KT16 8PN. Sat navs sometimes use that postcode to send you to Norlands Lane. The place you want is the main entrance on Staines Road, with a coaster track running over it. From the M25, take exit 11 or 13 and follow the A320. You can't get there from exit 12 because of the road layout.

Parking costs £12, Priority Parking £20. The park advises paying for parking in advance, online or in the app, which saves time when you leave.

By train, go from London Waterloo to Staines and take the 950 bus there. It runs every 15 to 20 minutes, the first departure is at 9:05 am, and you pay by cash or contactless only. Oyster cards, Travelcards and Freedom Passes aren't valid on the 950. On the rail line to Staines, according to the park, Oyster and contactless cards aren't valid either, so you need a regular ticket. The alternative is to go to Chertsey, where the 446 bus stops at Heriot Road. The 446 also connects the park with Heathrow Terminal 4 and the Hatton Cross Underground station. From Chertsey station to the park it's about two kilometres.

## Food and overnight stays

The park's website lists Vibes Bar & Kitchen in the Dome, Planet Doughnut, Camden Junction Bar with beer from the Camden Town Brewery, Boulevard Bites, Spice Kitchen with Indian dishes, Cape Cod Fish & Chips, Tacotaria, Last Call Café, Veggie Box and a Pizza & Pasta Buffet, among others, plus a Burger King in Fearless Valley. At all the venues the park lists, you pay by card only.

The **Thorpe Shark Cabins** are 90 rooms built from shipping containers, with a shark's mouth as the entrance. They opened in 2013 as "The Crash Pad" by the provider Snoozebox, and the park bought and renamed them in 2014. A permanent 250-bed hotel was approved as early as 2011 but never built. Each room has a double bed, a bunk bed and a bathroom with a walk-in shower. Check-in is from 3 pm, and the breakfast buffet is in Vibes Bar & Kitchen. New for 2026 is a Fright Nights themed room.

The "Overnight Stay" package includes two days of park admission, breakfast, parking worth £12, Wi-Fi, ten percent off in the shops and the hour of Fastrack. Prices start at £77 per person, with four people in the room, on selected days from Monday to Thursday. The Fright Nights packages start at £109 per person. In the themed rooms for Hyperia, The Swarm, Nemesis Inferno, Stealth and Colossus, guests also get a Coasters Fastrack on the second day, with six rides across Colossus, Hyperia, Nemesis Inferno, SAW, Stealth and The Swarm.

## What's new and what has closed

On 12 March 2026 the park confirmed that the white water rapids ride Rumba Rapids won't reopen. It ran for 39 years, since 1987 as Intamin's Thunder River, and was one of the park's oldest attractions. The park doesn't say what will replace it. For 2026 about half of Amity Beach was also turned into The Launch Pad, a covered area with seating for resting and eating that's meant to be open all year. The smaller half of Amity Beach remains a swimming area for families with small children.

The park hasn't confirmed anything new for 2027. There are planning documents, which we cover in the post on [theme park novelties 2027](/blog/new-theme-park-attractions-2027). The short breaks for 2027 can already be booked, from £77 per person.

## A day in the park, roughly planned

On normal days the park opens at 10 am, the coasters close between 5 and 7 pm depending on the season, and on Fright Nights days at 9 pm. Queues close at the stated times, and anyone already in line still gets to ride.

1. Be at the main entrance around 9 am, as the park advises. The gates open at 10. Nemesis Inferno or SAW first, then Colossus.
2. Mid-morning, The Swarm, Stealth and Samurai. Hyperia hardly gets shorter during the day, so if you ride without Fastrack, queue for it right at opening.
3. Lunch, then the Ghost Train, which opens at 12, and The Walking Dead indoors if it rains.
4. Afternoon, Storm Surge and Tidal Wave in the warmest hours, then Vortex, Rush and Zodiac if the wind allows.
5. In the last hour, the big coasters once more. On Fright Nights, from about 4 pm the scare zones and, with a time slot, the mazes.

A second day mostly pays off with an overnight stay. The Overnight Stay package includes the second park day and an hour of Fastrack, from £77 per person.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many rides does Thorpe Park have?

The park lists over 25 rides and attractions. That includes eight coasters if you count Flying Fish and The Walking Dead. We list 45 attractions for the park in autumn 2026, including scare mazes, scare zones and shows that only run at Halloween.

### How tall does my child have to be for which ride?

Hyperia from 1.30 metres. The other big coasters from 1.40 metres, Stealth and Colossus with a maximum height of 1.96 metres, The Swarm 1.95 metres. Flying Fish from 0.90 metres, with an escort up to 1.10 metres.

### What does a day at Thorpe Park cost?

From £32 online in advance, and from £39 during Fright Nights. At the gate it's £66 during Fright Nights. Parking is £12, Fastrack from £6 per ride. Children under 1.20 metres get in free with an online booking.

### When is Thorpe Park least busy?

On Thursdays, and in May, June and September. The busiest day is Saturday, the busiest month July.

### Is Fastrack worth it for Hyperia?

If Hyperia is the only ride you want, buy the One Shot Fastrack at the ride, because its queue is the longest in the park and barely changes over the day. The Platinum package from £135 is the only one that includes Hyperia, and it covers 15 rides. The alternative is the single rider queue.

### Can you get to the park without a car?

Yes. From London Waterloo take the train to Staines and then the 950 bus, or from Heathrow Terminal 4 take the 446 bus.

### Is Thorpe Park open in winter?

Not so far. The 2026 season ends after Fright Nights on 1 November, and the park hasn't given a date after that.

### Which rides suit guests with a disability?

Hyperia is currently not suitable for people who can't walk. For SAW you have to be able to walk 25 metres without help, for the Ghost Train at least 50 steps. The full list is in the park's Rider Requirements.
