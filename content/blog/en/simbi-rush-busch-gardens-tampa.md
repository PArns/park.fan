[Busch Gardens Tampa Bay](ref:busch-gardens-tampa) in Florida is building Simbi Rush, a Mack Rides water coaster, for summer 2027. It launches its boats twice and hits up to 40 mph (about 64 km/h) on a course through the park's Congo area. The park unveiled the ride on November 18 at the IAAPA Expo in Orlando. It calls Simbi Rush North America's first launched water coaster and the fastest of its kind in the world.

## What Simbi Rush is

A launched water coaster speeds its boats up the way a roller coaster does and can send them uphill. The track, the water and the splashdown work as they do on a water slide. Each car seats eight, two across in four rows, and every rider gets their own hydraulic lap bar. Busch Gardens hasn't released the height, the track length or the capacity yet.

The name comes from Simbi, a Central African guardian spirit of water and nature. The ride starts as a calm tour across a sacred lake until the water takes hold of the boat. Whether that's the spirit's anger or a trick of the lake is up to the riders, and they get wet whichever answer they pick.

## Who it's for

The park is planning Simbi Rush as a family ride, but it hasn't set a height requirement yet. The Congo area has two rides today, the [Congo River Rapids](ref:busch-gardens-tampa/congo-river-rapids) raft ride and the [Ubanga-Banga Bumper Cars](ref:busch-gardens-tampa/ubanga-banga-bumper-cars), and both take riders from 42 inches (107 cm).

## Who rides first and what it costs

2027 annual pass holders get to ride before the public opening, though there are no dates for that yet. In the Black Friday sale, the Silver pass costs $198 instead of $279, Gold $222 instead of $315 and Platinum $351 instead of $549, all three with free parking. The cheaper 2027 Fun Card is $94.99 instead of $148.99. It gets you unlimited visits to Busch Gardens and the Adventure Island water park from now through December 31, 2027, except on blockout dates. Parking isn't included and starts at $32 plus tax. The park hasn't announced whether the Fun Card gets you into the preview. It does get you into [Christmas Town with its drone show](/news/busch-gardens-tampa-christmas-town-2026) this winter, though, on select evenings until January 4.

## Getting there from Orlando

If you don't have a car, the Mears Destination Services shuttle takes you from the Orlando area to Tampa, free both ways with any paid Busch Gardens ticket. Book early, because the bus fills up. It makes up to three stops on the way and sometimes doesn't pull up at the park until around 11:30 am, so you may not be there for opening.

## What else is changing in the park

Simbi Rush is the biggest piece of a five-year plan that has Busch Gardens Tampa Bay putting $200 million into the park. Lion & Hyena Ridge opened on July 25 as the first part of the plan, a 38,000-square-foot habitat (about 3,500 square meters) for five lions and two spotted hyenas. Lions and hyenas are rivals in the wild, so they take turns in the different parts of the habitat, like two siblings sharing one game console.

Stanley Falls Flume, the log flume that had run since the park opened in 1973, closed on September 7, 2025, to make room for a new attraction. Busch Gardens hasn't announced exactly where in the Congo area Simbi Rush's track will run, or whether the ride is going in the flume's place. Kumba also ran for the last time on August 2, 2026. The 1993 Bolliger & Mabillard coaster stood 143 feet (44 m) tall, reached 60 mph (97 km/h) and had seven inversions. The park has announced Kumba's Revenge as its successor, so far without a layout or an opening date.

## Other Mack Rides water coasters

The two Mack water coasters at [Europa-Park](ref:europa-park) are faster than Simbi Rush: [Poseidon](ref:europa-park/water-rollercoaster-poseidon) has run at 44 mph (70 km/h) since 2000, and [Atlantica SuperSplash](ref:europa-park/atlantica-supersplash) at 50 mph (80 km/h) since 2005. Both pull their boats up on chain lifts, though, so Tampa's record only holds among launched rides. The closest match is Dønnervind at Freizeitpark Plohn in Saxony, open since June 2026. Like Simbi Rush, it has eight-seat cars and two launches, but it tops out at 33 mph (53 km/h). In October, Dønnervind was [out of service with a technical problem](/news/doennervind-plohn-out-of-service).
