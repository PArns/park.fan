# SEO and machine-facing rules

These are the standing rules for this area, moved out of `CLAUDE.md` so that a session reads an index first and opens only the pages its task needs. The wording is unchanged; each rule links on to the document that carries the measurements behind it.

## The favicon is generated from two sources, and it carries no wordmark (REQUIREMENT)

the icon Google draws beside a result is composed at **16 CSS px**, and the set that shipped before this put the whole lockup in it — „park.fan" under the pin took a quarter of the artwork's height, i.e. ~4 px of cap height, and cost the mark a quarter of its canvas. Worse, both pins are an **outline with a hole for a head**, so they have no figure/ground of their own: the light colourway is #293B47 and Google's dark result page is #202124, which is why the reported screenshot showed a smudge. A transparent icon does not choose its background; a **full-bleed tile** does, and then the icon looks the same in a tab, a bookmark bar, a home screen and both of Google's modes. **The split between the two sources is about pixels, not taste, and the line is the smallest size any surface may draw a file at — not the file's own size:** the detailed pin (`logo-big-dark.svg` — the mark the footer, the OG images, the Organization logo and the maintenance page show) stops working below ~24 px, where the orbit cuts through the white ring and the three bars merge into one smear. So `app/favicon.ico`, `public/icon.svg` **and `apple-touch-icon.png`** take the simple castle pin (`logo-small-dark.svg`, the header's own) — the 180 px Apple icon included, because Google reads `apple-touch-icon` as a favicon candidate and documents no priority against `rel="icon"`, so it is free to scale that file to 16 px itself; on the simple source it leaves Google no detailed candidate at all, the manifest not being a favicon source. What keeps the detailed pin is the app icon: the manifest's 192, 512 and maskable 512, which every documented surface draws large and which **move together**, since a launcher free to pick the 192 or the 512 must not get a different mark depending on which it picked. The detail source is `logo-big-dark.svg`, the **lockup**, whose wordmark is clipped off by the measurement — not `logo-dark.svg`, which is that same pin already cut out and would need no clip. That looks like the obvious simplification and is not one: `logo-dark.svg` is itself generated from the lockup by `generate-brand-pin.mjs`, so reading it here would put a generated file in front of the artwork and make the icon set depend on two generators agreeing. (Until Sep 2026 the reason was duller — `logo-dark.svg` was a 1563×1116 PNG in an SVG wrapper, 77 KB, and not vector at all.) `pnpm generate:icons` writes all six, `pnpm check:icons` fails on drift; before, three different artworks sat in the set and nothing compared them. Two things are **measured by rendering and walking the alpha channel, never typed**: the ink box (the simple pin **used to** fill 62.5 % of a 144×144 viewBox horizontally and 86 % vertically, so scaling the viewBox left a seventh of the icon empty on every edge; it is cropped to its ink now and the two agree, which is the point — the measurement is what makes the icon independent of whatever margin an export carries, in either direction, and it is why the crop turned `check:icons` red on all three simple-pin files for a placement shift of 0.27 in 180 units) and the wordmark's position — the widest fully empty row band, counted as a separator only above 4 % of the ink height, with the mark **clipped** to what sits above it, because scaled to fill the tile the wordmark starts exactly at the pin's lower edge and would paint into the bottom strip rather than fall outside the viewBox. Two traps beyond the artwork. The `link` element Next writes for `app/favicon.ico` reports the **largest frame in the file** as its `sizes` — with 16 and 32 in there it claimed `48x48` and handed Google 32 to upscale, so the frame list (16/32/48/96) is what the markup promises. And **only `app/layout.tsx` may declare `icons`**: metadata does not merge across segments, the nearest declaring segment replaces the whole object, and `icon: '/favicon.ico'` in `app/[locale]/layout.tsx` silently dropped the SVG favicon from every page on the site — the same shape as the `alternates` trap in the blog-feeds rule. A rounded tile is for surfaces that composite the icon as-is; iOS and an Android adaptive icon mask it themselves and get a **square** one, and the maskable icon is its own file because a launcher may keep only the central 80 % circle and cut the pin's tip off. Keep the URLs stable — Google recrawls a favicon on its own schedule, days to weeks. See [favicon](docs/seo/favicon.md).

## Nothing here has a reader inside the site (REQUIREMENT)

the machine-facing surface — `/robots.txt`, `/llms.txt`, `/auth.md`, the four documents under `/.well-known/` (`api-catalog`, `agent-skills`, `ai-catalog.json`, `mcp/server-card.json` — two carry the extension and two do not, so the list is spelled out: typing the wrong one is a 404), the homepage `Link` header, `/api/mcp` and the WebMCP tools — is the only part of this app that **no page renders**. It can rot through a green build and every passing suite, and the first symptom would be an agent getting a 404, which is why `pnpm check:agent-ready` and `pnpm check:webmcp` fetch all of it from outside (a running site) and why a new document belongs in that script in the same commit. Everything it describes lives in `lib/agents/`, and the reason is always the same: **two copies of one claim, one of them invisible.** The `Link` header and the catalog document name the same URLs, so a URL fixed in one and forgotten in the other keeps pointing at a 404; the skills index publishes a **SHA-256 of the bytes served**, computed at build time from `content/agent-skills/<name>/SKILL.md` and never written down beside the file, because an agent is entitled to check that digest and throw the skill away; the MCP server card lists the tools by asking the server for them. Same rule one layer up: the browser's WebMCP tools **call `/api/mcp`** rather than the API, because the rules an answer depends on sit with the data. Two of them are absolute. A park with **no wait-time source** aggregates to Ø 0 minutes over an empty set and is byte-for-byte a park shut for the night — the tools read the curated flag through `noLiveWaitTimesReason()` and answer `waitTimesAvailable: false` with a sentence, and the check asserts exactly that against Hansa-Park; a ride **out of season** is reported by `effectiveStatus`, never the raw `status`. The policy is three questions, not one: `Content-Signal: search=yes, ai-input=yes, ai-train=no`, repeated per crawler because a bot reads only its own block — a training corpus gets `Disallow: /`, a fetch made while a person waits gets what a search engine gets, which is Cloudflare's own Search/Agent/Training split. The same three answers ship a second time as an **RSL licence** at `/license.xml` (`License:` in robots.txt, `Link: …; rel="license"` on every localized page — the one header here that is deliberately not homepage-only, because the audience for a licence is the crawler that skipped robots.txt), and the Content Signals preamble is **Cloudflare's text copied verbatim**, down to the Article 4 EU-copyright reservation: a reworded legal notice is a different legal notice. Two things stay outside the repo on purpose — enforcement (Cloudflare AI Crawl Control) and the markdown negotiation that answers `Accept: text/markdown`, which is an edge conversion, so nothing in this codebase renders it and the check reports it instead of asserting it. And **`/admin` is in none of it**: robots.txt, an `X-Robots-Tag` on the response (the JSON routes under `/api/admin` render no meta tag), the layout metadata, and the navigation tool's own refusal — four fences because each stops a different visitor, and the tool is the only one an agent could otherwise walk through. Two scanner checks stay red on purpose: DNS-AID is a DNS change, and an A2A agent card would describe an agent that does not exist. See [agent readiness](docs/seo/agent-readiness.md).

## A `<lastmod>` is observed, never stamped (REQUIREMENT)

Google ignores `changefreq` and `priority` outright, so until the content-change crawl existed the attraction sitemaps carried **42,756 URLs and no signal at all**, and the main one dated 1,662 of 3,480 — the glossary and the blog, the two things that are files in this repo with a date on them. The API has no per-entity content timestamp (`/v1/sitemap/attractions` answers `{url, slug}`; a park payload dates only its live readings), so the date is **observed**: a daily 05:30 UTC run fingerprints the _stable_ half of every park and ride, and the day the fingerprint moves is the day the page changed (`lib/seo/content-changes/`, snapshot in Vercel Blob because it has to outlive both a deploy and an ISR regeneration). **The whole value is in what stays out of the hash.** A park page repaints every five minutes, so `queues`, `status`, `crowdLevel`, `statistics`, `typicalWaits`, `bestVisitTimes`, `ropeDrop`, `isHeadliner`, `isCurrentlyInSeason`, `weather`, `schedule` and `analytics` are excluded by hand — let one of them in and all 44,000 URLs change every morning, which is a build stamp, and one identical value everywhere is exactly what gets a sitemap's lastmod discounted, taking the glossary's and the blog's honest dates down with it. In goes what a reader sees and what stays put: name, land, height limits, the ride profile with its glossary terms, the curated park facts, the media versions and the blog backlinks. Three rules are load-bearing and each fixed a real hole: a park the API did not answer **keeps its dates** (`retainUncovered`), because dropping it re-adds its rides tomorrow and a re-add reads as a change — one timeout would be 82 false recrawl invitations; a **`FINGERPRINT_VERSION` bump** adopts the new hashes and keeps every date, since editing the detector is not the catalog changing; and which rides count comes from `getAttractionPaths()`, the sitemap's own list, not from re-deriving the variant-slug rule against the park payload — the two disagree on seven rides, which was seven `<lastmod>` values for URLs no sitemap lists. The same snapshot feeds IndexNow, which used to submit all ~46,000 URLs every morning and now sends the paths that moved (Mondays still sweep everything, so a detector that stops detecting cannot silently mute the route; `?dry=1` builds the list without sending it). `pnpm test:content-changes` pins the blindness and the three rules, because a green build shows none of it. See [sitemaps](docs/seo/sitemaps.md#lastmod-observed-not-stamped).

## A feed nobody links to is a file with a URL (REQUIREMENT)

the six blog feeds (`/{locale}/blog/feed.xml`) existed for as long as the blog did, and the discovery link that makes them reachable was spelled out by hand in four `page.tsx` files — so the **category pages**, added later, had none, and neither did the homepage, where a reader looks first. It cannot be fixed centrally: **Next replaces the whole `alternates` object at the nearest segment that declares one** instead of merging into it, proved by `contribute/thanks`, which sets only `canonical` and ships zero hreflang links though the locale layout declares `languages` — and all 25 locale routes declare their own. So every route carries it, through **`blogFeedAlternates(locale)`** (`lib/blog/feed.ts`), which returns the whole `types` object so a call site cannot half-build it, `undefined` where the locale publishes nothing (the route 404s there and a broken feed is recorded against the site, not the locale), and the **array-of-descriptors** form because a bare string carries no `title`. Park and glossary pages get none on purpose: the spec wants a page's own main feed. And the `<head>` was never the whole job — the link was on `/en/blog` all along and **no page rendered one a person could click**, so the footer now does, inside the same `showBlog` guard the feed 404s under. In the items, **a widget fence must never be rendered into the feed**: a reader archives an item on arrival and never re-fetches it, so today's numbers would sit frozen in every subscriber's client forever, which is the exact thing the fences exist to prevent — each becomes a link to the live table, in place, because the prose around it refers to it. `ref:` links resolve through **`resolvePark` only** (a ride URL is its park's href plus the slug; `resolveAttraction` would cost 425 KB per ride for a URL), and `react-markdown` needs its `urlTransform` overridden or every one of them arrives as `href=""`. Three old bugs are pinned by `pnpm check:agent-ready`: `lastBuildDate` came off `posts[0]` of the **featured-first** `listPosts`, so pinning an older post moved the channel's timestamp backwards; items ended with `<readingTime>` in no namespace plus a `<source>` naming this feed, i.e. every item claiming to be a repost of itself; and every `<enclosure>` said `length="0"` — it is measured off the file now, never from the media database's `bytes`, which describes the **source** photo and not the crop a cover usually is (89 KB against 290 KB for Voltron). WebSub is two halves and both are load-bearing: the feed declares the hub, and `/api/cron/websub` (plus `pnpm ping:websub` for the minutes after publishing) tells it to look — a declared hub that is never pinged is exactly as fast as polling. See [blog feeds](docs/seo/blog-feeds.md).

## Umami event budget (REQUIREMENT)

Umami Cloud bills **every event property as another event**, so a 5-property event costs 6× a pageview and the plan (100k/month) is blown by properties, not traffic. Before adding one: never send what Umami already has (URL, referrer, `navigator.language` → so no `locale`, no `path`) and never send what a sibling property implies (`in_park` was `type === 'in_park'`, `hasQuery` was `queryLength > 0`). Events firing on **load** cost their property count on every pageview — price them accordingly. Keep `data-exclude-hash="true"` on the script tag: the tracker patches `replaceState` and bills a changed hash as a fresh pageview, which the park tabs and the calendar month stepper both write. See [analytics](docs/development/analytics.md).
