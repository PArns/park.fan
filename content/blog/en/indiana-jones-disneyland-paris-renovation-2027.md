Disneyland Paris is closing [Indiana Jones and the Temple of Peril](ref:/parks/europe/france/paris/disneyland-park/indiana-jones-and-the-temple-of-peril) in [Disneyland Park](ref:/parks/europe/france/paris/disneyland-park) for several months from 4 January 2027. In its announcement of 26 October, the park calls the work the biggest renovation since the coaster opened in 1993. Disney hasn't announced a reopening date yet.

Teams from Walt Disney Imagineering Paris, Design & Delivery and maintenance are doing the work, and Disney says the aim is more comfort for guests and Cast Members alike.

## What's being done to the ride

At the temple, Disney is working on the stones it's built from and rebuilding parts of it from scratch. The track's support structures get a thorough overhaul, above all the rails and the catwalks. Wooden and metal railings and cladding are being replaced, and the decor comes down and goes to the resort's central workshops to be restored.

In the queue, the wooden walkways are being rebuilt completely. The decor on the staircase gets restored, and so do the camp scenery and the jeep you walk past while you wait.

New spotlights and updated lighting effects go in around the ride: for the attraction's scene lighting, for the Indiana Jones Adventure Outpost shop and for the area around the coaster.

## If you're visiting in January or spring

Indiana Jones normally runs all year, winter included. If you're in the park over Christmas and New Year, you'll still catch it before it closes. For a trip from 4 January onwards I'd plan without the ride until there's a date, and that goes for Easter too.

Families with small children lose the least. You have to be at least 1.40 metres tall to ride, and no other Disney attraction anywhere in the world has a higher minimum. Anyone shorter isn't missing a spring ride they'd have been allowed on before.

## A coaster with one loop

Indiana Jones and the Temple of Peril is a steel coaster from Intamin, a Multi Inversion Coaster model, designed by the engineering firm Stengel and built with Giovanola as subcontractor. A chain lift pulls the trains up to 18 metres. After that come a loop and a helix, the trains hit 58 km/h, and after roughly 600 metres of track the ride's over in one minute and 15 seconds.

Each train has two cars, each with three rows of two seats, so twelve riders in all. The layout was borrowed from the TL-59, Pinfari's first looping coaster. No coaster in a Disney park had turned anyone upside down before this one.

## In Adventureland since 1993

Indiana Jones opened on 30 July 1993. On 1 April 2000 it became a backwards coaster, and that wasn't an April Fool's joke: until 27 November 2004 the trains went through the loop in reverse. From 28 November to 3 December 2004 the ride was converted back to run forwards.

The ride has closed in January before. On 7 January 2014 it went into a major renovation, in which parts of the structure and track were torn down and rebuilt with more durable materials. It reopened on 28 May 2014, on the same layout and with a slightly bigger loop.
