Anyone trying to dodge Walibi Holland’s busy days usually thinks of August. Turns out there’s no
need: in the 2026 summer holidays you didn’t queue any longer in Biddinghuizen than on an average
day of the season. The real crowds come in October, on the evenings when up to 20,000 people pay
good money to be chased through the dark by someone with a chainsaw.

Since 26 December 2025 we’ve been reading the wait time of every ride at
[Walibi Holland](ref:walibi-holland) every five minutes. That gives each day a crowd level in our
[crowd calendar](ref:walibi-holland?calendar), and for this article I’ve lined those days up by
weekday, by month and by school holiday. For the rides themselves, the park’s history and the
prices, read [our guide to Untamed and "hard gaan"](/blog/walibi-holland-untamed-hard-gaan).

## When is it quiet at Walibi Holland?

Walibi Holland is quietest in May and June, after the May holiday and outside Whitsun, from
Wednesday to Friday and, against expectations, on Saturdays too. From 4 May to 21 June 2026 our
calendar showed Low on 23 of the 37 open days, and on six of the seven Saturdays between 9 May and
20 June as well. The summer holidays are better than their reputation: from 18 July to 16 August
2026, when the Noord, Midden and Zuid regions were all off school, queues at the headliners were
as long as on an average day of the season. The busiest stretch is the autumn half-term with the
Halloween Fright Nights. On those evenings, open until 23:00, queues were more than one and a half
times as long as on an average day from April to September, and the calendar showed Extreme on 17,
18 and 22 October. Mondays and Tuesdays show too few days measured, because the park is usually
closed on those days outside the holidays.

## How the crowd levels are worked out

Every past day gets a crowd level in our calendar, measured against the park’s own wait times.
**Normal** is as busy as an average open day at Walibi Holland, **Low** and **Very Low** are
quieter, **High**, **Very High** and **Extreme** are busier. So a Low in Biddinghuizen isn’t the
same thing as a Low at the Efteling: it only says that Walibi itself was quieter that day than
usual. The calendar counts [YOY THRILL](ref:walibi-holland/yoy-thrill),
[YOY CHILL](ref:walibi-holland/yoy-chill), [Lost Gravity](ref:walibi-holland/lost-gravity),
[Untamed](ref:walibi-holland/untamed) and [Goliath](ref:walibi-holland/goliath) as the
headliners.

Up to 25 October 2026 that adds up to 165 open days with measurements. Of those, 56 came out at
Low or lower, 55 at Normal and 54 at High or higher. Sounds nicely balanced, until you see how
unevenly they’re spread across the year: thirteen of the sixteen measured days in October were High
or higher, while in May it was five of 25.

For the days still to come, our model calculates a forecast, and it shows up as a crowd level in
the same calendar. For today’s crowds, check the park page: it lists the rides that are open right
now and how long you’ll queue for each of them at this moment.

## The days of the week

```stats-widget slug=walibi-holland show=weekdays

```

On the park page, Wednesday to Sunday all show **about average**. Across every measured day since
December 2025 the five sit so close together that none of them comes out as the quietest or the
busiest. Thursday comes nearest to quiet, Sunday nearest to busy.

Monday and Tuesday say **too few days measured**, and that’s no accident. Outside the school
holidays and public holidays Walibi Holland is almost always closed on those two days: in April,
May, early June, September and the first half of October 2026 the park only opened on a Monday or
Tuesday on 6 April (Easter Monday), on 20 and 21 April, during the May holiday, on 5 May
(Liberation Day) and on 25 May (Whit Monday). From 22 June it opened every day, through to 30
August. So the few Mondays and Tuesdays we do have nearly all fall in a holiday, and comparing them
with an ordinary Wednesday in May would be comparing apples with oranges. On 12 and 13 October, the
Monday and Tuesday of the regio Noord autumn half-term, the park was closed too.

The gap between Saturday and Sunday is bigger than the one between the weekdays, just not in the
direction you’d expect. From 9 May to 20 June 2026, six of the seven Saturdays were Low and the
seventh was Normal. Of the Sundays in the same weeks, three were High: 24 May (Whit Sunday), 31 May
and 14 June. If you can only manage one weekend day in spring, make it the Saturday. We don’t
measure why that is. My guess is the weekly shop and the kids’ football matches.

An ordinary Friday can spike too. On 17 April and 26 June 2026, two Fridays with no holiday or
public holiday anywhere in the Netherlands, the calendar showed Very High.

## The months

```stats-widget slug=walibi-holland show=months

```

On the park page, April is listed as **Busiest**. That’s right for the months with enough measured
days to compare against each other, April to August. September and October fall outside that,
because the park only opens at weekends and in the autumn half-term then. On the October days we
did measure, it was by far the busiest of the year.

Here’s how the crowd levels of the 2026 season are spread over the months:

| Month 2026            | Open days measured | Low or lower | Normal | High or higher |
| --------------------- | ------------------ | ------------ | ------ | -------------- |
| April                 | 26                 | 4            | 12     | 10             |
| May                   | 25                 | 16           | 4      | 5              |
| June                  | 24                 | 10           | 9      | 5              |
| July                  | 31                 | 13           | 13     | 5              |
| August                | 30                 | 9            | 11     | 10             |
| September             | 10                 | 2            | 4      | 4              |
| October (to the 25th) | 16                 | 1            | 2      | 13             |

**April** starts with Easter and ends in the May holiday. The park’s open Wednesday to Sunday,
from 10:00 to 17:00 during the week and until 18:00 at weekends. Ten days came out at High or
higher, and they were scattered across the whole month: only three of them in the May holiday, the
rest on ordinary weekends and in the week of 20 April, when the park opened every day.

**May** was the best month of 2026. After the May holiday, from Wednesday 6 May, every Saturday
and eight of the eleven weekdays were Low. The busy days in May were 1 and 2 May (still the May
holiday), Liberation Day on 5 May and the Sundays of 24 and 31 May.

**June** runs the same way until roughly the 20th. From 22 June the park opens every day, and the
two weeks before the summer holidays started in regio Noord were busier than any week of the
summer holidays themselves. From 22 June to 3 July 2026 queues at the headliners were more than a
third longer than in the May and June weeks before. The 29th and 30th of June and the 1st of July
were all High, and 26 June even made it to Very High.

**September** is weekend season: the park opens on Fridays (not every week), Saturdays and
Sundays. In 2026 every weekend was busier than the one before. The first, 4 to 6 September, was
Low and Normal, the weekend of 19 and 20 September High, and Sunday 27 September, the last day
before the Fright Nights, Very High. If September’s your month, go on the first or second weekend.

From **November to March** Walibi Holland is closed. The park dropped its winter event Bright
Nights, which ran for four winters in December and January, in April 2026. Walibi hasn’t announced
the opening date for the 2027 season yet.

## School holidays by region

The Netherlands splits its summer, autumn and spring school holidays across three regions, Noord,
Midden and Zuid, so that the whole country isn’t off at the same time. **Biddinghuizen is in regio
Noord**: the municipality of Dronten, like all of Flevoland except Zeewolde, belongs to Noord, as do
Noord-Holland and Overijssel. Utrecht and Zuid-Holland are Midden; Limburg, Zeeland and nearly all
of Noord-Brabant are Zuid. Gelderland is split across all three regions: Harderwijk, Ede and
Apeldoorn belong to Midden, Arnhem and Nijmegen to Zuid.

### The 2026 summer holidays

In 2026 regio Noord had its summer holidays from 4 July to 16 August, Zuid from 11 July to 23
August and Midden from 18 July to 30 August. That cuts the summer into five pieces:

| Period 2026       | Off school      | Days measured | Low or lower | Normal | High or higher |
| ----------------- | --------------- | ------------- | ------------ | ------ | -------------- |
| 4–10 July         | Noord           | 7             | 3            | 3      | 1              |
| 11–17 July        | Noord and Zuid  | 7             | 5            | 0      | 2              |
| 18 July–16 August | all three       | 30            | 11           | 12     | 7              |
| 17–23 August      | Midden and Zuid | 7             | 3            | 3      | 1              |
| 24–30 August      | Midden          | 7             | 0            | 4      | 3              |

The first two weeks were the quietest of the whole summer, with headliner queues as short as in
the May and June weeks after the May holiday. In the four weeks when the whole country was off, you
queued as long as on an average day of the season. The last week, when only Midden is still off,
was almost a tenth busier than that average, and not a single day came out at Low.

Four weeks in which every schoolchild in the Netherlands is off, and the queues at the headliners
don’t grow. I’d expected the opposite, and so, probably, had my August days off. My explanation is
that in summer the park’s open until 19:00 every day, and until 22:00 on the evening openings, so
the same visitors spread out over far more hours and days than in spring.

On six Wednesdays (15, 22 and 29 July and 5, 12 and 19 August 2026) **#Lekkergaan** ran until
22:00, and on five Saturdays (25 July and 1, 8, 15 and 22 August) the **Zomerse Zaterdagen**
("summer Saturdays") did the same. On those eleven evening days queues were about a tenth longer
than on the other summer days. On 15 July and 5, 12 and 15 August the calendar showed High, on 22
August Low.

### The May holiday and public holidays

In 2026 the May holiday fell at the same time for the whole of the Netherlands, from 25 April to 3
May. Not one of those nine days came out at Low, and queues were more than a quarter longer than in
the weeks that followed.

The public holidays, on the other hand, barely stand out. This is how they looked in
the 2026 calendar:

| Holiday                         | Date 2026       | Crowds |
| ------------------------------- | --------------- | ------ |
| Good Friday                     | Friday 3 April  | Normal |
| Easter Sunday                   | Sunday 5 April  | Low    |
| Easter Monday                   | Monday 6 April  | Low    |
| King’s Day (in the May holiday) | Monday 27 April | Normal |
| Liberation Day                  | Tuesday 5 May   | High   |
| Ascension Day                   | Thursday 14 May | Low    |
| Friday after Ascension          | Friday 15 May   | Normal |
| Whit Sunday                     | Sunday 24 May   | High   |
| Whit Monday                     | Monday 25 May   | Low    |

If you’ve got a Monday off, and on Easter Monday and Whit Monday most people have, you can visit
Walibi on a day the park would otherwise be closed. Both came out at Low. Liberation Day on a
Tuesday, on the other hand, was one of the five busy days in May.

### The autumn half-term

In 2026 regio Noord had its autumn half-term from 10 to 18 October, Midden and Zuid from 17 to 25
October. Just across the border in North Rhine-Westphalia the autumn holidays also started on 17
October. The weekend of 17 and 18 October was the only one when all three Dutch regions were off at
once, the first weekend of the German autumn holidays and a weekend with two Fright Nights. On those
two days queues at the headliners were more than twice as long as on an average day of the season.
In the regio Noord week before, they were about half as long again as average, and in the Midden
and Zuid week after, a little more than that.

The weekdays without a Fright Night (14, 15, 19, 20 and 21 October) were Spooky Days, held in the
daytime and aimed at families.

has the first week, 10 to 16 July, to itself.

## Halloween Fright Nights

> [!NOTE]
> In 2026 the Halloween Fright Nights run on 3, 4, 10, 11, 16, 17, 18, 22, 23, 24, 25 and 31
> October and on 1 November, each time from 10:00 to 23:00.

At Walibi Holland a Fright Night is a whole day on its own ticket, from 10:00 to 23:00. From 2026
the park keeps them separate from the **Spooky Days**, the Halloween days for children aged 6 to
12, which run in the daytime until 18:00 and come with an ordinary day ticket. Walibi’s reasons:
evening guests were already filling the park in the afternoon, families with young children stayed
away because of it, and the actors had to switch from child-friendly to no-holds-barred within a
few hours. The Fright Nights have no minimum age, but Walibi recommends them from 16.

On a Fright Night the first haunted house opens at 13:00, and The Villa and Jefferson Manor close
as early as 22:00. The houses and experiences cost extra and have daily quotas. Doing them in the
afternoon is cheaper: in 2026 the **Before-Before Dark** package got you into The Villa and US vs
YOU before 16:00, and **Before Dark** into Jefferson Manor, Psychoshock and Camp of Curiosities
before 19:00.

![Lit wooden towers at the entrance to US vs YOU at dusk | US vs YOU is one of the Fright Nights haunted houses. Before 16:00 the Before-Before Dark package got you in for less. | wide](/media/halloween-2026/kulissen/07-us-vs-you-eingang.jpg)

### How busy the Fright Nights were

Of the eleven Fright Nights up to 25 October 2026, three came out at Extreme (17, 18 and 22
October), five at Very High, two at High and one at Low. On the five Spooky Days the calendar
showed High three times and Normal twice. At the headliners, queues on the Fright Nights were
about 40 percent longer than on the Spooky Days, and more than one and a half times as long as on
an average day from April to September.

What sets the Fright Nights apart is that the day of the week hardly matters any more. The busiest
day of the year was Saturday 17 October, but right behind it came **Thursday 22 October**, a Fright
Night in the middle of the Midden and Zuid autumn half-term, busier than any Saturday in summer.
The quietest Fright Night was **Sunday 4 October**, the second evening of the season, and it came
out at Low, while the Saturday before was High. Every evening at Very High or Extreme fell in the
autumn half-term of one of the three regions. The early-bird tickets for 17, 24 and 31 October
sold out first, and according to the park the evening of Saturday 24 October was sold out, with an
estimated 20,000 visitors.

That Saturday evening, groups of visitors got into fistfights, just as on the last Saturday of 2025. Our [news piece on the trouble at the Fright Nights](/news/walibi-holland-fright-nights-2026)
covers what happened and the rules at the gate. For
planning, it means this: if you want to see the Fright Nights without the busiest evening of the
year, pick a Sunday early in October. On 4 October 2026 you queued for less time there than on an
average day in August.

![An actor dressed as a gravedigger with a shovel between two columns, lit in blue | A gravedigger from Walibi Holland’s Halloween cast. He doesn’t start work until after sunset. | right](/media/halloween-2026/darsteller/22-totengraeber-blau.jpg)

### Tickets, and the Fright Nights with a pass

In 2026 a Walibi Pass or another pass gets you two free visits to the Fright Nights, which you
have to book in advance. Unlimited Fright Nights cost an extra €27.50. You don’t need a parking
ticket on Fright Nights days. The Spooky Days are simply covered by the pass.

If you want more from a Fright Night than the free scare zones, book the houses in advance and the
experiences first of all: Slaughterhouse, The Clinic and Below run with very small groups, so
there are only a few places each evening. On the busy evenings they’re gone before you’ve parked in
Biddinghuizen. Prices for the houses and Walibi’s four levels of Halloween are in
[our guide to Walibi Holland](/blog/walibi-holland-untamed-hard-gaan), the other Halloween parks
of 2026 in [the Halloween overview](/blog/halloween-theme-parks-2026).

## Which rides are worth the queue, and when

```ride-waits-widget park=walibi-holland top=10 columns=land,peak,days highlight=untamed

```

The longest queue in the park is at [YOY THRILL](ref:walibi-holland/yoy-thrill), the green track of
the 2025 RMC dual single-rail coaster. Lost Gravity, Untamed, YOY CHILL and Goliath follow close
behind.

How the queues change over the day varies more from ride to ride than from weekday to weekday:

```hourly-profile-widget slug=walibi-holland top=8

```

At opening, in the hour from 10:00 to 11:00, the queue at nearly every big ride is half as long as
in the middle of the day, or shorter. The difference is biggest at YOY THRILL and Lost Gravity. The
exception is [Xpress: Platform 13](ref:walibi-holland/xpress-platform-13) on Main Street, the only
big ride with its longest queue in the morning. From 14:00 it’s down to half.
[Condor](ref:walibi-holland/condor) and [Goliath](ref:walibi-holland/goliath) get shorter after
16:00, YOY THRILL in the last hour.

### Untamed

[Untamed](ref:walibi-holland/untamed?full)

RMC’s hybrid coaster has stood on Robin Hood’s old wooden frame since 2019, and for me it’s the
reason to drive to Biddinghuizen at all. Its queue peaks later than the other rides’, around
14:00 and 15:00, and it’s at its shortest of the whole day at opening. So head straight for the
Wilderness and ride it twice in a row. After 16:00 it drops a little again, but it never gets as
short as at 10:00.

### YOY THRILL and YOY CHILL

The two rides start on the same lift hill and run alongside and around each other for the whole
ride. YOY THRILL (six inversions) has the longest queue in the park, YOY CHILL (no inversions) a
shorter one. With a child who doesn’t dare go upside down yet, you can ride at the same time, each
on your own track. Do them at opening or in the last hour, not between 12:00 and 15:00.

### Lost Gravity, Goliath and Condor

![A Lost Gravity car next to a shipping container, with old railway signals in the foreground | Lost Gravity in the Zero Zone, between a shipping container and railway signals that don’t point anywhere any more. | left](/media/walibi-holland/lost-gravity-4x3.jpg)

[Lost Gravity](ref:walibi-holland/lost-gravity) by Mack Rides fills up fastest in the first hour,
and after that its queue stays about the same all day. So take it straight after Untamed or YOY.
[Goliath](ref:walibi-holland/goliath), the 2002 Intamin Mega Coaster, has a queue that barely
changes between 11:00 and 16:00. Slot it in wherever it suits you, ideally after 16:00.
[Condor](ref:walibi-holland/condor), Vekoma’s very first Suspended Looping Coaster, is longer in
the morning than after lunch. If you don’t walk into the park until 14:00, start there.

### Xpress and the rest

![A station board in the queue line showing "Next Departure 06:66" and Platform 13 | Glenbrook Station, the queue of Xpress: Platform 13. The next train leaves at 06:66. | right](/media/walibi-holland/xpress-platform-13-4x3.jpg)

Xpress is the one ride you deliberately don’t do in the morning. In the afternoon the queue is
half as long. [Speed of Sound](ref:walibi-holland/speed-of-sound) barely changes over the day, and
[El Rio Grande](ref:walibi-holland/el-rio-grande) and [Crazy River](ref:walibi-holland/crazy-river)
suit a warm afternoon, when you’re soaked in sweat anyway.
[Space Shot](ref:walibi-holland/space-shot) and [Eat My Dust](ref:walibi-holland/eat-my-dust) fit
in between.

You don’t need a Fast Lane on a Low or Normal day. On a High day, on a Fright Night and for YOY
THRILL it can pay off.

## Walibi next to the Efteling, Toverland and Walibi Belgium

```park-comparison-widget slugs=walibi-holland,efteling,attractiepark-toverland,walibi-belgium show=quietest highlight=walibi-holland

```

Over the whole year you queue for less time at Walibi Holland than at the Efteling, and for longer
than at Toverland. The Efteling, Toverland and Walibi Belgium each have one weekday that’s clearly
quieter than the rest. Walibi Holland doesn’t: the week spreads out too evenly here to name any day
the quietest, and for planning that’s good news. If you can go in May or June, you hardly need to
think about the day of the week.

## Frequently asked questions about crowds at Walibi Holland

### When is Walibi Holland at its quietest?

In May and June, after the May holiday and outside Whitsun, and then from Wednesday to Saturday.
In those weeks of 2026 nearly two thirds of the open days came out at Low. From 22 June, when the
park opens every day, it actually gets busier until the summer holidays start.

### Is Walibi Holland busy in the summer holidays?

No busier than an ordinary day of the season. In the four weeks of 2026 when all three regions
were off, queues at the headliners were as long as on an average day of the season. The quietest
summer weeks were the first two in July, the busiest the last week of August.

### Is Walibi Holland open on Mondays and Tuesdays?

Outside the school holidays and public holidays, usually not. The park is open in the summer
holidays, on Easter Monday, on Whit Monday and in the May holiday. That’s why the number of
measured Mondays and Tuesdays is too small to compare them with the other weekdays.

### How busy are the Halloween Fright Nights?

They’re the busiest days of the year. Of the eleven Fright Nights up to 25 October 2026, three
came out at Extreme and five at Very High, and queues were more than one and a half times as long
as on an average day between April and September. The quietest Fright Night was Sunday 4 October.

### Which school-holiday region is Walibi Holland in?

Regio Noord. Biddinghuizen is part of the municipality of Dronten, and all of Flevoland except
Zeewolde falls under Noord. If you’re coming from Utrecht or Zuid-Holland, you’ve got the regio
Midden holidays.

### Where can I see today’s crowds and the forecast for the coming days?

The [Walibi Holland park page](ref:walibi-holland) shows the current wait times, updated every
five minutes. The [crowd calendar](ref:walibi-holland?calendar) has the expected crowds for every
upcoming open day, with the opening times and the school holidays. From 2 November until the 2027
season starts, every day there shows closed.

### When does Walibi Holland reopen?

After the last Fright Night on 1 November 2026 the park is closed. There’s no winter event any
more, and Walibi hasn’t announced the opening date for the 2027 season yet. In 2026 the season
started in April.
