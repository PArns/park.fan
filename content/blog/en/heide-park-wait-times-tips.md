On **28 July 2016**
[Colossos](ref:heide-park/colossos-kampf-der-giganten) in the Land of the Forgotten went offline in the
middle of the season, because after fifteen years the track was worn out. It
didn’t run again until **19 April 2019**.

Since 2011 Rocky
Mountain Construction has been taking worn-out woodies, bolting steel rails onto
the old structure and turning them into hybrids. In the same year Colossos
returned, Walibi Holland opened [Untamed](ref:walibi-holland/untamed?bare), such a conversion of the wooden coaster Robin Hood. Merlin went the other way in Soltau and had new wooden track laid, with
new trains to match. The first ride was **25 years** ago on 13 April 2026; the
park celebrated on 12 September with an evening party open only to winners of a
golden ticket.

## Four German firsts

Around Colossos stand four coasters that were each the first of their type in
Germany, and the last three arrived within seven years.

The run started in **1999**, two years before Colossos, with a coaster that was
called Limit back then and is called
**[Toxic Garden](ref:heide-park/toxic-garden)** today: the country’s first
suspended looping coaster, a Vekoma model where the train hangs below the track
and your feet dangle free. 33 metres tall, 689 metres long, five inversions.

**[Desert Race](ref:heide-park/desert-race)** arrived on **15 May 2007** as
Germany’s first launch coaster. There’s no lift hill; a hydraulic catapult
takes the train to **102 km/h** in two seconds. After 650 metres and 49 seconds
it’s back in the station.

Intamin built Desert Race to a design by Ingenieurbüro Stengel. The track climbs to 19 metres, the park quotes forces of up to 4.7 g, and each of the two trains seats 20.


**[Krake](ref:heide-park/krake)** followed on **16 April 2011** as Germany’s
first dive coaster, built by Bolliger & Mabillard for 12 million euros, with its
station on the site of the old dolphinarium. 41 metres tall, six people side by
side in one row, and at the top the train stops for a few seconds with the
front row hanging out over the edge. Then it goes down at 87 degrees, so almost
vertically, at **103 km/h** through a splashdown and into an Immelmann.

**[Flug der Dämonen](ref:heide-park/flug-der-daemonen)** was the country’s
first wing coaster on **29 March 2014**. On this layout the seats hang off the left and right sides of the track, with nothing in front of your feet. 40 metres tall, 772 metres long, 100 km/h, five inversions,
three minutes of ride time, 1,060 guests per hour.

## The wooden coaster Colossos

```glossary-widget slug=wooden-coaster

```

When it opened in April 2001, Colossos was the steepest wooden coaster in the
world according to Guinness. The only taller and faster one was Son of Beast at
Kings Island in the US, which not everyone counted as a true woodie because of
its steel loop. Balder at Liseberg took the Guinness title in 2003, and since
2006 Colossos has held no world record at all. In Europe it’s still the tallest
all-wooden coaster. Wildfire at Kolmården in Sweden is faster at 115 km/h and,
according to the Roller Coaster DataBase, taller too, but it runs on steel-topped
Topper Track from Rocky Mountain Construction.

The Roller Coaster DataBase lists
**50 metres** of height, a first drop of **48.5 metres** at 61 degrees,
**110 km/h** and **1,344 metres** of track in two minutes and 25 seconds; the
park itself says 60 metres and 1,500 metres. The design came from
Ingenieurbüro Stengel, and Intamin built it together with the carpentry firm
Cordes.

For Colossos, Intamin had used a process in which the running surface is
prefabricated from glued wooden layers and then assembled. The first old rail came out in June 2018, and when it opened again it was called
**Colossos – Kampf der Giganten**. Since then a fire-breathing giant has stood
on the last hill, and the train rides straight through it.

## The other rides and the tower

The park’s first coaster is the **[Big Loop](ref:heide-park/big-loop)** from
**1983**, a Vekoma looper with two vertical loops and a double corkscrew, just
over 700 metres long and done in under two minutes. In 2010 it got the trains of the
demolished Corkscrew from Alton Towers, and for the 2026 season Vekoma
delivered new trains styled after the original 1983 cars.

The Big Loop is 30 metres tall and reaches 70 km/h. The Roller Coaster DataBase lists it as a Vekoma MK-1200 carrying 1,200 riders an hour, and its layout is the mirror image of Dragon Fyre at Canada’s Wonderland. Its very first trains came from Arrow.

Toxic Garden has only carried that name since **May 2023**. Vekoma replaced a
section of track during the rebuild, the entrance and station have stood in a
garden of poisonous plants ever since, and the ride is now themed like the
Transylvania land its site has belonged to since 2011. The train reaches 80 km/h, and the model was designed by Peter Clerx.

The **[Bobbahn](ref:heide-park/bobbahn)** by Mack Rides has run without fixed
rails in a half pipe since **1993**. The bobs start from 27 metres up, the ride
lasts 166 seconds according to the park, and in bad weather it opens late or
not at all. The **[Grottenblitz](ref:heide-park/grottenblitz)** has been doing
its laps since **1985** for everyone still too small for the big rides, and the
**[Indy-Blitz](ref:heide-park/indy-blitz)** from 2008 is the smallest coaster
on the property at 4.5 metres.

The Bobbahn used to be called Schweizer Bobbahn (Swiss bobsled). It is 826 metres long, and the park gives 58 km/h as its top speed. The Grottenblitz, formerly Mountain-Train and Mountain-Blitz, is also a Mack ride. Over 370 metres it reaches 45 km/h and banks at up to 40 percent in the curves. The Indy-Blitz is a Zierer Force One, 128 metres long, with a top speed of 23 km/h.

**[Scream](ref:heide-park/scream)** is **103 metres** tall and lets you fall
freely for 71 of them, at just under 100 km/h (the park says 98.3). The tower
started out in 1992 as an observation tower; Intamin rebuilt it into a gyro
drop tower between October 2002 and April 2003 for 7.5 million euros. For years
the park advertised it as the tallest gyro drop tower in the world, until the
120-metre Highlander opened at Hansa-Park in 2019. It has only been the tallest
structure on the site since **March 2022**, though: until then a second tower
from 1985 stood next to it, the 104-metre Panoramaturm, whose gondola no longer
met current safety standards according to the park. Three days after the
announcement, dismantling began.

The newest ride is the
**[Dämonen Gruft](ref:heide-park/daemonen-gruft)** by Preston & Barbieri. Since
**2024** it has run for about three minutes at a time through the old station of
the second log flume, which closed in 2011 and stood empty for thirteen years.
Flug der Dämonen now stands where the flume used to run.

The **[Wildwasserbahn](ref:heide-park/wildwasserbahn)** log flume from 1980
and **[Ghostbusters 5D](ref:heide-park/ghostbusters-5d)**, which has sat since
2017 in the hall where the puppet show Hallo Spencer used to live, sit high in
our wait-time table. You also get wet
on **[Mountain Rafting](ref:heide-park/mountain-rafting)** (1992, an Intamin
rapid river). The youngest visitors get two corners of their own, Peppa Pig
Land and the "How to Train Your Dragon" island.

The four big coasters all start at **140 cm**, Toxic Garden and Scream at 130, Big Loop
and Bobbahn at 120. The lowest bar in the park is **86 cm**, at George’s Dino
Adventure in Peppa Pig Land. On the Indy-Blitz the bar is 100 cm; the Grottenblitz has none and asks for a minimum age of four instead.

## Shows and events in 2026

The park’s show ensemble plays four shows in daily rotation in 2026. New this year is "Aye Aye Käpt’n!", a hands-on show of about 30 minutes for children aged three to eight, who get a diploma after passing a pirate test. Alongside it run the acrobatics show "Time", the magic show "Smilie, it’s magic" and "The Movie Maker". Photos with Toothless and a Viking, with Peppa and George and with the Ghostbusters at the Ecto-1 are free, taken on your own phone.

The 2026 pirate festival in the Bucht der Piraten had a shanty choir, walkabout acts and a pirate camp. The Pyro Games are a competition between fireworks teams from all over Germany, and the first display starts at nightfall. They are run by THF Events from Magdeburg, the park provides the grounds, and tickets are also sold at an evening box office. The park will announce the dates for 2027 later.

### The Express Ticket

```glossary-widget slug=express-pass

```

The **Express Ticket** comes in four tiers: Bronze from **€25**
for the log flume, Big Loop and Bobbahn, Silver from €39 for five of the big
attractions, Gold from €59 for all eight, Platinum from €99 for all eight with
no limit. The park’s own page lists the five in Silver in two different ways. Its comparison table names Colossos, Desert Race, Flug der
Dämonen, Krake and Scream, while the list below it puts Colossos and Scream in
Gold only. Toxic Garden isn’t in any tier.

The three lower tiers include **one ride per attraction**. If you want a second go on Colossos
with Gold, you join the back of the normal queue like everyone else. And it’s
sold on selected days only, in 2026 mostly weekends and school holidays. The
park advises booking online in advance, because once a day is sold out there
are none left on site.

## When to go

```stats-widget slug=heide-park show=months,weekdays

```

The 2026 season runs from **28 March to 31 October**, with a preview Sunday on
22 March. Soltau has no winter season and no Christmas market either, unlike
other German parks
([here’s the overview](/blog/winter-theme-parks-2026)).

In September the Mondays of 7, 14, 21 and 28 September are closing days, and in
October it’s open daily again. An ordinary
operating day runs from 10am to 5pm, an hour longer on Sundays. Those times are
for the rides; the park itself closes an hour later.

The long autumn days start late, at noon, and rides run until 9pm. On 19 and
26 September and 3 October they’re called Late Rides, and the five after that
belong to Halloween. On all of these days the water rides and a few other
attractions close at dusk, from around 5pm.

### The weekday

Saturday sits at the top, Friday at the bottom, and the gap between the two is
small. Monday rests
on only about half as many measured days as the rest,
because the park regularly takes it out of the calendar in spring and in
September. So the Mondays that do get counted are mostly high-season ones, and
those aren’t quiet.

### The month

The month moves roughly as much as the weekday does. July and August sit high, September drops off noticeably after
them. Our analysis runs to 25 September 2026 and holds no October day yet; the
park itself names the school holidays as its busiest periods, and October
brings autumn holidays and Halloween at once.

In Lower Saxony and Bremen the 2026 summer school holidays ran from 2 July to 12 August, in Hamburg from 9 July to 19 August. The autumn holidays in Lower Saxony and Bremen fall between 12 and 24 October 2026, in Hamburg between 19 and 30 October. The Lower Saxony Easter holidays ran from 23 March to 7 April, so they spanned the start of the season.

### Halloween: 10 to 31 October 2026

On five Halloween days the rides run from noon to 9pm (Sat 10, Sat 17, Sat 24,
Fri 30 and Sat 31), with the park open until 10pm; the other days keep normal
hours. During the day the decoration is family-friendly, with a wizard’s forest
and a trick-or-treat rally for children. On the five long days the scare zone
and three horror mazes open from around 4pm: Parasomnis, Grand Hotel Morton
and SubTerra. The mazes have a minimum age of **16** and you must show ID.
Each maze needs its own timed ticket, sold online only, €9 per maze or €22 for
all three. If you only want the evening, the evening ticket from €24 gets you
into the park from 5pm. Toverland, Movie Park and Walibi are in the [Halloween guide 2026](/blog/halloween-theme-parks-2026) too.

## From travelling showmen to a Merlin park

Heide Park opened on **19 August 1978** with six rides. Its founder, **Hans-Jürgen Tiemann**, came from a family of travelling showmen. The purchase of the land came with the condition that the **Heidenhof chapel from 1350** stay standing and that native animals keep being kept. On the first day 2,000
guests came. Today the park covers 85 hectares.

Three of those six still run today: the raft ride, the elevated railway (now
the Panoramabahn) and the western railway, which has been called Heide Park
Express since 1997. A fourth, the Oldtimer circuit with its replica Ford
Model T cars, ran until 2025, when the park closed it with little announcement.

The other two rides of opening day were the Monza race track, called Monza-Piste from 1997, and the Wichtelhausenbahn by Mack Rides. Since 2018 Opa Pig’s Zugfahrt in Peppa Pig Land, built by Metallbau Emmeln, has run on its track. The Heidenhof chapel is made of oak and was later converted into a two-storey mill granary. It has been a chapel again since 1951 and has been called "Offener Raum der Stille" (open room of silence) since 2007, and the Lutheran church district of Soltau occasionally holds short services there.

The alligator show ran from 1978 to 2003, from 1985 in an arena of its own. A dolphin show followed in 1979 and got a domed roof a year later, and the park closed the dolphinarium after the 2008 season. The sea lions performed until 2007, and the pirate arena stands on their stage today. The Hallo Spencer show ran from 2004 to 2016 and ended because the licence expired.

From
**1986** until the winter of 2011/2012 a 35-metre replica of the Statue of
Liberty stood here, unveiled on 4 July 1986 for the original’s hundredth
birthday, with congratulations from Ronald Reagan. After it came down, the
upper half stood around as decoration at Colossos for a few more years, and the
rest was scrapped for the 2019 reopening.

On **31 December 2001** the British **Tussauds Group** bought the park. In the nineties Soltau counted up to two million
guests, in 2001 it was 2.1 million, in 2023 still **1.68 million**. In 2007 the
investment firm Blackstone, which owned **Merlin Entertainments** at the time,
bought the Tussauds Group from Dubai International Capital and merged it into
Merlin. Since 2019 Merlin has belonged to a consortium of Kirkbi (the holding
company of the family that owns Lego), Blackstone and the Canadian pension fund
CPP Investments. Merlin leases the land from a British property group, now
LondonMetric Property, and the lease was extended by 35 years at the end
of 2021. In September 2026 the Spanish paper Expansión also named Merlin as a
possible buyer of Parques Reunidos, the group behind Movie Park
([more on that](/blog/parques-reunidos-sale-movie-park)).

The Heide-Dorf, with replicas of typical houses from the Lüneburg Heath, stood from 1988 to 2009. The Dutch quarter with its windmill and canals was added in 1996/97, was open only to hotel guests from 2009, and in 2014 its facades were torn down to make room for Sultan’s Spaßbad. Wild Wild West opened in 2008 with the Indy-Blitz, a steam carousel and a western Ferris wheel. In 2011 the park divided the grounds into lands including Land der Vergessenen and Transylvania, and the Bucht der Totenkopfpiraten was finished in 2012. After the "How to Train Your Dragon" island (2016) and Peppa Pig Land (2018), the park merged those two with Wild Wild West into a single land called Exploria in 2019. Peppa Pig Land has also had a balloon ride by Technical Park since 2020.

## Staying at the park

If you’re coming from southern Germany, make two days of it. The
**Abenteuerhotel** has 680 beds and four stars and was converted in 2015 from
the former Hotel Port Royal for 20 million euros, themed as the villa of an
adventurer named Lord Alexander Explorus. Sultan’s Spaßbad, the hotel’s water
park, opened next to it the same year. The **Holiday Camp** from 2005 consists,
after an extension, of 81 wooden houses with 536 beds between them. The seven
converted VW T2 vans of the **Bulli Camp**, which you could sleep in from July
2014, were gone by 2025; the camp now only books huts.

The Abenteuerhotel has 150 family rooms and 16 comfort rooms, fitted out as pirate rooms, captain’s cabins, western or demon rooms, or as a Ghostbusters suite. Five former pirate rooms have been Peppa Pig rooms since 2018. At the Holiday Camp you book huts with their own bathroom for two or four guests, or cheaper huts without one, whose guests use the shared washrooms nearby.

On the long autumn days hotel and
camp guests are let in at 11am, an hour before everyone else, and a few rides
are already running then, Krake among them.

## A day in Soltau, roughly planned

At opening go to **Colossos** first and straight afterwards to **Krake**,
because both start climbing at eleven. **Flug der Dämonen** and
**Desert Race** fit in well after that, and the last hour before closing is the
second-best window of the day on the big rides. Save the water rides for the
warmest hour, even though that’s exactly when the log flume has its longest
queue. On the long autumn days
that plan falls away, because the water rides close at dusk. **Scream** and the
**Big Loop** are the gap fillers between two big rides.

## Practical: getting there, tickets, opening hours

**Getting there.** Heide Park 1, 29614 Soltau, on the **A7** between Hamburg
and Hannover, exit **Soltau-Ost**, signposted from there. The site has around
8,000 parking spaces plus 300 for coaches and motorhomes; parking costs €14
online for a car and €20 for a motorhome. On site, parking tickets are sold only
in exceptional cases, by card only and at a higher price, €16 for a car. By
train you go to Soltau or to Wolterdingen; from Wolterdingen it’s a 20-minute
walk, and a coach runs from Hamburg and Hannover straight to the gate. It’s
called Heide Park Express, like the park railway, and has nothing to do with it. It is run by Elbe-Leine-Express GmbH. In Hannover it leaves from Hamburger Allee 4, opposite the central bus station; a single trip costs €15 and a return €25.

**Opening hours.** 28 March to 31 October 2026, daily in the core of the season
and in October, with some Mondays closed in spring and in September. The times
are for the rides; the park closes an hour later, and the turnstiles open
about half an hour before the first ride.

**Tickets.** From **€37** online. There’s no ticket booth: the ticket machines
at the entrance charge **€64** for the day (€49 for children from 90 cm up to
age 11), and only while that day’s allocation lasts. If you arrive without a
ticket, the park says you can still book in the online shop on site as long as
there’s availability; the €37 only applies on selected days, though. The
undated ticket for the whole season is €52. Children under 90 cm get in free
but need a €0 ticket from the online shop.

**Annual pass.** The Bronze Pass costs €69 new and €59 to renew, and is not valid on 45 days in 2026, including most Saturdays from April to October and 1 to 9 August. The Silver Pass for €89 (€69 to renew) has no blackout days. The Merlin Abenteuer-Pass for €139 (€119 to renew) includes parking and also covers Legoland Deutschland, Peppa Pig Park Günzburg, the German Sea Life centres and Madame Tussauds Berlin, plus up to two consecutive days at Legoland Billund and at Gardaland. Silver and Abenteuer-Pass holders who want to come on 31 October need a free reservation ticket. All three passes are valid for twelve months from purchase.

**Food.** Our park page lists 21 food outlets, from the
Schmalzkuchen-Schmiede doughnut stand on the Colossos terrace to the Wirtshaus
des Admirals, a buffet restaurant in the Bucht der Piraten. There is pizza at Pizza Scream and tarte flambée at the Flammkuchen Factory, both in Transylvania, and Thuringian bratwurst from a lava grill at the Heide Park Grill by the entrance. Frau Mümmels Kiosk in Peppa Pig Land sells bowls, and many of the restaurants have plant-based options.

## Frequently asked questions about Heide Park

### How tall and how fast is Colossos?

According to the Roller Coaster DataBase it’s 50 metres tall, with a first
drop of 48.5 metres at 61 degrees, a top speed of 110 km/h and 1,344 metres of
track; the park itself gives 60 metres and 1,500 metres. When it opened in 2001
it was the steepest wooden coaster in the world according to Guinness, and it’s
still the tallest all-wooden coaster in Europe.

### Why was Colossos closed for years?

The prefabricated wooden track was worn out. The ride closed on 28 July 2016 in
the middle of the season, Merlin decided to refurbish it in January 2018, and
it reopened on 19 April 2019 with new wooden track and new trains. A conversion
to a steel-tracked hybrid, which Walibi Holland carried out on Robin Hood the
same year, never happened in Soltau.

### When is Heide Park open in 2026?

From 28 March to 31 October 2026, plus a preview Sunday on 22 March. Some
Mondays are closed in spring and in September, October is open daily, and
there’s no winter season. The published calendar is on the
[park page](ref:heide-park).

### When is Heide Park least busy?

On a Friday in September, well ahead of a Saturday in August. Weekday and month
each move about the same amount on their own, and together they move more than
either one alone. The quietest of the next operating days are marked on the [park page](ref:heide-park), recalculated daily.

### Which coasters at Heide Park were German firsts?

Four: Limit, now Toxic Garden (1999), was the country’s first suspended looping
coaster, Desert Race (2007) the first launch coaster, Krake (2011) the first
dive coaster and Flug der Dämonen (2014) the first wing coaster.

### How many roller coasters does Heide Park have?

Nine, counting the Grottenblitz and the Indy-Blitz, both built for small
children. The park markets seven headline attractions as the "Big 7": six of
those coasters plus the Scream drop tower. The Bobbahn isn’t one of them.

### How tall does my child have to be?

Colossos, Desert Race, Flug der Dämonen and Krake require 140 cm, Toxic Garden
and Scream 130 cm, Big Loop and Bobbahn 120 cm. At George’s Dino Adventure in
Peppa Pig Land, 86 cm is enough.

### What does admission cost?

From €37 per person online, €64 at the ticket machines on site; there’s no
ticket booth. Parking costs €14 online for a car. Current prices are on
[heide-park.de](https://www.heide-park.de/tickets-paesse/tickets/).

### What does an annual pass for Heide Park cost?

The Bronze Pass costs €69 new but is not valid on 45 days in 2026, including most Saturdays from April to October. The Silver Pass for €89 is valid on every operating day of the season. The Merlin Abenteuer-Pass for €139 includes parking and entry to other Merlin attractions in Germany, such as Legoland Deutschland.

### Is it worth staying overnight at the park?

If the drive takes more than two hours, yes. What you gain is the first hour
after opening, and that counts for a lot here, because the crowd concentrates
around midday. On the long autumn days hotel and camp guests also get in an
hour earlier.
