# Feature rules

These are the standing rules for this area, moved out of `CLAUDE.md` so that a session reads an index first and opens only the pages its task needs. The wording is unchanged; each rule links on to the document that carries the measurements behind it.

## The planner's day ends when the park closes, and a headliner is not a residual (REQUIREMENT)

`PlanDayContext.closeHour` is the hour the closing time FALLS IN, never the last open hour — Phantasialand shuts at 18:00 and the API answers `18`. `buildDayGrid` read it the other way and added sixty minutes, so a nine-hour day was planned against a ten-hour one and the optimiser filled the difference: Winja's Fear queued at **18:15**, day finishing 18:55. Measured over the catalogue's own calendars, **3,046 of 3,540 operating park-days close exactly on the hour (86.0 %)**, 486 at half past, 8 at quarter to — so the hour IS the closing minute six days in seven and the API's truncation is real for the seventh, which is why `DayGrid` carries two numbers: `closeMin` is the certifiable end that nothing the app files by itself may start past, `closeSlackMin` (60) is the hour above it that is **drawn** (the hatched feather sits below the band now, not in its last hour) and reachable by a **drag** — the person dragging knows their park — and never planned into. Same hard/soft split `rideFloor` makes at the other end. And the rule runs on the **start**, never the end: a queue may be JOINED right up to closing (`fits = start < closeMin`), because what happens after that is the park emptying a line it already let you into, and `freeAt <= closeMin` refused a forty-minute queue at 17:45 that is one of the best slots on the day. Two things follow. **A queue nobody joins costs nobody anything** — the wait and the idle in front of a non-fitting stop are out of the totals, and with them IN two plans giving up different rides tied on queued minutes, so the choice fell through to the clock, which prefers the short walk; that is what makes „Wartezeit ist Laufwegen vorzuziehen" true rather than intended (measured: the far ride now wins on a five-minute difference against a kilometre, where before the near one won up to fifteen). And **`nowFloor` is not capped at the end of the day**, because the cap beat the lower bound the function exists to impose: at 17:58 it answered 17:45 and „alle Headliner" filed a queue thirteen minutes before the press. **Which headliner falls out is a decision, not a remainder.** Three overflow tiers counted how many fall out and never which, so among equal counts cost decided — and cost minimisation drops the most expensive ride, i.e. the longest queue, i.e. the one the most people are there for. On 2026-09-12 the button gave up **F.L.Y. and Taron** and kept both Winja's, and gave up **Taron in 9 of the 10** ways of leaving one out, over a five-minute margin (280 vs 275) against a model error of 14.3 for that lead time. A fourth tier (`Candidate.dropWeight`, ranks not minutes) fixes it: `OptimizeInput.priority` — the visitor's own order — beats everything, the day's expected queues rank the rest. The beam cannot decide this on its own (overflow only appears on the LAST stop, so every prefix scores `overflow: 0` and is pruned on cost), so `peeled()` sets the least important aside, re-runs, and scores the result **in the original context** with them appended. Where it is still tight the app **asks** — see the paragraph below. Four more holes the same review found: the overflow branch skipped `clearFixed` (a ride lay across the dinner block, so the button answered „Der Tag ist umgestellt" on **every** press over an unchanged day), the hour was clamped at 23 so a past-midnight park was priced off the 23:00 curve (680 reported against 630 drawn), `compareLabels` pruned the beam on the flat count while `better` ranked by the tiers, and the incumbent covered every movable entry while the search saw only `MAX_STOPS` of them (26 rides reshuffled the day on every press, for ever). `MAX_STOPS` now has **three** things pinned to it, not two — the bitmask, the map key and `OVERFLOW_STRIDE`. See [trip planner](docs/features/trip-planner.md#a-queue-is-joined-before-closing-and-never-after).

## A day that does not fit opens an assistant, not a footnote (REQUIREMENT)

ten headliners and nine hours is a decision the app cannot make, and for a while it made it in a grey eleven-pixel clause („eine passt nicht mehr in den Tag") under a block drawn at **18:45 in a park that shuts at 18:00**. The information was there; there was nothing to answer it with. So both presses probe first (`needsFitHelp`) and open `PlannerFitAssistant` where anything would be left out, writing nothing until the last step. **Every hint is a measured difference between two plans, never advice**: `fitLevers` re-runs the whole optimiser once per free block without it and once with it cut to `SHORT_BLOCK_MIN`, and offers only what buys a ride — Phantasialand 2026-09-12 goes 9/10 → **10/10 without lunch**, Movie Park 2026-09-20 → 10/10 on **30 minutes instead of 60**, and 2026-10-03 says „dann passen 9 von 10" because that is what it is. Shortening is offered before dropping and dropping is not offered at all where half an hour already solved it; a lever is a **toggle**, so pressing it again puts the break back. The list is `fitOrder` and never a sort of its own — what is at the bottom of the screen is what the engine gives up first — with a tick (wanted at all) and a **pin** (`OptimizeInput.priority`) per row, both recomputing against the same search that runs on the press: on 2026-10-03 the default gives up Crazy Bats and River Quest, pinning those two moves it to Winja's Fear and Colorado Adventure. Two rules underneath. **A ticked wish is re-planned rather than parked past the gate** — every ride with a payload row goes to the engine as an ADD, which is what turns "which one falls out" from a remainder into a decision; `optimizeDay` alone may never delete somebody's entry, and this is the one place that is relaxed, in front of a list where the ride is named. And **the visitor's order beats the park's curation**: `isWanted` counts a ride named in `priority` as one somebody is there for, so a pinned filler no longer goes before an unpinned headliner (a no-op for every call site that predates it, since the only thing that ever passed `priority` was a list of headliners). The wizard asks the same question on a **step of its own** (`park → date → setup → headliners`) with the same two pieces inline, and `finish` reads the lunch block back out of `evaluateFit`'s answer rather than off a second piece of state, so the day filed is the day shown. The block on the axis says it too, in **two** shapes because the API reports a closing HOUR: past `closeMin + closeSlackMin` „Liegt nach Parkschluss", inside the hatched hour „Kann schon nach Parkschluss liegen", and nothing at all on a free block — telling somebody their dinner is after closing time is the app having an opinion about dinner. The result line is a bordered warning with an **„Anpassen"** button back into the assistant wherever something was left out. **And solving it may not take the screen away**, which is what the first version did: the wizard's block hung on the conflict alone, so the ride that made the day fit removed the thing used to fit it, and in the dialog the „fällt weg" marks simply went out with nothing saying the problem was over. Both carry a **state** now — the crowd tint with a warning mark while something is left out, `status-operating` with a check and „So passen alle 10 Bahnen in den Tag" once nothing is — and the wizard's block stays for as long as anything has been answered (`fitChoiceTouched`, derived, never latched, so a change of park or date cannot leave it standing). Step one has a third sentence for the same reason: „an den Blöcken liegt es nicht" is true while the day is short and a lie the moment somebody has made it fit. **And solving it may not take the screen away**, which is what the first version did: the wizard's block hung on the conflict alone, so the ride that made the day fit removed the thing used to fit it, and in the dialog the „fällt weg" marks simply went out with nothing saying the problem was over. ## Weather day chart is built around the park's hours (REQUIREMENT)

the hourly chart's time axis is **piecewise linear, not linear** — an open hour is drawn 4× as wide as a closed one, which takes the median park's opening hours from 42 % of the width to 74 % and is what makes room for hour-by-hour ticks and a few temperature readings inside the visit. Both kinks sit **exactly on the dashed borders of the opening-hours band**: a change of slope the eye cannot account for reads as weather, so it has to land on a line that is already there and is labelled with the opening time. Parks with no OPERATING row for today (about a quarter of the catalogue on any given day) keep the exact linear axis they had — `buildDayScale` returns `null` and every formula reduces to the old one, which the unit tests assert. Geometry is pure and lives in `lib/utils/weather-chart-axis.ts` (`pnpm test:weather-chart-axis`), never in the component; it works in a continuous **point index**, never in hours-since-midnight, because a DST day has 23 or 25 points and `points[i]` is then not the `i`-th hour. The chart owes the card **143 px** whatever it draws (`h-28` + `mt-1` + a fixed `h-[27px]` axis), so every tick and label is out of flow and `whitespace-nowrap` — as a flex row of 24 equal cells the German labels wrapped and it came out 154.5 px against a 143 px reservation on every phone. Tick density is a **container query** on the chart, not a `sm:` breakpoint: `/ui` renders this card three to a row, so a 355 px chart at a 1280 px viewport would otherwise get a desktop's worth of labels. See [weather day chart](docs/features/weather-day-chart.md).

## The admin holds no credential (REQUIREMENT)

`/admin` authenticates with an **httpOnly session cookie** — client code sends nothing, `adminFetch` is a plain same-origin fetch, and `app/api/admin/[...path]` turns the cookie into `Authorization: Bearer …` server-side. The shared password in `sessionStorage` it replaced was readable by any script on the origin and unlocked every administrative endpoint on api.park.fan, which until this change had **no guard of its own at all** — the `?pass=` it advertised was checked by a Cloudflare rule and by nothing in the application. Four things in the proxy are load-bearing and were each a bug before: it must export **every verb** (the editing API uses PATCH and DELETE, which 405'd at Next), it must not call `response.json()` unconditionally (a 204 from a logout or a delete threw), it must move a re-issued token from a password change into the cookie — passing it through would leave the browser holding a cookie that was just revoked, i.e. logged out by succeeding — and it must **validate** that cookie against `auth/me` rather than notice one is set, because reading a cookie back only proves the caller can send a header: `Cookie: parkfan_admin_session=x` satisfied the gate written to keep strangers away from `getServerApiHeaders()`'s `x-auth-key`, the header the API reads as "this is our own frontend" and therefore as licence to skip the throttler and to trust the forwarded address. Not `requireAdmin` though: the password change and the TOTP enrolment are behind this proxy and a session owing either must still reach them, so the role floor stays where the API enforces it. The deprecated `pass` query parameter is dropped rather than relayed. The login form carries the **same Turnstile challenge `/contribute` uses** (widget now `components/common/turnstile-widget.tsx`, verify `lib/security/turnstile.ts`), checked in `/api/admin/session` **before** the credentials are forwarded — everything behind it only counts attempts that were already made, and the per-account lockout is a weapon pointed the wrong way, since anybody who knows an editor's address can spend their five tries and keep them out. A token is **single-use**, so a two-factor login solves twice and the form resets its widget in the `finally` of every attempt; a missing secret in production is a hard failure, so an unset `TURNSTILE_SECRET_KEY` locks the admin out rather than leaving it open. **`success: true` is not the answer to „may this proceed"**: Cloudflare confirms a token is genuine and says nothing about which form it was solved on, so the **action** (`TURNSTILE_ACTIONS`, its own `server-only`-free file so widget and check share it across the client boundary) and the **hostname** (`TURNSTILE_HOSTNAMES`) are checked too — `/contribute` shares this widget and is a challenge anybody may solve as often as they like, so without the action check the upload form is a token vending machine for the admin login. The two unconfigured cases are asymmetric on purpose: a missing secret refuses in production, a missing hostname allowlist **skips** its check, because an unset variable must not be able to take both forms down on the deploy that shipped it (`pnpm test:turnstile`). **This side is not the only side:** `/v1/admin/auth/login` is public, so a bot skipping this app never meets the challenge — the API asks for a solved token from every caller that does _not_ send a valid `THROTTLE_BYPASS_KEYS` value, and this app is exempt because it already redeemed the token (single-use), which is also why the API cannot be the only verifier without a lockstep deploy. The second-factor step **looks like six boxes and is one input** — the boxes are drawn and a transparent field lies over the row. The six real inputs it replaced could not be autofilled and it was one line: `value.replace(/\D/g,'').slice(-1)` per box is right for a person typing one digit and wrong for 1Password, which writes all six characters into the first box in a single event — the slice kept the last of them, so a fill produced a lone `6` and five empty boxes, with no error anywhere. `autocomplete="one-time-code"` belongs on one field, a `readOnly` username rides along hidden on that step (the e-mail input is gone from the DOM by then, and a manager with nothing to match on offers codes from every item that has one), and a complete code submits itself, because six pasted digits behind a button save nobody the typing. The curated-field editor is **generated from backend descriptors**, so a new curated column appears with no frontend change; two of its controls exist for a reason — a curated height of `0` means "no minimum at all" and empty means "accept upstream", and a curated boolean has three states because a curated `false` is a real correction. Media and blog stay a separate write model (files → pull request), which is why the entity media panel is a view with links rather than an editor. See [admin](docs/features/admin.md).

## The header menu is three kinds of content, and the split is about the link graph (REQUIREMENT)

everything the full-width band renders is a link on ~35,000 pages, so the parks panel server-renders **continents and countries only** — 28 hub links, 420 B brotli, all 23 countries visible at once now that the band is container-wide (the first version swapped country lists with four of five on `display:none`; going full width deleted that state). The 144 cities and 212 parks under them are fetched per opened country from `/api/nav/geo/[continent]/[country]`: they are already reachable from those hubs and from the sitemap, so putting them in the template would spread the same weight over 356 more targets and buy no discovery. The **photo rail is a fixed six**, not a thumbnail per park — the media database holds a picture for **14 of 212 parks** — and it reuses the homepage's per-locale `FEATURED_PARK_SLUGS` intersected with the parks that have one, resolved in the layout because `@/lib/media` is the 107 KB catalog and the header is a Client Component. **Hover has to be rested on, not crossed**: the detail row sits under the country columns, so the way to it leads over the countries below — entering a row only arms the switch and leaving before 140 ms disarms it (focus commits at once). That same gesture used to break the fetch, and it was one bug: the effect discarded its response on cleanup while leaving the key in `requested`, so skimming past a country threw its answer away and the guard then refused to ask again — **23 of 23 countries stayed empty, 0 of 23 after**. A response keyed by country is a cache write and is correct whenever it lands, so nothing cancels it; a failed request drops its key so the next hover retries. Same rule shaped the blog panel: 3 categories and the 6 newest posts with their covers in, **31 tags out** — 31 tag pages over 7 posts are mostly one post's teaser at a second URL. The band is **glass, not opaque** (`bg-popover/95` + `backdrop-blur-xl` + the popover ring): flat means no pointer-depth tilt, not no blur, and `/80` let a park page's headline and body text read straight through the menu. That glass is also why the opening animation (`lib/hooks/use-menu-reveal.ts`) only ever targets **descendants** of the surface: a transform or an opacity on it, or on any ancestor, makes it a backdrop root and the blur goes flat exactly while somebody watches it appear. Same split as the header's own reveal — CSS owns visibility (a `hidden` class), GSAP animates `y` and never `opacity`, and under `prefers-reduced-motion` the chunk is never even imported. It is positioned against the `<header>`, not the trigger — centred on an entry 276 px from the left edge a 700 px panel starts at −36 px — and carries `overflow-hidden`, because the rows' `-mx-2` bleed gave the document a horizontal scrollbar at exactly 1024 px. Watch two traps: a single `useTranslations('blog')` in a header component pulls the whole namespace into the layout chrome that **every** page serializes (6066 B → 9047 B × 6 locales, for one label — the word lives in `navigation` now), and the bar has **one** breakpoint, not two: the nav used to start at `md` while the search input waits for `lg`, so 768–1023 px carried the full nav plus a 256 px search button plus no burger, 789 px of content in a 736 px box, wrapping German onto two lines with a horizontal scrollbar. Measured cost of the whole menu: **+5.65 KB brotli per page** (~4.2 of it the 23 flags) for 4 → 48 crawlable nav links, all resolving 200, CLS unchanged, and zero photo requests until somebody opens it. **Six equal entries did not fit that bar**, and the measurement is the French one: at a 1024 px container the row ran 23.7 px over its box and took the document to 1032 px, i.e. a horizontal scrollbar on every page, with 0.0 px left at 1280. „Beste Reisezeit", „Wörterbuch" and „So funktioniert's" sit behind a third trigger — **„Mehr"**, a catch-all with no page of its own, so `NavMenu.href` is optional and its label is the button; the band is `hidden` rather than unmounted, so the destinations stay in the HTML of every page exactly as the entries did (verified: 24 of 24 across six locales). **The blog is not one of them any more.** It went in with the other three and came back out: it is the site's strongest entry point, and behind a catch-all it was visible only to somebody who opened one. It is a fourth bar entry labelled **„Backstage"** — one word, unchanged in all six locales — with `href="/blog"` and the panel it always had, and `navigation.blog` is what all three of its readers print: the bar entry, the phone sheet's link and the `SiteNavigationElement` the locale layout emits for `/blog`. The footer keeps its own `footer.blog`, and the breadcrumb on the index page keeps `blog.blog`, because a breadcrumb names the page and not the menu entry. The row reads **+245.0 px** of slack in French at a 1024 px container with four entries plus favorites (346.8 with three; the entry costs 101.8 px, and the 23.7 px of overflow that started all this came from **six**). Its three sections are a flat `grid-cols-3` with no threshold under it — the 256 px rail they used to form beside the blog block went out with the block, because `w-64` and `border-r` describe a relationship to a neighbour and there is none left. The blog panel's own `/blog` link is its **one** heading now, a `MenuSectionHeading` over both columns: it carried two, and the second of them repeated the word that labels the bar entry the panel hangs from. Every row of that post list prints its category, in the opener's accent rather than the muted tone `BlogPostRow` uses elsewhere, so the two halves of one band agree — which needed `getBlogMenu` to stop resolving `category` under an `index === 0` (+517 B raw, +36 B gzip-9 on a country listing page). **A panel's content column is the BAR's column, and a panel is a page rather than a row in the bar:** both used to be Tailwind's `container mx-auto … md:px-0`, and when the bar moved to container-query tiers against the `<header>` (the planner insets it without the window moving) with `px-4` as a floor, `MenuBand` kept the old class — which reads its max-width off the **window** — so the band's contents sat 16 px left of the nav row at every viewport and 96 px left of it with the planner open at 1920. The same `<nav>` is `whitespace-nowrap`, because a menu word may never wrap in a 48 px bar, and `white-space` **inherits**: labels and `truncate`d card titles survive that, prose does not, so the blog teasers ran off the band on one line each and the favorites empty state — the state nearly every visitor sees — pushed its three steps past the right edge into the `overflow-hidden`. The surface resets `whitespace-normal`. **And every card in the favorites band is the same width**, which it was not: each group got `flexGrow: <its card count>` and filled it with `repeat(auto-fill, minmax(10.5rem, 1fr))`, so the width followed the count, the column count followed the width, and the quantization between them broke the proportion the whole thing rests on — three parks beside five rides got 336 px, twelve short of two 168 px columns, so the parks group drew ONE column of 336 px cards against the rides' 179 and stacked all three, for a band 868 px tall (taller than the window it hangs in) on eight favorites. `planBand` measures the band, derives one card width from it (≥168, ≤248 — past that a card in a menu is a billboard), and cuts each group a whole number of those tracks, greedy on `count / (has + 1)`; what will not fit in `MAX_CARD_ROWS` rows goes behind the "+N" line that already existed, so the height follows the row count and not how much somebody has starred. Measured at 1440 with 3 parks + 5 rides + 3 shows: **868 → 610 px, every card 188 px**. That arithmetic lives in `lib/utils/favorites-band-plan.ts` rather than in the component, like `weather-chart-axis` and for the same reason — it broke once in a way a green build showed nothing of — and `pnpm test:favorites-band` pins both halves: one card width across every group at every band width, and every group combination that predates the alerts group planning byte-for-byte as it did. **The band's fourth group is the browser's own alerts**, with a remove button per row, because the link to `/alerts` it replaced meant leaving the page to find out what was armed. Its gate is `countPushFollowsLocal()` — a `localStorage` read, so a visitor who never pressed a bell loads neither the chunk nor a request — and the LIST is the server's, since the mirror is a cache for bells and not a truth for a surface somebody deletes from; a cleared mirror over a surviving push subscription is the one gap, which is why the link stays unconditional beside the panel's title. `next/dynamic` behind that gate, because `lib/push/push-follows` reaches the service worker and the VAPID key and none of that belongs in the chunk the header ships on ~35,000 pages. The 300 px sheet keeps the link and no list. And **a row leaves only where the server let it go**: `removeRideAlert`/`unfollowShow` wrote the local mirror first, sent the DELETE second and never read `response.ok`, so a 500 took the row off the screen over an alert that was still armed — and since this group is gated on that mirror, removing the last one unmounted the whole group in the same commit as the click, taking its own spinner and any error with it. Both return a `PushWriteResult` now, with the write path's own error classes, and the order is inverted: API first, mirror only on success, query cache after that and only on success (`usePushFollowRemoval`, shared with `AlertsOverview` so one rule decides for both surfaces). A **404 is a success** — a row the server does not have is what the visitor asked for, and an error over it would stand for ever since every retry answers 404 too; the write path reads that same status the other way round (`setRideAlert` re-syncs the subscription and retries), and the asymmetry is the point. The sentence for a refused removal comes from `pushAlerts.menu` in the band and from `usePushErrorMessage` on `/alerts`, because pulling `pushAlerts.pushErrors` into the chrome would put it on ~35,000 pages for one line. `pnpm test:push-follow-delete` pins the order and the classes. **The read path had the same blindness one level down**: `fetchRideAlertsRemote`/`fetchShowFollowsRemote` asked `getExistingPushIdentity()`, which answers `null` for a `getRegistration()` that THREW as well, so a refused storage access arrived as `{ ok: true, items: [] }` — „Noch nichts eingerichtet" in front of five armed alerts, and the band's alerts group gone. Both read `lookupExistingPushIdentity()` now and pass a failed lookup on as `{ ok: false }`, which `PushListResult` exists for and both surfaces already had a branch for; `getExistingPushIdentity()` is unchanged and keeps exactly one caller, `identityForWrite`, where the three cases may collapse because a `null` is followed by `ensurePushRegistered()` anyway (`pnpm test:push-follow-read`). ## The guide page teaches the real cards with the ride's real numbers (REQUIREMENT)

`/{locale}/{howto-segment}` (`app/[locale]/how-park-fan-works/`, localized slug in `lib/howto/segments.ts`, the old `/howto` 301s per locale, bare `/howto` deliberately left to the middleware so it lands in the reader's language) answers „was macht diese Seite, was ein Wartezeiten-Feed nicht macht“, so every block renders a **production** component — `AttractionCard`, `AttractionTypicalWaits`, `RopeDropCard`, `ParkCalendarDay`, `NoLiveWaitTimesNotice` — and every figure in it is a value the API actually returned, dated in `_fixtures.ts`. A redrawn lookalike defeats the exercise and starts lying at the first restyle; an invented number is worse, because the block next to it links to the ride's own page. **Audit before you claim**: `AttractionTypicalWaits` prints the weekday's **P90** above each bar and no measured-day count (its hint names the _window_, not the sample size), and **no surface anywhere puts sample days next to a ride** — `ParkStatsAttractionsCard` renders rank/ride/(now)/P50/P90, and the count exists only in the stats section's subtitle, the `Tage` column of the by-month and by-weekday cards, and the blog's `ride-waits-widget`; four sentences claiming a per-ride column on the park page shipped in German and were translated into five more languages before anyone read them; `typicalWaitThisHour`/`currentVsTypical` exist on the payload and are rendered **nowhere**; the calendar reaches a year out only for year-round parks, while a seasonal one returns CLOSED past its published season; and „früh kommen“ is a per-ride finding, not a rule — Taron's day is flat (60/60/54/53/59 by hour) where Chiapas climbs 22 minutes. The thresholds are visible in one park: Colorado Adventure saves 40 off a 50-minute peak and gets no rope-drop tip against the ≥60/≥45 gate. **A displayed wait time is always a multiple of five** — parks post them that way, so every raw observation is, and what breaks it is the maths on top: `PERCENTILE_CONT` interpolates between two stored values and `AVG()` across days blurs the rest, which is how the hourly table printed 51, 53 and 47. The API rounds on the way out and `roundWaitTo5` (`lib/utils/wait-time.ts`) does it again at render, so a surface is right whichever build answers. **Round only what is displayed**: a crowd ratio, a ranking, a colour tier and the pick of a ride's peak hour all read the raw value, or five-minute buckets invent ties the data does not have — rounding first moved Wodan's peak from 12:00 to 11:00 and broke a test. And a **difference** is not a wait time: `roundWaitTo5` floors everything under 2.5 to zero because no queue is −15 minutes long, so pointing it at the attraction card's trend delta deleted the whole falling half of the scale and every shrinking queue on every park page rendered „stabil" — visible on the guide itself, where Black Mamba sat at a grey „Stabil" under a caption saying its queue was being worked off. Signed deltas go through `roundWaitDeltaTo5`. Two live blocks are exceptions to the frozen-fixture rule (`LiveHourlyProfile`, `LiveTopAttractions`): where the point is „das läuft gerade", a frozen copy is the wrong exhibit, and the prose beside them names no figure they render. Two fixture anchors: the queue history, best-visit slot and rope-drop instants come from `getServerNowMs()` because a card's sparkline stretches its axis to the reader's clock and a hard-coded date decays into a flat week-long tail, while the rope-drop instants are wall-clock times in the **park's** zone. Motion follows `use-menu-reveal`'s split plus **no ScrollTrigger**: `WaitScaleBar` is a server-rendered `<figure>` whose geometry is three CSS custom properties, an `IntersectionObserver` picks the step and GSAP only tweens between two readings. Two traps the visuals cost: `Sparkline` fits each instance to its own max, so two charts meant to be compared need the shared `yMax` prop or the flat one is drawn as dramatically as the steep one; and the wide decorative layers (the wait-time display's glow, `Ambience`) must be clipped with **`overflow-x-clip`**, never `overflow-hidden`, which would make the element a scroll container and stop the sticky figure pinning. Structured data is Article + Breadcrumb + `FaqList`'s `FAQPage`, never `HowTo` — but the two are dead for **different** reasons and only one of them is a reason to remove anything. `HowTo` was deprecated outright (Sept 2023). `FAQPage` still validates and still describes the page truthfully; what ended is the SERP feature, and not only for the health and government sites the Aug 2023 restriction named: **Google retired FAQ rich results for every site on 2026-05-07**. So the markup stays where it is (it costs nothing, and it is an honest description of a block that really is a list of questions), and no new page gets `FAQPage` _in order to_ win a rich result, because there is none to win. Do not claim an AI-citation benefit for it either — nobody has measured one. Genuine user-submitted Q&A is `QAPage`, which is a different type for a different thing. All six locales ship the guide as a full `content/<locale>.tsx` with localized chapter ids, the same per-locale-module convention as `fancast` — so an edit lands six times, and two greps guard it: `CHAPTERS` must match the rendered `<SectionShell id= index=>` calls exactly, and each file's `locale=` prop on the two live blocks must be its own. See [the guide page](docs/features/how-park-fan-works.md).

## Ride ↔ Glossary link

rides carry a curated `rideProfile` from the API (track figures **in ride order**, ride type, builder) stored as **glossary term ids**, so the ride page links into the glossary (`RideProfileSection`) and each term page lists the rides that feature it (`GlossaryTermRides`). Element order is meaningful and repeats are intentional — never dedupe or sort it. **This app is the only place a term id is defined** — the API's mirrored allowlist is gone, so nothing validates them and a renamed term silently shortens every ride layout that used it; check the API's `attraction_ride_profiles` rows before renaming or removing one. See [glossary](docs/features/glossary.md#ride--glossary-link).

## Blog manifest is split (REQUIREMENT)

the generator writes `manifest.ts` (frontmatter + build-time derivations, small) and `manifest-bodies.ts` (~900 KB of markdown). Galleries are no longer its job — they come from the media database. Import listings from **`@/lib/blog/listing`** — `@/lib/blog` drags every post body into that route's bundle, and it is imported by the root layout, the homepage and every park page. Body-derived values (reading time, `parkRefs`) are computed at build time by the shared `lib/blog/derive.mjs`, never a second copy of those regexes. See [scripts](docs/development/scripts.md#the-blog-manifest-is-three-files-on-purpose).
