## New

- Parky, a round face with a Ferris wheel for a hat, sits in the bottom right corner and opens a
  chat window, a sheet from below on a phone. He answers questions about parks, rides, wait times,
  forecasts, the weather and our posts in whichever of the six site languages you write in, and if
  you ask, he switches the page to it with the chat still open. Every wait in an answer is
  drawn in the site's wait chip, a list of parks comes as cards with photo, status, crowd level and
  average wait, and every ride in a list links to its page.
- Parky can also act on the page. He takes you to a park, a ride or a post, finds the parks near
  you after you press "Share location", gives directions, and plans a day by adding rides to the
  day planner and sorting them with its optimiser. Tell him about a bug, a wrong wait or a park we
  are missing, and he passes your words on to us. Each answer can be rated with a thumb up or
  down, and the "Feedback" tab in the same window sends us a note about the site.
- `/parky` introduces him in six languages: screenshots of the chat on a phone, questions to try
  about wait times, a planned day and search, and a small stage where he performs five of his acts
  and keeps the rest a secret.
- Now and then Parky rides a loop, a launch, airtime hills or a drop tower and comes back dizzy.
  He reads a book while he looks something up, holds a magnifying glass to one eye while you search
  and dozes off after 75 seconds without input. Saving a favourite gives him heart eyes, dark mode
  puts a nightcap on him, and a hard flick of the page leaves him hanging on at the top until it
  stops.
- On 4 of 10 visits to a park page Parky says which ride he heads for first: his favourite in 16
  parks, and the coaster with the longest waits everywhere else. The Konami code, typed anywhere,
  sends him on a ride.
- The homepage shows the park photo across the full width with the question "Where is the wait
  worth it today?" on it. Cards over its lower edge list the rides near you whose queue is well
  below usual right now, each with its photo, the current wait as the largest number, how many
  minutes shorter it is and a bar comparing now with usual. Without such rides they show the parks
  near you, inside a park that park's rides, and without a location the most popular parks. Five
  cards fit from 1024 px, three from 640 px, and a phone swipes through a row.
- On a desktop from 1024 px, Parky stands in the homepage photo under the question, next to a
  speech bubble that opens the chat. Scroll on and he hops into his corner; scroll back and he hops
  up again.
- Under the photo, a board lists the six newest news items and the four guides read most over the
  last 30 days. On a phone it shows three of each and is 837 px tall instead of 1,427. A tour of
  three phone screenshots follows: live waits on a park page, the crowd calendar and the forecast of
  a ride. The homepage had 21 sections before and has seven now.
- Your favourites now sit on the homepage right under the news. Without any, two phone screenshots
  show how to save a park or a ride and how to reach the list from the menu on every page.
- Search opens with the `/` key on every page, as long as no field has the focus; inside a field
  `/` stays a slash. Every search field the key reaches shows a `/` on its right. Ctrl K and ⌘K are
  left to the browser's own search, and on the homepage `/` puts the cursor in the search field
  over the photo while that is on screen.
- Every language has its own five most read guides of the last 30 days and five most read news
  items of the last 7 days. They stand in the guides and news menus on screens from 1280 px and
  under every post, and the clear leader of each list carries a "Most read" badge.
- `/parks/six-flags` and `/parks/universal` list the parks that carry the two names, by country,
  each with whether it is open today and how busy it is. At the end of October that was 13 Six Flags
  parks and 11 Hurricane Harbor water parks, and eight Universal parks from Hollywood and Orlando
  to Osaka, Singapore and Beijing. Each park page of the two brands links to its list.
- A ride the park reports as under maintenance says "Under maintenance since …" and how much
  longer maintenance like this usually took, the way an outage does.

## Improved

- The parks and rides named in a guide or a news item appear as live cards right after the share
  line at its end, with a search field under them. They used to sit at the foot of the read-more
  box with a stored status.
- The popular parks in the footer, on the homepage and in the parks menu follow what is read in
  your language over the last 30 days. The footer keeps its columns for Germany, the USA, France
  and Japan.
- From 360 to 1023 px the header shows search as a field between the logo and the buttons instead
  of an icon, 121 px wide on a 360 px phone.
- In the list under the homepage search, a closed park's status pill says when it opens again,
  in the park's own time zone. On a desktop it adds how long that is: "in 1:30 hours", "in 45
  minutes", "tomorrow", the weekday within the coming week and "in 9 days" after that.
- The ride list of a closed park no longer marks every ride "Closed" on a phone. At night
  Europa-Park showed 97 red badges in a row; the status card above says it once.
- The links to a park's website, tickets, social media and map moved to the foot of the info card
  on a phone. At Europa-Park that lifts "In the park today" from 650 px to 543 px, onto the
  first screen.
- On a phone, a ride page shows the latest 8 days of its history and keeps the other 23 behind a
  button, where all 31 used to take about eight screens. Its planner button is an icon there, so a
  long ride title breaks onto two lines instead of three.
- A park page names the abbreviations people use for the park, such as "Europa-Park (EP)" in its
  heading.
- The statistics and crowd calendar pages of a park link to its live wait times under their
  introduction, for example "Europa-Park: live wait times".
- Dutch park pages have "Drukte" (crowds) in their title next to "Wachttijden LIVE".
- Pages no longer load the Umami script. Cloudflare's Network Error Logging is switched off, so
  browsers stop reporting failed requests with their address and referrer to Cloudflare.
- The privacy policy describes the server logs in more detail and has a section on Parky: your
  location only after you allow it, rounded to about a kilometre and not stored; questions passed
  to Anthropic's Claude without your IP address; chats stored without it and deleted after 90
  days.

## Fixed

- The weekday strip on a park page glued the short and the long day name together in every text
  copy of the page, so the German one read "MoMontag: ruhig". It reads "Montag: ruhig" now, and
  nothing changes on screen.
- Text in the site's pills sat too high, with 13 pixels of space above the capitals and 16 below
  at double resolution. It is 14 and 14 now, and an icon sits as far from the left edge as from
  the top.
- Links inside guides and news pointed at addresses without a language, 32 per language, and
  each one took a redirect; two German ones ended on English pages. They now lead straight to the
  page in the post's language.
- Text typed into the homepage search before the page had finished loading was lost. On a desktop
  the open list now takes it over with the cursor, and on a phone the search opens with it.
- An old address of a ride that was merged, corrected or moved to a sister park answered "not
  found". It now forwards to the ride's page, also when that ride has closed for good.
- The British flag is no longer clipped by the US flag on the same page, and the US flag keeps
  its stars inside the blue field.
