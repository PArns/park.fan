Parc Astérix opened to everyone on Sunday 30 April 1989, and by the afternoon
people were already being sent home. Le Parisien reports that 12,000 visitors
were inside when the ticket desks stopped letting anyone in; French Wikipedia
puts the whole day at 20,000. The park had been built for 15,000 a day. Over
its first year it turned around 150,000 people away at the gate because it was
full. It had expected two million visitors and got 1.34 million.

Today it covers 37 hectares in Plailly, in the Oise département, 35 kilometres
north of Porte de la Chapelle by the park's own count, with its own exit off
the A1. The 2024/25 season brought in 2.9 million visitors. Count the parks one
by one and it's third in France, behind
[Disneyland Park](ref:/parks/europe/france/paris/disneyland-park) and Disney
Adventure World; the park calls itself second, because it counts Disneyland
Paris as one destination. Its coasters include Goudurix, which was already
running on opening day, and [Toutatis](ref:parc-asterix/toutatis), the tallest
coaster in France.

What follows comes from our own wait-time measurements since Christmas 2025,
from the park's website as it stood on 2 October 2026, and from the articles and
databases linked at the end. Where two of them disagree, you get both numbers.

## How a comic book became a theme park

The idea came from Albert Uderzo, who drew Astérix, after a visit to Disneyland
in California in 1981. René Goscinny, who wrote the stories, had died four years
earlier; Le Parisien says the two of them had often talked about a park like
that. Parc Astérix SA was founded in 1985 with 250 million francs of capital.
Barclays brought in around twenty investors, among them Accor, the Compagnie
générale des eaux, the insurers GMF and UAP, and the Picardie region.
Construction started in the summer of 1987 and cost 850 million francs.

On 20 February 1989 the Astérix statue went up on its rock: seven metres tall,
four tonnes, thirty metres above the ground. Culture minister Jack Lang
inaugurated the park, on 27 April according to French Wikipedia, on 30 April
according to Le Parisien and English Wikipedia, which was the day the public
was let in.

In 1990 the park had 1.45 million visitors. Then Euro Disney opened in
Marne-la-Vallée on 12 April 1992, and Parc Astérix dropped to 990,000, 30 per
cent down on the year before, according to French Wikipedia. Revenue fell 19
per cent to 170 million francs. Olivier de Bosredon, then head of operations,
cut staff and budgets, and the park pitched itself against the
American giant as the "exception culturelle française". In 1994 it made a profit
for the first time, 15 million francs, with a new Greek quarter.

In 1997 the park added a wooden coaster, Tonnerre de Zeus, for 7 million
euros, and attendance climbed to 1.9 million. That year the park capped
admissions at 20,000 a day. In 1999 it merged with the Musée Grévin and France
Miniature to form Grévin & Cie, and the first hotel opened. In June 2002 the
Compagnie des Alpes took over the group for around 140 million euros. The
company, which also owns [Walibi Belgium](ref:walibi-belgium) and
[Walibi Holland](ref:walibi-holland), still runs the park; Delphine Pons has
been its director since May 2021.

It's kept growing since. In 2012 came the Égypte area and OzIris, 20 million
euros for the pair and the biggest investment in the park's history at that
point. Pégase Express followed in 2017 for 16 million, Toutatis in 2023.
According to the Themed Entertainment Association (TEA) index and the park's own figures, attendance was 2,326,000 in 2019, 2,632,000 in 2022, 2,815,000 in 2023,
2,842,000 in 2024, and 2.9 million in the 2024/25 season. On 20 October 2025 the
park announced 250 million euros of investment by 2030, for 20 per cent more
capacity.

On 2 December 2025 the group announced the first Parc Astérix outside France.
The Compagnie des Alpes bought Belantis near Leipzig from Parques Reunidos in
April 2025 and plans to turn it into a Parc Astérix by 2030 or 2031. Chief
executive François Fassier told franceinfo it was "a very complex and very
ambitious project", meant only for the German market. How the sale came about
is in our [Parques Reunidos story](/blog/parques-reunidos-sale-movie-park).

## Five themed areas and the old Paris

Officially the park has five themed areas. The entrance leads onto the Via
Antiqua, a shopping street that stopped counting as an area of its own in 2012.
From there:

- La Gaule, Astérix and Obélix's village, with Cétautomatix, Menhir Express,
  La Revanche des Pirates, the Épidemaïs Croisières boat ride and the 4D
  cinema. It also takes in Festival Toutatis, 2.8 hectares opened in 2023 with
  Toutatis and La Trace du Hourra, and the Forêt d'Idéfix from 2014, with rides
  for small children.
- L'Empire romain, with the arena, the Romus et Rapidus rapids and Les Espions
  de César.
- La Grèce, with Tonnerre 2 Zeus, Pégase Express, Discobélix, Le Vol d'Icare and
  the Théâtre de Poséidon.
- Les Vikings around the Grand Lac, with Goudurix.
- L'Égypte, since 2012, with OzIris, SOS Tournevis, the Tour de Numérobis and,
  since 2026, La Descente du Nil.

The sixth area, À travers le temps, was Paris from the Middle Ages to the Belle
Époque, with the Rue de Paris as a covered street. The park closed it in November
2025, apart from the stunt show Main basse sur la Joconde, and is demolishing
it. Londinium is due to replace it in 2028.

## The coasters

RCDB counts nine coasters at Parc Astérix. The height limits below are the
park's: the first figure applies when an adult rides along, the second to
children riding alone. Where there's only one, there's no lower limit with an
adult.

### Toutatis

**[Toutatis](ref:parc-asterix/toutatis)** opened on 8 April 2023. Intamin built
the launch coaster. There's no chain lift, and linear motors push the train,
several times and backwards too. Along the way it goes over a 101-degree drop,
steeper than vertical.

```glossary-widget slug=launch-coaster

```

The sources don't quite agree on the numbers. RCDB gives 51 metres and 107
km/h, Intamin 53 metres and also 107 km/h, the park 51 metres and 110 km/h,
and with those 110 km/h it calls Toutatis "the fastest attraction in France". The
number of launches depends on how you count: seven according to Intamin, five
forwards and two backwards, four according to Wikipedia, three of them on the
same launch track. RCDB gives 1,075 metres of track and, since the train
covers some of it twice, 1,361 metres travelled, which is where the park's "more
than 1,300 metres" comes from. Three inversions, just over two
minutes. The park also claims 23 moments of airtime, which it calls a world
record for a steel coaster. Height limit 130 cm.

Toutatis was announced in November 2018 at the IAAPA trade show in Orlando,
for 2021. The park pushed it back to 2022 because demolition permits were
missing, then to 2023 because of Covid. The ride alone cost 28 million euros
according to Le Parisien; the whole Festival Toutatis area cost 36 million
according to the park.

You don't need to run here at opening. The queue is already as long at ten as
it is at midday, and by our analysis it's shortest about three and a half hours
after opening. Hotel guests get into the Toutatis and Égypte areas half an hour
before everyone else; whether that's the whole explanation, we can't tell
from our data. If you can ride alone, use the single rider line, which only exists here
and at OzIris.

### OzIris

**[OzIris](ref:parc-asterix/oziris)** opened on 7 April 2012 along with the
Égypte area. The park spent 20 million euros on the two, half of it on theming
according to NewsParcs. Bolliger & Mabillard built the inverted coaster. The
train hangs below the track and your legs dangle. A 40-metre lift, 1,000 metres
of track, 90 km/h, five inversions. Which five is disputed. RCDB lists a dive
loop, a vertical loop, an Immelmann and two zero-g rolls; Wikipedia has a
corkscrew instead of the first zero-g roll. Wikipedia also says the train dives
into a tunnel under a pool. Three trains of 32, 1,600 riders an hour, height
limit 130 cm.

```glossary-widget slug=inverted-coaster

```

OzIris is short in the first half hour after opening and again from late
afternoon. In between, it's about as long as the other big rides.

### Tonnerre 2 Zeus

**[Tonnerre 2 Zeus](ref:parc-asterix/tonnerre-2-zeus)** opened on 5 April 1997
as Tonnerre de Zeus, a wooden coaster by Custom Coasters International. French
Wikipedia says it won an Ultimate European Theme Parks Award four years running,
2002 to 2005. Over the winters of 2019 to 2021 The Gravity Group rebuilt it.
English Wikipedia says 85 per cent of the track and structure was replaced; the
helix went, and small hills and a hill banked at 90 degrees were added. The new
Gravitykraft trains have two backward-facing seats in the last row. It reopened
as Tonnerre 2 Zeus on 9 April 2022.

```glossary-widget slug=wooden-coaster

```

Over the winter of 2025/26 Rocky Mountain Construction replaced about 350
metres of the wooden rail with its steel track, according to the park; RCDB says
it'll be 530 metres by 2027, around 60 per cent of the layout according to
Dimension Parcs. Height and speed vary by source between 30 and 33 metres and
84 and 92 km/h. Children can ride alone from 120 cm; at Toutatis and OzIris it's 130.

Tonnerre 2 Zeus is the one ride where, by our analysis, arriving at opening
pays off. The queue is short for the first half hour, its worst hour is eleven
o'clock, and the gap between the two is wider than at any other coaster in the
park.

### Goudurix

**[Goudurix](ref:parc-asterix/goudurix)** has been running since opening day,
over the Grand Lac in the Viking area. Vekoma built it to a design by Peter
Clerx: 36 metres tall, 950 metres long according to RCDB (the park says 970),
90 km/h and seven inversions. That made it the second coaster in the world with
seven, and it held the European record until Dragon Khan opened at PortAventura
in 1995 with eight. French Wikipedia adds that it was the first coaster with a
butterfly. 1,100 riders an hour. At 140 cm it has the highest height limit in
the park, and its queue is the shortest of the big rides.

### Pégase Express

**[Pégase Express](ref:parc-asterix/pegase-express)** (Gerstlauer, 11 June
2017, 16 million euros) runs through La Grèce. Tyres push the train out of the
station, and a chain lift takes it up to 21 metres. Halfway round it stops in a
dark show scene and carries on backwards from there, for about a third of the
ride. 52 km/h, three minutes, height limit 100 and 130 cm. For a family coaster
the wait is long, and it's only short in the first quarter of an hour after
opening.

### Cétautomatix

**[Cétautomatix](ref:parc-asterix/cetautomatix)** is the newest coaster, a
Gerstlauer spinning coaster in La Gaule, on the site of the old Nationale 7
vintage-car ride. In the comics Cétautomatix (Fulliautomatix in English) is the
village blacksmith; here he's opened a new and used chariot dealership, and the
cars spin freely. It was due to open in mid-May 2025. On 15 May the park said
parts had been made and delivered late, and the ride finally opened on 18
August 2025. 14 metres, 420 metres of track, 45 km/h, a tyre-driven boost
halfway and a pass through the station. The park hasn't said what it cost.
Height limit 100 and 130 cm.

### La Trace du Hourra

**[La Trace du Hourra](ref:parc-asterix/la-trace-du-hourra)** (Mack Rides, 31
March 2001, 70 million francs) is a bobsled coaster. The cars run in an open
trough instead of on rails and find their own line through the bends. 900
metres, close to 60 km/h. The park calls it one of the tallest bobsleds in the
world and the second-longest in Europe. Height limit 120 and 130 cm. The queue
is short in the first half hour and in the last hour of the day.

### The small ones

[Le Vol d'Icare](ref:parc-asterix/le-vol-dicare), a Zierer coaster from 1994 in
La Grèce, is a little over ten metres tall and reaches 42 km/h, height limit 100
and 130 cm. [SOS Tournevis](ref:parc-asterix/sos-tournevis), also by Zierer, has
been in the park since 1990 and was called SOS Numérobis until 2024. With an
adult, children can ride from 90 cm. RCDB doesn't count
[Discobélix](ref:parc-asterix/discobelix) (Zamperla, 2016) as a coaster. It's a
Disk'O, a round platform for 40 people that spins while it swings back and
forth along a curved track. Height limit 120 cm.

## Water rides, a tower and the shows

The water rides close one after the other in autumn. Romus et Rapidus shuts on
11 October 2026 according to the park, La Revanche des Pirates on 1 November,
and at Noël Gaulois La Revanche des Pirates, Romus et Rapidus and Menhir Express
don't run.

- [La Descente du Nil](ref:parc-asterix/la-descente-du-nil) was called
  L'Oxygénarium until the end of 2025: a big WhiteWater West slide from 1999
  that you ride down in a round tube. For 2026 the park rethemed it and moved it
  into the Égypte area, which took over part of the old Paris for it. Height
  limit 100 and 130 cm.
- [La Revanche des Pirates – Le Grand Splatch](ref:parc-asterix/la-revanche-des-pirates-le-grand-splatch)
  is an Intamin shoot-the-chute from the opening year, with 20-seat boats and an
  eleven-metre drop. Since 2024 it's had pirates and the long name.
- [Romus et Rapidus](ref:parc-asterix/romus-et-rapidus) is the rapids ride,
  also Intamin, also 1989, formerly La Descente du Styx. You need to be 140 cm
  to ride it without an adult.
- [Menhir Express](ref:parc-asterix/menhir-express) is the log flume, by
  Hopkins, since 1995, with a 13-metre final drop.


You stay dry on [Épidemaïs Croisières](ref:parc-asterix/epidemais-croisieres),
a boat ride round the village, and on the
[Tour de Numérobis](ref:parc-asterix/la-tour-de-numerobis), a swing ride from
2024 that climbs to 40 metres and spins at the top. The 4D cinema has shown
[Attention Menhir !](ref:parc-asterix/attention-menhir), a ten-minute film,
since 2019.

There have been no dolphins since 2020. The former dolphinarium, the Théâtre de
Poséidon, has had a high-diving show, Les Plongeons de l'Olympe, since June 2022. In the 940-seat arena, Gauls play Romans in Gaulois vs Romains : Le
Match; the Théâtre de Panoramix has had the musical C'est du délire ! since
2024; and in what's left of Paris, the stunt show Main basse sur la Joconde has
run every summer since 1996. In summer 2026, from 11 July, several hundred
drones flew over the lake in the evenings for a show called "Astérix et la
potion d'étoiles".

## The year's records and Christmas week

Of the ten rides in the table at the top, five had their longest wait of the
last twelve months between 26 and 30 December 2025: Toutatis, OzIris,
Cétautomatix, Discobélix and La Trace du Hourra. For the other five, the worst
day fell in summer 2026, between May and August (as of 2 October 2026).

For Noël Gaulois the park opens this year from 19 December 2026 to 3 January
2027, which is exactly the French Christmas school holiday, sixteen days in
all. There are two explanations for the records, and we can't tell from our
measurements which one matters more. One is that everyone who wants to come over Christmas has
those sixteen days to do it. The other is in the park's FAQ: in winter the water
rides are closed, and in sub-zero temperatures some other rides are too, so the
crowd spreads over fewer rides.

### French school holidays

France splits its winter and spring holidays into three zones. Paris and the
Île-de-France (the Paris, Créteil and Versailles school districts) are in Zone
C, the Oise with Plailly in Zone B. Everyone has the autumn and Christmas
holidays at the same time: 17 October to 1 November 2026 and 19 December 2026
to 3 January 2027. In spring 2027 Zone C is off from 3 to 18 April, Zone A from
10 to 25 April, Zone B from 17 April to 2 May. The summer holidays start on 3
July 2027. The park is closed in February; according to French Wikipedia the
season usually starts at Easter.

## A day at Parc Astérix, in order

```hourly-profile-widget slug=parc-asterix top=8

```

In the main season the park opens at 10 am and the car park at 9. Peur sur le
Parc and Christmas have different hours, listed further down.

At ten, head for La Grèce. Three rides stand close together there with short
queues for the first half hour: Tonnerre 2 Zeus, Pégase Express and Discobélix.
Start with Tonnerre 2 Zeus, where you save the most. Pégase Express fills up
fastest; after a quarter of an hour the head start is gone.

```glossary-widget slug=rope-drop

```

Next, La Descente du Nil. It stays short for about an hour after opening and is
at its busiest in the early afternoon, when it's warm. OzIris is short in the
first half hour as well; if you miss that, save it for late afternoon.

The shows run around midday. Toutatis comes in the early afternoon, because by
our analysis its best window is about three and a half hours after opening. If you
can ride alone, take the single rider line.

Towards evening, do La Trace du Hourra and OzIris again. In the last hour most
queues are short again; only Toutatis and Cétautomatix stay roughly as long as
in the afternoon.

## Filotomatix and single rider

The park's fast pass is called Filotomatix. You buy it in advance on the website
or in the park, use it in the app, and it's valid for one person for one day.
There are four levels; prices are for buying before your visit, with the on-site
price in brackets:

- Bronze, 19 euros (25): a virtual queue, the app tells you when it's your turn.
  One go per ride.
- Argent, 35 euros (39): your wait is cut in half, one go per ride.
- Or, 49 euros (55): no wait, one go per ride.
- Illimité, 139 euros online: as many goes as you like with no wait, plus
  better seats at the shows.

Bronze, Argent and Or cover ten attractions: Tonnerre 2 Zeus, OzIris, La Trace
du Hourra, La Revanche des Pirates, Goudurix, Pégase Express, La Descente du
Nil, Romus et Rapidus, Menhir Express and Attention Menhir !. **Toutatis isn't
one of them**, and neither are Cétautomatix, Discobélix or the tower. Those
only come with Illimité. The park says you'll still queue for ten to fifteen
minutes with Filotomatix, and the number of passes per day is limited.

```glossary-widget slug=single-rider

```

On a weekday outside the school holidays I'd keep the money and stick to the
order above. On a Saturday in August or between Christmas and New Year, Or is the level
most likely to pay for itself, because it covers the big rides except Toutatis,
and you ride Toutatis in the single rider line. According to the park that line only exists at Toutatis and OzIris, and
it may stay closed on busy days.

## Peur sur le Parc and Noël Gaulois

### Peur sur le Parc

The park's Halloween runs from 3 October to 8 November 2026. On normal days it's
open from 10 am to 7 pm. On twelve days it opens at 9, closes at 6 pm and
reopens at 7 pm until 1 am for a nocturne with its own ticket: 53 euros online,
58 at the gate, on 10, 16, 17, 21, 22, 23, 24, 28, 29, 30 and 31 October and 7
November. 31 October was already sold out on 2 October. In 2025 the park says
557,500 people came over 20 days and twelve nocturnes.

There are three haunted houses this year: Le Tombeau des Dieux (not recommended
under 14), Catacombes and Les Enfers de Pompéi (both not recommended under 16;
in the evening Pompéi costs 6 euros with a booked time slot). New are La Forêt
sans Retour, a walk through the woods at the nocturnes only, also 16 and up, and
Les Bas-Fonds du Souk, a scare zone in the Égypte area. La Colère d'Anubis went
with the Rue de Paris. What the other parks are doing this autumn is in our
[Halloween guide 2026](/blog/halloween-theme-parks-2026).

### Noël Gaulois

The winter season runs from 19 December 2026 to 3 January 2027, 11 am to 8 pm,
until 7 pm on 24 and 31 December. There's an ice rink, the Patinoire Viking,
with free skates. The park sets up Father Christmas's gardens in the Romus et
Rapidus queue, and there's a tube slide, La Glissade d'Obélix, a parade and a
Christmas market. The coasters "generally" run, the park says, the water rides
don't. An undated Christmas ticket costs 57 euros. What other parks open in
winter is in our [winter guide](/blog/winter-theme-parks-2026).

Between 9 November and 18 December 2026 the park is closed, and again from 4
January until the season starts in spring. Since 2007 it's stayed open until
after the autumn holidays; before that it closed in September. It opened in
winter from 2007 to 2010 and again since 2019.

## What's closing and what's coming

The Rue de Paris is gone. The park announced its closure on 7 November 2025, and
with it went the Chevaux du Roy carousel and three Halloween haunts; the
animatronics in the Hôtel des Artistes and the Maison du 14 juillet had already
stopped in September. A carousel and parts of the sets are going to the
Festiland theme park, according to the park, and other pieces to the Musée des
Arts Forains in Paris.

In 2027 La Grèce gets bigger. The park has announced two family rides, a
playground and a restaurant, La Table de Dionysos, with 450 seats. Androland and
Parcs Actus report that the design takes its cue from the Greek island of Symi;
Androland mentions two small Zierer drop towers and a Zamperla Go Go Bounce. The park hasn't confirmed that. A fourth hotel, L'Odyssée, with 300 rooms and
four stars, is due the same year.

Londinium arrives in 2028. On the site of the old Paris, the park is building
an indoor area based on the album "Asterix in Britain". It has announced a big
themed coaster, an interactive family dark ride, a climbing playground, a pub
and shops. RCDB already lists the coaster: an Intamin Multi Dimension Coaster
with spinning cars, 17 metres tall, inside the building. Tonnerre 2 Zeus gets
the second part of its new steel track over the winter of 2026/27.

## Tickets, getting there, hotels and food

### Tickets and prices 2026

At the gate a day costs 68 euros for adults from 12, 59 for children aged 3 to
11 and 63 for over-60s; under-3s go free. Online is cheaper, depending on the
date. The Billet Malin is 49 euros, the same for adults and children, but only
on a limited number of days. The Billet Futé starts at 56 euros for adults and
53 for children if you book at least seven days ahead. Two consecutive days cost
125 and 109 euros at the gate. Tickets bought online are for a fixed date, and
you don't need to book anything else. On 2 October the park wasn't selling
season passes for 2027 yet.

### Getting there

On the A1 towards Lille the park has its own exit between exits 7 and 8. It's 35
kilometres from Porte de la Chapelle according to the park, about half an hour,
and 257 kilometres from Brussels. Parking has cost 25 euros a day since 3
October 2026; before that it was 20.

Without a car, take the RER B to Aéroport Charles de Gaulle 1. The Keolis
shuttle leaves from stand A3 at the Roissypôle bus station every 15 to 30
minutes, from an hour before opening until an hour after closing, with gaps
between 1 pm and 3:30 pm. The ride takes just under half an hour and costs 14
euros return, 8 one way, free for under-3s; book by the day before at the
latest. The desk at Roissypôle sells admission and shuttle together for 81
euros (72 for children). From central Paris, Paris City Vision runs from Avenue
de Suffren near the Eiffel Tower, from 52 euros return, about an hour each way.
FlixBus and BlaBlaCar Bus also stop at the park.

### Hotels and food

The park has three hotels: Les Trois Hiboux from 1999, with 150 rooms and no air
conditioning; La Cité Suspendue from 2018, with 150 rooms; and Les Quais de
Lutèce from 2020, with 149 rooms and four stars. For one night with one day's
admission the park quotes from 99, 102 and 111 euros per person, based on two
adults and two children in high season. The park is less than ten minutes' walk
away, parking is free, and you get in half an hour early.

You can book tables in the app at three restaurants: Le Relais Gaulois
(self-service, 28.90 euros, Astérix and Obélix come round the tables at lunch),
Les Comptoirs d'Épidemaïs (buffet, new since June 2026, 37.20 euros) and Le
Restaurant du Lac (table service, 32.90 euros). Picnics are allowed in the
marked areas, cool boxes and barbecues aren't.

Bags can't be bigger than 55 × 40 × 25 cm, and selfie sticks and glass stay
outside.

## Frequently asked questions about Parc Astérix

### What's the best coaster at Parc Astérix?

On paper, Toutatis: 51 metres, at least 107 km/h and a 101-degree drop. For the
time you save by turning up early, Tonnerre 2 Zeus. Current waits are on the
[Toutatis page](ref:parc-asterix/toutatis).

### Does Parc Astérix have a fast pass?

Yes, Filotomatix, in four levels from 19 to 139 euros. Toutatis is only in the
most expensive one. The single rider line at Toutatis and OzIris is free.

### When should you get to Parc Astérix?

At opening, ten o'clock, and go straight to Tonnerre 2 Zeus. Getting there early
doesn't help at Toutatis; early afternoon is better there.

### How do you get to Parc Astérix without a car?

RER B to Aéroport Charles de Gaulle 1, then the shuttle from stand A3 at
Roissypôle, 14 euros return. Coaches from Paris City Vision, FlixBus and
BlaBlaCar Bus also run from Paris.

### How tall does your child need to be?

130 cm for Toutatis and OzIris, and to ride Pégase Express and Cétautomatix
alone; 140 cm for Goudurix; 120 cm for Tonnerre 2 Zeus and Discobélix. With an
adult, children can ride Pégase Express, Cétautomatix and La Descente du Nil
from 100 cm, and SOS Tournevis from 90 cm.

### Is Parc Astérix busier at Christmas than in summer?

For five of the ten rides in the table, yes: their worst day of the last twelve
months fell between 26 and 30 December 2025.

### Is Parc Astérix open all year?

No. The 2026 season started on 4 April, ran with Peur sur le Parc until 8
November, and Noël Gaulois runs from 19 December to 3 January. In between, and
from January until spring, the park is closed.

### Who owns Parc Astérix?

The Compagnie des Alpes, which has run it since 2002. The group also owns Walibi
Belgium, Walibi Holland and, since 2025, Belantis near Leipzig, which is due to
become a Parc Astérix by 2030 or 2031.
