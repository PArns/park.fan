## New

- Southern California (park.fan/en/california), Japan (park.fan/en/japan) and Paris
  (park.fan/paris) each have a crowd calendar with a page for each of the next eleven months,
  best-time pages and the trip planner. Southern California covers Disneyland, California
  Adventure, Universal Studios Hollywood, Knott's Berry Farm, Six Flags Magic Mountain and the three
  parks around San Diego. Japan covers Tokyo Disneyland, Tokyo DisneySea, Fuji-Q Highland, LEGOLAND
  Japan and Universal Studios Japan. Paris covers both Disneyland Paris parks and Parc Astérix.
- A Japan trip sleeps in more than one city. The planner picks the cities and the nights in each,
  keeps them in order along the line from Tokyo to Osaka, and plans the morning you move with your
  luggage sent ahead. Under "Route" you choose the airports you land at and leave from, fix the
  nights of a city yourself, or set a city to 0 nights to visit its parks as a day trip.
- Around Paris the planner chooses between three bases: the Disney hotels, Parc Astérix and the
  city. Landing at Marne-la-Vallée–Chessy starts you at Disney and Orly in the city; with Charles de
  Gaulle the planner decides.
- A travel guide for each region, in six languages: where to stay, how long the drives and train
  rides take, how the tickets work and which weeks are busy. Each hub links its guide at the bottom.
- Park pages of the parks in a trip region carry a planner tile with Parky, directly above "Today
  in the park". It plans the park into a trip and links the region's crowd calendar.
- Parky reads the trip you're planning on the hub and the last one you planned, and changes it
  when you ask: dates, parks, cities, nights, the airport and the hotel. It also sees every day
  planned in the day planner from today, not just the next six, and offers replies to tap.

## Improved

- The trip planner asks for your dates first, with the region's headliners already picked, and
  shows a plan as soon as you choose the day you leave. Tickets come after the plan ("Already have
  tickets? Add them"), and the hotel, rest days, second park days, evenings and park hopping wait
  under "More options".
- Under each date in the date picker stands the best park of the day, for the whole region or for
  one resort ("All · Disney · Universal" in Orlando). The month view draws each day as a bar, and
  both mark the hurricane or typhoon season, dashed for the season and solid for its peak.
- The line under the plan gives the peak wait on your days and how much shorter it is than the
  average of your trip's days. "How accurate is this?" next to it holds that average and how far a
  forecast for one ride on one day can be off.
- Each hub opens on its question, "Which park on which day?", with the best park at each resort and
  the quietest days ahead, then the calendar. Its questions sit at the bottom, folded, and one of
  them says how far the forecast reaches and how far off it was over the last weeks.
- Within the next 60 days a planner day shows the same minutes as the park's own page. Further out,
  the calendar, the planner and the best-time pages use the waits we measured on the same weekday
  last year, and say so once. The best weeks to travel are ranked on that scale; in Orlando they
  are mid-April, late October to early November and mid-May. A trip into the peak of the hurricane
  or typhoon season ranks lower.
- The Disney Springs Resort Area hotels on Hotel Plaza Boulevard, the Renaissance Orlando Resort and
  Spa among them, plan with Disney's Early Entry.
- The trip guides open on their region's Parky scene, and the blog's front page shows one of the
  four scenes each day. On a phone the big header shows the scene's landscape, so the headline never
  covers Parky's face.

- Disneyland in Anaheim showed photos of Disneyland Park in Paris, because the two parks share a
  name.

- With "Planen" or "More" open, the language banner covered the top of the band.

  page looked like nothing happened.
- The title card of a park page reached into the guide window next to it, by 173 to 204 px at
  1024 px wide.
- The line under the plan could say "4 less than average" next to an average 5 minutes above the
  peak; both now come from the same rounded figures.
- The best-time pages printed two full stops after a date that ended a sentence in German and
