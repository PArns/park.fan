You aren't supposed to see a roller coaster at Alton Towers from the outside. The Staffordshire
park lies in a Conservation Area, a protected zone under British planning law, and nothing built
there may rise above the treetops. Anything that does go higher has to be designed so that it can't
be made out from the surrounding countryside. That's why the track of [Rita](ref:alton-towers/rita)
is painted red, and dark green only at its highest points.

So the park's big rides are built low or sunk below ground level. **[Nemesis Reborn](ref:alton-towers/nemesis-reborn)** runs through
an excavated quarry, and its riders hang under the track between the rock faces.
**[Oblivion](ref:alton-towers/oblivion)** is just under 20 metres tall and still drops 55 metres,
because most of the drop lies in a tunnel underground. **[The Smiler](ref:alton-towers/the-smiler)**
turns you upside down fourteen times and gets by with a height of 30 metres.

The villages of Alton and Farley sit close to the grounds, and in 2004 a couple from Farley took
Alton Towers to court in a private prosecution over noise. The court found the park guilty, and the
judge named Oblivion specifically in his ruling. The £5,000 fine was cut to £3,500 on appeal. A
noise abatement order first set the limit at 32 decibels, and the appeal raised it to 40. The park
then dropped the announcement that used to play before Oblivion's drop, because it believed the
announcement was what made riders scream.

## The coasters, area by area

The Skyride, a chairlift, shuttles between the areas, with stations
on Towers Street behind the entrance, in Forbidden Valley and in the Dark Forest.

### Nemesis Reborn and Galactica in Forbidden Valley

Nemesis opened on **19 March 1994** as Europe's first inverted coaster. Bolliger & Mabillard built
it to a design by Werner Stengel, and the idea came from the park developer John Wardley. The track
is 716 metres long and 13 metres high, and the train reaches 81 km/h and turns upside down four
times. In 2023 the ride stood still for a whole season and got new track. It's been called Nemesis
Reborn since 16 March 2024.

![A train of riders lying face down passes through a blue and white metal ring. | On Galactica you lie face down under the track. The portal sits right behind the station. | right](/media/alton-towers/galactica.jpg)

**[Galactica](ref:alton-towers/galactica)** stands in the same area. It opened on 16 March 2002
under the name Air and was the manufacturer B&M's first flying coaster. You lie face down under the
track and fly close to the ground, under footpaths and past rocks. The track measures 840 metres,
and the ride is 20 metres high, reaches 75 km/h and has two inversions. It can take up to 1,500
riders an hour. From 2016 to 2018 every rider wore a VR headset, and since 2019 Galactica has run
without them again.

```glossary-widget slug=flying-coaster

```

**[Nemesis Sub-Terra](ref:alton-towers/nemesis-sub-terra)**, also in Forbidden Valley, is a drop tower inside a building; it
ran from 2012 to 2015 and has been back in operation since 2023.
**[Toxicator](ref:alton-towers/toxicator)** is a Top Spin that opened on 15 March 2025. It stands on
a concrete platform just under five metres high above the path, so you can watch it from below.

### Oblivion and The Smiler in the X-Sector

X-Sector is themed as a testing facility. Oblivion arrived there on **14 March 1998** at a cost of
£12 million and was the world's first dive coaster. The car stops at the edge, hangs over the drop
for one to three seconds and then falls at 87.5 degrees into the tunnel. At the bottom it reaches
109 km/h, and riders feel 4.5 g. Each car seats sixteen people in two rows of eight. The whole ride
lasts 75 seconds over 372 metres of track.

```glossary-widget slug=dive-coaster

```

The Smiler opened on 31 May 2013, and its fourteen inversions were a world record at the time.
Gerstlauer built it as an Infinity Coaster for £18 million. The track is 1,170 metres long and the
ride 30 metres high, the train reaches up to 85 km/h, and a ride takes two minutes and 45 seconds.
The Black Hole, a coaster in the dark under a tent roof, used to stand on this spot.

On 2 June 2015 a full train on The Smiler ran at about 80 km/h into an empty test train that was
standing on the track. Five riders were seriously injured, and two of them lost parts of a leg in
the weeks that followed. Britain's Health and Safety Executive (HSE) prosecuted the operating
company, Merlin Attractions Operations, and Merlin pleaded guilty. According to the HSE, there were
no procedures that let engineers work safely on the ride while it was running. On 27 September 2016
Stafford Crown Court imposed a fine of £5 million; without the guilty plea it would have been £7.5
million. The Smiler has been running again since 19 March 2016, with additional safeguards.

### Rita and TH13TEEN in the Dark Forest

**[Rita](ref:alton-towers/rita)** has run in the Dark Forest since **1 April 2005**. The Intamin
coaster is launched hydraulically and goes from 0 to 98.3 km/h in 2.5 seconds, at 4.7 g. It's just
over 18 metres tall, and the ride is over after 25 seconds. It's themed as a drag race, and a
countdown runs before the launch, the way it would at a race track.

```glossary-widget slug=launch-coaster

```

**[TH13TEEN](ref:alton-towers/th13teen)** opened on **20 March 2010** as the first coaster with a
vertical free fall in the middle of its track. The train rolls into a building, a gate closes behind
it, and then the section of track drops about five metres with the train still on it. After that
it runs backwards into a tunnel. Intamin built it on the spot where the Corkscrew stood from 1980
to 2008.

### Wicker Man in Mutiny Bay

**[Wicker Man](ref:alton-towers/wicker-man)** opened on **20 March 2018** as Britain's first new
wooden coaster in 22 years. Great Coasters International built it for £16 million. The track is 795
metres long, the biggest drop is 22 metres, the train reaches 70 km/h, and a ride lasts three and a
half minutes. A six-storey wooden figure stands at the start of the track, and the train runs
through it while flames burn inside. According to the park, it was the first wooden coaster with
fire.

```glossary-widget slug=wooden-coaster

```

The **[Battle Galleons](ref:alton-towers/battle-galleons)** are in Mutiny Bay as well, family boats
that carried water cannons until 2025. During the winter refurbishment the park took the cannons out
and fitted mist jets in their place. The boats have been back since 23 May 2026.

### Spinball Whizzer

**[Spinball Whizzer](ref:alton-towers/spinball-whizzer)** has been running since 2004. The Maurer
coaster has freely spinning cars, is 450 metres long and 17 metres tall, and takes 950 riders an
hour. It's themed as a pinball machine, with the silver cars as its balls. From 2010 to 2015 it was
called Sonic Spinball, because Sega sponsored it.

### Family coasters in Katanga Canyon and CBeebies Land

The park's oldest coaster is the
**[Runaway Mine Train](ref:alton-towers/runaway-mine-train)** in Katanga Canyon. The powered Mack
train opened on 21 March 1992, does two laps of its 305-metre track at 36 km/h and has 48 seats.
Like Nemesis, its layout is John Wardley's. It shares a tunnel with the
**[Congo River Rapids](ref:alton-towers/congo-river-rapids)**, a rapids ride in round boats that
was built during the big expansion between 1985 and 1988.

In CBeebies Land, the
**[Octonauts Rollercoaster Adventure](ref:alton-towers/octonauts-rollercoaster-adventure)** has
stood since 2015 where the Beastie used to run. Since 28 March 2026 the area has had a second children's coaster,
**[Bluey the Ride: Here Come The Grannies!](ref:alton-towers/postman-pat-parcel-post)**, a Zierer
train with 28 seats on the site of the dismantled Postman Pat Parcel Post. In the queue, children
can walk through the family's house from the series.

### Hex inside the Towers

**[Hex](ref:alton-towers/hex-the-legend-of-the-towers)** has been inside the walls of the stately
home that gives the park its name since 2000. You first walk through the rooms and hear the legend
of the curse on the Earl of Shrewsbury, read by the actor Jim Carter. At the end you sit in a vault,
and the room seems to turn around the benches. Technically it's a Vekoma Madhouse.

### Height limits

The park sorts its attractions into four height bands:

- 140 cm: Nemesis Reborn, Oblivion, Galactica, Rita, The Smiler, Nemesis Sub-Terra and Toxicator
- 120 cm: TH13TEEN, Wicker Man and Spinball Whizzer
- 110 cm: Congo River Rapids, Runaway Mine Train and Marauder's Mayhem
- 90 cm: the family attractions, from Gangsta Granny to the two children's coasters

Rita, Galactica, Toxicator, TH13TEEN and Spinball Whizzer also have an upper limit of just under two
metres. Children under 90 cm get into the park free.

The park opens at **10am**, and the car park gates and the monorail at **9:30am**. From the standard

By our measurements, the queue at five rides is shorter in the first hour after opening than later

## When to go

```stats-widget slug=alton-towers show=months,weekdays

```

The season runs from March to early November. After that the theme park closes for winter; the
waterpark, the hotels and the mini golf stay open, and there's a Christmas programme in November and
December. The park opens at 10am every day and closes between 4pm and 9pm, depending on the time of
year.

According to the park, it's busiest in the British school holidays: at Easter, over the summer
holidays and in the half terms, the week-long breaks in the middle of each school term. If you're
travelling from mainland Europe, your own school holidays won't line up with the English ones, so
check the English holiday calendar before you book. The autumn half term falls in the second half of
October. The Scarefest weekends before it, from 26 September, have the same decorations without the
English school holidays.

### Scarefest: 26 September to 1 November 2026

The Halloween event here is called **Scarefest**. In 2026 it runs on 26 and 27 September, from 2 to
4 October and from 9 to 11 October, and then every day from 16 October to 1 November. On those days
the park is open until 9pm, and Nemesis Reborn, Wicker Man, The Smiler, Oblivion and Toxicator run
in the dark.

Four **scare mazes** cost extra: the new Final Exhibit, plus Edge of the Forest, COMPOUND and
Altonville Mine Tours: Tiny's Revenge. Two **scare zones** come with the day ticket, Dark Hollow in
the woods and the Burial Grounds. Families get Amigos in the Afterlife and Trick O' Treat Town as
add-on tickets, and three shows at no extra charge. New for 2026 is Dine with the Dead, a dinner
with performers at your table, for ages 14 and up.

On Scarefest days the day ticket starts at £37 online. A combination ticket with all four mazes
starts at £80, and one with the two family attractions at £53.

The event has gone by the name Scarefest since 2007. The park's first maze, in 2002, was called
Terror of the Towers. In 2006 there was no public Halloween programme, only an unannounced maze
called Room 13 for hotel guests.

The season ends with the **Fireworks** weekend on the Great Lawns. Since a court order in 2005,
another consequence of the noise dispute, there have been three fireworks displays a year.

## Fastrack

```glossary-widget slug=fastrack

```

**Bronze from £35** gives you five rides, spread however you like across fifteen attractions. The
Smiler and Wicker Man aren't among them, and those are the two rides where you queue longest.
**Silver from £65** covers seventeen attractions, those two included, with one ride on each.
**Gold from £95** is valid at the same seventeen attractions all day, with no limit. In all three
tiers your ticket can be scanned at most once every three minutes, so you can't queue for two rides
at the same time.

CBeebies Land has had its own package since 2026: £7 for two rides, £10 for three, each attraction
once. On the day itself you can buy single rides in the app as One Shot tickets, while they last.

On a weekday outside the English school holidays, an early start means you can do without Fastrack. On a Saturday in the holidays I'd buy Silver
for The Smiler and Wicker Man, not Bronze.

## A day at Alton Towers

Be at the monorail or the entrance at **9:30am** so you're inside at ten. An early start saves the most at the four rides for taller riders. I'd begin with Spinball
Whizzer, then ride Rita and TH13TEEN in the Dark Forest, and from there take the Skyride over to
Forbidden Valley for Nemesis Reborn. On a site this hilly, the chairlift spares you some long walks.

Galactica is in the same area as Nemesis and fits in straight after it. Leave Oblivion and The
Smiler in X-Sector for the late morning or the afternoon. On a Scarefest day you can save Wicker Man
for the evening, when it runs in the dark.

With small children, head for CBeebies Land and the Octonauts coaster first,
while it's still quiet there, then go on to Mutiny Bay and Katanga Canyon. The monorail runs back to
the car park and the hotels until an hour after the rides close.

## Tickets, parking and getting there

### Tickets

A day ticket costs from **£32** online and **£68** at the gate. The park requires a booking in
advance for every day you visit, annual passholders included, and sold-out days disappear from the
calendar. Two days cost from £44, so the second day adds £12. The annual pass for Alton Towers alone
is **£64**, and the Merlin Essential Pass, valid at more than twenty UK attractions, is £139. Parents
with toddlers get their own ticket from £29, which isn't valid at weekends or in the school holidays.
The waterpark costs extra, from £23.

### Parking

Standard parking costs **£13** online and £15 on the day; from there it's a 15 to 25 minute walk,
or you take the monorail. Express parking costs from **£25** and is one to three minutes from the
entrance. Blue Badge holders pay the standard price. Gold, Platinum and Premium passholders and
hotel guests park free. There are 26 charging points for electric cars: ten in Car Park 1, ten at
the Splash Landings Hotel and six at the Alton Towers Hotel. If you play mini golf, you'll get the
standard parking fee back when you hand in your stamped scorecard.

### Getting there

The address is Alton, Staffordshire, ST10 4DB. The park lies between the M1 and the M6. The nearest
junctions are 23a heading north on the M1 and 28 heading south, and on the M6 it's 15 heading north
and 16 heading south. For the last few miles the park asks you to follow the road signs rather than
your sat nav, because some devices send you along the B5417 onto a farm track.

Without a car, you can travel to **Uttoxeter** (16 km, 20 minutes by taxi, 30 by bus),
**Stoke-on-Trent** (24 km, about 25 minutes) or **Derby** (40 km, 50 to 60 minutes). D&G's 32X bus
runs from Hanley via Cheadle to the park. At weekends there are coaches from Birmingham,
Manchester, London and a dozen other cities.

### Food

The resort has 38 restaurants, snack bars and cafés. The best known is the **Rollercoaster
Restaurant** from 2016, where your food rolls to the table on rails. It's open until 9:30pm, and in
the evening you can eat there without a park ticket, except on Scarefest and Fireworks days.

### Ride Access Pass

If a disability means you can't stand in the normal queue, you can get Merlin's **Ride Access
Pass**. In early February 2026 Merlin announced a trial for the February half term that would
exclude guests whose difficulty lies in coping with crowds, at Alton Towers, Chessington and
Legoland Windsor. After protests, ADHD UK's among them, Merlin called the trial off on 13 February.
Its chief operating officer, Rob Smith, apologised and said the symbol for difficulty with crowds would
still be recognised.

## Staying at the park

The **Alton Towers Hotel** from 1996 has 180 rooms in thirteen categories and is themed around a
fictional explorer; the two top floors have themes of their own, from Gangsta Granny and Arabian
Nights to a room named after The Smiler. The **Splash Landings Hotel** from 2003 has 216 rooms and
direct access to the waterpark. The **CBeebies Land Hotel** from 2017 has 76 rooms in nine themes,
Bluey among them. Then there's the **Enchanted Village** from 2015, with 120 lodges and five
treehouses, and the **Stargazing Pods** from 2019, 102 pods for up to four people each, with no
restaurant of their own.

Staying on site saves you the morning drive along the A50. Hotel guests also park free, and guests
at the CBeebies Land Hotel get Express parking at no extra charge. A short break with a night,
breakfast and a day in the park starts at **£77** per person, or £100 on Scarefest dates.

## From country estate to theme park

![A grey stately home with battlements, towers and glazed conservatories behind clipped yews and a lavender bed | The Towers and their conservatories. Hex runs inside the house. | wide](/media/alton-towers/the-towers.jpg)

There was a fortified settlement on the site
as early as the Iron Age, and from 1412 to 1924 the estate belonged to the same family, the Talbots,
Earls of Shrewsbury. Most of today's house dates from the early 19th century.
**Augustus Welby Pugin**, who also worked on the Palace of Westminster, remodelled it. The
banqueting hall window is around ten metres high and six metres wide, with 27 panes in three rows.
The park calls it the most important window of its kind outside Parliament.

The 18th Earl first opened his gardens to visitors in **1860**. In that first year he took
£116 17s 5d. Thirty years later, 30,000 people came in a single day to see acrobats, lion tamers,
elephants, bands and fireworks.

The family sold most of the estate in 1918 and the rest in 1924, to a group of local businessmen who
turned it into Alton Towers Ltd. During the Second World War the military used the buildings and
only handed them back in the mid-1950s, in a state the owners then fought over in court. After that
came a railway, rides and, in the sixties, a boating lake and a chairlift.

It became a theme park in **1980** under its owner at the time, John Broome. He brought in a single
admission price that covered every ride, and in the same year opened the **Corkscrew**, Europe's
first coaster with two corkscrews in a row. It ran until 2008; a piece of its corkscrew has stood on
the forecourt as a monument since 2010. Between 1985 and 1988 came Towers Street, the monorail, the
car parks, the rapids ride and the 1,048-metre Skyride, which has been running since 1987.

In **1990** Broome sold to Britain's Tussauds Group, after overstretching himself on the conversion
of Battersea Power Station in London. Under Tussauds came the Runaway Mine Train, Nemesis and, in
1996, the first hotel. In **2007** the investment firm Blackstone bought the Tussauds Group and
merged it with **Merlin Entertainments**. Since then the park has belonged to the same group as
Thorpe Park, Legoland Windsor and [Heide Park](/blog/heide-park-wait-times-tips) in Soltau. Merlin
hasn't owned the land since 2007; the group sold it and leased it back.

The most visitors came in 1994, the year of Nemesis, and in 2010, the year of TH13TEEN, more than
three million each time. For 2019 the Themed Entertainment Association estimates 2,130,000 guests.
That put Alton Towers second in Britain, behind Legoland Windsor. Up to 28,000 people are allowed
on the site at once.

While they're being planned, big projects here carry the code name **Secret Weapon** and a number.
The name comes from a coaster that was planned around 1993 and never built; TH13TEEN was SW6, Wicker
Man SW8. The next project is known internally as **Project Horizon**, an indoor attraction where the
Alton Mouse used to run. The public consultation ran in September 2022, and planning permission came
with conditions in June 2023. Construction hasn't started, and the park hasn't given a date.

## Frequently asked questions about Alton Towers

### Which Alton Towers coasters were the first of their kind?

Nemesis opened in 1994 as Europe's first inverted coaster and has been called Nemesis Reborn
since 2024. Oblivion was the world's first dive coaster in 1998, and TH13TEEN in 2010 the first
coaster with a vertical free fall in its track. The Smiler set a world record in 2013 with fourteen
inversions.

### How many roller coasters does Alton Towers have?

Eleven. Seven of them are built for adults and older children: Nemesis Reborn, Oblivion, Galactica,
Rita, TH13TEEN, The Smiler and Wicker Man. Then there's Spinball Whizzer, the powered Runaway Mine
Train and the two children's coasters in CBeebies Land.

### How much does a ticket to Alton Towers cost?

From £32 per person online, or £68 at the gate. On Scarefest days the day ticket starts at £37. Two
days cost from £44, and the annual pass is £64. Children under 90 centimetres get in free, but like
everyone else they need a booking for the day. The park lists its current prices on
[altontowers.com](https://www.altontowers.com/tickets-passes/day-passes/).

### When is Alton Towers open in 2026?

From March to early November, every day from 10am. Closing time is between 4pm and 9pm depending on
the time of year, and 9pm on Scarefest days up to and including 1 November. In winter the theme park
is closed, while the waterpark and hotels stay open. The calendar with every opening time is on our
[park page](ref:alton-towers).

### When is Alton Towers least busy?

Outside the English school holidays, and then on a weekday. The holidays in England don't fall at
the same time as those in mainland Europe, and the autumn half term is in the second half of
October. It also gets busy on Scarefest days up to 1 November and at weekends in summer.

### Is Fastrack worth it at Alton Towers?

Bronze at £35 doesn't cover The Smiler or Wicker Man, the two rides with
the longest queues. If they're the main reason you're going, you need Silver at £65 or Gold at £95.
On a weekday outside the holidays, an early start is usually enough.

### How tall does your child need to be to ride?

At 140 cm they can ride everything. From 120 cm TH13TEEN, Wicker Man and Spinball Whizzer are
allowed, from 110 cm the Congo River Rapids and the Runaway Mine Train, and from 90 cm the family
attractions.

### What happened on The Smiler in 2015?

On 2 June 2015 a full train ran into an empty test train that was standing on the track. Five riders
were seriously injured, and two of them lost parts of a leg. Merlin pleaded guilty in court and had
to pay a £5 million fine. Britain's Health and Safety Executive (HSE) accused the operator of
lacking safety procedures for work on the ride while it was running. The Smiler has been running
again since March 2016, with additional safeguards.

### Why can't you see a coaster from outside the park?

The grounds are a Conservation Area, and nothing built there may rise above the treetops. Anything
that goes higher has to stay invisible from outside, so the highest sections of Rita's track are
painted dark green. That's why the big rides run through dips, a quarry and tunnels.
