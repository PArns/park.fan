## New

- The trip planner arrived on 4 September, in all six languages. Four questions lead in: the
  park, the day (picked on a month grid coloured by that park's crowd forecast), who is coming,
  and which headliners. The day then opens as a timeline where each ride is a block as tall as its
  predicted queue, with the walk to the next one in between and the weather down the edge. You can
  drag blocks around, pull rides in from the park page and add blocks of your own, such as lunch.
  The plan stays in your browser.
- On a day that does not fit, "plan all headliners" opens a three-step assistant: what would make
  room without dropping anything, the rides in the order the optimiser gives them up, and the
  result with the dropped rides by name. Before, the answer was an 11 px grey line under the
  buttons, and the ride that fell out was the one with the most minutes of queueing, usually the
  one people came for. Each suggestion is a measured difference between two plans: across 35 park
  days at six parks, five did not fit, and two of those were solved by shortening a break.
- A planned day ends when the park closes. The grid used to read a closing hour of 18 as "open
  until 18:59" and would plan a ride at 18:15 in a park that shuts at 18:00. The hour after
  closing is drawn as slack you can drag into, and nothing is planned there. What counts is when
  you join the queue, so a 40-minute queue at 17:45 is still a slot you can take on purpose.
- Wait-time alerts (8 September): the bell on a ride card sends a push notification once the wait
  falls to the number of minutes you set, and the bell on a show reminds you before it starts.
  `/alerts` lists every alert this browser has, across parks.
- Favourites have a page of their own at `/favorites` (16 September).
- "Today at this ride" on a ride page is the park page's panel one level down: the live wait and
  its trend, today's range and peak, the recommended slots, the typical and busy pair, and the
  park's own status and hours, which a ride page never stated before. The 30-day history became a
  chapter, drawn in the crowd calendar's colours with the queue curve along the floor of each tile.
- Every ride card already said "from 120 cm", one card at a time. Park pages now have a height
  filter, shown only where the park publishes minimum heights, with a scale taken from that park's
  own limits.
- Eleven curated fields put the address, phone number, website, ticket shop, Wikipedia, the park's
  social accounts, the year it opened and its area on a park page, none of which any of our three
  data feeds carries. Where a park has none of them, the section is left out.
- Where a park sells skip-the-queue access, the ride carries a badge with the product's name and
  price in your currency format. A price of zero means it is included, as at Europa-Park. Six
  brand names joined the glossary: QuickPass, Virtual Line, Fast Lane, Speedy Pass, Fastrack and
  Premier Access.
- 119 parks with enough measured days (as of 21 September) have a statistics page of their own:
  crowds by month and by weekday over two years, the rides ranked by typical wait, and the typical
  day hour by hour, with a note on how many opening days the numbers come from.
- An agent that knows only the hostname now finds `/llms.txt`, an API catalogue under
  `/.well-known/api-catalog`, three published skills with their SHA-256 digests, and an MCP server
  at `/api/mcp` with three read-only tools. A park that publishes no wait times is reported as
  such, not as a park averaging zero minutes.

## Improved

- An open hour on the weather chart is drawn four times as wide as a closed one, so the hours of
  a visit take 74 % of the chart instead of 42 %. Both changes of slope sit on the dashed edges of
  the opening-hours band, which now carry a door icon with the opening and closing time. That
  leaves room for an hourly tick through the visit, the temperature on arrival and on leaving,
  and a mark on the two wettest stretches of the day.
- The header menu opens as one wide panel: countries arrive with their flags, parks with their
  photos, and each section behind "More" is a card with an icon that links as a whole. The blog
  has its own entry in the bar again, under Backstage.
- The °C/°F switch sits in the header next to the language and theme controls, on every page. It
  governs the weather calendar, blog posts and the best-time hub, and it used to exist only in the
  weather card of a park page. Its width is fixed, so pressing it does not shift the row.
- The sitemap reports the day a page last changed for a reader. A daily crawl fingerprints what is
  on each page, instead of stamping all of its URLs (44,000 at the time) with today's date.

## Fixed

- On 18 September every map tile on every park page came back as "403 Access blocked", because
  OpenStreetMap's own tile server is not meant for embedding. Both maps draw from CARTO's basemap
  CDN now, credited to OpenStreetMap and CARTO.
- Going from a park page to its calendar and back cost a jump to the top and an animated scroll
  back down, in both directions. The tile row keeps its position now.
- The live column in the blog's wait-time tables disappeared at opening time, and four of those
  tables still pointed at a park that had been renamed.
- Seven layout shifts are gone, two of which our measuring script could not see from the top of
  the page.
- Smaller ones: the favourite star sits in its ring again, park names in the quiet-day card are no
  longer cut off, the quietest weekday shows again when two days tie, and the park.fan icon in
  Google's results is legible at the size Google draws it.
