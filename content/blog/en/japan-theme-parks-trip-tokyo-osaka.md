In Orlando, sixteen parks sit around one hotel room. In Japan, the five parks whose waits park.fan measures are spread over four cities: [Tokyo Disneyland](ref:tokyo-disneyland) and [Tokyo DisneySea](ref:tokyo-disneysea) in Maihama on the edge of Tokyo, [Fuji-Q Highland](ref:fuji-q-highland) at the foot of Mount Fuji, [LEGOLAND Japan](ref:legoland-japan) in Nagoya and [Universal Studios Japan](ref:universal-studios-japan) in Osaka. If you want all five, you'll sleep in at least two cities and ride the Shinkansen in between.

The Nozomi takes 147 to 150 minutes from Tokyo to Shin-Osaka on JR Central's timetable. Taking the cities in the wrong order costs you more than that, and so does changing hotels on the very day your park would have been at its emptiest. The [Tokyo Disney & USJ crowd calendar](/japan) has all five parks side by side, day by day. For the rides of the two big resorts, Premier Access and the Express Pass, there are separate guides to [Tokyo Disney Resort](/blog/tokyo-disney-resort-wait-times-tips) and [Universal Studios Japan](/blog/universal-studios-japan-wait-times-tips).

## The parks and their cities

### Tokyo Disneyland and Tokyo DisneySea

The two Disney parks stand side by side in Maihama. The address is Urayasu in Chiba Prefecture, not Tokyo, which only matters when you book a taxi. From Tokyo Station the JR Keiyo Line takes about 15 minutes to Maihama, and from there it's about a five-minute walk to the gate of Tokyo Disneyland. From a hotel at Tokyo Station, allow 35 minutes to the gate all the same, because the walk to the Keiyo Line platforms inside the station comes on top.

The Disney Resort Line, a monorail, links the parks. It runs one way round a loop of four stations: Resort Gateway by Maihama Station, Tokyo Disneyland, Bayside with its hotels, and Tokyo DisneySea. With the walks at each end, Tokyo Disneyland to the DisneySea gate takes about a quarter of an hour.

### Fuji-Q Highland

Fuji-Q Highland is the coaster park of the trip. It's in Fujiyoshida, one station before Kawaguchiko. In the trip planner you'll find it under Fujikawaguchiko, the town on Lake Kawaguchi. Entry is free. To ride you need a pass or single ride tickets, and the haunted house Senritsu Meikyu, the FUJIYAMA Walk and the FUJIYAMA Slider each need a timed ticket of their own on top. Free entry here is about as free as a restaurant that doesn't charge for the door.

![Snow-capped Mount Fuji above a band of cloud, with the steel hills of a roller coaster and a power pylon in front. | From Fuji-Q Highland you can see Mount Fuji on a clear day, here above a band of cloud. | wide](/media/fuji-q-highland/mount-fuji-coasters.jpg)

From Tokyo, Fuji-Q works well as a day trip. The highway buses from Busta Shinjuku take about 100 minutes on Fujikyu's timetable, and the first two, at 6:45 and 6:55, reach the park at 8:25, well before it opens. The last bus back leaves at 20:23 and gets to Shinjuku at 22:00. Seats are reserved, and sales open a month before the trip.

In winter the park is usually open only from 10:00 to 17:00 on weekdays. It's closed altogether on 10 December 2026 and on seven days between 13 January and 4 February 2027, and the buses from Shinjuku don't run to the park on those days either.

### LEGOLAND Japan

LEGOLAND Japan is at Kinjo-futo in the port of Nagoya. From Nagoya Station the Aonami Line gets you to Kinjo-futo in just under half an hour, and with the walks it's about 40 minutes to the gate. The park has no car park of its own and sends drivers to the city's Kinjo Futo car park. In January 2027 LEGOLAND Japan is closed on five days: the 13th, the 14th, and the 19th to the 21st.

Nagoya is on the Shinkansen line, and by Nozomi it's closer to Osaka than to Tokyo. So LEGOLAND fits into a trip either as a stop of its own, with a night in Nagoya, or as a day trip from Osaka. For the day trip from Umeda in Osaka, count on an hour and 45 minutes each way by Shinkansen and the Aonami Line.

### Universal Studios Japan

Universal Studios Japan, USJ for short, is in the west of Osaka. From Universal City Station on the JR Yumesaki Line you walk through Universal CityWalk to the gate in a few minutes. From Umeda you're at the gate in about 25 minutes, from Kyoto in just over an hour. I'd still sleep in Kyoto, because to me the evenings there are worth an hour on the train each morning.

![The cream entrance arch of Universal Studios Japan with a Mario banner, a coaster track to the right. | The first Super Nintendo World opened here in March 2021.](/media/universal-studios-japan/entrance-gate.jpg)

On busy days Super Nintendo World needs a timed ticket on top, the Area Timed Entry Ticket. Some Express Passes include one when you buy them, and otherwise you get one in the park's app on the day while they last. On quiet days you can walk in without one. Mario Kart and Mine Cart Madness are both inside Super Nintendo World, so on a busy day you won't ride either without that ticket.

## How many days each park needs

A Tokyo Disney ticket is for one park on one day, so I'd give Tokyo Disneyland and Tokyo DisneySea a full day each, and a third Disney day, if it fits, goes to DisneySea, where the queues in our measurements are longer than next door.

I'd give USJ two days if the trip has room for them. Of the five parks, it's where an early start saves the most in our rope-drop figures, and USJ sells the 2 Day and the 1.5 Day Studio Pass for exactly that. If you have one spare day, it goes to USJ and the next one to DisneySea, and the trip planner hands them out in the same order.

One day is enough for Fuji-Q. LEGOLAND Japan is a full day with small children and more like half a day for everyone else, and in winter it closes at 17:00 on weekdays. On a ten-day trip with all five parks, that leaves a good four days for arriving, leaving, moving, Kyoto and Tokyo itself.

## The order of the cities

From east to west the cities run Tokyo, Fujikawaguchiko, Nagoya, Osaka. Jump back and forth and you spend hours more on trains, so the stops in the [trip planner](/japan#planner) always follow that line, westbound or eastbound.

Your flights decide which direction. Tokyo has two big airports, and Tokyo Disney Resort gives about 50 minutes from Haneda to Maihama with two changes and about 80 minutes from Narita with one. At the other end of the line are Kansai and Itami airports near Osaka. Land at Haneda or Narita and fly home from Kansai, and the trip goes west. Land in Osaka and it goes east. If you fly in and out of Tokyo, you travel back from Osaka on the day you leave, and that day has no park in it.

The trip planner spreads the nights so that each park lands on a day with a low peak wait. If you've already booked a hotel in one city, you fix those nights and the planner spreads the others around them. It only suggests a change of hotel when that saves you more than a few minutes of queuing. For Fuji-Q Highland it rarely does, so the park usually stays a day trip from Tokyo. A night at Lake Kawaguchi joins the plan when it pays off in shorter waits, or when you pick the town yourself.

You can lay out a moving day like this:

- Move in the morning, park in the afternoon. Leave your hotel in Tokyo at 7:30 and, with the detour to the new hotel, you're at USJ around half past eleven, with the rest of the day for the park.
- Move in the evening, after the park. The day belongs to the park in the old city, the journey starts after it closes, and you have to arrive by 23:00.
- A pure travel day. From Fujikawaguchiko to Nagoya you take the bus to Mishima and then the Kodama, about three hours and 45 minutes door to door. No park fits next to that.

## Luggage and changing hotels

Send your suitcases ahead to the next hotel with a luggage service, takkyubin in Japanese, and board the train with just a backpack. The trip planner assumes you'll do that. On the Tokaido line, a bag of 161 to 250 centimetres in total, length, width and height added up, needs a seat with an oversized baggage area, which you reserve with your ticket. Without the reservation JR Central charges a fee, and anything over 250 centimetres can't come on board at all.

The best-known service is Yamato Transport's TA-Q-BIN. You don't need a reservation. You hand the suitcase in at your hotel's front desk, at a counter at the airport or a station, or at one of their offices, and if your hotel doesn't take luggage, you take it to the nearest office. Check with the next hotel beforehand that it will accept a bag for you. The earliest delivery is the next day, for suitcases handed in by around 18:00. You collect the case at the other end with ID or the receipt. For a morning move, that means the suitcase goes the evening before and you pack a small bag for the last night, as if you were staying over at a friend's.

If your suitcase has gone ahead, you check out and walk straight to the train, otherwise you drop it at the next hotel before heading to the park. The trip planner allows 30 minutes for each change of hotel. If you're carrying your cases yourself, I'd set it to 60 minutes, and with small children to 90 or 120. You pick the value in the options, and it applies to every move of the trip.

## Getting between the cities

Between Tokyo, Nagoya and Osaka you ride the Tokaido Shinkansen. On the timetable the Nozomi takes 96 to 99 minutes from Tokyo to Nagoya and 48 to 51 minutes on to Shin-Osaka. Door to door, with getting to the station and waiting for the train, it's two and a half hours from Tokyo to Nagoya, an hour and a half from Nagoya to Osaka and three and a half from Tokyo to Osaka, and those are the times the trip planner uses.

Besides the bus, you can reach Fuji-Q on the Fuji Excursion, a limited express that leaves Shinjuku at 7:30 and stops at Fujikyu-Highland Station at 9:26. The park entrance is right outside the ticket gate. The last Fuji Excursion back leaves there at 17:44, so if you stay until closing, you take the 20:23 bus.

Heading west from Fuji-Q you don't need to go back through Tokyo. Buses take just under an hour and a half to Mishima Station, where the Kodama to Nagoya stops. A direct bus links Lake Kawaguchi with Meitetsu's bus centre in Nagoya in about four and a half hours. For Osaka there's the Fujiyama Liner night bus, leaving Fuji-Q at 21:01 and arriving in Kyoto at 5:57 and in Umeda at 6:53. Booking opens two months before the trip, and you'll be in Osaka before breakfast, though also before you've slept in.

During Golden Week, Obon, Silver Week and over New Year, every seat on the Nozomi is reserved, in 2026–27 from 25 December to 5 January. In winter 2026–27 the long weekends of 9 to 11 January and 20 to 22 March are added. Without a reservation you'll stand in the aisle or on the deck between cars. Hikari and Kodama trains keep their unreserved cars on those days too.

With the Japan Rail Pass you only get on the Nozomi with an extra ticket, the NOZOMI MIZUHO Ticket, and you pay for the buses to Fuji-Q on top. After a rise in October 2023, the pass has cost more again from travel agencies abroad since 1 October 2026, while online from JR the price stays the same for now. If you go from Tokyo to Osaka once and take the bus to Fuji-Q, the pass rarely pays off, and I'd price up the single tickets before buying it.

## Tickets

### Tokyo Disney Resort

Tokyo Disney Resort doesn't sell multi-day tickets. You choose the park and the date when you buy the 1-Day Passport. For a later start there's the After 3 Passport, from 15:00 on weekends and holidays, and the After 5 Passport, from 17:00 on weekdays that aren't holidays. Neither gets you into the second park. For school and university students the resort sells the Campus Day Passport in 2027, valid for visits from 4 January to 19 March.

Each date goes on sale two months ahead at 14:00 Japan time, and popular days do sell out. The gate ticket booths are closed, so tickets are sold online and in the app. If you want a break at the hotel at lunchtime, tell a cast member at the exit and come back later on the same ticket.

### Universal Studios Japan

USJ sells every Studio Pass for a set date: the 1 Day, the 2 Day for two full days, the 1.5 Day with the first day from 15:00 and the second from opening, and the Twilight Pass from 15:00. Leave the park and the Studio Pass won't get you back in. USJ doesn't accept tickets from resale or auction sites.

### Fuji-Q Highland

Fuji-Q sells the One Day Pass for every ride except the timed-ticket ones, optionally with early admission or with a virtual queue in the park's app. Then there's the Afternoon Pass from 13:00, the Sunset Night Pass from 16:00 on days the park stays open past 19:00, and the Thomas Land Pass for the twelve rides in Thomas Land.

### LEGOLAND Japan

LEGOLAND Japan sells the 1DAY Passport, optionally together with SEA LIFE Nagoya, in which case both are for the same day. Bought online before the day it's cheaper than at the box office, where each ticket carries a fee. If you stay at the LEGOLAND Japan Hotel, a few minutes' walk from the gate, you get the Combo 2 Day Pass for your arrival day and the day after, with re-entry.

At all four, the price depends on the date. If you already have tickets, add them in the trip planner and the parks go on their days.

## Paying to skip the queue

At Tokyo Disneyland and DisneySea, the paid fast lane is called Disney Premier Access. You buy it in the app once you're inside the park, for one ride and one time, and you can book the next ride 60 minutes after buying or once the booked time has come, whichever is sooner. On a busy day, buy it as soon as you're through the gate, before it's gone.

USJ sells the Universal Express Pass for a date, as a package with a fixed set of rides, some at a set time. Only some packages include the timed entry to Super Nintendo World, so for a busy day buy one of those as soon as your date is fixed.

The Fuji-Q Premium Pass is a One Day Pass with early admission and a wristband that takes you through the priority entrance of twelve thrill rides as often as you like, until an hour before closing. It also comes with one timed ticket each for Senritsu Meikyu, the FUJIYAMA Walk and the FUJIYAMA Slider. On their own, the park sells priority tickets for one ride at a set time, online from 18:00 three days ahead.

LEGOLAND Japan sells the Skip Pass, either as a 3-pack for three rides that a group can share or as an UNLIMITED wristband for a whole day. Both work on about 25 rides, only on top of an admission ticket, and in limited numbers each day.

## Early entry with a hotel or a ticket

With Happy Entry, guests of the Disney Hotels get into Tokyo Disneyland 15 minutes before everyone else, through their own entrance. At Tokyo DisneySea only guests of Hotel MiraCosta and the Fantasy Springs Hotel get in early, and while building work goes on at the entrance it's 5 to 15 minutes. Happy Entry starts on the morning after check-in. Some hotels have blackout dates, listed on the resort's Happy Entry page, and the next one at Tokyo Disneyland is 12 January 2027.

![A row of Italian-style buildings in ochre and pink along a harbour basin, boats moored at the quay. | Hotel MiraCosta is built into this waterfront, so some of its rooms look straight onto the park.](/media/tokyo-disneysea/mediterranean-harbor.jpg)

The six Official Hotels at Bayside Station and the four Partner Hotels in Shin-Urayasu run shuttles to the parks but don't get Happy Entry. USJ's seven partner hotels are a few minutes' walk from the gate, and their guests can buy Studio Passes at the hotel for the check-in day and the day after.

Next to Fuji-Q is the Highland Resort Hotel & Spa, with its own passage into the park on the basement level. According to the hotel, its guests get in early, 15 minutes before opening on weekdays and 30 minutes on Saturdays, Sundays and holidays, on the day they check out too. During that time you can only ride with a One Day Pass or ride tickets. The trip planner sticks to 15 minutes at weekends and on holidays too, so the extra quarter of an hour on those days is a bonus.

Without the hotel, Fuji-Q sells early entry itself as the One Day Pass with Priority Admission, half an hour before opening in November 2026. Only the big coasters run in that half hour, and the pass isn't sold from December 2026 to 5 February 2027.

## When it gets busy

### Holidays and travel peaks

Golden Week is the run of holidays at the end of April and start of May. In 2027 that's Showa Day on Thursday 29 April and the three holidays from 3 to 5 May, which with the weekend before make five days off in a row. Obon isn't a public holiday, but it usually falls on 13 to 16 August, and Japan's tourism board JNTO counts it among the busiest and most expensive times of year to travel. Mountain Day falls just before it, on Wednesday 11 August 2027.

Government offices close by law from 29 December to 3 January. In September 2026 three holidays lined up to give five days off from 19 to 23 September, the Silver Week. There's none in 2027, because Respect for the Aged Day on 20 September and the equinox holiday on the 23rd are three days apart.

## Events and openings until summer 2027

### Tokyo Disney Resort

Disney Christmas runs at both parks until 25 December 2026, and the New Year programme until 11 January 2027. At Tokyo DisneySea, Sparkling Jubilee, the 25th anniversary, ends on 31 March 2027, and the evening projection Sparkling Jubilee Night ends with it. The night show Believe! Sea of Dreams isn't performed on 27 November 2026, from 6 January to 4 February, or on 19 February 2027. At Tokyo Disneyland, Pal-Palooza with Minnie's Funderland runs from 13 January to 31 March.

Sugar Rush: Sweet Rescue opens in Tokyo Disneyland's Tomorrowland on 15 April 2027, and Space Mountain, closed since July 2024, comes back during the year as Space Mountain Earthrise.

### Universal Studios Japan

The Discover U!!! anniversary year for the park's 25th birthday runs until 30 March 2027, and the Christmas programme Universal Holiday Wonders until 17 January. On 31 December 2026 day guests can only stay until 17:00. From 19:00 only Party Pass holders get in for the countdown.

Universal Cool Japan 2027 starts on 2 December 2026 with Sailor Moon, running until 5 April. Detective Conan World follows from 10 February to 30 June. Playing with Curious George closes for good after 28 February 2027.

### Fuji-Q Highland and LEGOLAND Japan

On New Year's Eve 2026 Fuji-Q stays open until 1:00, according to its calendar. From 4 December 2026 to 31 January 2027 the park marks 30 years of the Persona games. On FUJIYAMA, Takabisha, Eejanaika and Tondemina you'll hear newly recorded voices of Morgana and Teddie, music from the series plays around the park, and some of the buses from Shinjuku and the Fujikyu trains run in Persona wraps. The Ferris wheel is closed for renovation from 9 November to 31 January. Tentekomai stays closed until the end of July 2027, the park says, for the building of a new large attraction, and for summer 2027 Fuji-Q has announced a splash coaster next to Eejanaika: 44 metres tall, with three launches, forwards and backwards.

LEGOLAND Japan turns ten on 1 April 2027. Merry Christmas runs there until 25 December, and Kids' Countdown & Happy New Year from 31 December to 12 January.

## Rainy season, typhoons and summer heat

The rainy season, tsuyu, starts around 6 or 7 June and ends around 19 July on the Japan Meteorological Agency's 1991–2020 averages, at almost the same time in Tokyo and Osaka. In 2026 it lasted from 7 June to 20 July in the Tokyo region and from 4 June to 8 July around Osaka. The parks open as normal through those weeks, rain or no rain.

Typhoon season runs from June to November. A typhoon comes close most often in September, on average 1.2 times in the Tokyo region and 1.1 times around Osaka, and less often in August and October.

In August the average daily high is 31.3 °C in Tokyo, 33.7 °C in Osaka and Kyoto, and 33.2 °C in Nagoya. By Lake Kawaguchi at Fuji-Q the August high is 28.1 °C, and that alone would get me to book a night there.

## Frequently asked questions

### In which order should I travel through the cities?

East to west, Tokyo, Fujikawaguchiko, Nagoya, Osaka, or the other way round. If you land in Tokyo and fly home from Osaka, you travel west.

### Is a night in Fujikawaguchiko worth it?

Yes, if you're carrying on west from Fuji-Q or coming in August. As a day trip from Shinjuku, the bus gets you there in about 100 minutes, and the last bus leaves the park at 20:23.

### How long does a change of hotel take?

With your luggage sent ahead, the trip planner allows 30 minutes, and 60 is more realistic if you carry your own suitcase. You can choose 30, 60, 90 or 120 minutes.

### Are there multi-day tickets for Tokyo Disney?

No. You buy a 1-Day Passport for one of the two parks for each day, on sale from 14:00 Japan time two months before the visit.

### Is the Japan Rail Pass worth it?

Rarely for this trip. With the pass the Nozomi needs an extra ticket, you pay for the highway buses to Fuji-Q anyway, and since October 2026 the pass costs more when bought through a travel agency abroad.

### When do I hand in my suitcase so it reaches the next hotel?

The day before you move, by around 18:00. Yamato Transport delivers the next day at the earliest.

### Do hotel guests get early entry at Fuji-Q Highland?

Yes, at the Highland Resort Hotel & Spa next to the park. The hotel gives 15 minutes before opening on weekdays and 30 minutes on weekends and holidays.

### When are Tokyo Disney Resort and USJ at their quietest?

Since late December 2025, both Disney parks were quietest in September and USJ in July. Maihama was busiest in February and March.
