Saturday is the day with the longest waits at nine of the twelve parks we
measure. At Movie Park it ties with Tuesday and Wednesday. The quietest day is
usually a Thursday or Friday: Friday at Europa-Park, Heide Park, Movie Park,
Legoland and Disneyland Paris, Thursday at Phantasialand, Efteling and Walibi
Holland. Every weekday looks the same at Toverland, and at Plopsaland
Deutschland six days sit close together. At Walibi Holland, Saturday is even one
of the emptier days.

At most parks the wait in the first hour after opening is clearly shorter than
later on. Then the queues build, and at nine parks they peak between ten and
twelve. At eight parks the last or second-to-last hour is the quietest. At
Phantasialand and Heide Park the wait barely changes after the peak until
evening. At Legoland and Movie Park it gets busier again in the evening.

The quietest days for the month you're travelling are on the
[best time to visit](/best-time-to-visit) page. We compare the twelve parks
using the wait times park.fan has been collecting since 24 December 2025.

## Where the numbers come from

For every attraction with a live wait time, park.fan stores the posted wait
every five minutes. That's where the values on the park pages and in this post's
tables come from. The tables load the current values when you open the post.

As of 6 October 2026. For the weekday, we took every day a park was open and
averaged the waits at its big rides. For the hour, we averaged the waits at the
eight rides with the longest queues in each park, hour by hour, over that park's
opening hours. Waits are rounded to five minutes, and at many parks several
weekdays land on the same value. A "quietest day" therefore often means two or
three days are level. Where I name only one, it was also the quietest on the
finer crowd score in our statistics.

I analysed the school holidays for 13 April to 30 September 2026. The comparison
is between weekdays, Monday to Friday without a public holiday, that our calendar
marks as school holidays for the park, and weekdays without holidays. The
surcharge is the percentage by which the wait at the big rides was longer on
average. Weather and events aren't factored out. In our
[post on school holidays in Germany](/blog/school-holidays-theme-parks-germany)
we look at single holiday blocks like Easter and summer and compare them with
weekdays on which no German state at all had a holiday. The figures there aren't
directly comparable with the ones here.

Hansa-Park is missing because it only shows its wait times in its own app.

## The weekday at a glance

Europa-Park, Phantasialand, Efteling, Heide Park, Legoland, Parc Astérix and
Walibi Belgium have a weekend peak. Saturday is the busiest day there, and
Sunday is a little less busy or as busy as a weekday, depending on the park. The
quietest day is Wednesday, Thursday or Friday.

At three parks the weekday makes almost no difference. At Toverland you queue
the same time on average every day. At Plopsaland Deutschland six days are
equally busy, and only Saturday is a little busier. At Disneyland Paris the busiest and the quietest day are one rounding step apart.

At Walibi Holland Tuesday is the busiest day and Saturday one of the quieter
ones. At Movie Park Saturday, Tuesday and Wednesday are level at the top. But
Monday to Wednesday there are measured on only about 15 opening days, the
weekend on about 30.

## The hour at a glance

In the first hour after opening, the wait is clearly shorter than later at most
parks. At Plopsaland, Heide Park and Walibi Holland it's about half as long as
at the peak, or shorter. Then it jumps. At Europa-Park the peak comes at ten
o'clock, at Efteling, Parc Astérix and both Walibis at eleven, and at
Phantasialand, Heide Park, Plopsaland and Disneyland Paris at twelve.

At Disneyland Paris the wait in the quietest hour is only a good third as long
as in the busiest, at Parc Astérix a good two fifths, at Walibi Holland half and
at Europa-Park about six tenths. At Phantasialand the quietest hour is only
about a sixth shorter than the busiest, at Heide Park and Movie Park about a
seventh.

At Legoland Deutschland and Movie Park Germany the last hour we measure, 5 pm,
is one of the busiest. At Heide Park 5 pm is also the last hour we measure, but
there it doesn't get busier at the end.

## Europa-Park

At [Europa-Park](ref:europa-park) Saturday is clearly busier than the other
days, and the other six are close together. Friday is the quietest, followed
closely by Sunday, Wednesday and Thursday. If you want short queues, plan
Wednesday to Friday or a Sunday.

The longest waits come early, around ten o'clock. After that they get shorter
and stay about the same into the early evening. The quietest hour is the
second-to-last before closing, and in the last one it gets a little busier
again.

On a weekday in the holidays you queued about 11% longer than on a weekday in
term time, measured over 40 holiday days and 79 school days. That's the smallest
figure among the German parks. On a weekend in term time it was 16%.

Europa-Park opened on 12 July 1975. It was first run by Franz Mack and his son Roland.

## Phantasialand

[Phantasialand](ref:phantasialand) is busy every day, and least busy on
Thursday. Saturday is the busiest, followed by Sunday. Monday, Tuesday, Friday
and Wednesday sit in between and are hard to tell apart.

After the early climb until midday, the wait stays almost as long until closing.

A holiday weekday was about 65% busier than one in term time, and a weekend in
term time about 39%. Phantasialand is already full before the holidays start in
North Rhine-Westphalia.

Phantasialand was founded by the showman Gottlieb Löffelhardt and the puppeteer
Richard Schmidt, who opened it on 30 April 1967 as a fairy-tale park with around
40 stories around a lake.

## Efteling

At [Efteling](ref:efteling) the weekdays barely differ. Saturday and Sunday are
a little busier, and Monday sits in between. Thursday is the quietest day,
followed by Tuesday and Wednesday.

The peak is at eleven o'clock. It stays about as busy through midday and the
early afternoon, and then the crowds thin out. In the last hour, the quietest,
the wait is about seven tenths as long as at the peak.

In the holidays the wait at Efteling was only 11% longer on weekdays, measured
over 36 holiday and 84 school days. At the weekend in term time it was about 14%
more than on a weekday.

The fairy-tale forest opened on 31 May 1952, two years after the Stichting
Natuurpark de Efteling was founded.

## Heide Park

At [Heide Park](ref:heide-park) the holidays matter more than at any other park.
On a weekday in the holidays you queued about 84% longer than on a weekday in
term time, over 32 holiday days and 75 school days. On a weekend in term time it
was about 47%. Saturday is the busiest day and Friday the quietest. Sunday sits
between them, close to Friday.

The daily curve is one of the flattest, similar to Phantasialand. It's busiest around twelve o'clock and quietest at four in the afternoon. The opening times on the park's website are for the rides, and the park itself closes an hour later.

Hans-Jürgen Tiemann, from a family of showmen, opened Heide Park on 19 August 1978. The British Tussauds Group took it over on 31 December 2001, Merlin
Entertainments bought a majority of Tussauds in early 2007, and the park has
belonged to Merlin ever since.

## Movie Park Germany

At [Movie Park Germany](ref:movie-park-germany) Friday is clearly the day with
the shortest waits. Saturday, Tuesday and Wednesday are the busiest, but Tuesday
and Wednesday are measured on only about 15 to 16 opening days. After Friday, Thursday and Sunday are the quietest. Outside the [holidays](/blog/school-holidays-theme-parks-germany) the park is closed on many weekdays.

The daily curve has no morning peak. The wait stays about the same from eleven
to five. It's quietest in the afternoon, and in the last hour it's as busy again
as at midday.

In the holidays a weekday was about 54% busier than in term time, over 33
holiday and 47 school days. On a weekend in term time you queued only 15% longer
than on a weekday.

On 30 June 1996 the park opened as Warner Bros. Movie World, in 2005 it got its
current name, and since 2010 it has been run by the Spanish group Parques
Reunidos.

## Plopsaland Deutschland

At [Plopsaland Deutschland](ref:plopsaland-deutschland) in Haßloch six days of
the week are equally busy, and only Saturday is a little busier. Tuesday and
Friday are lowest on paper, but the gap is smaller than the five minutes we round
to. There's no "quietest day" here.

The first two hours are almost empty, then the wait rises towards twelve o'clock
and stays about the same until closing, at a very low level. In the quietest
hour it's a good half as long as in the busiest.

A weekday in the holidays was about 49% busier than one in term time, a weekend
in term time about 53%.

The Schneider family opened the park in 1971, and for a long time it was called
Holiday Park. On 3 November 2010 Studio 100 announced it had bought it, and on
28 June 2025 it was renamed Plopsaland Deutschland. In 2026 it's open every day of the season for the first time.

## Legoland Deutschland

At [Legoland Deutschland](ref:legoland-deutschland) in Günzburg Saturday is the
busiest day and Sunday the second busiest. Friday is the quietest, followed
closely by Wednesday and Thursday.

The wait rises until twelve o'clock and eases slightly at midday. It's longest
in the last hour we measure, at 5 pm, and the rides close an hour before the park does. The only time you'll queue briefly here is
right after opening.

In the holidays you queued about 54% longer on a weekday than on a weekday in
term time, and about 46% longer on a weekend in term time.

On 17 May 2002 Legoland Deutschland became the fourth Legoland park, after
Billund, Windsor and Carlsbad.

## Parc Astérix

At [Parc Astérix](ref:parc-asterix) near Paris Saturday is the busiest day and
Sunday the second busiest. Wednesday is the quietest, just ahead of Tuesday, then
come Thursday, Friday and Monday.

The park has the steepest daily curve after Disneyland Paris. It's busiest around eleven o'clock, and after that the wait sinks until the evening. The curve runs until 9 pm, and the last hour is the quietest. At 5 pm the
wait is still about two thirds as long as at the peak. If you're in the park
from 7 pm, you queue at the big rides for only about half as long as at eleven.

In the holidays you queued about 35% longer on weekdays, measured over 50
holiday and 51 school days, and about 32% on weekends in term time.

Albert Uderzo had the idea for the park in 1981, after a visit to Disneyland in
California, and on 30 April 1989 the first visitors came to Plailly.

## Toverland

At [Toverland](ref:attractiepark-toverland) in Sevenum you queue the same time
on average from Monday to Sunday.

You queue less at the big rides here than at most other parks in this
comparison. In the holidays the wait on weekdays was nevertheless about 27%
longer than in term time, over 31 holiday and 88 school days. A weekend in term time was about 22% above a weekday.

We show no daily curve for Toverland, because our values there run from midnight
to 11 pm, including hours when the park is closed.

On 19 May 2001 Toverland opened as a purely indoor park with one hall, aimed at
families with small children. The second hall and the outdoor area followed in 2004.

## Walibi Holland

At [Walibi Holland](ref:walibi-holland) in Biddinghuizen the order of the days is
almost reversed. The busiest day is Tuesday, followed by Sunday and Monday.
Saturday is one of the quieter days, and the quietest is Thursday. Tuesday and
Monday, though, are measured on only about 14 and 15 days, the other days on 21
to 28. So I'd take Tuesday as the busiest day with a pinch of salt.

On a weekend in term time the wait was 3% shorter than on a
weekday in term time. On a weekday in the holidays it was only about 1% longer.

It's busiest at eleven o'clock. After that the wait gets steadily shorter,
noticeably so from 4 pm, and in the last hour it's at its shortest.

The park started in 1971 as Flevohof, an educational park about farming. In
autumn 1993 the Walibi Group bought the bankrupt Flevohof, and on 7 May 1994 it
opened as Walibi Flevo. From 2000 it was called Six Flags Holland, from 2005
Walibi World, and since 2011 Walibi Holland.

## Walibi Belgium

At [Walibi Belgium](ref:walibi-belgium) in Wavre the order is clearer. Saturday
is the busiest day and Wednesday the quietest. Monday, Tuesday and Thursday sit
level between them, and Sunday comes just before Saturday. Wednesday is measured
on only about 16 days, Saturday on 29.

There's no holiday figure for Walibi Belgium, because our calendar has no school
holiday days for the park in the whole period. On a weekend in term time you
queued about 10% longer than on a weekday. That's less than at most of the other
parks.

Through the day it looks like Walibi Holland. The peak comes early, around
eleven o'clock, and then the wait gets steadily shorter. In the last hour it's a
little more than half as long as at the peak.

It was founded by the entrepreneur Eddy Meeùs and opened on 26 July 1975 as
Walibi. From 1982 the park was called Walibi Wavre, from 2001 to 2004 Six Flags
Belgium, and since 2006 it has belonged to Compagnie des Alpes.

## Disneyland Paris

At [Disneyland Park](ref:/parks/europe/france/paris/disneyland-park) in
Marne-la-Vallée the waits are the longest in this comparison. Saturday and Tuesday are the busiest, and Friday is
the quietest, followed closely by Wednesday, Sunday, Monday and Thursday.

In the holidays the wait on weekdays was only about 3% longer than in term time,
and on weekends in term time about 5%. I suspect the park draws visitors from
all over Europe and that somewhere there are always holidays. I can't prove it.

You queue longest around twelve o'clock, and the wait stays long into the early
evening. It's shortest in the last hour.

The park opened on 12 April 1992 as Euro Disneyland and has been called
Disneyland Park since March 2002.

Saturday, you're likely to queue longer than on a weekend in term time. At
Europa-Park, Efteling, Disneyland Paris and Walibi Holland neither holidays nor
weekends matter much.

## School holidays 2026/27 in neighbouring countries

In the Netherlands the northern region has its autumn holiday from 10 to 18
October, the central and southern regions from 17 to 25 October. In France the
All Saints' holiday starts on 17 October in all three zones, and school resumes
on 2 November. French-speaking schools in Belgium are off from 19 to 30 October.
Flanders only has its holiday from 2 to 8 November.

Christmas holidays are from 19 December to 3 January 2027 in the Netherlands,
from 21 December to 3 January in Flanders, and in France from 19 December until
school starts again on 4 January.

## Planning your trip

If you want to queue as little as possible, Plopsaland or
Toverland fits, because neither is busy on any day. If you're going to one of the
busy parks, Friday is the safest choice at Europa-Park or Heide Park, and
Thursday at Phantasialand and Efteling.

On a Saturday, pick a park where the difference is small: Walibi Holland, Disneyland Paris, Walibi Belgium or
Plopsaland Deutschland. At Phantasialand and Parc Astérix Saturday costs you the
most.

Before you book a weekday in the holidays, check the table
above to see how much the holidays matter at your park. The holiday dates of the
German states are in the
[school holidays post](/blog/school-holidays-theme-parks-germany).

Until the peak builds around ten to twelve,
you get through the big rides with shorter waits. If you stay into the evening,
you gain at Disneyland Paris, Parc Astérix, Europa-Park, Efteling and both
Walibis, but hardly at Phantasialand and Heide Park. You can split the hours with the [trip planner](/blog/trip-planner).

## Frequently asked questions

### Which day is quietest at a theme park?

At most parks Thursday or Friday. At Europa-Park, Heide Park, Movie Park,
Legoland and Disneyland Paris Friday was the quietest day, at Phantasialand,
Efteling and Walibi Holland Thursday, and at Parc Astérix and Walibi Belgium
Wednesday. At Toverland and Plopsaland Deutschland there's no quietest day.

### Is Sunday emptier than Saturday?

At most parks, yes. At Europa-Park, Heide Park and Movie Park Sunday is one of
the quieter days of the week. At Phantasialand, Efteling, Legoland and Parc
Astérix it's in second place behind Saturday.

### What's the best time of day at a theme park?

Right at opening, then late afternoon or evening. At most parks you queue longest
between ten and twelve. At parks that stay open late into the evening, the wait
after that drops to between a third and a half.

### Are school holidays worse than a Saturday?

At Heide Park, Phantasialand, Movie Park and Legoland yes, at Parc Astérix and
Toverland narrowly. There, holiday weekdays have longer waits on average than
weekends in term time. At Europa-Park, Efteling, Disneyland Paris and Walibi
Holland the difference matters little.

### Does this hold for other years?

We don't know yet. The figures cover 24 December 2025 to 5 October 2026. The
holiday values also depend on this year's holiday dates. We'll only know whether
the ranking of the weekdays stays the same once we've measured a second season.
