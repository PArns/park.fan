Saturday is the day with the longest waits at nine of the twelve parks we
measure. At Movie Park it ties with Tuesday and Wednesday. The quietest day is
usually a Thursday or Friday: Friday at Europa-Park, Heide Park, Movie Park,
Legoland and Disneyland Paris, Thursday at Phantasialand, Efteling and Walibi
Holland. Three parks don't fit the pattern. Every weekday looks the same at
Toverland, and at Plopsaland Deutschland six days sit close together. At Walibi
Holland, Saturday is even one of the emptier days.

The time of day has a pattern too. At most parks the wait in the first hour
after opening is clearly shorter than later on. Then the queues build, and at
nine parks they peak between ten and twelve. At eight parks the last or
second-to-last hour is the quietest. At Phantasialand and Heide Park the wait
barely changes after the peak until evening. At Legoland and Movie Park it gets
busier again in the evening.

The quietest days for the month you're travelling are on the
[best time to visit](/best-time-to-visit) page. We compare the twelve parks
using the wait times park.fan has been collecting since 24 December 2025.

## Where the numbers come from

For every attraction with a live wait time, park.fan stores the posted wait every
five minutes. That's where the values on the park pages and in this post's
tables come from. The tables load the current values when you open the post, so
I don't give any minutes in the text, only weekdays and ratios that don't change
from one day to the next.

As of 6 October 2026. For the weekday, we took every day a park was open and
averaged the waits at its big rides. For the hour, we averaged the waits at the
eight rides with the longest queues in each park, hour by hour, over that park's
opening hours. Waits are rounded to five minutes, and at many parks several
weekdays land on the same value. A "quietest day" therefore often means two or
three days are level. Where I name only one, it was also the quietest on the
finer crowd score in our statistics.

I analysed the school holidays for 13 April to 30 September 2026. The comparison
is between weekdays, Monday to Friday without a public holiday, that our calendar
marks as school holidays for the park, and weekdays without holidays. The
surcharge is the percentage by which the wait at the big rides was longer on
average. Weather and events aren't factored out. In our
[post on school holidays in Germany](/blog/school-holidays-theme-parks-germany)
we look at single holiday blocks like Easter and summer and compare them with
weekdays on which no German state at all had a holiday. The figures there aren't
directly comparable with the ones here.

Hansa-Park is missing because it only shows its wait times in its own app.

## The weekday at a glance

The twelve parks fall into three groups.

The first group has a weekend peak: Europa-Park, Phantasialand, Efteling, Heide
Park, Legoland, Parc Astérix and Walibi Belgium. Saturday is the busiest day
there, and Sunday is a little less busy or as busy as a weekday, depending on the
park. The quietest day is Wednesday, Thursday or Friday.

The second group is parks where the weekday explains almost nothing. At
Toverland you queue the same time on average every day. At Plopsaland
Deutschland six days are equally busy, and only Saturday is a little busier. At
Disneyland Paris there are just five minutes between the busiest and the
quietest day: Saturday and Tuesday are the busiest, Friday the quietest. Waits at
these parks are similar every day, and at Plopsaland and Toverland they're short
as well.

The third group is parks that scramble the ranking. At Walibi Holland Tuesday is
the busiest day and Saturday one of the quieter ones. At Movie Park Saturday,
Tuesday and Wednesday are level at the top. But Monday to Wednesday there are
measured on only about 15 opening days, the weekend on about 30.

## The hour at a glance

The curve through the day looks similar at most parks. In the first hour after
opening, the wait is clearly shorter than later at most parks. At Plopsaland,
Heide Park and Walibi Holland it's about half as long as at the peak, or shorter.
Then it jumps. At Europa-Park the peak comes at ten o'clock, at Efteling, Parc
Astérix and both Walibis at eleven, and at Phantasialand, Heide Park, Plopsaland
and Disneyland Paris at twelve. After that the curve falls, slowly at some parks
and fast at others.

The parks differ in how far it falls. At Disneyland Paris the wait in the
quietest hour is only a good third as long as in the busiest, at Parc Astérix a
good two fifths, at Walibi Holland half and at Europa-Park about six tenths. At
Phantasialand the quietest hour is only about a sixth shorter than the busiest,
at Heide Park and Movie Park about a seventh. Waiting for the evening there saves
you very little.

Two parks run against the pattern. At Legoland Deutschland and Movie Park
Germany the last hour we measure, 5 pm, is one of the busiest. At Heide Park 5 pm
is also the last hour we measure, but there it doesn't get busier at the end.

## Europa-Park

[Europa-Park](ref:europa-park) is a Saturday park. Saturday is clearly busier
than the other days, and the other six are close together. Friday is the
quietest, followed closely by Sunday, Wednesday and Thursday, so Sunday is one of
the emptier days of the week here. If you want short queues, plan Wednesday to
Friday or a Sunday.

The longest waits come early, around ten o'clock. After that they get shorter
and stay about the same into the early evening. The quietest hour is the
second-to-last before closing, and in the last one it gets a little busier
again.

Holidays matter little at Europa-Park. On a weekday in the holidays you queued
about 11% longer than on a weekday in term time, measured over 40 holiday days
and 79 school days. That's the smallest figure among the German parks. On a
weekend in term time it was 16%, a bit more. Which rides to do first is in the
[Europa-Park guide](/blog/europa-park-wait-times-tips).

## Phantasialand

[Phantasialand](ref:phantasialand) is busy every day, and least busy on
Thursday. Saturday is the busiest, followed by Sunday. Monday, Tuesday, Friday
and Wednesday sit in between and are hard to tell apart. The gap between
Saturday and Thursday is among the biggest in this comparison.

The daily curve is flat. After the early climb until midday, the wait stays
almost as long until closing. In the quietest hour it's only about a sixth
shorter than in the busiest. If you wait for the evening at Phantasialand, you
won't get an empty ride.

Only at Heide Park do the holidays matter even more. A holiday weekday was about
65% busier than one in term time, and a weekend in term time about 39%. So the
holidays fill the park more than a weekend does. That fits our
[school holidays post](/blog/school-holidays-theme-parks-germany): Phantasialand
is already full before the holidays start in North Rhine-Westphalia. How to cope
with it anyway is in the [Phantasialand guide](/blog/phantasialand-wait-times-tips).

## Efteling

At [Efteling](ref:efteling) the weekdays barely differ. Saturday and Sunday are a
little busier, and Monday sits in between. Thursday is the quietest day,
followed by Tuesday and Wednesday. A Monday at Efteling is a little busier than a
Tuesday.

The peak is at eleven o'clock. It stays about as busy through midday and the
early afternoon, and then the crowds thin out. In the evening the wait gets
clearly shorter: in the last hour, the quietest, it's about seven tenths as long
as at the peak.

In the holidays the wait at Efteling was only 11% longer on weekdays, measured
over 36 holiday and 84 school days. At the weekend in term time it was about 14%
more than on a weekday. The rides and the order to do them in are in the
[Efteling guide](/blog/efteling-disney-of-the-netherlands).

## Heide Park

At [Heide Park](ref:heide-park) the holidays matter more than at any other park.
On a weekday in the holidays you queued about 84% longer than on a weekday in
term time, over 32 holiday days and 75 school days. On a weekend in term time it
was about 47%. So in the holidays the park can be busier midweek than on a
weekend day in term time. Otherwise too, Saturday is the busiest day and Friday
the quietest. Sunday sits between them, close to Friday.

The daily curve is one of the flattest, similar to Phantasialand. It's busiest
around twelve o'clock. In the quietest hour, at four in the afternoon, the wait
is only about a seventh shorter. At Heide Park too you gain little in the
evening. What to start with is in the
[Heide Park guide](/blog/heide-park-wait-times-tips).

## Movie Park Germany

At [Movie Park Germany](ref:movie-park-germany) Friday is clearly the day with
the shortest waits. Saturday, Tuesday and Wednesday are the busiest, but Tuesday
and Wednesday are measured on only about 15 to 16 opening days. So I name the
quietest day with caution: Friday, then Thursday and Sunday. Outside the
holidays the park is closed on many weekdays, as our
[school holidays post](/blog/school-holidays-theme-parks-germany) also says.

The daily curve has no morning peak. The wait stays about the same from eleven
to five. It's quietest in the afternoon, and in the last hour it's as busy again
as at midday.

In the holidays a weekday was about 54% busier than in term time, over 33 holiday
and 47 school days. On a weekend in term time you queued only 15% longer than on
a weekday. So at Movie Park a holiday weekday matters clearly more than a
weekend. Details on rides and Halloween operation are in the
[Movie Park guide](/blog/movie-park-germany-wait-times-tips).

## Plopsaland Deutschland

At [Plopsaland Deutschland](ref:plopsaland-deutschland) in Haßloch six days of
the week are equally busy, and only Saturday is a little busier. Tuesday and
Friday are lowest on paper, but the gap is smaller than the five minutes we round
to. There's no "quietest day" here.

Through the day you can see even more clearly how little goes on here. The first
two hours are almost empty, then the wait rises towards twelve o'clock and stays
about the same until closing, at a very low level. In the quietest hour it's a
good half as long as in the busiest.

The holidays, on the other hand, matter a lot. A weekday in the holidays was
about 49% busier than one in term time, a weekend in term time about 53%. A
holiday weekday at Plopsaland is therefore about as busy as a weekend day in term
time. How that ties in with the holidays in Rhineland-Palatinate and North
Rhine-Westphalia is in the
[school holidays post](/blog/school-holidays-theme-parks-germany).

## Legoland Deutschland

[Legoland Deutschland](ref:legoland-deutschland) in Günzburg is a weekend park.
Saturday is the busiest day and Sunday the second busiest. Friday is the
quietest, followed closely by Wednesday and Thursday. From Wednesday to Friday
you queue for the shortest time there.

The wait rises until twelve o'clock and eases slightly at midday. It's longest
in the last hour we measure, at 5 pm. The only time you'll queue briefly here is
right after opening.

In the holidays you queued about 54% longer on a weekday than on a weekday in
term time, and about 46% longer on a weekend in term time. A holiday weekday at
Legoland is therefore busier than a weekend day in term time, though by a
narrower margin than at Heide Park.

## Parc Astérix

At [Parc Astérix](ref:parc-asterix) near Paris Saturday is the busiest day and
Sunday the second busiest. Wednesday is the quietest, just ahead of Tuesday, then
come Thursday, Friday and Monday. The gap between Saturday and Wednesday is one
of the larger ones in this comparison.

The park has the steepest daily curve after Disneyland Paris. It's busiest
around eleven o'clock, and by the evening the wait sinks to a good two fifths of
that. The curve runs until 9 pm, and the last hour is the quietest. At 5 pm the
wait is still about two thirds as long as at the peak. If you're in the park
from 7 pm, you queue at the big rides for only about half as long as at eleven.

In the holidays you queued about 35% longer on weekdays, measured over 50
holiday and 51 school days. On a weekend in term time it was about 32%. The
order to do the rides in is in the
[Parc Astérix guide](/blog/parc-asterix-wait-times-tips).

## Toverland

At [Toverland](ref:attractiepark-toverland) in Sevenum you queue the same time
on average from Monday to Sunday. There's no quietest day and no busiest. By our
measurements it makes no difference whether you go on a Tuesday or a Saturday.

You queue less at the big rides here than at most other parks in this
comparison. In the holidays the wait on weekdays was nevertheless about 27%
longer than in term time, over 31 holiday and 88 school days. On a weekend in
term time it was about 22%.

There's no daily curve for Toverland: our values there run from midnight to 11
pm, including hours when the park is closed. The individual rides are in the
[Toverland guide](/blog/toverland-troy-wait-times-tips).

## Walibi Holland

At [Walibi Holland](ref:walibi-holland) in Biddinghuizen the order of the days is
almost reversed. The busiest day is Tuesday, followed by Sunday and Monday.
Saturday is one of the quieter days, and the quietest is Thursday. Tuesday and
Monday, though, are measured on only about 14 and 15 days, the other days on 21
to 28. So I'd take Tuesday as the busiest day with a pinch of salt.

If you go to Walibi Holland on a Saturday, you don't queue longer than on a
weekday. On a weekend in term time the wait was even 3% shorter than on a
weekday in term time. On a weekday in the holidays it was only about 1% longer.
Neither holidays nor weekends make a difference there.

It's busiest at eleven o'clock. After that the wait gets steadily shorter,
noticeably so from 4 pm, and in the last hour it's half as long as at the peak.
More on the park is in the
[Walibi Holland guide](/blog/walibi-holland-untamed-hard-gaan).

## Walibi Belgium

At [Walibi Belgium](ref:walibi-belgium) in Wavre the order is clearer. Saturday
is the busiest day and Wednesday the quietest. Monday, Tuesday and Thursday sit
level between them, and Sunday comes just before Saturday. Wednesday is measured
on only about 16 days, Saturday on 29.

There's no holiday figure for Walibi Belgium, because our calendar has no school
holiday days for the park in the whole period. On a weekend in term time you
queued about 10% longer than on a weekday. That's less than at most of the other
parks.

Through the day it looks like Walibi Holland. The peak comes early, around
eleven o'clock, and then the wait gets steadily shorter. In the last hour it's a
little more than half as long as at the peak. The rides and the best order are in
the [Walibi Belgium guide](/blog/walibi-belgium-wait-times-tips).

## Disneyland Paris

At [Disneyland Park](ref:/parks/europe/france/paris/disneyland-park) in
Marne-la-Vallée the waits are the longest in this comparison. The weekday makes
almost no difference there. Saturday and Tuesday are the busiest, and Friday is
the quietest, followed closely by Wednesday, Sunday, Monday and Thursday. There
are five minutes between the busiest and the quietest day, the smallest step we
round to.

In the holidays the wait on weekdays was only about 3% longer than in term time,
and on weekends in term time about 5%. I suspect the park draws visitors from
all over Europe and that somewhere there are always holidays. I can't prove it.

The time of day, on the other hand, matters a lot here. You queue longest around
twelve o'clock, and the wait stays long into the early evening. In the last hour
it's only a good third as long, the widest gap in this comparison. How to split
the day is in the [Disneyland Paris guide](/blog/disneyland-paris-wait-times-tips).

## School holidays against term time

The table shows how much longer you queued on a holiday weekday and on a weekend
in term time than on a weekday in term time.

| Park                   | Holiday weekday, surcharge | Weekend in term time, surcharge |

| Heide Park             |                       84 % |                            47 % |
| Phantasialand          |                       65 % |                            39 % |
| Movie Park Germany     |                       54 % |                            15 % |
| Legoland Deutschland   |                       54 % |                            46 % |
| Plopsaland Deutschland |                       49 % |                            53 % |
| Parc Astérix           |                       35 % |                            32 % |
| Toverland              |                       27 % |                            22 % |
| Europa-Park            |                       11 % |                            16 % |

| Disneyland Paris       |                        3 % |                             5 % |
| Walibi Holland         |                        1 % |                            −3 % |
| Walibi Belgium         |    no holidays in calendar |                            10 % |

The period is 13 April to 30 September 2026, and each value rests on 24 to 88
days. The figures come from a single season. Part of the holidays fall in the
summer months that are busy anyway, so holidays and season blur together. A
comparison within one park is therefore sturdier than one between two parks.

At Heide Park, Phantasialand, Movie Park and Legoland the holidays matter more
than the weekend: 54 to 84 percent on holiday weekdays, only 15 to 47 on weekends
in term time. At Parc Astérix and Toverland the holiday weekday is just above
the weekend. If you take a Tuesday in the holidays at these parks to avoid
Saturday, you're likely to queue longer than on a weekend in term time. At
Europa-Park, Efteling, Disneyland Paris and Walibi Holland neither holidays nor
weekends matter much. There the choice of day brings little and the evening
brings more: at Disneyland Paris and Walibi Holland the wait then drops to a
third to a half of the peak, at Europa-Park and Efteling to about six to seven
tenths.

## Planning your trip

**The park first.** If you want to queue as little as possible, Plopsaland or
Toverland fits, because neither is busy on any day. If you're going to one of the
busy parks, Friday is the safest choice at Europa-Park or Heide Park, and
Thursday at Phantasialand and Efteling.

**Then the day.** If Saturday is the only day you can go, pick a park where the
difference is small: Walibi Holland, Disneyland Paris, Walibi Belgium or
Plopsaland Deutschland. At Phantasialand and Parc Astérix Saturday costs you the
most.

**Then the holidays.** Before you book a weekday in the holidays, check the table
above to see how much the holidays matter at your park. The holiday dates of the
German states are in the
[school holidays post](/blog/school-holidays-theme-parks-germany).

**Then the hour.** Arrive at opening: until the peak builds around ten to twelve,
you get through the big rides with shorter waits. If you stay into the evening,
you gain at Disneyland Paris, Parc Astérix, Europa-Park, Efteling and both
Walibis, but hardly at Phantasialand and Heide Park. How to split the hours is in
the post on the [trip planner](/blog/trip-planner).

## Frequently asked questions

### Which day is quietest at a theme park?

At most parks Thursday or Friday. At Europa-Park, Heide Park, Movie Park,
Legoland and Disneyland Paris Friday was the quietest day, at Phantasialand,
Efteling and Walibi Holland Thursday, and at Parc Astérix and Walibi Belgium
Wednesday. At Toverland and Plopsaland Deutschland there's no quietest day.

### Is Sunday emptier than Saturday?

At most parks, yes. At Europa-Park, Heide Park and Movie Park Sunday is one of
the quieter days of the week. At Phantasialand, Efteling, Legoland and Parc
Astérix it's in second place behind Saturday.

### What's the best time of day at a theme park?

Right at opening, then late afternoon or evening. At most parks you queue longest
between ten and twelve. At parks that stay open late into the evening, the wait
after that drops to between a third and a half.

### Are school holidays worse than a Saturday?

At Heide Park, Phantasialand, Movie Park and Legoland yes, at Parc Astérix and
Toverland narrowly. There, holiday weekdays have longer waits on average than
weekends in term time. At Europa-Park, Efteling, Disneyland Paris and Walibi
Holland the difference matters little.

### Does this hold for other years?

We don't know yet. The figures cover 24 December 2025 to 5 October 2026. The
holiday values also depend on this year's holiday dates. We'll only know whether
the ranking of the weekdays stays the same once we've measured a second season.
