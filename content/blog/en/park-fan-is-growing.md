This screenshot from Google Search Console has been sitting on my computer for a
few days now, and I’ve looked at it more often than I’d like to admit. The
bottom badge and the top one are three weeks apart, and in those three weeks
autumn has arrived.

In the photo at the very top, which I took at Phantasialand on 27 September, the
first leaves are already turning yellow. Lots of parks are running
[Halloween](/blog/halloween-theme-parks-2026) now,
[Europa-Park](ref:europa-park) with more than 180,000 pumpkins until 1 November,
and the first ones are about to take their winter break,
[Heide Park](ref:heide-park) after 31 October and
[Walibi Holland](ref:walibi-holland) until April. Phantasialand, in turn, switches
on the lights of [Wintertraum](/blog/winter-theme-parks-2026) on 14 November.

## How park.fan started

A little over three years ago I spent sixty minutes in the queue for
[Taron](ref:phantasialand/taron) at Phantasialand with no idea whether I was
simply trading lifetime for a handrail. That turned into
[a script only I used](/blog/welcome-to-park-fan-blog), then a data collector
for two hundred parks, and in between came a lot of nights staring at wait-time
curves at two in the morning because yet another park reported its data
differently from everybody else.

park.fan is free and will stay that way, because these parks have given me more
than I ever paid them at the gate. Part of that is a stroller by the Märchensee,
and the gondola rides through “1001 Nacht” with my father beside me. park.fan is
my way of giving some of it back, and for a long time I wasn’t sure anyone would
take me up on it.

## Who visits park.fan now

According to our stats in Umami, around 24,000 visitors opened park.fan in the
last 30 days, 41 percent more than in the 30 days before, and a few hundred of
them were in Japan. I’ll never meet most of them, but I like to picture where
they are. Some sit on the sofa with the school holiday calendar and look for the
[quietest days at their park](/best-time-to-visit). Others
[plan their park day the evening before](/trip-planner), and a few might be in
Orlando, where I was myself in May, in front of a queue that’s longer than they
expected, and they open the [compass](/blog/in-park-compass) to see what’s
shorter nearby. I build a little more for them every evening.

On Google, the search that brings in the most clicks is, of all things, “six
flags wait times”, and most of those clicks land on the page for
[Six Flags Great Adventure](ref:six-flags-great-adventure) in New Jersey, a good
6,000 kilometres from Taron as the crow flies. I’d never have guessed that.

Right behind it comes “hansa park wartezeiten”, German for “Hansa Park wait
times”, even though that’s exactly where park.fan can’t show live wait times.
[Hansa-Park](ref:hansa-park) only gives them out in its own app, on the park’s
Wi-Fi. If you’re in the park, you’ll find them in the
[Hansa-Park app](https://www.hansapark.de/app/).

Counting every visitor, not just the ones from Google, the most-read page was
our news post on the
[search for a buyer for Parques Reunidos](/news/parques-reunidos-sale-movie-park).
Movie Park belongs to Parques Reunidos, and that’s where, back when it was still
Warner Bros. Movie World, I rode my first ever roller coaster with my mother,
Lethal Weapon Pursuit. I was glad that this park of all parks had the most
readers, even if the story is about a possible sale.

Searches for Phantasialand wait times now bring in more than three times as many
clicks as in the four weeks before.

## Thank you

Thank you all, whether you opened park.fan once before your summer holiday or
every morning before rope drop. Thanks to everyone who passed park.fan on.
Facebook alone sent around 1,400 visitors in those 30 days. And thanks to
[@pupilbox](https://www.instagram.com/pupilbox/) for the logo.

## Feedback

I haven’t had a single message from a reader yet, and I’d be really glad to get
the first one. With more than 200 parks in six languages, a fair bit slips past
me. Maybe an opening time for your park is wrong, a ride is missing, a forecast
was way off one Saturday, or a sentence reads awkwardly in Italian. If so, write
to me, by email to the address in the [imprint](/impressum) or on Instagram at
[@part_82](https://www.instagram.com/part_82/), where my park photos end up too.
I read every message myself.

Write to me as well if something’s missing for you, a park, a feature or a topic
for the blog. Feedback is always welcome, criticism included. And if you take
photos in the park, you can upload them under [Share your photos](/contribute).
Maybe one of them will be on a park page soon.

— Patrick
