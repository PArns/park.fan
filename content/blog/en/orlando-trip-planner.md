The reason is Mickey's Very Merry Christmas Party. It starts at 7 pm that evening at [Magic Kingdom](ref:magic-kingdom-park), and it needs a ticket of its own. On the Monday before there's no party, and the park stays open to everyone until 10 pm.

For days like that, [park.fan/orlando](/orlando) now has a trip planner. You say when you're in Orlando and which parks you want to see. For ten days from 5 to 14 December 2026, Magic Kingdom then gets Saturday the 12th, one of the five days without a party. Monday goes to Epic Universe.

## Every Orlando park in one calendar

The same page puts Walt Disney World, Universal with Epic Universe, SeaWorld, their water parks and three day trips into one calendar, 16 parks in November 2026. Each square is one park on one day, and the first column is today in Orlando. The number in it is the peak wait, the minutes we expect at the park's big rides at the busiest time of day, and that's what you compare parks by. The colour is set another way, by comparing the day with a typical day at the same park, which is why a park with longer queues can still be green.

A folded corner means the park closes early for day guests, at Magic Kingdom for Mickey's Very Merry Christmas Party and at Hollywood Studios for Disney Jollywood Nights. Hatched squares are parks closed for the season, in winter 2026/27 Typhoon Lagoon and Volcano Bay among them.

![The Orlando Crowd Calendar with 16 rows for the Disney, Universal and SeaWorld parks and the day trips, the key above them, one square per day with the peak wait. | On 20 November, Animal Kingdom gets the frame at 45, not Magic Kingdom at 40, because Magic Kingdom closes at 6 pm that evening for the party. | wide](/media/reiseplaner/orlando-kalender-en.webp)

The page opens with the question "Which park on which day?" and the answer right below it: the best park at each resort today in Orlando time, early closings counted, and the quietest days until the calendar ends. Four chapters follow, 01 the calendar, 02 the trip planner, 03 the best time to go and 04 questions about the crowds. Tap 8 December in the calendar and you get the party with its start time, plus Early Entry, the drive and the big rides. "Open in the day planner" takes you to the [day planner](/trip-planner) with the park and the date filled in.

Without traffic, EPCOT to Universal Studios is a 25-minute drive, LEGOLAND in Winter Haven just under an hour, and Busch Gardens and Adventure Island in Tampa about an hour and a half. Strictly speaking you don't drive to Magic Kingdom at all. You drive to its car park at the Transportation and Ticket Center, and from there it's another quarter of an hour by monorail or ferry, which is built into every trip there.

The three best-time pages for [Orlando](/orlando/best-time-to-visit), [Disney World](/orlando/best-time-to-visit/disney-world) and [Universal Orlando](/orlando/best-time-to-visit/universal-orlando) open on the three quietest ten-day trips of 2027, and every September they move on a year. For Orlando as a whole, that's 12 to 21 April, 26 October to 4 November and 8 to 17 May. February and March aren't on the list: in 2026, we measured the year's longest queues there after December's. The pages' links open the trip planner straight inside the school holidays of your state or region.

## What the plan takes into account

The planner first asks for nothing but your dates, up to 21 days. Under each date it shows the park with the shortest peak wait, overall or, with the switch above, only at Disney or only at Universal. The parks are already picked, the big seven: Magic Kingdom, EPCOT, Hollywood Studios and Animal Kingdom at Disney, and Universal Studios, Islands of Adventure and Epic Universe at Universal. As soon as you tap your departure date, the plan is there. Each park says "1–2 days", so it gets a second day where your trip has room for one. Hotel, hotel area, rest days and park hopping sit under "More options".

Each day is weighed by the hours you actually get as a day guest. A party day that sends you out at 6 pm instead of 10 pm weighs up to one and a half times as much as a full day with the same waits. It only comes up when its waits are short enough to make up for the missing hours. Disney After Hours starts after the regular close, so the day loses no hours to it. Set "Magic Kingdom fireworks" under "More options" to "I want to see them", and a party day only comes up when nothing else fits.

The hotel counts too. A Disney hotel brings Early Entry, a Disney deluxe hotel Extended Evening Hours on top, and a Universal hotel Early Park Admission. The Universal Premier hotels, which are the Royal Pacific, the Hard Rock and the Portofino Bay, include Express at Universal Studios and Islands of Adventure, and on those days the queues there count for a lot less.

With a Park Hopper, two parks end up on one day when your trip has fewer days than park visits and the two are under 30 minutes apart door to door. When a park closes early, you get an idea for the evening: a second park if you have a hopper, otherwise Disney Springs or CityWalk, which are free to enter. Rest days fall on the busiest days of your trip, and you won't get more than four park days in a row unless your parks don't fit any other way. On a rest day you don't queue anywhere, or at least nowhere we measure.

Under the plan sits one line of minutes: the peak wait on your days, and how much shorter that is than the average of your trip days. For the ten December days, it's about 44 minutes, five less than the average. Open "How accurate is this?" right after it and you see how far a forecast for one ride on one day can be off, about 17 minutes here. The two numbers share a line on purpose. Three minutes a ride is also the line below which all you're told is that the order barely matters. A bigger number would have sold better.

You can view the plan as an agenda, a week or a month. You can pin a day or swap it with another one, and before you swap, you see how many minutes it costs you. Single days or the whole trip go into the day planner, into a calendar file in Orlando time, or out as a link to exactly this plan.

## When your dates are still open

Under the date calendar, "No dates yet? Find the best time" takes you to the search. There you pick a trip length of 3 to 21 days and a period: the next 12 months, a year, a spring, summer or autumn, or dates of your own. Back come the three best stretches, each with its peak wait and how far that is below the period's average. With "Only in my school holidays" ticked, only stretches that lie entirely inside your region's school holidays are left, and you see how far our holiday dates reach. "Plan the trip on these dates" turns a stretch into a plan.

Heat and the hurricane season show up as short notes on each stretch. A day counts as hot from a forecast of 35 °C, and further ahead, where there's no forecast yet, from a long-term average of 31.5 °C. The hurricane season runs from 1 June to 30 November, with its peak from mid-August to mid-October. A hurricane doesn't show up in any wait time, so during the season we add up to eight minutes per park day, for the ranking only. That's why, on the Orlando page, the stretch from 26 October sits behind April even though it's at 42 minutes and April at 43.

If a stretch touches the peak on even one day, another ten minutes go on per park visit. Without them, late August would come first: on the waits we measured in 2026, it's Orlando's quietest stretch, at 34 minutes a park day against 42 to 43 for the best stretches outside the peak. A stretch in September therefore ends up behind an equally busy one in May and is marked "ranked lower", while its minutes stay as they are. The date calendar and the month view mark the hurricane season in violet, dashed, and solid during the peak.

## Tickets you already have

The planner no longer opens with tickets. Under the plan it says "Already have tickets? Add them", and that's where you pick them by name for Disney, Universal, SeaWorld or LEGOLAND. US tickets include Disney's date-based ticket for 1 to 10 days and Universal's for 1 to 5 days, both with or without the hopper, and at Universal, Epic Universe only comes in from three days. If you're not sure which one you have, the plan uses the strictest version of that length.

The 14-day tickets sold outside the US are in the list too, tagged "Sold outside the US". Disney's 14-Day Magic Ticket is good for 14 park days within 18 days, park hopping and water parks included. Universal's 14-day ticket is valid every day for 14 days in a row, with park-to-park and Epic Universe. So on the Universal ticket, the day by the pool counts too.

The Disney and Universal Combo Ticket, as Attraction Tickets sells it, fills in both at once, and one first day counts for both. If you arrive between 20 October 2026 and 31 March 2027, the Universal half is the Three Park Adventure Ticket, without Volcano Bay, because the water park is closed until the end of March.

![Under the plan, the "Your tickets" card with the 14-Day Magic Ticket, used on 8 of its 14 days, and the Three Park Adventure Ticket, and below it the open ticket step with both tickets, tagged "Sold outside the US", each with its first day and dates. | A Combo Ticket starting Saturday 6 February 2027. Leave the first day open and the plan picks the start that fits best. | wide](/media/reiseplaner/tickets-uk-en.webp)

With 6 February as the first day, the Disney ticket runs to 23 February and the Universal one to the 19th. Both windows sit as bars under the days in the calendar, and every park day stays inside its ticket's window. With the big seven, that's eight of the 14 Disney days in those 18 days, two per park. They don't need more. A water-park day doesn't come off those days on the Magic Ticket. Disney and Universal days only get mixed where that saves the same three minutes a ride.

On days when a park hasn't published its opening hours yet, the plan has no hours for it, and the block says "no hours yet". For a trip that's still months away, that's often every day. Open a saved link again later and it plans with whatever hours have come out by then.

## No tickets yet

If you don't add any, the plan comes with a suggestion of which tickets you need. For the ten December days with the big seven, it reads: 3 days Universal from Sunday 6 December, then 4 days Disney from Thursday the 10th.

A four-day Disney ticket is good on any four of the seven days from its first, a three-day Universal ticket on any three of five. Under "Ticket details" you also see what a hopper would add, and on which date. "Worth adding" only appears where it opens up an evening in a second park, fits in a park that otherwise wouldn't fit, or saves the same three minutes a ride. In December it's a single evening. On the 13th, Animal Kingdom closes at 6 pm, and with a Park Hopper you can head over to Hollywood Studios afterwards. I'd take it for that evening: 6 pm in Orlando is too early for me to call it a day. For the three Universal days, park-to-park doesn't save a minute, it only gets you onto the Hogwarts Express.

You'll have to look up the extra cost yourself, though, because the planner shows no prices. Instead, each ticket comes with links to where you buy it: the cheapest authorised seller we found, Attraction Tickets and the park's own shop, with the pages in your language first. If one of them is a partner link, it's marked as one, and if you buy through it, park.fan earns a commission and your price stays the same. Because the trip is ten days long, there's also a line about the 14-day tickets sold outside the US, including the Combo.

## "Disney day 2 of 4" in the week view

Take both suggestions as your tickets, add the Park Hopper on the Disney one, and the week view lays the plan on the same hour axis as the day planner. A block runs from opening to the time you have to leave as a day guest, so for Animal Kingdom on Sunday the 13th it ends at 6 pm. Above each column you see which day of your ticket it is.

On this trip, Universal comes first. It starts on Sunday with Islands of Adventure, and the evening at CityWalk is dashed because it isn't a park. Friday at Hollywood Studios is "Disney day 2 of 4". The arrow takes you to the last three days: Saturday at Magic Kingdom is "Disney day 3 of 4" and runs from 9 am to 10 pm, and on Sunday, Animal Kingdom is followed after a 17-minute drive by the evening at Hollywood Studios. On a party day, the time after the block would be hatched, with the party's name and "separate ticket". Nothing is hatched, because Magic Kingdom is on the 12th and not the 8th.

## "Plan" in the header

The menu at the top has a new entry, "Plan", with the trip planner by region, the day planner and quick starts for popular parks. So far the only region is Orlando. Japan, Southern California and Paris will show up there once their pages are ready.

On the picture for Orlando, Parky is already on holiday, on a beach with sunglasses, a suitcase and a map with a route drawn on it. He probably planned that route in the chat, since he can do that now. "Plan a day" takes you into the day planner, and if you already have a plan there, "Open my plan" takes you straight to it.

Under "Plan a day at" you'll find six parks, the ones opened most often on park.fan in your language, in English Fuji-Q Highland, Lotte World Adventure and Ocean Park among them. A click opens the park's page and the day planner's wizard on it straight away.
