[Euro-Mir](ref:europa-park/euro-mir) at [Europa-Park](ref:europa-park) runs
until the end of the 2026/27 winter season. The park gives 9 January 2027 as the
date of a farewell event, and dismantling starts after that. By 2028 a space
area of its own is due to open on the site, with a new family coaster from
MACK Rides.

## How the announcement came out

Roland Mack, the park's owner and managing director, spoke about it in autumn 2025. According to
[Stimme](https://www.stimme.de/baden-wuerttemberg/europa-park-rust-weltraum-themenbereich-euro-mir-neu-2028-art-5103880)
on 26 October 2025, the ride is to be taken down and rebuilt from scratch with
new technology, and the area is expected to be finished in 2028. Mack also said
the park was in talks with the European Space Agency, ESA.

The park confirmed it itself on 26 March 2026. According to the
[Schwarzwälder Bote](https://www.schwarzwaelder-bote.de/lokales/lahr/europa-park-bestaetigt-die-achterbahn-euro-mir-ist-ab-winter-geschichte-78821559.html),
Europa-Park wrote in a statement that it was time to say goodbye to an icon. A
new family attraction replaces the ride, the space theme grows into an area of
its own, and the Russian-themed area gets smaller in return. The park also said
the towers are coming back.

## How long you can still ride it

The park's website names the 2026/27 winter season as Euro-Mir's last and
9 January 2027 as the date of the farewell event, ahead of the dismantling. It
doesn't give a separate last day of operation. If you want to be sure, plan
around 9 January.

The ride dates from 1997, was built by MACK Rides, stands 28.3 metres tall and
reaches 80 km/h. A ride lasts five minutes and a train holds 16 riders. What
the park is like in winter and when a trip is worth it is in the
[Europa-Park guide](/blog/europa-park-wait-times-tips).

## What's due in 2028

According to the park's website, a "standalone space-themed area" is being built
with a new family coaster from MACK Rides, targeting 2028. The ESA is involved
as well: on 8 June 2026, ESA and Europa-Park signed a letter of intent in Rust.
Rolf Densing, ESA's Director of Operations, signed for the agency and Roland
Mack for the park. According to the
[ESA](https://www.esa.int/Space_in_Member_States/Germany/ESA_und_Europa-Park_buendeln_ihre_Kraefte_um_Europas_Raumfahrtambitionen_zu_foerdern),
it covers education, knowledge transfer and visitor experiences.

## What's still open

The park hasn't named the new coaster yet, and it hasn't published a layout or
ride data either. It's also unclear what the towers will look like and which
attractions will join the coaster. In March the park wrote that it had to keep
a few secrets to itself for now. The letter of intent with the ESA doesn't say
what visitors will actually see in the area, either.
