# park.fan Coaster — Art bible

**Stylised-realistic.** Real proportions, real materials, real light — with the palette pushed
one notch towards saturation and the geometry cleaned of clutter. The reference is a well-kept
European park photographed at golden hour, not a cartoon and not an archviz render. Nothing may
read as programmer art: no untextured grey, no roughness-1.0 plastic, no flat-shaded toy geometry
(that look belongs to the homepage hero and stays there).

## Identity

- park.fan's own: the brand blue `oklch(0.628 0.137 241)` is the HUD accent and appears in the
  world only on park.fan-branded things (entrance signage, the resort's flags).
- The HUD is the site's dark glassmorphism (`GlassCard` heavy/tile recipes, `backdrop-blur-xl`,
  hairline borders, Geist). Panels float over the world; they never take a solid colour.
- World-space labels (Babylon GUI) use the same type and glass: a rounded dark pill at 62 %.

## Light and atmosphere

- Physically plausible sun: colour temperature 6500 K at noon → 2600 K at the horizon, elevation
  from a real solar model (`environment.getSun`), intensity in lux scaled by the tone mapper.
- Sky: procedural analytic sky (Preetham/Hosek style gradient + sun disc + haze) reflected into the
  IBL; a Poly Haven HDRI replaces it on `high`+ when the asset is present.
- Shadows: cascaded, soft (PCSS on `ultra`, PCF elsewhere), contact-hardening near the ground.
- Night: the park is alive — path lamps (warm 2700 K), ride light rigs (chase, strobe, colour
  cycles from the pack), shop signage emissive, water reflecting it all. Bloom is restrained
  (threshold 0.9, weight 0.3); the night reads by contrast, not by glow.
- Weather: clear / overcast / rain / storm; rain darkens albedo, raises roughness → wet look,
  puddles on paths, guests raise umbrellas or leave; seasons tint foliage.
- Fog: height fog at dawn, distance fog scaled with weather.

## Materials

- PBR everywhere. Metallic/roughness with ORM packing; normal maps on every hard surface.
- Texel density: 512 px/m for things a camera can touch (paths, walls, track), 256 px/m for
  mid-ground, 128 px/m for terrain macro; detail normal on terrain.
- Sources: Poly Haven and ambientCG PBR sets (CC0), KTX2 (UASTC for normals, ETC1S for albedo).
  Procedural fallbacks are real materials too: a generated brick or plank texture with normal,
  never a flat colour.
- Water: a single custom material for pools, flumes and lakes — refraction with depth-based
  absorption, planar/SSR reflection by preset, animated normal (two scrolling layers), foam from
  depth and flow, caustics projected on the floor, edge wetness on the surrounding tiles.
- Track: extruded profiles with real rail radius, ties/spine per style, steel with scuffed
  clear-coat, wood with grain; supports auto-generated by terrain raycast with footings.

## Scale and silhouette

- Guests 1.65–1.85 m; paths 4 m; a coaster car 2.8 × 1.8 m; a lamp post 3.2 m.
- Every prop grounds: contact shadow (ambient occlusion decal) under everything that touches the
  ground, footings under supports, kerbs along paths.
- Foliage: broadleaf and conifer sets with three LODs and wind sway; scatter density follows the
  paint layer, never uniform.

## Guests

- Stylised proportions (1:7 head ratio), real skin tones, park clothing palettes per season.
  Rigged and animated (walk, idle, queue-shuffle, ride, swim, sit, cheer); thin instances with
  baked vertex animation textures for the far LOD.
- Thoughts are HUD, not speech bubbles in the world (world-space labels only in the guest inspector).

## What "AAA" means for the critic

10: could ship next to a commercial park sim. 8.5: AAA with nits. 7: good indie. 5: programmer
art. A module passes at ≥ 8.5 with zero console errors and the budget met, judged from its own
screenshots at three times of day and three zoom levels.
