Saturday is the day with the longest waits at nine of the twelve parks we
measure, and at Movie Park it ties with Tuesday and Wednesday. The quietest day
is usually a Thursday or Friday: Friday at Europa-Park, Heide Park, Movie Park,
Legoland and Disneyland Paris, Thursday at Phantasialand, Efteling and Walibi
Holland. Three parks don't fit the pattern. Every weekday looks the same at
Toverland, six days sit close together at Plopsaland Deutschland, and at Walibi
Holland Saturday is one of the emptier days.

The time of day has a pattern too. At most parks the wait in the first hour
after opening is clearly shorter than later on. Then the queues build, and at
nine parks they peak between ten and twelve. At eight parks the last or
second-to-last hour is the quietest. Phantasialand and Heide Park stay flat
through the rest of the day, and at Legoland and Movie Park it gets busier
again in the evening.

The [best time to visit](/best-time-to-visit) page shows you the quietest days
for the month you're travelling. This post puts twelve parks side by side, using
the data park.fan has been collecting since 24 December 2025.

## Where the numbers come from

For every attraction with a live wait time, park.fan stores the posted wait every
five minutes. That's where the values on the park pages and in this post's
tables come from. The tables are widgets that fetch the current data when the
post loads, so I don't type out any minute values here. The text sticks to
weekdays and ratios that don't change from one day to the next.

As of 6 October 2026. The weekday comparison uses every day a park was open and
averages the waits at its big rides. The hourly curve is the average of the
eight longest rides per hour, over that park's opening hours. Waits are rounded
to five minutes, and at many parks several weekdays land on the same step. A
"quietest day" therefore often means that two or three days are level. Where I
name only one, that day was also ahead on the finer crowd score in the
statistics.

I analysed the school holidays for 13 April to 30 September 2026. The
comparison is between weekdays, Monday to Friday without a public holiday, that
the park's calendar marks as school holidays and weekdays without holidays. The
surcharge says how much longer the wait at the big rides was on average. Weather
and events are not factored out. The
[post on school holidays in Germany](/blog/school-holidays-theme-parks-germany)
looks at single holiday blocks like Easter and summer, and its yardstick is
weekdays on which no German state at all had a holiday. Its figures aren't
directly comparable with the ones here.

Hansa-Park is missing because it only shows its wait times in its own app.

## The weekday at a glance

The twelve parks fall into three groups.

The first group has a weekend peak: Europa-Park, Phantasialand, Efteling, Heide
Park, Legoland, Parc Astérix and Walibi Belgium. Saturday is on top, Sunday
comes behind it or level with a weekday depending on the park, and the dip falls
on Wednesday, Thursday or Friday.

The second group is parks where the weekday explains almost nothing. Toverland
has the same average every day. At Plopsaland Deutschland six days sit on the
same step and only Saturday is a little busier. At Disneyland Paris the days are
one rounding step apart, with Saturday and Tuesday on top and Friday at the
bottom. Waits at these parks are similar every day, and at Plopsaland and
Toverland they're short as well.

The third group is parks that scramble the ranking. At Walibi Holland Tuesday is
the busiest day and Saturday one of the quieter ones. At Movie Park Saturday,
Tuesday and Wednesday are level at the top, but Monday to Wednesday there are
measured on only about 15 opening days, against about 30 at the weekend.

## The hour at a glance

The curve through the day looks similar at most parks. In the first hour after
opening, the wait is clearly shorter than later at most parks, and at Plopsaland,
Heide Park and Walibi Holland it's about half or less. Then it jumps. At
Europa-Park the peak comes at ten o'clock, at Efteling, Parc Astérix and both
Walibis at eleven, and at Phantasialand, Heide Park, Plopsaland and Disneyland
Paris at twelve. After that the curve falls, slowly at some parks and fast at
others.

The parks differ in how far it falls. At Disneyland Paris the quietest hour is
at a good third of the busiest, at Parc Astérix at a good two fifths, at Walibi
Holland at half and at Europa-Park at about six tenths. At Phantasialand the
quietest hour is only about a sixth below the busiest, at Heide Park and Movie
Park about a seventh. Waiting for the evening there saves you very little.

Two parks run against the pattern. At Legoland Deutschland and Movie Park
Germany the last hour with data, 5 pm, is one of the busiest. At Heide Park the
curve also ends at 5 pm, but there it doesn't pick up at the end.

## Europa-Park

[Europa-Park](ref:europa-park) is a Saturday park. Saturday sits clearly above
the other days, and the other six are close together. Friday is the quietest,
followed closely by Sunday, Wednesday and Thursday, and Sunday is one of the
emptier days of the week here. If you want the park quiet during the day, plan
Wednesday to Friday or a Sunday.

Through the day the peak comes early, around ten o'clock, and then drops to a
plateau that lasts into the early evening. The quietest hour is the
second-to-last before closing, and after that it picks up slightly again.

Holidays matter little at Europa-Park: a weekday in the holidays was about 11%
above a weekday in term time, measured over 40 holiday days and 79 school days.
That's the smallest figure among the German parks. A weekend in term time is a
bit higher at 16%. The [Europa-Park guide](/blog/europa-park-wait-times-tips)
explains which rides to do first.

## Phantasialand

[Phantasialand](ref:phantasialand) is busy every day, and least busy on
Thursday. Saturday is the busiest, followed by Sunday. Monday, Tuesday, Friday
and Wednesday sit in between and are hard to tell apart. The gap between
Saturday and Thursday is among the biggest we compare here.

The daily curve is flat. After the early climb until midday, the wait stays
almost as high until closing, and the quietest hour is only about a sixth below
the busiest. If you wait for the evening at Phantasialand, you won't get an
empty ride.

Only Heide Park has a bigger holiday surcharge: a holiday weekday was about 65%
above a school weekday, and a weekend in term time about 39%. So the holidays
fill the park more than a weekend does. That fits the
[school holidays post](/blog/school-holidays-theme-parks-germany), which says
Phantasialand is already full before the holidays start in North
Rhine-Westphalia. How to cope with it anyway is in the
[Phantasialand guide](/blog/phantasialand-wait-times-tips).

## Efteling

[Efteling](ref:efteling) is flat across the weekdays. Saturday and Sunday are a
little busier, Monday sits between, and Thursday is the quietest day, followed by
Tuesday and Wednesday. A Monday at Efteling is a little busier than a Tuesday.

Through the day the curve drops clearly in the evening. The last hour is the
quietest, at about seven tenths of the peak. The peak is at eleven o'clock, it
stays about as busy through midday and the early afternoon, and then the crowds
thin out.

Holidays raise the wait at Efteling by only 11%, over 36 holiday and 84 school
days. The weekend in term time is about 14% above a weekday. The rides and the
order to do them in are in the
[Efteling guide](/blog/efteling-disney-of-the-netherlands).

## Heide Park

[Heide Park](ref:heide-park) has the biggest holiday difference of all. A
weekday in the holidays was about 84% above a school weekday, over 32 holiday
days and 75 school days. A weekend in term time comes to about 47%. So in the
holidays the park can be busier midweek than on a weekend day in term time.
Saturday is also the busiest day otherwise, Friday the quietest, and Sunday sits
between them and close to Friday.

The daily curve is one of the flattest, similar to Phantasialand. The peak comes
around twelve o'clock, and the quietest hour at four in the afternoon is only
about a seventh below it. At Heide Park too you gain little in the evening. The
[Heide Park guide](/blog/heide-park-wait-times-tips) says what to start with.

## Movie Park Germany

At [Movie Park Germany](ref:movie-park-germany) Friday is at the bottom, and
clearly so. Saturday, Tuesday and Wednesday are on top, but those three
weekdays are measured on only about 15 to 16 opening days. I therefore name the
quietest day with caution: Friday, then Thursday and Sunday. The school holidays
post points out that outside the holidays the park is closed on many weekdays.

The daily curve has no morning peak. The wait stays about the same from eleven
to five, the quietest hour is in the afternoon, and in the last hour it's as
busy again as at midday.

In the holidays a weekday is about 54% busier than in term time, over 33 holiday
and 47 school days. The weekend in term time is only 15% above a weekday. So at
Movie Park a holiday weekday matters clearly more than a weekend. Details on
rides and Halloween operation are in the
[Movie Park guide](/blog/movie-park-germany-wait-times-tips).

## Plopsaland Deutschland

At [Plopsaland Deutschland](ref:plopsaland-deutschland) in Haßloch six days of
the week sit at the same level, and only Saturday is a little busier. Tuesday and
Friday are formally the lowest, but the difference is within the five-minute
rounding. There's no "quietest day" here.

The curve through the day shows it more clearly. The first two hours are almost
empty, then the wait rises towards twelve o'clock, stays about the same until
closing and is very low throughout. The quietest hour is at a good half of the
busiest.

The holidays, on the other hand, are clear: a weekday in the holidays was about
49% above a school weekday, a weekend in term time about 53%. A holiday weekday
at Plopsaland is therefore about as busy as a weekend day in term time. The
link with the holidays in Rhineland-Palatinate and North Rhine-Westphalia is in
the [school holidays post](/blog/school-holidays-theme-parks-germany).

## Legoland Deutschland

[Legoland Deutschland](ref:legoland-deutschland) in Günzburg is a weekend park.
Saturday is the busiest day and Sunday the second busiest. Friday is the
quietest, followed closely by Wednesday and Thursday. From Wednesday to Friday
you queue for the shortest time there.

The wait rises until twelve o'clock, dips slightly at midday and is highest in
the last hour with data, at 5 pm. A quiet moment comes only right after opening.

In the holidays a weekday was about 54% above a school weekday, a weekend in
term time about 46%. A holiday weekday at Legoland is therefore busier than a
weekend day in term time, though by a narrower margin than at Heide Park.

## Parc Astérix

At [Parc Astérix](ref:parc-asterix) near Paris Saturday is the busiest day,
Sunday the second busiest and Wednesday the quietest. Tuesday follows closely,
then Thursday, Friday and Monday. The gap between Saturday and Wednesday is one
of the larger ones in this comparison.

The park has the steepest daily curve after Disneyland Paris. The peak comes
around eleven o'clock, and by the evening the wait sinks to a good two fifths of
it. The quietest hour is the last, and the curve runs until 9 pm. At 5 pm the
curve is only at about two thirds of the peak, from 7 pm at about half. If
you're in the park from 7 pm, you queue at the big rides for only about half as
long as at eleven.

Holidays fill the park by about 35%, over 50 holiday and 51 school days. A
weekend in term time comes to about 32%. The
[Parc Astérix guide](/blog/parc-asterix-wait-times-tips) has the order of the
rides.

## Toverland

At [Toverland](ref:attractiepark-toverland) in Sevenum every weekday looks the
same, the average is identical from Monday to Sunday. There's no quietest day
and no busiest, and whether you'd rather go on a Tuesday or a Saturday is
something the data doesn't answer.

The wait at the big rides is low compared with the other parks in this post. A
holiday weekday was nevertheless about 27% above a school weekday, over 31
holiday and 88 school days, and a weekend in term time about 22%.

I'm leaving out Toverland's daily curve. It contains hours between zero and 23,
including ones outside opening hours, and it isn't readable like that. The
individual rides are in the
[Toverland guide](/blog/toverland-troy-wait-times-tips).

## Walibi Holland

[Walibi Holland](ref:walibi-holland) in Biddinghuizen is the park where the
weekday turns the ranking around. The busiest day is Tuesday, followed by Sunday
and Monday. Saturday is one of the quieter days, and the quietest is Thursday.
Tuesday and Monday are measured on only about 14 and 15 days, against 21 to 28
on the other days, so I take Tuesday with a reservation.

If you go to Walibi Holland on a Saturday, you don't queue longer than on a
weekday. The surcharge of a weekend in term time over a school weekday is minus
3%, and a holiday weekday is only about 1% above a school weekday. At Walibi
Holland neither holidays nor weekend shift anything.

The daily curve falls steadily from late morning and ends at half of the peak.
It's busiest at eleven o'clock, and from 4 pm it eases noticeably. More on the
park is in the [Walibi Holland guide](/blog/walibi-holland-untamed-hard-gaan).

## Walibi Belgium

[Walibi Belgium](ref:walibi-belgium) in Wavre is easier to read. Saturday is the
busiest day and Wednesday the quietest. Monday, Tuesday and Thursday sit level
between them, and Sunday comes just before Saturday. Wednesday is measured on
only about 16 days, Saturday on 29.

For the holidays I can't give a figure at this park. The calendar lists not a
single holiday day for Walibi Belgium in the period, so there's no comparison.
The weekend in term time is about 10% above a weekday. That's less than at most
of the other parks.

The daily curve resembles Walibi Holland's: an early peak around eleven o'clock,
then a steady decline, with the last hour at a little more than half of the
peak. The rides and the best order are in the
[Walibi Belgium guide](/blog/walibi-belgium-wait-times-tips).

## Disneyland Paris

At [Disneyland Park](ref:/parks/europe/france/paris/disneyland-park) in
Marne-la-Vallée the waits are the longest in this comparison, and the weekday
makes almost no difference. Saturday and Tuesday are the busiest, and Friday is
the quietest, followed closely by Wednesday, Sunday, Monday and Thursday. The
difference between the busiest and the quietest day is one rounding step, so five
minutes.

I suspect the park draws visitors from all over Europe and that somewhere there
are always holidays. I can't prove it. A holiday weekday was only about 3%
above a school weekday, and a weekend in term time about 5% above.

For the time of day the difference is large here. The peak comes around twelve
o'clock, then it stays high into the early evening, and the last hour is at a
good third of it, the widest gap in this comparison. The
[Disneyland Paris guide](/blog/disneyland-paris-wait-times-tips) says how to
split the day.

## School holidays against term time

The table shows the surcharge a weekday in the holidays has over a weekday in
term time, and next to it the surcharge of a weekend in term time.

| Park                   | Holiday weekday, surcharge | Weekend in term time, surcharge |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------: | ------------------------------: |
| Heide Park             |                       84 % |                            47 % |
| Phantasialand          |                       65 % |                            39 % |
| Movie Park Germany     |                       54 % |                            15 % |
| Legoland Deutschland   |                       54 % |                            46 % |
| Plopsaland Deutschland |                       49 % |                            53 % |
| Parc Astérix           |                       35 % |                            32 % |
| Toverland              |                       27 % |                            22 % |
| Europa-Park            |                       11 % |                            16 % |
| Efteling               |                       11 % |                            14 % |
| Disneyland Paris       |                        3 % |                             5 % |
| Walibi Holland         |                        1 % |                            −3 % |
| Walibi Belgium         |    no holidays in calendar |                            10 % |

Both columns use a weekday in term time between 13 April and 30 September 2026
as the baseline. The number of days per cell is between 24 and 88. The figures
come from one season, and part of the holidays fall in the summer months that
are busy anyway, so holidays and season blur together. A comparison within one
park is therefore sturdier than one between two parks.

At Heide Park, Phantasialand, Movie Park and Legoland the holiday weekday sits
clearly above the weekend, at 54 to 84 percent against 15 to 47. At Parc Astérix
and Toverland it's slightly above. If you want to avoid Saturday and take a
Tuesday in the holidays instead, you're more likely to queue longer on a holiday
weekday than on a weekend in term time. At four parks, Europa-Park, Efteling,
Disneyland Paris and Walibi Holland, all four cases are close together. There
the choice of day brings little, but in the evening the wait falls to between
half and a third of the peak, at Europa-Park to about six tenths.

## Planning your trip

**The park first.** If you only want a ride without a queue, Plopsaland or
Toverland fits, because neither is busy on any day. If you want the most out of
a busy park, Friday is the safest choice at Europa-Park or Heide Park, and
Thursday at Phantasialand and Efteling.

**Then the day.** At nine parks Saturday is on top. If Saturday is the only day
you can go, pick the parks where the difference is small: Walibi Holland,
Disneyland Paris, Walibi Belgium or Plopsaland Deutschland. At Phantasialand and
Parc Astérix Saturday costs you the most.

**Then the holidays.** Before you book a weekday, check whether your park has a
surcharge in the holidays. At Heide Park, Phantasialand and Movie Park a holiday
weekday can be a Saturday. The holiday dates of the German states are in the
[school holidays post](/blog/school-holidays-theme-parks-germany).

**Then the hour.** Arrive at opening: until the peak builds around ten to twelve,
you get through the big rides with shorter waits. If you stay into the evening,
you gain at Disneyland Paris, Parc Astérix, Europa-Park, Efteling and both
Walibis, but hardly at Phantasialand and Heide Park. How to split the hours is
shown by the [trip planner](/blog/trip-planner).

## Frequently asked questions

### Which day is quietest at a theme park?

At most parks Thursday or Friday. At Europa-Park, Heide Park, Movie Park,
Legoland and Disneyland Paris Friday was the quietest day, at Phantasialand,
Efteling and Walibi Holland Thursday, and at Parc Astérix and Walibi Belgium
Wednesday. At Toverland and Plopsaland Deutschland there's no quietest day.

### Is Sunday emptier than Saturday?

At most parks, yes. At Europa-Park, Heide Park and Movie Park Sunday is one of
the quieter days of the week. At Phantasialand, Efteling, Legoland and Parc
Astérix it's in second place behind Saturday.

### What's the best time of day at a theme park?

Right at opening, then late afternoon or evening. At most parks the peak comes
around ten to twelve, and at parks that stay open late into the evening the wait
then falls to between a third and a half.

### Are school holidays worse than a Saturday?

At Heide Park, Phantasialand, Movie Park and Legoland yes, at Parc Astérix and
Toverland narrowly. There, holiday weekdays have longer waits on average than
weekends in term time. At Europa-Park, Efteling, Disneyland Paris and Walibi
Holland the difference matters little.

### Does this hold for other years?

We don't know yet. The figures cover 24 December 2025 to 5 October 2026, and the
holiday values depend on this year's holiday dates. Whether the ranking of the
weekdays stays the same in a second year, only a second year will show.
