# The header menu is three kinds of content, and the split is about the link graph (REQUIREMENT)

One standing rule. It is indexed from the repo's [`CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md), which carries the rule in one line and links here for the reasoning, the measurements and the counter-examples.

everything the full-width band renders is a link on ~35,000 pages, so the parks panel server-renders **continents and countries only** — 28 hub links, 420 B brotli, all 23 countries visible at once now that the band is container-wide (the first version swapped country lists with four of five on `display:none`; going full width deleted that state). The 144 cities and 212 parks under them are fetched per opened country from `/api/nav/geo/[continent]/[country]`: they are already reachable from those hubs and from the sitemap, so putting them in the template would spread the same weight over 356 more targets and buy no discovery. The **photo rail is a fixed six**, not a thumbnail per park — the media database holds a picture for **14 of 212 parks** — and it reuses the homepage's per-locale `FEATURED_PARK_SLUGS` intersected with the parks that have one, resolved in the layout because `@/lib/media` is the 107 KB catalog and the header is a Client Component. **Hover has to be rested on, not crossed**: the detail row sits under the country columns, so the way to it leads over the countries below — entering a row only arms the switch and leaving before 140 ms disarms it (focus commits at once). That same gesture used to break the fetch, and it was one bug: the effect discarded its response on cleanup while leaving the key in `requested`, so skimming past a country threw its answer away and the guard then refused to ask again — **23 of 23 countries stayed empty, 0 of 23 after**. A response keyed by country is a cache write and is correct whenever it lands, so nothing cancels it; a failed request drops its key so the next hover retries. Same rule shaped the blog panel: 3 categories and the 6 newest posts with their covers in, **31 tags out** — 31 tag pages over 7 posts are mostly one post's teaser at a second URL. The band is **glass, not opaque** (`bg-popover/95` + `backdrop-blur-xl` + the popover ring): flat means no pointer-depth tilt, not no blur, and `/80` let a park page's headline and body text read straight through the menu. That glass is also why the opening animation (`lib/hooks/use-menu-reveal.ts`) only ever targets **descendants** of the surface: a transform or an opacity on it, or on any ancestor, makes it a backdrop root and the blur goes flat exactly while somebody watches it appear. Same split as the header's own reveal — CSS owns visibility (a `hidden` class), GSAP animates `y` and never `opacity`, and under `prefers-reduced-motion` the chunk is never even imported. It is positioned against the `<header>`, not the trigger — centred on an entry 276 px from the left edge a 700 px panel starts at −36 px — and carries `overflow-hidden`, because the rows' `-mx-2` bleed gave the document a horizontal scrollbar at exactly 1024 px. Watch two traps: a single `useTranslations('blog')` in a header component pulls the whole namespace into the layout chrome that **every** page serializes (6066 B → 9047 B × 6 locales, for one label — the word lives in `navigation` now), and the bar has **one** breakpoint, not two: the nav used to start at `md` while the search input waits for `lg`, so 768–1023 px carried the full nav plus a 256 px search button plus no burger, 789 px of content in a 736 px box, wrapping German onto two lines with a horizontal scrollbar. Measured cost of the whole menu: **+5.65 KB brotli per page** (~4.2 of it the 23 flags) for 4 → 48 crawlable nav links, all resolving 200, CLS unchanged, and zero photo requests until somebody opens it. **Six equal entries did not fit that bar**, and the measurement is the French one: at a 1024 px container the row ran 23.7 px over its box and took the document to 1032 px, i.e. a horizontal scrollbar on every page, with 0.0 px left at 1280. „Beste Reisezeit", „Wörterbuch" and „So funktioniert's" sit behind a third trigger — **„Mehr"**, a catch-all with no page of its own, so `NavMenu.href` is optional and its label is the button; the band is `hidden` rather than unmounted, so the destinations stay in the HTML of every page exactly as the entries did (verified: 24 of 24 across six locales). **The blog is not one of them any more.** It went in with the other three and came back out: it is the site's strongest entry point, and behind a catch-all it was visible only to somebody who opened one. It is a fourth bar entry labelled **„Backstage"** — one word, unchanged in all six locales — with `href="/blog"` and the panel it always had, and `navigation.blog` is what all three of its readers print: the bar entry, the phone sheet's link and the `SiteNavigationElement` the locale layout emits for `/blog`. The footer keeps its own `footer.blog`, and the breadcrumb on the index page keeps `blog.blog`, because a breadcrumb names the page and not the menu entry. The row reads **+245.0 px** of slack in French at a 1024 px container with four entries plus favorites (346.8 with three; the entry costs 101.8 px, and the 23.7 px of overflow that started all this came from **six**). Its three sections are a flat `grid-cols-3` with no threshold under it — the 256 px rail they used to form beside the blog block went out with the block, because `w-64` and `border-r` describe a relationship to a neighbour and there is none left. The blog panel's own `/blog` link is its **one** heading now, a `MenuSectionHeading` over both columns: it carried two, and the second of them repeated the word that labels the bar entry the panel hangs from. Every row of that post list prints its category, in the opener's accent rather than the muted tone `BlogPostRow` uses elsewhere, so the two halves of one band agree — which needed `getBlogMenu` to stop resolving `category` under an `index === 0` (+517 B raw, +36 B gzip-9 on a country listing page). **A panel's content column is the BAR's column, and a panel is a page rather than a row in the bar:** both used to be Tailwind's `container mx-auto … md:px-0`, and when the bar moved to container-query tiers against the `<header>` (the planner insets it without the window moving) with `px-4` as a floor, `MenuBand` kept the old class — which reads its max-width off the **window** — so the band's contents sat 16 px left of the nav row at every viewport and 96 px left of it with the planner open at 1920. The same `<nav>` is `whitespace-nowrap`, because a menu word may never wrap in a 48 px bar, and `white-space` **inherits**: labels and `truncate`d card titles survive that, prose does not, so the blog teasers ran off the band on one line each and the favorites empty state — the state nearly every visitor sees — pushed its three steps past the right edge into the `overflow-hidden`. The surface resets `whitespace-normal`. **And every card in the favorites band is the same width**, which it was not: each group got `flexGrow: <its card count>` and filled it with `repeat(auto-fill, minmax(10.5rem, 1fr))`, so the width followed the count, the column count followed the width, and the quantization between them broke the proportion the whole thing rests on — three parks beside five rides got 336 px, twelve short of two 168 px columns, so the parks group drew ONE column of 336 px cards against the rides' 179 and stacked all three, for a band 868 px tall (taller than the window it hangs in) on eight favorites. `planBand` measures the band, derives one card width from it (≥168, ≤248 — past that a card in a menu is a billboard), and cuts each group a whole number of those tracks, greedy on `count / (has + 1)`; what will not fit in `MAX_CARD_ROWS` rows goes behind the "+N" line that already existed, so the height follows the row count and not how much somebody has starred. Measured at 1440 with 3 parks + 5 rides + 3 shows: **868 → 610 px, every card 188 px**. That arithmetic lives in `lib/utils/favorites-band-plan.ts` rather than in the component, like `weather-chart-axis` and for the same reason — it broke once in a way a green build showed nothing of — and `pnpm test:favorites-band` pins both halves: one card width across every group at every band width, and every group combination that predates the alerts group planning byte-for-byte as it did. **The band's fourth group is the browser's own alerts**, with a remove button per row, because the link to `/alerts` it replaced meant leaving the page to find out what was armed. Its gate is `countPushFollowsLocal()` — a `localStorage` read, so a visitor who never pressed a bell loads neither the chunk nor a request — and the LIST is the server's, since the mirror is a cache for bells and not a truth for a surface somebody deletes from; a cleared mirror over a surviving push subscription is the one gap, which is why the link stays unconditional beside the panel's title. `next/dynamic` behind that gate, because `lib/push/push-follows` reaches the service worker and the VAPID key and none of that belongs in the chunk the header ships on ~35,000 pages. The 300 px sheet keeps the link and no list. And **a row leaves only where the server let it go**: `removeRideAlert`/`unfollowShow` wrote the local mirror first, sent the DELETE second and never read `response.ok`, so a 500 took the row off the screen over an alert that was still armed — and since this group is gated on that mirror, removing the last one unmounted the whole group in the same commit as the click, taking its own spinner and any error with it. Both return a `PushWriteResult` now, with the write path's own error classes, and the order is inverted: API first, mirror only on success, query cache after that and only on success (`usePushFollowRemoval`, shared with `AlertsOverview` so one rule decides for both surfaces). A **404 is a success** — a row the server does not have is what the visitor asked for, and an error over it would stand for ever since every retry answers 404 too; the write path reads that same status the other way round (`setRideAlert` re-syncs the subscription and retries), and the asymmetry is the point. The sentence for a refused removal comes from `pushAlerts.menu` in the band and from `usePushErrorMessage` on `/alerts`, because pulling `pushAlerts.pushErrors` into the chrome would put it on ~35,000 pages for one line. `pnpm test:push-follow-delete` pins the order and the classes. **The read path had the same blindness one level down**: `fetchRideAlertsRemote`/`fetchShowFollowsRemote` asked `getExistingPushIdentity()`, which answers `null` for a `getRegistration()` that THREW as well, so a refused storage access arrived as `{ ok: true, items: [] }` — „Noch nichts eingerichtet" in front of five armed alerts, and the band's alerts group gone. Both read `lookupExistingPushIdentity()` now and pass a failed lookup on as `{ ok: false }`, which `PushListResult` exists for and both surfaces already had a branch for; `getExistingPushIdentity()` is unchanged and keeps exactly one caller, `identityForWrite`, where the three cases may collapse because a `null` is followed by `ensurePushRegistered()` anyway (`pnpm test:push-follow-read`).
