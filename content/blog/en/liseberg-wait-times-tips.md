On **4 October 2026** the Radiobilarna ran their last lap at Liseberg. They were
dodgems that had stood in the park since **12 May 1927**, in a hall that is
about to be torn down. More than 300,000 guests a year rode them, and anyone
with a ticket on the last three days could ride for free. What will be built on
the spot, the park isn’t saying yet.

Liseberg in Gothenburg was built in 1923 for a jubilee exhibition and was meant
to disappear again afterwards. Today the grounds hold four big coasters, the
[AtmosFear](ref:liseberg/atmosfear) tower with a 90-metre free fall and, in
autumn, five haunted houses. In November the Christmas market joins in. The site
is on the edge of the city centre, and according to the park the central station
is a 10 to 15 minute walk away. For 2025 the park gives around **3 million
guests** for the year.

## How an exhibition became a park

The hill got its name from a woman. The merchant Johan Anders Lamberg, who took
over the land in 1752, named it "Lisas berg", Lisa’s Hill, around 1753 after his
wife Elisabeth Söderberg. Before that the 38-metre ridge was called Geteberget,
Goat Hill. In 1908 the city of Gothenburg bought the plot, buildings included,
for **225,000 kronor**.

On **8 May 1923** the jubilee exhibition for the city’s 300th birthday opened
there, with an amusement park and a congress park. Among the attractions was the
roughly 1,000-metre wooden railway "Gamla bergbanan". The park was meant to be
temporary. Because more than 80,000 guests came in the first month, it stayed.
On **24 November 1924** the city council decided to buy it for a million kronor,
and in 1925 the municipal company Liseberg AB took over operations. The first
director, until 1942, was the carpenter Herman Lindholm. The park still belongs
to the city of Gothenburg. In May 1950 the concert hall hosted Sweden’s first
television trials, with Pye from Cambridge and the Chalmers University of
Technology. ABBA, the Rolling Stones and Led Zeppelin are among those who have
played the big stage, and in 1947 Édith Piaf sang there.

The green-and-pink rabbit only became the mascot in **1983**, for the 60th
birthday. The pink of the buildings dates from the 1923 exhibition. The
mountain railway of that time was torn down in **1987** and replaced by the
[Lisebergbanan](ref:liseberg/lisebergbanan), which Anton Schwarzkopf had
designed for the same hill.

Since **2000** Liseberg has opened in winter ("Jul på Liseberg"), and 418,000
guests came in the first year. **2015** brought Halloween. And in **2020** the
park stayed closed the entire season because of the pandemic, for the first time
since it was founded. In 2023 it turned 100.

You can follow how many guests come across the decades. In 1936 it was more
than a million, in 1954 a good 1.7 million, and in 1979 1.8 million. In 2003,
the year of Balder, the park reported its record of 3.4 million guests across
summer and Christmas. In 2021, the first year after the pandemic, it was 1.5
million, and in 2023 2.2 million according to the Swedish Wikipedia. The most
expensive investment until then was **Spaceport Liseberg**: in 1990 the park
opened a simulator cinema and the observation tower, for 110 million kronor. The
observation tower later became AtmosFear.

## Balder: the wooden coaster

[Balder](ref:liseberg/balder) opened on **12 April 2003**. According to the
Roller Coaster DataBase it’s a wooden coaster by Intamin with prefabricated
track, designed by the engineering firm Stengel and built by Cordes Holzbau. It’s
**36 metres** tall, has **1,070 metres** of track and reaches **90 km/h**; the
Roller Coaster DataBase puts the construction cost at **100 million kronor**.
The train runs for over two minutes, and the height requirement is **130 cm**
plus an age of seven.

The name comes from Norse mythology. Balder is the son of Odin and Frigg, of whom
it was said that nothing bad could be said about him, and who died anyway
because his blind brother Höder hit him with a sprig of mistletoe. The ride
stands on the other side of the Mölndalsån river, like the Kållerado.

## Helix, Valkyria and the Lisebergbanan

**[Helix](ref:liseberg/helix)** opened on **26 April 2014** and, according to the
park, cost almost 240 million kronor; the Roller Coaster DataBase gives 200
million, plus another 40 million for blasting rock. It’s a Mack Rides launch
coaster that is accelerated twice by linear motors, is **1,381 metres** long,
reaches **100 km/h** and has seven inversions. At its opening it was the most
expensive ride investment in the park’s history. The height is quoted
differently: 41 metres on the English Wikipedia, 52 metres from the park and on
the Swedish Wikipedia. The height requirement is **130 cm** plus an age of seven,
and the train runs for about two minutes and ten seconds.

```glossary-widget slug=launch-coaster

```

**[Valkyria](ref:liseberg/valkyria)** opened on **10 August 2018** on the site of
the Kanonen. The Kanonen was an Intamin launch coaster that opened on 23 April
2005 and ran for the last time on 30 December 2016. Valkyria is a Bolliger &
Mabillard dive coaster with three inversions and a steep first drop **50 metres**
long that leads straight into a tunnel underground. The park gives **47 metres**
of height and **105 km/h**. Valkyria needs **132 cm** plus seven years and, like
Mechanica, has an upper limit of 195 cm.

The Kanonen was the first ride of its kind in the north. The train shot out
hydraulically to 75 km/h in 1.84 seconds, climbed 24 metres steeply, ran through
a loop and a heartline roll, and was back in the station after about 50 seconds.
In 2010 it had 651,000 rides, which made it the park’s fifth most popular ride.
Valkyria replaced it on the same plot, and the path of the first drop now leads
under the ground, into a tunnel.

And there’s the **[Lisebergbanan](ref:liseberg/lisebergbanan)**, the oldest of
the big rides. It opened on **18 April 1987**, was built by Zierer to a design by
Werner Stengel and Anton Schwarzkopf, and runs at **80 km/h** through the woods
on the hill. According to the Roller Coaster DataBase, five trains of eleven cars
each carry up to 2,000 guests an hour, and the station is designed as a railway
station. Underneath it is a restaurant with replica carriages. The height
requirement is 130 cm, or 110 cm with an accompanying adult. In autumn and
winter it only runs during Halloween; it isn’t on the list for the Jul programme.

Then there’s **[Luna](ref:liseberg/luna)**, opened in 2023: a Vekoma boomerang,
33.5 metres tall and 242 metres long, that sends the train first forwards, then
backwards through the same course. Luna requires 120 cm plus an age of six, or
95 cm plus an age of three with an accompanying adult. And for the little ones
there are the **[Rabalder](ref:liseberg/rabalder)** by Zierer (2009) and the
**[Stampbanan](ref:liseberg/stampbanan)** by Preston & Barbieri (2013), both in
Rabbit Land. On both a child needs 110 cm, or 90 cm with an accompanying adult.

## AtmosFear, water rides and the other rides

The **[AtmosFear](ref:liseberg/atmosfear)** tower was built in 1990 as an
observation tower ("Lisebergstornet"); the last group went up it on 30 May 2010,
and a day later Intamin began the conversion. It opened as a ride in April 2011.
The gondola drops around 90 metres in free fall, in roughly three seconds, at up
to 110 km/h. The conversion cost about 50 million kronor, and according to the
Swedish Wikipedia it’s the tallest free fall in Europe. The height requirement
is 140 cm. According to the park, AtmosFear only runs in summer, not during
Halloween and not at Christmas.

There are two kinds of water ride. The **[FlumeRide](ref:liseberg/flumeride)**,
a log flume by Arrow Development, opened in **1973**, is more than 600 metres
long and had 732,000 riders in its first season. The
**[Kållerado](ref:liseberg/kallerado)** from 1997 is an Intamin river rapids
ride that came in just behind the Lisebergbanan in popularity in 2010 (1.11
million rides, against 1.32 million for the Lisebergbanan). The FlumeRide
requires 130 cm, or 100 cm with an accompanying adult, and the Kållerado 130 cm,
or 110 cm with an accompanying adult.

The park has more than thirty rides in all, and the others include:

- **[Loke](ref:liseberg/loke)** (2017), an Intamin gyro swing for up to 40 guests,
  with a height requirement of 130 cm plus seven years,
- **[Mechanica](ref:liseberg/mechanica)** (25 April 2015), a Zierer Star Shape
  whose gondolas turn around three axes, 140 cm plus ten years, 30 million
  kronor according to the Swedish Wikipedia,
- **[AeroSpin](ref:liseberg/aerospin)** (2016), a Gerstlauer Sky Roller where
  guests steer the rotation of their own machine, 130 cm,
- **[Uppswinget](ref:liseberg/uppswinget)** (2007), a giant swing with two
  swinging platforms, 140 cm,
- **[Hanghai](ref:liseberg/hanghai)** (2009), a Zamperla Disk’O, 130 cm,
- **[Lisebergshjulet](ref:liseberg/lisebergshjulet)**, a Ferris wheel 60 metres
  tall that has stood in the park since 2012 and was called "Göteborgshjulet" at
  Kanaltorget in Gothenburg before that.

Things are calmer in **[Underlandet](ref:liseberg/underlandet)**, a gondola ride
through several rooms in the world of the Liseberg rabbits. It dates from 2020
and 2021, is on the list of rides with a virtual queue, and a child needs 130 cm
for it, or 90 cm with an accompanying adult. The
**[Slänggungan](ref:liseberg/slaenggungan)**, a Zierer chain swinger, has existed
since 1989 and the current ride since 2018, from 120 cm. The
**[Kaffekoppen](ref:liseberg/kaffekoppen)** from 1985 are big cups that you spin
by hand yourself.

The newest is **[Stormvåg](ref:liseberg/stormvag)**, an Art Engineering Wild
Swing XL with 24 seats and a height of 14.5 metres, opened on **14 May 2026** and
themed on the harbour. It stands where the Kristallsalongen used to be, a hall of
mirrors from 1962 that closed on **14 September 2025** and, according to the
park, had around 15 million visitors over its lifetime. Before that, in 2025,
**[Kraftverket](ref:liseberg/kraftverket)** opened, a fast-spinning carousel
(130 cm, or 120 cm with an accompanying adult). For Stormvåg a child needs
120 cm, or 95 cm with an accompanying adult.

Rabbit Land (opened in 2013) has seven rides for the little ones, plus the two
coasters from above and a playground. It’s also the only part that opens on days
of its own in autumn and winter.

The JukeBox requires 110 cm, and the haunted houses have a minimum age: eleven
for Vinden, Zombie, Nattsvart på Gasten and Skogen, **15 years** for "7".

## Haunted hotel, stages and a path with stars

The **Spökhotellet Gasten** opened in 1998 and won a THEA Award from the Themed
Entertainment Association in California in autumn 1999. It’s on the list of
rides with a virtual queue. The Halloween houses are younger: according to the
park, Zombie has run since 2015, Vinden since 2017, the house "7" since 2022 and
Nattsvart på Gasten since 2024.

Between the Korsvägen stop and the main entrance runs the **Stjärnstigen**, a
path with brass stars set into it, a kind of Walk of Fame for Gothenburg. The
earlier Hedersplatsen with the handprints of celebrities, laid out in 1977 with
50 prints, was removed in 2012 for the 90th birthday and made way for the Farfars
Bil. The park still has several stages. On the **Stora Scenen**, which has stood
since 1923, the TV4 television show "Lotta på Liseberg" ran in summer from 2009
to 2023, and for 21 June 2027 the park has announced a Take That concert.
Admission to concerts on this stage costs extra (75 kronor); the Lilla Scenen is
included in the entry price.

The grounds also include the **Lustgården**, a garden with waterfalls and
sculptures. It opened in 2008 as "Göteborgs Lustgårdar" on a meadow of a good
20,000 square metres between the tower and the Korsvägen.

### No Express pass, but a virtual queue

Liseberg doesn’t sell a pass for skipping the queue. In the park’s questions and
answers it says: "Vi har inte Express-pass längre. Numera har vi virtuell kö."
(We no longer have an Express pass. We now have a virtual queue.) The Express
pass was first called Quick Pass from 2010 and could be pre-booked for at most
three rides a day; today the virtual queue in the Liseberg app takes its place,
and it costs nothing extra.

You join the queue in the app and get a time at which you should come to the
ride. It exists for AeroSpin, Balder, FlumeRide, Helix, Kållerado, Kraftverket,
Lisebergbanan, Loke, Luna, Mechanica, the haunted house Gasten, Underlandet and
Valkyria. Each group can only have one reservation at a time, for one to six
people, and the normal queue stays alongside it. Whether the virtual queue runs
on a given day depends, according to the park, on how many guests are there. If
you want to ride Valkyria or Helix, you join the queue in the app first and ride
something else in the meantime.

## When to go

```stats-widget slug=liseberg show=months,weekdays

```

Summer is the busy time, with July and August on top and May and June a little
below. From January to March it’s quiet, because only Rabbit Land is open, on
weekends, and almost nothing is measured. Tuesday is the busiest day and Sunday
the quietest, and with 40 measured days Sunday has more than three times as many
as Monday or Tuesday. In autumn the park closes at 20:00 on Sundays and at 22:00
on Fridays and Saturdays.

The 2026 summer season began on **18 April** at 11:00. When it ends, the park
doesn’t say. The calendar on the [park page](ref:liseberg) shows what has been
published for the next few weeks.

### Halloween: 2 October to 1 November 2026

The park is fully open on all Halloween days, Rabbit Land included. On Fridays
it opens from 15:00 to 22:00, on Saturdays from 12:00 to 22:00, on Sundays from
12:00 to 20:00; from 24 to 31 October it’s open daily from 11:00 to 23:00. The
haunted houses open later than the park: on the Zombie page Liseberg gives 15:00
for days when the park opens at 11:00. There are five:

- **Vinden**, **Zombie**, **Nattsvart på Gasten** and **Skogen** from age 11,
- **7**, the house about the seven deadly sins, from **age 15**.

On top of that come three areas with actors: Clown, Demonernas Skugga and
Nemesis, though Nemesis has no performers, only film projections. The actors are
there from 18:00. There’s no age limit for entry, and children under 110 cm get
in free. You can dress up, but your face must not be fully made up, masks are
banned in the houses, and nothing may look like a weapon. Valkyria doesn’t run
during Halloween; Helix and the Lisebergbanan do.

Halloween at Liseberg started in 2015 with two houses, Smitthärden Gasten and
Dockkabinettet, and with "The Experiment", which gave AtmosFear a theme with
performers. Zombie and Vinden followed, and in 2018 the setting Cirkus Bisarr.
According to the Swedish Wikipedia the best-attended seasons were 2019 and 2024;
for 2024 it gives 343,581 guests.

There’s also "Klubb Nattsvart", a party in the harbour area, on 9, 10, 16 and 17
October from 22:30 to 2:00, and the Oktoberfest in the Tyrol area from 2 to 31
October. And for children there’s "Höst i Kaninlandet": on single days only
Rabbit Land is open, from 11:00 to 17:00, on 26 and 27 September, 30 September
and 1 October, and 14 and 15 October.

According to the park, Halloween 2025 brought around **330,000 guests** over 16
opening days. The dates of other parks are in the
[Halloween guide 2026](/blog/halloween-theme-parks-2026).

### Jul på Liseberg: mid-November to the end of December

The first Julmarknad took place on 7 December 1974, over two weekends, once more
in 1975, and then not for 25 years. Since 2000 Liseberg has opened every winter.
The park calls itself "Sveriges största julmarknad", Sweden’s biggest Christmas
market. 2025 was the 25th anniversary, with 2,100 trees, seven tonnes of
artificial snow, five million lights and a 116-metre tree, according to the
park’s announcement of 6 November 2025.

The 2026 opening days, as the park API returns them: in November the 14th and
15th, 20th to 22nd and 27th to 29th, in December the 2nd to 6th, 9th to 13th and
16th to 23rd (daily from the 20th) and the 26th to 30th. On Fridays it’s open
from 15:00 to 22:00, on Saturdays from 12:00 to 22:00, on Sundays from 12:00 to
20:00. On 24 and 25 December the park is closed.

There are three markets: the Tyrolermarknad between the Radiobilarna and the
Slänggungan, the Gammaldags Jul in the Storgatan quarter and the Vintermarknad
at the Stora Scenen. There are also the Tomtehuset, the ice rink and a new light
show in the harbour area. In 2025 around 30 rides ran, including Balder,
Valkyria, Loke and Mechanica, but not Helix and the Lisebergbanan. The park
hasn’t yet published the admission prices for Jul 2026. In 2026 Rabbit Land is
open on 42 additional days in winter, every weekend and in the school holidays.

## Tickets and prices

Liseberg sells admission and the ride wristband separately. The **Åkpass**
covers all rides and the haunted houses; admission to the park alone doesn’t
include them. Online is always cheaper than at the ticket window, and there are
seven price tiers depending on the day. The prices for the 2026 Halloween
season, online:

- admission and Åkpass together: from **425 kronor**, up to 625 kronor in the
  price calendar,
- Åkpass only: from 335 kronor, up to 510 kronor,
- admission only: from 125 kronor, up to 185 kronor,
- admission and Åkpass Kanin (children’s rides only): from 295 kronor, up to
  395 kronor, under 110 cm from 195 kronor.

Without an Åkpass a single ride costs one Åkkupong at 25 kronor, and a haunted
house two to four coupons. On the "Höst i Kaninlandet" days admission costs 95
kronor, and 245 kronor with the children’s rides. The park’s site no longer
shows the 2026 summer prices; only the Halloween prices are left there. Which of
the seven tiers applies on which day is in the park’s price calendar as a PDF.

If you come more often, you buy an annual pass (twelve months from activation):
the **Lisebergspasset** for 395 kronor covers admission, and the **Guldpasset**
for 2,595 kronor includes all rides and the houses. For people aged 65 and over
there’s a Senior pass for 195 kronor. When sales started in March 2025, the
Lisebergspasset cost 365 kronor.

## Getting there

There are two entrances. The **Norra entrén** is at Örgrytevägen 5 by the
Korsvägen, with the stops Korsvägen and Liseberg Station, and the **Södra
entrén** at Herman Lindholms torg 1 with the stop Liseberg Södra. From the
central station, Göteborg Central, it’s 10 to 15 minutes on foot according to the
park, and in summer a historic tram, the Lisebergslinjen, runs between Central
and the park. The park doesn’t give the tram’s line numbers; it refers you to
Västtrafik.

If you come by car, there are three car parks: **P-huset Liseberg Södra** under
World of Volvo (1,170 spaces), **P-huset Liseberg Västra** at Södra Vägen 70
(162 spaces) and **P-huset Focus** at Fabriksgatan 45 (1,057 spaces). The parking
fees are listed at Parkering Göteborg, not on the park’s site.

## Staying and eating

The **Liseberg Grand Curiosa Hotel** belongs to the park, has 457 rooms and
opened on 22 April 2023. It’s at the Södra entrén, every room has at least five
beds, and the firm Wingårdh designed it. There’s also the **Lisebergsbyn** with
cabins, camping, a guesthouse and a hostel, ten minutes from the city. The
Gothia Towers next to the park belongs to the foundation Svenska Mässan, not to
Liseberg.

You’re allowed to bring food. The park writes: "Det går bra att ta med egen mat"
(Bringing your own food is fine), and there are picnic areas. Dogs aren’t
allowed, guide dogs are. If you eat in the park, you have a choice: the
Lisebergs Wärdshus with Nordic cooking, the Hamnkrogen for fish, the
Järnvägsrestaurangen in the Lisebergbanan station, Al Parco with pizza, and the
Mei Rose Rooftop Bar & Bistro.

## Oceana: the water park that isn’t there yet

In November 2020 construction began on Oceana, an indoor-outdoor water park with
14 attractions on around 13,600 square metres. It was due to open in 2024. On 12
February 2024 the building burned down. Since July 2024 it has been settled that
the park will rebuild it. According to Liseberg the rebuild costs "drygt 1
miljard kronor" (just over a billion kronor), the opening is planned for "i
början av 2027" (early 2027), and tickets are due to go on sale from the end of
November 2026. More on this in the post
[Theme park novelties 2027](/blog/new-theme-park-attractions-2027).

According to the park, Oceana has around 6,000 square metres of indoor pools and
4,000 square metres of outdoor area. The water park belongs to the project that
was decided in 2017 and also included the Grand Curiosa Hotel. The hotel has
stood since 2023; the water park is missing.

## A day at Liseberg, roughly planned

If you come in summer, go to **Helix** or **Valkyria** first, and if the virtual
queue is running, join it in the app for the other ride on your way there.
**Balder** is on the other side of the river, so plan a block of its own for it.
**Loke** and **Mechanica** fit in between, and the **Lisebergbanan** squeezes
into the gaps. Save the FlumeRide and the Kållerado for the warmest hour.

In autumn, Halloween days on Fridays don’t start until 15:00, and the haunted
houses don’t open until the afternoon. If you want both the houses and the
rides, ride first until it gets dark, and then go into the houses.

## Frequently asked questions about Liseberg

### Does Liseberg have a Fast Pass?

No. The park itself says it no longer has the Express pass. The free virtual
queue in the app has taken its place, with one reservation per group. There’s no
paid fast lane.

### What is the height requirement for Balder, Helix and Valkyria?

For Balder and Helix 130 cm plus seven years, for Valkyria 132 cm plus seven
years (and 195 cm at most). For the Lisebergbanan it’s 130 cm, or 110 cm with an
accompanying adult.

### When is Liseberg open in 2026?

The summer season began on 18 April. Halloween runs from 2 October to 1
November, and Jul på Liseberg on selected days from 14 November to 30 December.
There are also winter weekends in Rabbit Land. The calendar on the
[park page](ref:liseberg) always shows the next few weeks.

### When is Liseberg least busy?

On Sunday, by a clear margin over Tuesday in our measurements. In winter it’s
quieter still, but then only a few rides run.

### Can I bring food?

Yes, the park allows it and has picnic areas. Dogs aren’t allowed.

### What does admission cost?

In autumn 2026, online, from 125 kronor for admission and from 425 kronor with
the Åkpass. Children under 110 cm get in free. The park’s site currently doesn’t
show the 2026 summer prices.

### How old do I have to be for the haunted houses?

Vinden, Zombie, Nattsvart på Gasten and Skogen from eleven years, the house "7"
from 15 years. They open later than the park, at 15:00 on days when the park
opens at 11:00.

### When does Oceana open?

According to Liseberg, in early 2027, with ticket sales from the end of November 2026.
