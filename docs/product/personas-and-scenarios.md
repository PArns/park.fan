# Personas & Scenarios — Product Gap Analysis

> **Status:** Draft for discussion · July 2026 · re-checked against the code 2026-09-21
> **Scope:** Six user personas for park.fan, walked through the full visit lifecycle
> (discovery → planning → final week → visit day → in park → after the visit),
> mapped against what the product ships today (frontend `v2.12.0`, API `v4.10.1`)
> and what is missing for each persona.
>
> Feature claims below were verified against the codebase (frontend `app/`,
> `components/`, `lib/` and backend `src/` entities/controllers), not against wishes.
>
> **What moved since July.** Four of the nine gaps changed state, and each one changes
> who the product can now serve: the trip planner shipped and orders a day by itself
> (G5), web push shipped as a channel with four planner topics plus ride and show
> alerts (G3), rider height and a wet-ride flag arrived in the API and are filterable
> on the park page (part of G1), and a service worker exists that deliberately caches
> nothing (G4). The rows below carry the re-checked verdict. §5 is new: it turns the
> result into the landing-page pilot order, because the answer to "which persona
> first" is no longer the same as it was in July.

## 3. Persona → biggest unmet need (TL;DR)

| Persona                | Served well today                                          | #1 gap                                                                                                                              |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| P1 Sandra (family)     | Crowd calendar, weather, best days, height filter, planner | **A way in that says the site plans for kids** — the data and the planner are there, no page addresses her                          |
| P2 Tim (enthusiast)    | P50/P90, rope drop, history, Fancast, planner              | **Multi-park comparison he can drive + ride log** (G6, G7)                                                                          |
| P3 Mehmet (local)      | Live status, nowcast, in-park view, ride and show alerts   | **The "green day" ping** — the alerts he can set are per ride and per show, never "today is worth the drive" (G3)                   |
| P4 Emily (first-timer) | Glossary, howto, i18n, planner, the park guides            | **A primer per park** rather than a blog post among blog posts, and in her language (G5a, G9)                                       |
| P5 Renate & Wolfgang   | Quiet-day data, the grandkids' half of the height question | **Accessibility & intensity metadata** — unchanged, and it is the half that is about the two of them (G1)                           |
| P6 Lena (drive-by)     | Fast SSR answer, SEO                                       | **Conversion to retained user** — the alerts to convert her to now exist; nothing on the page she lands on asks her to set one (G3) |

The pattern has shifted. In July the product was excellent at "when to go" and "what's happening now" and had nothing to offer a visitor planning _for someone else_ or needing _guidance_ rather than data. The planner, the height filter and push answered most of that. What is left is narrower and different in kind: **the capabilities landed and the ways in did not.** Sandra can plan for a 110 cm rider, and no page tells her so. Lena can be converted into a returning visitor by an alert, and the page she lands on never offers one. That is a landing-page and entry-point problem, which is what §5 is about — not another data investment.

### Suggested sequencing

Re-cut on 2026-09-21. Steps 1 and 3 of the July list are done or half done, and the order below says what that leaves.

1. **Ways in, not capabilities** (§5): one landing page per persona, starting with the persona whose capability shipped and whose entry point did not.
2. **Finish the half-gaps the shipped work exposed:** the green-day ping and the "your day changed" watcher (G3, the channel is built) · a visitor-driven park comparison (G6, the card exists) · a first-visit primer slot on the park page for the guides that already exist (G5a).
3. **Data investment (backend + curation):** the missing half of G1 — indoor/outdoor, accessibility, intensity. It is what P5 is waiting for and what the rain plan needs, and nothing else unblocks three scenarios at once.
4. **Platform step (design decision needed):** G2 for real, then G7. Push proved a device identity can carry a feature without an account; a ride log cannot be carried that way, because losing a browser would lose the count.
5. **Remaining quick win:** G8 event badges.

## 5. Landing-page pilot order

Decided for [PAR-355](https://linear.app/parkfan/issue/PAR-355) on 2026-09-21, and the input to PAR-356, PAR-357 and PAR-358.

### The criterion

A persona landing page earns its build where **the persona's need is met by the product but not addressed by a page**. That is a different test from "which persona brings the most traffic", and it fails in both directions: where the need is already met by a page, a second page competes with it for the same query; where the need is not met at all, the page promises something the product cannot do.

It is also not a new rule. [dedicated-landing-pages.md §2](../seo/dedicated-landing-pages.md) states the SEO half of it already — a new URL earns its place by server-rendering something no existing URL server-renders — and the test above is the same rule read from the product side, with "a need the product can serve" in place of "content no URL holds". Where they disagree, the SEO rule is the one with measurements behind it.

Run against the six, the test is decided by what shipped since July, not by who is biggest.

### The order

1. **P1 Sandra — the family trip planner.** The capability landed and the entry point did not. A visitor can filter a park's rides to a 110 cm child and read "23 of 40", tell the planner the shortest rider in the party and get a day ordered around them — and nothing on the site says any of that to somebody searching for a family day out. This is the only persona where a page would announce a feature that is finished, which makes it the cheapest page to write and the only one whose promise cannot outrun the product.
2. **P4 Emily — the first-timer.** Second because the material exists as the park guides and the glossary, and the work is arrangement rather than writing: a primer slot per park that opens the guide instead of listing it among posts. One caveat belongs in the ticket rather than in the build: the guides are seven in German and six everywhere else, and Emily reads Spanish.
3. **P5 Renate & Wolfgang.** Third and **blocked**, not deprioritised: the accessibility and intensity metadata their page would be built on is the missing half of G1. A page for them before that data lands would be a page about rides we cannot describe.

P2, P3 and P6 are deliberately **not** in the pilot, and each for its own reason:

- **P6 Lena** is the persona the traffic supports — search referrers are the largest inbound channel and Six Flags Great Adventure is the most-visited page through them. She is left out anyway, and not on a judgement call: the park page is `force-dynamic` and already server-renders her answer, and [dedicated-landing-pages.md §2](../seo/dedicated-landing-pages.md) settled what follows from that. A bare `/wartezeiten` page would be a duplicate with its own URL, competing with the park page for the query the park page already ranks for. Her gap is the second step, and since July the thing to convert her _to_ exists: an alert. That belongs on the page she already reaches — **and no ticket in this chain owns it.** PAR-357 reviews the hubs and names new landing pages as a non-goal, PAR-358 is the homepage. The park page's own conversion step is unowned, which is a hole worth a ticket rather than a line here.
- **P2 Tim** wants the comparison he can drive himself and a ride log. Both are features first; a page for him before them would be a page about a roadmap.
- **P3 Mehmet** is served live by the park page and by the alerts he can already set. What is missing for him is one notification that does not exist yet (the green-day ping), not a page.

### What would overturn this

The order above optimises for **conversion**: a visitor who does something the product can already do for them. If the goal is **visibility** instead — impressions and sessions, with conversion left to a later step — then P6 comes first. Stating that plainly is better than arguing it away, and it is worth knowing where the board already stands: PAR-371 is scheduled in the current cycle and builds the _historical_ statistics page per park, on the rule from [dedicated-landing-pages.md §2](../seo/dedicated-landing-pages.md) that a new URL earns its place by server-rendering something no existing URL server-renders. It is the visibility play, and it is deliberately not the live park-query page. So the two halves of the bet do not collide, and neither of them covers the alert nudge on the park page.
