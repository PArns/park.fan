Get out of the car in Staffordshire, walk across to the entrance, and you won't
see a roller coaster. Almost nothing of the park's eleven tracks is visible
from outside, and that isn't luck. The estate sits inside a **Conservation
Area**, and the planning condition that follows shapes everything built here:
nothing may rise above the treeline, and whatever comes close has to disappear
from the view outside. You can check it on
[Rita](ref:alton-towers/rita). The highest pieces of track are painted green.

So they build downwards. **[Nemesis Reborn](ref:alton-towers/nemesis-reborn)**
lies inside an excavated quarry, riders hanging under the track between rock
faces. **[Oblivion](ref:alton-towers/oblivion)** stands barely 19 metres tall
and still sends its train down 55 metres, because most of that drop is a hole
in the ground. And **[The Smiler](ref:alton-towers/the-smiler)** stacks
fourteen inversions into a tight footprint instead of building one tall hill.

There's a second reason. The villages of Alton and Farley are close by, and in
2004 a couple from Farley won a private prosecution against the park over
noise; the judge singled out Oblivion. The park was fined £5,000, later reduced
to £3,500, and served an order capping emissions at 32 decibels, raised to 40
on appeal. In response it removed the soundtrack that used to play before
Oblivion's drop, because it encouraged people to scream.

## Five times this park went first

That's what makes Alton Towers interesting to coaster fans, and the run spans
three decades.

It started in **1980** with the **Corkscrew**, Europe's first coaster with two
inversions back to back. It was also the moment the place became a theme park:
that same year the owner, John Broome, introduced a single admission price that
covered every ride. The Corkscrew ran until the end of the 2008 season; one of
its helices has stood on the entrance plaza as a monument since 2010.

**Nemesis** opened on **19 March 1994** as Europe's first inverted coaster,
built by Bolliger & Mabillard to a design by Werner Stengel from an idea by
park developer John Wardley. For B&M it was one of the first installations
outside the United States. 716 metres, 13 metres tall, 81 km/h, four
inversions. The ride sat out the whole of 2023 for a retrack, and since
**16 March 2024** it's been **Nemesis Reborn**.

```glossary-widget slug=inverted-coaster

```

**Oblivion** followed on **14 March 1998** at a cost of £12 million, the first
Dive Coaster anywhere. The train stops at the lip, hangs over the edge for one
to three seconds, then falls at **87.5 degrees** into a tunnel at 109 km/h and
4.5 g. The whole ride takes 75 seconds over 372 metres of track, and sixteen
people sit side by side in one car.

**Air**, now **[Galactica](ref:alton-towers/galactica)**, opened on **16 March
2002** as the first flying coaster B&M ever built. You lie face down under the
track and fly close to the ground, under footpaths and past rocks. 840 metres,
20 metres tall, 75 km/h, two inversions, 1,500 riders an hour. From 2016 to
2018 it ran with VR headsets, which came off again for 2019.

**[TH13TEEN](ref:alton-towers/th13teen)** was, on **20 March 2010**, the first
coaster with a vertical freefall drop in the middle of its layout. The train
rolls into a building, a shutter closes behind it, wood creaks, and then the
piece of track falls about five metres with the train and the floor still on
it. After that the train reverses into a tunnel. Intamin built it on the
Corkscrew's old plot.

And The Smiler has held the world record for the most inversions on a roller
coaster since **2013**: **fourteen**. Gerstlauer built it as an Infinity
Coaster for £18 million, 1,170 metres long, a 30-metre drop, 85 km/h, two
minutes forty-five.

## X-Sector, and what happened there in 2015

![On the right the grey and yellow tangle of The Smiler with several inversions, on the left Oblivion's descending track against woodland | On the left Oblivion heads into its tunnel, on the right The Smiler stacks its inversions. | wide](/media/alton-towers/oblivion-the-smiler.jpg)

Oblivion and The Smiler share an area, **X-Sector**, themed as a testing
facility. Before The Smiler, this plot held the **Black Hole**, a Jet Star
coaster under a tent.

On **2 June 2015** two trains collided on The Smiler. Sixteen people were
injured, and two riders later lost a leg. Merlin first described the crash as
human error, withdrew that account in court and pleaded guilty. The Health and
Safety Executive concluded the company had never put workable operating
procedures in place for the ride; the court spoke of a catalogue of errors and
imposed a **£5 million** fine. The coaster has been running again since 2016.

If this is news to you, better now than in the queue. What you'll find today is
a ride that has been back in service for ten years, with procedures the
regulator has been through since.

## The rest of the line-up

**[Wicker Man](ref:alton-towers/wicker-man)**, from **20 March 2018**, is the
newest big ride and was the first new wooden coaster in Britain in 22 years.
Great Coasters International built it for £16 million: 795 metres, a 22-metre
drop, 70 km/h, three and a half minutes. What sets it apart sits at the start
of the layout. A six-storey wooden figure stands there, and the train passes
straight through it while fire burns inside. Nobody had put flame and a wooden coaster together
before.


![A full train climbs steeply up a rust-red lattice track, grey supports in front of a blue sky | Rita accelerates at ground level to 98 km/h and only climbs afterwards. | right](/media/alton-towers/rita.jpg)

**Rita** has stood in the Dark Forest since **1 April 2005** and is an Intamin
hydraulic launch: **0 to 98.3 km/h in 2.5 seconds** at 4.7 g, and done after 25
seconds. It's themed as an abandoned drag strip, which turns the short ride
into the concept.

```glossary-widget slug=launch-coaster

```

**[Spinball Whizzer](ref:alton-towers/spinball-whizzer)**, from **2004**, is a
Maurer coaster with freely spinning cars, 450 metres long, 17 metres tall, 950
riders an hour. It was Sonic Spinball from 2010 to 2015 while Sega sponsored
it; before and since, it's a pinball machine whose silver cars are the balls.

The park's oldest coaster is the
**[Runaway Mine Train](ref:alton-towers/runaway-mine-train)** from **21 March
1992**, a powered Mack train in Katanga Canyon, 305 metres long, 36 km/h, 48
seats. It normally runs two circuits, and three on quiet days. The layout is
John Wardley's again.

CBeebies Land holds two children's coasters. **Octonauts Rollercoaster
Adventure** has stood on the old Beastie plot since 2015, and since **28 March
2026** there's **Bluey the Ride: Here Come The Grannies!**, a Zierer train with
28 seats on the ground the dismantled Postman Pat Parcel Post used to occupy.
The family's house from the series stands in the queue line to look at.

Four non-coasters are worth the wait.
**[Nemesis Sub-Terra](ref:alton-towers/nemesis-sub-terra)** is an indoor drop
tower that ran from 2012 to 2015 and reopened in 2023.
**[Toxicator](ref:alton-towers/toxicator)** arrived on **15 March 2025** as
Britain's first top spin since Ripsaw in 1997, raised on a concrete platform
almost five metres above the path.
**[Hex](ref:alton-towers/hex-the-legend-of-the-towers)** has been inside the
walls of the house itself since 2000: first a walkthrough with a narration,
then a Vekoma Madhouse where the room turns around the riders. The actor Jim
Carter reads the story. And the
**[Congo River Rapids](ref:alton-towers/congo-river-rapids)** are the rapids
ride that shares a tunnel with the mine train, built during the big expansion
between 1985 and 1988.

One change this year is a downgrade. At
**[Battle Galleons](ref:alton-towers/battle-galleons)** in Mutiny Bay the park
took the water cannons out during winter maintenance and fitted smoke effects
instead. The ride reopened on 23 May 2026, dry.

Height limits come in four steps. **140 cm** for Nemesis Reborn, Oblivion,
Galactica, Rita, The Smiler, Nemesis Sub-Terra and Toxicator; **120 cm** for
TH13TEEN, Wicker Man and Spinball Whizzer; **110 cm** for Congo River Rapids,
the Runaway Mine Train and Marauder's Mayhem; **90 cm** for the family rides
from Gangsta Granny to both children's coasters. Rita, Galactica, Toxicator,
TH13TEEN and Spinball Whizzer also have an upper limit just under two metres.
Children under 90 cm get in free.

### Fastrack

```glossary-widget slug=fastrack

```

If the day turns out to be one of the busy ones, part of the queueing can be
bought off. **Fastrack** comes in three tiers, and the differences are bigger
than the prices suggest.

**Bronze from £35** gives you five uses, freely chosen from fifteen
attractions. The Smiler and Wicker Man are **not** among them, which is to say
the two rides whose queues grow longest. **Silver from £65** covers seventeen
attractions including those two, once each. **Gold from £95** covers the same
seventeen all day with no limit. In every tier your ticket scans at most once
every three minutes, so you can't line up two rides at once.

CBeebies Land has had its own small package since 2026: **£7** for two rides,
**£10** for three, each attraction once. If the day turns busy while you're
there, the app sells single One Shot tickets for as long as they last.

## When to go

```stats-widget slug=alton-towers show=months,weekdays

```

The season runs from March to early November. After that the theme park closes
for winter while the waterpark, the hotels and the mini golf carry on, with a
Christmas programme in November and December. The park opens at 10am every day
and closes somewhere between 4pm and 9pm depending on the season and the
daylight.

The park names UK school holidays as its busiest periods: Easter, the summer
holidays and half terms. For anyone travelling from mainland Europe that's the
single most useful line in this guide, because the British holidays don't line
up with the German ones. The autumn half term in England falls in the second
half of October, so coming before it buys you the same Scarefest decor on one of
the weekend dates from 26 September, with fewer people in it.

### Scarefest: 26 September to 1 November 2026

The Halloween event is called **Scarefest** and runs on selected dates: 26 and
27 September, 2 to 4 October, 9 to 11 October, then daily from 16 October to
1 November. On those days the park stays open until **9pm**, and Nemesis
Reborn, Wicker Man, The Smiler, Oblivion and Toxicator run in the dark.

Four **scare mazes** cost extra: the new **Final Exhibit**, plus Edge of the
Forest, COMPOUND and Altonville Mine Tours: Tiny's Revenge. Two **scare zones**
are included in the day ticket, Dark Hollow in the woods and Burial Grounds.
Families get Amigos in the Afterlife and Trick O' Treat Town as paid add-ons
and three shows at no charge. New for 2026 is **Dine with the Dead**, a dinner
with performers working the room, ages 14 and up.

The sums are done through combination tickets: day entry plus all four thrill
mazes from **£80**, day entry plus the two family attractions from **£53**. The
day ticket on its own starts at £37 online on Scarefest dates. What's new this
year and how the dates fall in detail is in our report on
[Scarefest 2026](/blog/alton-towers-scarefest-2026).

Scarefest has carried that name since 2007. The park's first maze was **Terror
of the Towers** in 2002, and in 2006 there was no public Halloween programme at
all: that year brought one unadvertised maze called Room 13, for hotel guests
only.

The season closes with the **Fireworks** weekend on the Great Lawns. The noise
dispute left its mark here too: since a 2005 court order it's three dates a
year rather than a long run.

## From country seat to theme park

![A grey stately home with battlements, towers and glazed conservatories behind clipped yews and a lavender bed | The Towers and their conservatories. Hex runs inside the house. | wide](/media/alton-towers/the-towers.jpg)

The park is named after a house that still stands. An Iron Age camp came first,
and from **1412 to 1924** the estate belonged to one family: the Talbots, Earls
of Shrewsbury. Today's building is largely early 19th century and was remodelled
by **Augustus Welby Pugin**, the architect who also shaped the Palace of
Westminster. The banqueting hall window is roughly ten metres tall and six
wide, carries 27 panes in three tiers, and counts as the most important of its
kind outside Parliament.

The gardens first opened in **1860**, under the 18th Earl. Income in that first
year: £116 17s 5d. Thirty years later 30,000 people were turning up at once for
acrobats, lion tamers, elephants, bands and fireworks, and some of them for the
gardens.

**1924** ended the family's run. Most of the estate had gone in 1918, and the
remainder went to a group of local businessmen who formed Alton Towers Ltd.
During the Second World War the military requisitioned the buildings and only
handed them back in the mid-1950s, in a condition that ended up in a legal
argument. After that the site grew: a railway, a fairground, and by the
sixties a boating lake and a chairlift.

It became a theme park in **1980** under John Broome. Between 1985 and 1988
came today's entrance complex with Towers Street, the monorail, the car parks,
the Grand Canyon Rapids and the Skyride cable car, whose 1,048-metre run has
operated since 1987 and was refurbished in 2009. In **1990** Broome sold to
Britain's **Tussauds Group** after overreaching on the Battersea Power Station
development. Under Tussauds, 1992 to 1997 brought the Runaway Mine Train, The
Haunted House, Toyland Tours and Nemesis, and 1996 the first hotel.

In **2007** the investment firm Blackstone bought the Tussauds Group for
US$1.9 billion and merged it with **Merlin Entertainments**. Since then the
park has belonged to the same group as Heide Park, Thorpe Park and the LEGOLAND
Windsor Resort. Merlin doesn't own the land: property worth £622 million was
sold and leased back in 2007, the leases were extended by 35 years in December
2021 for a payment of £33.45 million, and in October 2022 the freeholds of
Alton Towers and Thorpe Park changed hands for £257 million together. For Alton
Towers alone, **£122,789,028** was recorded on 19 October 2022.

Attendance peaked a while ago. More than three million came in 1994, the year
of Nemesis, and again in 2010, the year of TH13TEEN. For 2019 the TEA report
puts the figure at **2,130,000**, up 1.4 per cent on 2018, making Alton Towers
Britain's second most visited theme park behind the LEGOLAND Windsor Resort.
The site holds 28,000 people at any one time.

What comes next has been the same project for years: an indoor attraction on
the former Alton Mouse plot, internally **Project Horizon**. The public
consultation ran in September 2022, approval with conditions came in June 2023,
construction hasn't started and the park gives no date. That it has a code name
at all is tradition. Big projects here have been called **Secret Weapon** and
numbered for decades. TH13TEEN was SW6, Wicker Man SW8.

## Staying at the park

From mainland Europe this isn't a day trip, and the park has five kinds of
accommodation on site.

The **Alton Towers Hotel** from 1996 has 180 rooms in thirteen categories and
is themed around a fictional explorer; the top two floors carry themes of their
own, from Gangsta Granny through Arabian Nights to a room named after The
Smiler. **Splash Landings Hotel** from 2003 has 216 rooms and a direct entrance
to the waterpark. The **CBeebies Land Hotel** from 2017 has 76 rooms in nine
themes, Bluey among them since recently. Then there's the **Enchanted Village**
from 2015 with 120 lodges and five treehouses, and the **Stargazing Pods** from
2019, 102 pods sleeping up to four each, with no restaurant of their own.

The practical gain is the usual one: standing at the turnstile at ten rather
than sitting on the A50 at ten. Merlin adds a concrete one on top that's easy
to miss. Resort guests park free, and CBeebies Land Hotel guests get Express
parking at no charge. For a family arriving in two cars that's a visible slice
of the room rate.

A short break with a night, breakfast and a day in the park starts at **£77**
per person, or £100 on Scarefest dates.

## A day at Alton, roughly planned

Be at the monorail or the gate at **9:30am** so you're inside at ten. Head
first into the **Dark Forest** for Rita and TH13TEEN, because both are on our
rope drop list and both sit over the first rise from the entrance. **Nemesis
Reborn** fits straight after, **Galactica** right next door.

**X-Sector**, with Oblivion and The Smiler, is the longest walk and pays off in
the late morning, once the first families have settled into CBeebies Land. Save
**Wicker Man** for the afternoon, or ride it in the dark if you're there on a
Scarefest date: the fire in the figure reads differently at night.

The **Skyride** isn't only a view, it's a shortcut. It links Towers Street, the
Forbidden Valley and the area by the house, and on a site with these gradients
that saves real time. The monorail runs until an hour after the last ride
closes, so there's no need to rush at the end of the day.

With small children the day inverts: CBeebies Land first while it's empty, then
Mutiny Bay and Katanga Canyon, and the afternoon belongs to the shows.

## Practicalities: getting there, tickets, opening times

**Getting there.** Alton, Staffordshire, ST10 4DB. The park sits between the M1
and the M6; the nearest exits are M1 northbound junction 23a, M1 southbound 28,
M6 northbound 15 and M6 southbound 16. The park asks you to follow the road
signs rather than the sat nav for the last few miles, because some devices send
you down a farm track off the B5417. The nearest stations are **Uttoxeter**
(16 km, 20 minutes by taxi, 30 by bus), **Stoke-on-Trent** (24 km, about 25
minutes) and **Derby** (40 km, 50 to 60 minutes). D&G Bus runs the 32X from
Hanley via Cheadle to the resort, and at weekends there are coaches from
Birmingham, Manchester, London and a dozen more towns.

**Parking.** Standard is **£13** online, £15 on the day, and a 15 to 25 minute
walk. **Express from £25** is a one to three minute walk. Blue Badge parking is
charged at the standard price. Gold, Platinum and Premium passholders and
resort guests park free. Electric cars will find 26 charging points: ten on Car
Park 1, ten at Splash Landings Hotel, six at the Alton Towers Hotel. Play the
mini golf and your standard parking is refunded against the validated
scorecard.

**Tickets.** From **£32** online, **£68** at the gate. The gap is wide enough
that booking ahead isn't just worth it, it's the design: the park requires a
booking for every visit day, passholders included, and sold-out dates drop off
the calendar. Two days cost from £44, so twelve pounds for the second. The
Alton Towers annual pass is **£64**; the Merlin Essential Pass, covering more
than twenty UK attractions, is £139. Parents with toddlers get their own ticket
from £29, which excludes weekends and school holidays. The waterpark is
separate, from £23.

**Food.** Our park page lists 38 food outlets. The best known is the
**Rollercoaster Restaurant** from 2016, where orders arrive by rail; it's open
until 9:30pm and can be used without a park ticket in the evening, except on
Scarefest and Fireworks dates.

**Accessibility.** Merlin changed the eligibility rules for the **Ride Access
Pass** in early 2026, removing visitors whose main difficulty is coping with
crowds. After criticism from disability organisations and national coverage,
the company called the change a temporary trial and announced a review. If you
need the pass, check the current terms before you book.

## Frequently asked questions about Alton Towers

### Which Alton Towers coaster is the famous one?

Nemesis Reborn, which opened in 1994 as Nemesis and was Europe's first inverted
coaster. It lies in an excavated quarry, runs 716 metres, stands 13 metres tall
and reaches 81 km/h with four inversions. It was retracked over the whole of
2023 and has been Nemesis Reborn since 16 March 2024.

### How many roller coasters does Alton Towers have?

Eleven. Seven are built for adults and older children: Nemesis Reborn,
Oblivion, Galactica, Rita, TH13TEEN, The Smiler and Wicker Man. Then there's
Spinball Whizzer, the powered Runaway Mine Train and the two children's
coasters in CBeebies Land.

### What does entry to Alton Towers cost?

From £32 per person online and £68 at the gate. On Scarefest dates the day
ticket starts at £37. Two days cost from £44 and the annual pass £64. Children
under 90 cm go in free but, like everybody else, need a booking for the day.
Current prices are at
[altontowers.com](https://www.altontowers.com/tickets-passes/day-passes/).

### When is Alton Towers open in 2026?

March to early November, daily from 10am. Closing is between 4pm and 9pm
depending on the season, and 9pm on the Scarefest dates up to 1 November. The
theme park doesn't run in winter, though the waterpark and hotels do. The
published calendar is on the
[park page](ref:alton-towers).

### When is Alton Towers quietest?

Outside the British school holidays, and on a weekday within those. The English
holidays fall differently from the German ones, and the autumn half term lands
in the second half of October. Which of the coming opening days will be quietest is
worked out daily by the widget above.

### Is Fastrack worth it at Alton Towers?

It depends on the tier. Bronze at £35 includes neither The Smiler nor Wicker
Man, the two rides with the longest queues. If they're why you came, you need
Silver at £65 or Gold at £95. On a weekday outside the holidays an early start
usually does the job.

### How tall does your child need to be?

Nemesis Reborn, Oblivion, Galactica, Rita, The Smiler, Nemesis Sub-Terra and
Toxicator all require 140 cm. TH13TEEN, Wicker Man and Spinball Whizzer start
at 120 cm, Congo River Rapids and the Runaway Mine Train at 110 cm, and the
family rides at 90 cm. Between 90 and 140 centimetres there are a few years
where a child can ride plenty, just none of the seven big ones.

### How safe is The Smiler after the 2015 crash?

On 2 June 2015 two trains collided, sixteen people were injured and two riders
lost a leg. The Health and Safety Executive traced the crash to missing
operating procedures at Merlin, the company pleaded guilty and paid a
£5 million fine. The ride has been running again since 2016, with changed
procedures and under regulatory oversight.

### Why can't you see a coaster from outside?

The estate is protected as a Conservation Area, and nothing built here may rise
above the treeline. What has to go high gets camouflaged: on Rita the topmost
track is painted green. That's why the rides sit in dips, quarries and tunnels
instead of on hills.

## What I'd take away from Staffordshire

The condition to stay under the treeline sounds like a restriction and is the
reason this park looks the way it does. Where others build up, Alton Towers had
to build down, and out of that came a quarry full of steel, a tunnel under a
vertical drop, and fourteen inversions on the footprint of a sports hall.

The walk from the car park is long, the paths inside are hilly, and the half
hour between 9:30 and 10 decides the morning. If you're coming from Germany,
check the English school holidays before you book, not your own.

And if you're uneasy about The Smiler: in 2013, whether fourteen inversions in
a row were possible was a question nobody had asked. It's still the world
record.
