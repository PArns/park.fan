I’ve been going to [Phantasialand](ref:phantasialand) for over thirty years. My
parents pushed me around its Märchensee in a stroller, my father and I glided
through an open storybook in the “1001 Nacht” gondola long before it was torn
down, and I invented [park.fan](/blog/welcome-to-park-fan-blog) here, in a sixty-minute Taron queue.

![A lit swing carousel turning, a light tree at the left | Wintertraum, Phantasialand’s winter season. | wide](/media/phantasialand/background-16x9.jpg)

## Seven themed areas in a tight space

![Taron in the basalt village of Klugheim | Taron races through the basalt village of Klugheim, and the village doubles as the queue. | right](/media/phantasialand/taron-4x3.jpg)

Phantasialand has won the Parkscout plus award as “Europe’s most immersive theme
park” eight years running. The queue for [Taron](ref:phantasialand/taron) is
Klugheim, a basalt village with crooked roofs and weathered walls. From inside the village you don’t get
to see much of the coaster until late.

The park counts seven themed areas, and none of them bleeds into the next:
Berlin in its Golden Twenties look at the entrance, China Town with its temples,
Deep in Africa with mud huts, Mexico with Maya ruins, Fantasy with Wuze Town on
the Mondsee, Mystery with the basalt village of Klugheim and, youngest of all,
Victorian Rookburgh. With car parks and hotels the site covers about 28
hectares; the park itself is a good deal smaller. Each area has its own music
and its own smell, and in summer you notice the smell most outside the churros
stand in China Town.

Because the park is so compact, on a busy day everyone wants the same two or three rides at the same
time. In [the art of waiting](/blog/the-art-of-waiting) we worked out why
[Taron](ref:phantasialand/taron) runs into saturation on a holiday Saturday just
as fast as Peter Pan’s Flight in Paris. [Europa-Park](ref:europa-park) spreads its visitors over fourteen roller
coasters. Phantasialand gets about a third of Europa-Park’s visitors, and by our
measurements you still queue longer here. On top of that, the park prices its
tickets so aggressively that it fills plenty of weekends too.

## From a brown-coal pit to a basalt village

It all began in 1967, in a mined-out brown-coal pit on the edge of Brühl.
Gottlieb Löffelhardt, a born Berliner, and Richard Schmidt, who had performed as
an artiste on the stage of Berlin’s Wintergarten variety theatre and produced
numerous puppet TV plays, had met in Berlin after the war, and they put their
money into it. What the two wanted to make of the worked-out pit called
“Berggeist” was a **Märchenwald** (fairy-tale forest): a quiet place for
families and grandparents, with puppets from Schmidt’s collection, some forty
fairy-tale scenes and plenty of greenery. The gates opened on 30 April 1967.

The two built it for 2.5 million marks, and 400,000 visitors came in the first
season. In 1970 Alt-Berlin opened at the new main entrance, a copy of Unter den
Linden with the Neptune Fountain and a half-scale Brandenburg Gate, and the 1001
Nacht gondola ride opened the same year. From 1972 the western town of Silver
City went up on new land, with Germany’s first log flume. The
Phantasialand-Jet monorail ran over the park from 1974. The first roller coaster,
Schwarzkopf’s Bobbahn 1 (later the Gebirgsbahn), arrived in 1975, and in 1978
the park put Bobbahn 2, later the Grand-Canyon-Bahn, on the same plot. Because of
the noise, both were wrapped in an artificial mountain.

China Town opened in 1981, built from 1.3 million parts the park had made in
Taiwan. The site has not grown since, and everything new replaced something old.
The Silbermine dark ride followed in Silver City in 1984, Vekoma’s indoor coaster
Space Center in 1988, and in 1994 the Galaxy simulator under a golden dome that
was torn down in 2016. The Space Center was renamed Temple of the Night Hawk in
2001 and has run with VR headsets as Crazy Bats since June 2019.

![F.L.Y. in Rookburgh | Rookburgh with F.L.Y. (2020), the world’s first flying launch coaster. | left](/media/phantasialand/fly-4x3.jpg)

1996 brought the mine train
[Colorado Adventure](ref:phantasialand/colorado-adventure), and a few weeks after
it opened, Michael Jackson christened it in person. On **1 May 2001** came the
fire. A technical fault on the Grand Canyon ride set light to the artificial
mountain that also housed the Gebirgsbahn coaster, and the flames spread to the
Tanagra theatre and parts of the western town. Around 20,000 guests were in the
park that day, 63 were injured, and the park stayed shut for 15 days.

Wuze Town, with the two spinning coasters
[Winja’s Fear](ref:phantasialand/winjas-fear) & Force, had been due to open in
2001 and arrived a year late. In that same year, 2002, the replacements for
what had burned were ready: the rapids ride
[River Quest](ref:phantasialand/river-quest) and the Feng Ju Palace madhouse,
both still there today.

In 2006 Phantasialand opened **Deep in Africa** and the inverted coaster
[Black Mamba](ref:phantasialand/black-mamba), the park’s first coaster with
inversions, laying out a complete African world with mud huts and watercourses
and the ride set low among the rocks. In 2016 came the basalt village Klugheim
with [Taron](ref:phantasialand/taron), in 2020 the Victorian aviator city
Rookburgh with [F.L.Y.](ref:phantasialand/fly), the world’s first flying launch
coaster according to the park. In both cases the themed area came first and the
ride was built into it.

To this day, Phantasialand has remained in **family hands**. In 1998 Gottlieb
Löffelhardt’s son Robert took over as sole managing director, three years after
buying out co-founder Richard Schmidt’s share. Movie
Park Germany, an hour’s drive north, has an owner that belongs to a private
equity firm, and that firm
[has been looking for a buyer since September 2026](/blog/parques-reunidos-sale-movie-park).

The TEA/AECOM Theme Index lists Phantasialand with 2.05 million visitors for 2019
and 2.1 million for 2022, 14th among Europe’s parks. For 2024 Blooloop, citing
the TEA index, again gives 2.1 million and 14th place.

The park has been fighting for an expansion since 2003, and in July 2026
Brühl’s planning committee opened the formal planning procedure for about 15
hectares at the Ententeich nature reserve. Opening it approves nothing yet.
Surveys on nature, traffic and flood protection come first, and since 28
September a network of conservationists has been collecting signatures for a [citizens’ petition](/news/phantasialand-expansion-petition) against it. According to the park, the plans include an
aquapark hotel resort, rides and theatres.

## The headliners

- **[Taron](ref:phantasialand/taron)**, for me the best launch coaster in
  Germany. Two launches, and the track runs close past the basalt columns of Klugheim. Between the two launches the train briefly slows down.
- **[F.L.Y.](ref:phantasialand/fly)**, a flying coaster in Rookburgh, which for me is the most densely designed themed area in the park. On the way to the ride you walk through an aviator workshop that is pure set.
- **[Black Mamba](ref:phantasialand/black-mamba)**, the invert in Deep in
  Africa, running since 2006. It threads so close past rocks, huts and treetops
  that on every element you’re sure you’re about to clip something.
- **[Chiapas](ref:phantasialand/chiapas-die-wasserbahn)**, which at 53 degrees
  has the steepest drop of any log flume in the world, the park says, plus a drop taken backwards and a soundtrack.
- **[Colorado Adventure](ref:phantasialand/colorado-adventure)**, the 1996 mine
  train that Michael Jackson christened shortly after it opened. Family-friendly,
  but fast.
- **[Talocan](ref:phantasialand/talocan)**, a top spin with fire and water
  effects in a temple set.
- **[Winja’s Fear](ref:phantasialand/winjas-fear)** & Winja’s Force, the two
  spinning coasters in Wuze Town. A vertical lift takes you up, and halfway
  round a section of track tips with the car on it, forwards on Fear, sideways on
  Force. The cars spin freely.
- **[Mystery Castle](ref:phantasialand/mystery-castle)**, the 65-metre tower in
  Mystery that shoots you up before it drops you, with changing ride programs and
  a pre-show that for me makes up half the terror.

For children there’s [Raik](ref:phantasialand/raik), the family boomerang in
Klugheim that runs forwards and backwards through the basalt gorge. It works
well as a first “real” coaster.

## Tickets and annual passes

There’s no box office any more. Phantasialand sells exclusively **dated
online tickets**, you book a specific visit day in advance, and without that
e-ticket on your phone you may well find yourself standing at a locked gate.

The prices are **dynamic**. Each price tier has a limited allocation per day,
and the closer the day and the more popular it is, the more you pay. The ticket
calendar in the online shop shows three price tiers for October and November
2026: **€35, €59 and €78**, more than double the price for the same park day.
The shop advertises tickets from **€44**. You can still go on a whim, but only
online and only until 2 p.m. on the day itself, at that day’s price and only if
the day hasn’t sold out.

There are no more annual passes. Phantasialand took its Club-Karte off sale
during the pandemic and never brought it back; the last ones expired in summer 2022. That makes it the exception among
[Germany’s big parks](/blog/best-theme-parks-in-germany); Europa-Park, for one,
still sells its ResortPass, even if the allocations go fast. In 2022 fans
launched **several
[petitions to bring them back](https://www.change.org/p/wiedereinf%C3%BChrung-der-phantasialand-jahreskarten)**,
and nothing has changed so far. If you come often, you pay for a dated day
ticket at that day’s price every visit. That’s one of the few things I seriously
hold against my favorite park.

In the last year or two I’ve noticed distinctly more **vandalism** in the park
than in all the years before. Whether that is connected to the end of the annual
passes and the ticket prices I don’t know, and I have no numbers on it.

## When Phantasialand wait times are shortest

A school-holiday day in NRW with sun in the forecast is
reliably full.

A few rules of thumb that almost always hold:

- **Weekdays beat weekends.** Monday to Friday come out level in our
  measurements, with Saturday and Sunday above them (measured since late
  December 2025). Which parks do have a quieter weekday is on the [best time to visit page](/best-time-to-visit).
- **Avoid NRW school holidays**, especially the summer and autumn breaks. The
  Cologne/Bonn/Ruhr catchment is large.
- **Bridge days are almost never a good idea.**
- Even Saturdays outside the holidays are often quite full, because of the
  dynamic ticket pricing.

We fold holiday calendars, weather and opening hours together with the park’s
wait-time history into one crowd level per day, from very low to extreme.

## Quick Pass, single or Ultimate

The **Quick Pass** lets you skip the regular line. Since Wintertraum 2025/26 it
works completely differently. There are two variants:

- **Quick Pass (Single)**, the fast track for **one ride** on one specific
  attraction. At launch the price was billed as _dynamic_, rising with the
  current wait time. I always saw the same price at the counter anyway, and by
  now the park’s information page lists fixed prices too: €5 for
  [Colorado Adventure](ref:phantasialand/colorado-adventure),
  [Die 3 Mausketiere](ref:phantasialand/die-3-mausketiere),
  [Maus au Chocolat](ref:phantasialand/maus-au-chocolat),
  [Mystery Castle](ref:phantasialand/mystery-castle) and
  [Talocan](ref:phantasialand/talocan), €7 each for
  [Winja’s Fear](ref:phantasialand/winjas-fear) and Winja’s Force, €10 for
  [Black Mamba](ref:phantasialand/black-mamba) and
  [Chiapas](ref:phantasialand/chiapas-die-wasserbahn), €12 for
  [River Quest](ref:phantasialand/river-quest) and
  [Taron](ref:phantasialand/taron).
- **Quick Pass Ultimate** for €80: one go on each of those eleven rides plus one
  on [F.L.Y.](ref:phantasialand/fly). **Only Ultimate gets you onto F.L.Y. with a
  Quick Pass**; you can’t buy it singly. All eleven single passes add up to €83,
  so Ultimate is cheaper and throws F.L.Y. in on top. It only pays off if you
  really want to do the lot, though.

Even with the Quick Pass you still wait at F.L.Y., up top, in the station.
The elaborate flying restraints make loading slow, and the fast track doesn’t
make that jam go away.

You can only buy it on the spot, at Guest Services on Kaiserplatz, and there
aren’t many. During Wintertraum in particular, the Quick Passes are often sold
out by midday on weekends.

After many visits, I think **on a green day you don’t need it.** Arrive early,
ride in a sensible order, and you’ll get the headliners with short waits. On a
deep-red holiday Saturday, on the other hand, it can save the day. Before you
buy, check the live wait times, and if the numbers are low, keep the money.

## The order you ride in

In the summer season the gates open at 9 a.m., and by 10
every ride is running. In the first hour the lanes are still empty and most
rides have lines shorter than they will be all day.

My order, which I rebuilt in one place after our hourly data:

1. **Straight to [Taron](ref:phantasialand/taron)** in Klugheim. The
   multi-launch coaster has the longest line in the park, and it is as long in
   the first hour as it is at midday. The head start belongs to whoever is at
   the front at 10 sharp; turn up at half past and you might as well ride it
   after lunch.
2. **Then the water rides**
   ([Chiapas](ref:phantasialand/chiapas-die-wasserbahn),
   [River Quest](ref:phantasialand/river-quest)). They have the biggest morning
   advantage in the park. In the first hour Chiapas has barely more than half its
   midday line, and River Quest is never that short again after eleven.
3. **[F.L.Y.](ref:phantasialand/fly) in the late afternoon.** I used to ride it
   straight after Taron because the station dispatches so slowly. In our data,
   though, its line peaks at 11 a.m. and is shortest in the late afternoon. Walk
   from Taron straight to Rookburgh and you land right in the peak.
4. **Put shows and meals into the peak** (around midday), when the lines
   are longest anyway. If you’ve already seen a show, use it the other way
   round: while it runs, a whole theatre full of people is sitting inside and
   not in the line ahead of you.

## Food

Three restaurants I recommend:

- **Rutmor’s Taverne** (Klugheim) is my favorite. À la carte, rustic down to the
  carved wooden benches, with hearty cooking: Eierknöpfli (spätzle), dumplings,
  veal schnitzel with fried potatoes, sharing boards of cold cuts and
  Kaiserschmarrn, for two also served in a giant pan.
- **Restaurant Uhrwerk** (Rookburgh) is its steampunk sibling, with burgers,
  homemade fries, pasta and bowls in a setting of gears and brass and a view of
  [F.L.Y.](ref:phantasialand/fly). Mornings and evenings it’s reserved for guests
  of the Charles Lindbergh, but by day anyone can go in.
- **Phenie’s Shakes & Pancakes** (Fantasy, on the Mondsee) has been open since
  July 2025 and is my obligatory sweet stop, with pancake towers and shakes.

In between, my snack is the **churros**, which are sold all year round.

**Eat against the flow**, around 11:30 a.m., before the restaurants fill up.

## The small things I keep coming back for

There was the **Märchenwald** around the Märchensee, some forty built scenes:
Little Red Riding Hood, Cinderella, Hansel and Gretel, each in its own little
set, each with figures that told a whole story once you pressed the switch. As a
small boy I rolled past in my stroller, convinced the figures would come to life
if only I looked long enough. The park tore the Märchenwald down in autumn 2007,
and the ground is part of the Wuze world in Fantasy now. What’s left is the
temple from “Little Muck”, which the water Wuze now use as an observatory, and a
tree with a face.

There’s the **[Maus au Chocolat](ref:phantasialand/maus-au-chocolat)**, the
interactive dark ride where my girlfriend and I have been competing for the high
score for years. She regularly snatches the 5,000-point mice away from me. In
the second room there’s a 10,000-point mouse that I only found after what felt
like a hundred rides; I’m not saying where it is. Right next door, since October
2025, the Residenz Theater has been showing the 4D film
**[Die 3 Mausketiere](ref:phantasialand/die-3-mausketiere)**: three mice against
a rat boss, motion seats and an LED screen instead of a projector. The same
building, then called the Schauspielhaus, showed “Pirates 4D” from 1999 until
early 2025, and the new film continues this corner’s mouse theme.

![The Geister Rikscha in China Town | Even a dark ride like the Geister Rikscha is set dressing from start to finish. | left](/media/phantasialand/geister-rikscha-4x3.jpg)

The [Geister Rikscha](ref:phantasialand/geister-rikscha) has been running
through China Town since 1981.

My favorite time is the evening in **Klugheim during the blue hour**, when the
lights come on, [Taron](ref:phantasialand/taron) runs overhead through the basalt
columns and the last of the sun turns the rocks orange.

## Staying over: the three themed hotels

If you’re planning two days, there are three elaborately themed hotels right at
the park: **Charles Lindbergh** (Victorian steampunk, right in the middle of
Rookburgh, guests from age 8 only), Ling Bao (imperial China Town) and Matamba
(African, by Deep in Africa). In the evening there’s the dinner show Fantissima.

Unlike a hotel at [Europa-Park](ref:europa-park), a Phantasialand hotel does **not** get you **early park entry**. Guests of the
**Charles Lindbergh** get **faster access to [F.L.Y.](ref:phantasialand/fly) once
a day** with their hotel ticket, through a separate entrance; on the usual
package of one night and two park days, that makes two rides. F.L.Y. has the
slowest dispatch in the park, and apart from the Quick Pass Ultimate this is the
only comfortable way onto it. At the Matamba and the Ling Bao you get one faster
entry per person per night on one of five rides (Chiapas, Maus au Chocolat,
Talocan, Winja’s or Colorado Adventure), but only with a park ticket bought
separately.

## Getting there & parking

Phantasialand sits in Brühl, between Cologne and Bonn.

- **By car:** via the A553, exit Brühl-Süd. The park warns that sat-navs like to
  suggest the Bornheim exit, which is the wrong one. You park in the _Mystery_
  (P1) or _China_ (P2) lots for **€8 per car per day**; hotel guests park free at
  their hotel.
- **By train:** take the RE5, RB26 or RB48 to **Brühl (Rheinland)**, or light
  rail **line 18** between Cologne and Bonn to **Brühl Mitte**. From both stops a
  **shuttle bus** runs to the park, about a quarter of an hour from Brühl Mitte
  and about 20 minutes from the station. The return trip costs €6 per person;
  children up to 1.00 m as well as wheelchair users and blind guests with a pass
  ride free, and transit passes aren’t valid on the shuttle. The published
  timetable runs until 13 November 2026. Without the shuttle, bus 985 goes to
  Berggeiststraße, about ten minutes’ walk from the park.

On busy days train plus shuttle is easier, because in the evening you aren’t
stuck in the car park exit with a few thousand other people.

## Wintertraum: lights and Taron in the dark

In our [Halloween guide](/blog/halloween-theme-parks-2026) we counted
Phantasialand among the “refuseniks”, because it deliberately doesn’t do a scare
festival. In winter it has the
**[Wintertraum](https://www.phantasialand.de/de/themenpark/wintertraum/)**
instead.

In 2026 there’s no break between summer and winter. The summer season runs
until 13 November, and on the 14th the park reopens as a winter village, with
millions of lights according to the park, decorative snow, fir trees, market
stalls and mulled wine, seven shows with the “Magic Symphony” finale every evening at around
7:50 p.m. on Kaiserplatz (it can be cancelled in bad weather), and an ice rink in
front of the big Christmas tree on Kaiserplatz.

Only in winter does Kaiserplatz serve flamed salmon, a kale pan and hot crumble.
My favorite is still the **flat croissants** from China Town, crisply pressed
and buttery. They aren’t a winter-only bake, mind you; the park sells them
outside Wintertraum too.

In winter it gets dark early, and I like **Taron in the dark** best, when it
races through the lit-up Klugheim and the basalt columns are reduced to shadows
against the night sky. That’s exactly when everyone wants on, though. Buy the
**Quick Pass early in the day**, then ride
Taron in the dark as often as you can.

Wintertraum 2026/27 runs from **14 November 2026 to 24 January 2027**, daily
from **11 a.m. to 8 p.m.** The park is closed on 24 and 25 December 2026 and on
1, 12 and 13 January 2027; on New Year’s Eve it’s open only until 6 p.m. As in
summer, tickets run through dated online day tickets, and, according to the
park, advance tickets from €30 were on sale until 12 October.

One exception is 22 November, [Totensonntag at Phantasialand](/blog/phantasialand-totensonntag), Germany’s Sunday of the Dead. The park still opens from 11 a.m. to 8 p.m., but because it’s a quiet public holiday, shows and outdoor music only start at 6 p.m.

- **Not everything runs.** Taron, F.L.Y., Black Mamba and the Winja’s keep
  running, and [Chiapas](ref:phantasialand/chiapas-die-wasserbahn) runs during
  Wintertraum too, the park says, down to minus five degrees (Taron down to minus
  ten). [River Quest](ref:phantasialand/river-quest), Wakobato and Wözl’s
  Wassertreter are closed in winter, and in extreme weather any outdoor ride can
  shut at short notice. If you ride Chiapas in December, pack a change of
  clothes.
- **Plan the weekends like holiday days.** Our calendar expects heavy crowds on
  the first Wintertraum weekends and light ones on December weekdays. We have
  only measured Wintertraum on a handful of days so far, between late December
  2025 and January 2026, and those were busy. For anything but the weekends,
  check the crowd calendar from above first.

## Frequently asked questions about wait times & visiting

### When are the shortest wait times at Phantasialand?

On weekdays outside the NRW school holidays; Monday to Friday come out level in
our data, and the quietest month we have measured was September, after the
summer holidays (measured since late December 2025). The
[crowd calendar for Phantasialand](ref:phantasialand?calendar) has an expected
crowd level for every day the park has published opening hours for; right now
that’s up to 24 January 2027.

### How do I avoid long wait times at Phantasialand?

First **pick the right day** (it matters most), then
**be there early** (Taron at 10 sharp, then the water rides), and **go against
the flow** (F.L.Y. in the late afternoon, coasters during the shows). Check the
live wait times on the go on the [Phantasialand park page](ref:phantasialand).

### Which attraction has the longest wait times?

[Taron](ref:phantasialand/taron). Behind it,
[Chiapas](ref:phantasialand/chiapas-die-wasserbahn) and
[F.L.Y.](ref:phantasialand/fly) are level (measured since late December 2025).
That’s why you ride Taron right at opening, Chiapas in the first hour and F.L.Y.
in the late afternoon, when its line is at its shortest.

### Do you need a Quick Pass at Phantasialand?

On a quiet (green) day, no; arrive early and you’ll ride everything with short
waits. On a busy day it can rescue Taron and F.L.Y. for you. Taron costs €12 as a
single pass, and F.L.Y. only comes with the €80 Ultimate.

### Are there still annual passes at Phantasialand?

No. Phantasialand took its Club-Karte off sale during the pandemic, the last
ones expired in summer 2022, and despite several fan petitions it hasn’t come
back. Every visit runs through a dated online day ticket whose price rises
dynamically with demand.

### Can you do Phantasialand in one day?

Yes. With a well-chosen day and the order above, everything important fits into
one day. Two days are more relaxed.
