[Liseberg](ref:liseberg) in Gothenburg opens Jul på Liseberg, its Christmas season, on Saturday 14 November. This year it adds a light trail of about a kilometre that loops right round the hill inside the park. It's called Luma Celestia and cost SEK 8.6 million, more than Liseberg has ever spent on a single Christmas project. The season runs until 30 December.

## The route through Luma Celestia

The trail runs from the Luna Park area over to Lustgården and back. There is no fixed route, so you pick your own way between the stations. The large installations along it take their names from the night sky and old legends: Norrsken (northern lights), Tre Månar (three moons), Sägnernas Eld (fire of the legends), Himmelstrappan (stairway to heaven) and Stjärnregn (star rain). Luma Celestia also includes "Den fallna stjärnan", the fallen star, a show with aerial acrobatics and light.

## Opening days and hours

- Saturday 14 November: noon to 10 pm; Sunday 15 November: noon to 8 pm
- every weekend from 20 November: Fridays 3 pm to 10 pm, Saturdays noon to 10 pm, Sundays noon to 8 pm
- in December up to the 20th: Wednesdays and Thursdays 3 pm to 9 pm, Fridays 3 pm to 10 pm, Saturdays noon to 10 pm, Sundays noon to 8 pm, and on 20 December until 9 pm
- Monday 21 to Wednesday 23 December: noon to 9 pm
- Saturday 26 to Wednesday 30 December: noon to 8 pm

Up to 20 December the park stays shut on Mondays and Tuesdays. It's also closed on Christmas Eve and Christmas Day, when the Santa from Tomtehuset presumably has other deliveries to make. From 31 December to 10 January, only Kaninlandet, the children's area, and the ice rink open, daily from 11 am to 5 pm. For Balder and the other big rides you'll then have to try one of the [parks that stay open over the winter](/blog/winter-theme-parks-2026).

## Tickets and prices

The first tickets went on sale on 3 November. Until 9 November, entry with an Åkpass is half price online. Liseberg releases the rest of the tickets in the week of 9 to 15 November. Buying online always works out cheaper than at the gate. The regular price depends on the day:

- Entry: SEK 125, 165 or 195
- Entry with Åkpass: SEK 285, 345 or 395
- Åkpass on its own: SEK 235; Åkpass Duo: SEK 470

The cheapest days are 15, 20, 22 and 27 November, plus the Wednesdays and Thursdays in December up to the 17th. You pay the most on 28 November, on the Saturdays and Sundays in December from the 5th and from 21 to 23 December. Children under 110 centimetres get in without an entry ticket, and their Åkpass starts at SEK 235. If a child wears the Åkpass Duo on their wrist, any adult can ride with them.

Without an Åkpass, you pay for each ride with coupons at SEK 25 each. [Balder](ref:liseberg/balder) takes four of them, so for more than two rides on Balder, the Åkpass comes out cheaper. The Lisebergspasset costs SEK 395 and covers entry for twelve months; the Guldpasset costs SEK 2,595 and covers entry and every ride for twelve months.

## Which rides run in winter

Entry includes Tomtehuset with Santa, the ice rink, Barnens Paradis (from age four) and Blomsterkarusellen. The big rides need an Åkpass or coupons:

- Balder: from 130 cm and age 7
- Valkyria: from 132 cm and age 7
- Loke: from 130 cm and age 7
- Mechanica: from 140 cm and age 10
- Kraftverket: from 130 cm, or 120 cm with an adult
- AeroSpin: from 130 cm
- Stormvåg: from 120 cm, or 95 cm with an adult
- Lisebergshjulet: from 130 cm, no limit with an adult

[Helix](ref:liseberg/helix) and Lisebergbanan aren't on the park's winter list. For the summer there is our [Liseberg guide](/blog/liseberg-wait-times-tips), with the queues for every ride and the virtual queue.

## Tomtebyn, markets and snow made of paper

For children there's Tomtebyn, an elf village between the harbour area and Valkyria. The elves sing and dance with the children, and anyone who brings a wish list can hand it straight to Santa.

Three Christmas markets share 46 stalls. Tyrolermarknad, with après-ski, sits next to Slänggungan and the [Radiobilarna, which stopped running in October](/news/liseberg-radiobilarna-closed). Gammaldags jul is an old-style market in Kvarteret Storgatan, and Vintermarknad stands by Stora Scenen next to the ice rink. The park puts up more than 2,100 Christmas trees and hangs 3.3 kilometres of fir garland. The seven tonnes of artificial snow are made from paper pulp, so for snow Liseberg trusts the paper industry more than a Gothenburg December. On top of the five million lights from last winter's 25th anniversary, Liseberg is hanging more than a million more this year. The park's 116-metre Christmas tree can be seen all over Gothenburg.

The park's own Grand Curiosa Hotel sells a package of one night, entry and julbord, the Swedish Christmas buffet. It can be booked on selected days from 14 November to 23 December.
