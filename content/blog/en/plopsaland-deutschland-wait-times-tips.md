The road into the park in Haßloch is still called
Holiday-Park-Straße, and the house number hasn't changed. The park itself has been called [Plopsaland Deutschland](ref:plopsaland-deutschland) since 28 June 2025. The name comes from the Belgian operator,
which has owned the park since November 2010.

Search for "Holiday Park" in 2026 and you also get pages from earlier years, with prices that no
longer apply. The operator hasn't used the old
name since the rename.

The park sits in the Palatinate, between Neustadt an der Weinstraße and Ludwigshafen, on
40 hectares. It drew 804,218 visitors in 2025, which the operator calls the best year in the
park's history. That makes it smaller than Europa-Park or Phantasialand and bigger than almost
anything else in Rhineland-Palatinate. We have been measuring wait times here for two years,
currently across 57 attractions.

## Expedition GeForce

[bigFM Expedition GeForce](ref:plopsaland-deutschland/bigfm-expedition-geforce) is an Intamin
machine from 2001. The park
gives 55 metres, 120 km/h, 1,220 metres of track, a ride time of 1:15 and up to 4.5 g. German
Wikipedia has 53 metres. That's the height of the track, while the park measures from the
highest point of the lift hill.

```glossary-widget slug=hyper-coaster

```

At opening it was the fastest coaster in Europe and, after The Big One at Pleasure Beach Resort
in England, the second tallest. It hasn't been either for a long time. But it has won the Golden Ticket Award of the trade magazine _Amusement
Today_ as Europe's best coaster several times, and the _Internet Coaster Poll_ has voted it the
best in the world more than once. The park now counts 40 awards.

Behind the first drop it has three hills that lift riders out of their seats at the crest, and a helix at the end
that presses them back down into them.

It opened on 18 June 2001 for the park's 30th birthday, at a cost of around 20 million
Deutschmarks. The track was calculated by the Stengel engineering office. Trains are pulled up
the lift hill by a sled on a steel cable, the same system as on Millennium Force in the USA, and
the first drop is 82 degrees steep. Two trains run with seven cars and 28 seats each, up to
1,300 riders an hour.

![A view from above of a red steel coaster whose track runs through wooded ground in long hills and one wide curve. | Expedition GeForce seen from the Free Fall Tower. In the picture, the three hills after the first drop. Foto: Hetamin1000, Wikimedia Commons (Public Domain) | right](/media/plopsaland-deutschland/expedition-geforce-airtime-huegel-4x3.jpg)

You can ride from 140 centimetres, and not below that even with an adult. It only runs from 8 degrees Celsius, so on cold days in March and November it stands still.

## The other three coasters

**Sky Scream** by Premier Rides has stood since 2014 where Superwirbel stood from 1979 to 2013,
Germany's first coaster with inversions. Its successor is a Sky Rocket II: 45 metres tall,
100 km/h, 263 metres of track, one inversion. The train is launched out of the station three
times, forwards and backwards, before it clears the vertical top hat. Minimum height
140 centimetres, minimum age 14, and the same eight-degree rule applies, measured at the track. It opened on 12 April 2014. The
train seats twelve riders in two cars, and a ride lasts 47 seconds.

**100% Wolf – The Family Roller Coaster** is the 2026 addition, opened on 22 March. Gerstlauer
built it: 17.5 metres tall, 760 metres long, 55 km/h, with a launch section, a track switch for
the backwards run and a spike as the closing element. It's a family coaster: children can ride from 100 centimetres with an adult
and from 130 alone. It runs from as low as 5 degrees. Three trains with ten cars for two people each run on the
track, built for 1,000 riders an hour.

In our measurements you wait longer here than at any other ride in the park.

**Tabaluga's Rollercoaster** by Zierer is the smallest of the four at 9 metres and 39 km/h (a Force Two model, 222 metres of track,
a ride of one minute and 30 seconds), and
the only one under a roof, and it belongs to the Holiday Indoor hall, opened in 2018 across
5,000 square metres.

## Water rides and towers

The Free Fall Tower was Germany's first drop tower when it opened in 1997, an Intamin Giant
Drop, 70 metres tall, from 120 centimetres. You can see it from the A65.

For hot days there are two water rides:

- **DinoSplash**, called Donnerfluss until 2019, dates from 1984 and was Germany's first rapid
  river ride. 500 metres, fed from groundwater, from 110 centimetres with an adult and from 130
  alone. During the Halloween Fright Nights it runs only until 20:00.
- **Wickie Splash**, called Teufelsfässer until 2013, is a Mack log flume from 1992 with
  20 metres of height and 1,000 metres of track. It was the first log flume in Europe with a
  backwards shot. From 100 centimetres with an adult, from 140 alone.

Next to them sits **Splash Battle** in Wickieland, where boat crews and bystanders soak each
other, and **The Big Wave**, a Zamperla Disk'O. Both are in Wickieland, like Wickie Splash.

**Sky Fly** by Gerstlauer, in place since 2015, is a ride on which you flip yourself over. The **Lighthouse Tower** by Funtime has stood since
2006 and is where you get a look at the whole site in one go.

**The Smurfs' Adventure** is a
Mack dark ride that was called Burg Falkenstein until the end of 2022 and reopened for the 2025
season with new theming.

## The themed areas, and what is in each

The park is laid out as a star. The areas branch off the Square of Fountains behind the
entrance, and you cross the middle several times a day.

The **Square of Fountains** holds the Free Fall Tower and the dancing fountains that turn up in
every Plopsa park.

**Majaland** holds eleven
attractions for small children, most of them by Zamperla and Zierer: Maya's Flower Splash as a
Flying Fish, the Flower Tower as the Free Fall Tower's little brother, the Swinging Tree, the
Butterfly Flight, The Frogs, Mini-Cars, Lara's Ladybug Flight, Willy's Raft Circuit and the Bee
Whirl.

**Holiday Indoor** is the 2018 hall, with Tabaluga's Rollercoaster, Mia's Elf Flight, the Farm
Carousel, a wave slide, a ball pool and a theatre seating over 500.

The **Palatine Village** holds DinoSplash and The Smurfs' Adventure, plus the Pfalzgraf
restaurant and a giant wine barrel by Maurer Söhne. **Wickieland** has Wickie Splash, Splash
Battle and The Big Wave. **Air Show 71** was finished in July 2016 on the plot of the sold Bounty
Tower and holds Sky Fly, Balloon Race, the Red Baron and the Wellenflug. The smaller areas are **The Beach** with the Lighthouse Tower and the fishing boats, **Blinky Bill** with the
Greenville Bus and Blinky's Soapboxes, and **Super Wings** with three slides and a water
playground.

### Shows and meet and greets

The park's programme has 16 items, and all but three are encounters with Studio 100 characters:
Maya and her friends, Vic and Halvar, Tabaluga, Heidi and Peter, the Smurfs, Blinky Bill, and
since 2026 Freddy Lupin from 100% Wolf. The Smurfs' Show, the Plopsa Heroes Show and a dance
session make up the rest.

The times are only fixed in the morning. They depend on the daily programme the park publishes
for each date on its opening-hours page. Meet and greets pull people away from the rides. While Maya is on the Majaland stage at 10:50, the Free Fall
Tower is emptier.

### The Express Pass

```glossary-widget slug=express-pass

```

The Express Pass costs 7 euros for one ride, 35 euros for nine rides (Classic) and 95 euros for unlimited
rides, each per person per day. It is valid at eight attractions: 100% Wolf, DinoSplash, Wickie
Splash, The Big Wave, bigFM Expedition GeForce, Sky Scream, Free Fall Tower and Sky Fly. At
least 15 minutes have to pass between two rides, and the park sells only a limited number.

With the 35-euro Classic you skip a short queue nine times on an ordinary day, and the park closes at 18:00 anyway. The price stays the same all year.

### The weekday

Saturday is the busiest day of the week by a clear margin, measured across 39 Saturdays.

The quietest day is **Friday**. Across 38 measured Fridays the median is below Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday. If you can take a day off and are choosing between Friday and Wednesday, take the Friday.

From late September the advantage flips, because the Halloween Fright Nights
run on October Fridays and the park stays open until 22:00. Friday evening is then the busiest
part of the week.

### The month

```stats-widget slug=plopsaland-deutschland show=months

```

July is the busiest month and sits, like October, above the rest of the main season. **September** is the quietest month in which the park is still open daily, and even the upper end of a day is lower than in any other month between April and October. And September weather in the Palatinate often beats June.

In January and February the main season is paused. The 2026
season runs from 22 March to 1 November. In the winter before, part of the site was open as
Majaland Pfalz, with the Majaland rides. The winter tickets were valid until
22 March 2026 and cost from 24 euros dated, 32 euros undated.

The summer school holidays in Rhineland-Palatinate run from 29 June to 7 August 2026, which covers all of July. The autumn holidays last from 5 to 16 October and include the Fright Nights on 9, 10 and 16 October. The Easter holidays were from 30 March to 10 April.

### Halloween 2026

The Halloween Fright Nights run every Friday and Saturday in October: the 9th and 10th, the 16th
and 17th, the 23rd and 24th, and the 30th and 31st. On those dates the park is open from 10:00
to 22:00 instead of 18:00. Every other October day stays at 10:00 to 18:00.

During the day there's the Plopsa Autumn Festival and a pumpkin display in the pumpkin village,
both fine for small children. The mazes, walk-through horror houses, open only on the Fright
Nights: Skrämma, NEXUS AI, Murder District,
Academy of Freaks, Scarecrow, Titty Twister and LOST: Deep in the woods, plus Holly's Halloween
Party and a meeting with King Pumpkin for younger guests.

On a Fright Night some daytime attractions close before the park does, DinoSplash at 20:00 for
instance. Instead of eight hours you get twelve, and the last four are for the mazes. What the park has announced for this season is in our post
on the [Halloween Fright Nights 2026](/blog/plopsaland-deutschland-halloween-fright-nights-2026).

## From fairy-tale park to Plopsaland

The Schneider family bought the Märchenpark Haßloch in 1970 and opened it under their own
management in 1971, at first with a wooden wild mouse, a dolphin show and a village later known
as Liliputstadt. The family had run a circus of short-statured performers for generations, and
the park gave it a permanent home; that part existed until 1996. The name Holiday Park arrived
in 1973.

The Holiday Transit monorail followed in 1973, and the wild mouse burned down in 1978. The
70,000 square metres of the first season had grown past 400,000 by 2015.

1972 brought a show theatre with 600 seats and a new dolphinarium with 1,800 seats, 1978 the Kino Monumental with a 180-degree screen. In 1983 came the Holiday Park lake, 30,000 square metres, and the Aqua-Stadion for the water-ski show. The dolphinarium closed in 1995, and the Aqua-Stadion was demolished in 2022.

Superwirbel was a Vekoma model of the Corkscrew with Bayerncurve type and cost 3.5 million
Deutschmarks. Its 731 metres of track held a double corkscrew, at 22.9 metres of height and
64 km/h. It made its last run on 31 October 2013. A piece of the corkscrew was kept from the
scrapyard and now hangs above the entrance to the Sky Scream area.

In 2002 and 2003 the American lecturer
Richard Rodriguez rode his coaster marathon here, days of continuous riding. A monument next to
the ride has stood there since.

On 3 November 2010 Studio 100 announced that its subsidiary, the Belgian Plopsa group, was
taking over the park, and promised 25 million euros for the first four years. Since then it has been a themed park with connected areas, and its mascots are Maya, Vic,
Heidi, Mia and Tabaluga rather than Holly, the parrot who lived here before. Majaland went up in
2012 on the site of the old entrance, bringing today's main gate with it. From 2010 to 2016 the park ran the wild mouse Hollys
Wilde Autofahrt, a Maurer coaster that had stood at Dreamland Margate in England and Loudoun
Castle in Scotland before.

According to the police, an eleven-year-old girl died in the park on 15 August 2014 on Spinning Barrels. The Breakdance ride from 2000 never ran again and was sold. In June 2016 the district court in Neustadt an der Weinstraße fined the operator 2,400 euros for negligent homicide and acquitted his two co-defendant supervisors.

The indoor hall followed in 2018 for more than 10 million euros, by Plopsa's account the largest
single investment in the park up to then. In June 2019 the group announced a further 25 million
euros over five years, aiming at more than a million visitors a year and a hotel of its own.
That plan included DinoSplash (2020) and Wickieland with Splash Battle and the Disk'O (2021).
The Super Wings water playground came in 2023, the Blinky Bill area in 2024, the new coaster in 2026.

The rename followed on 28 June 2025. The operator had already called its other parks Plopsaland and Plopsa, and Haßloch was the last one still carrying a name from before. By the operator's figures, around
50 million euros had gone into the park since 2010, and with the new name it announced another
100 million for the following three years. In 2025 visitor numbers rose 9 per cent on the year
before.

## What comes next

For 2028 the park is building a coaster that doesn't have a name yet: an
Xtreme Spinning Coaster by Mack Rides, due to get Tomorrowland theming of the sort The Ride to
Happiness already has at sister park Plopsaland Belgium. It's in the park's master plan and
listed as under construction at RCDB.

It would be the park's fifth coaster. Unlike the family coaster 100% Wolf, it's aimed at adults. When it was announced in March
2025 the operator listed launches, spinning cars and inversions, and in January 2026 it said
construction would start this year.

Since 2025 Plopsa has also been building the Plopsaqua Haßloch water park in Haßloch, for
40 million euros. The operator says it will open by early 2028, with slides, a wave pool, a
lap pool and a sauna area.

## Staying and eating

There's no park hotel. The park works with partner hotels in Haßloch, Speyer, Deidesheim,
Neustadt and Mannheim instead and sells ticket-and-room packages. If you are on holiday in the
Palatinate anyway you don't need them, because from Neustadt or Speyer the car park is twenty minutes away.

There are 22 places to eat in the park, from snack stand to restaurant. The address for a hot meal
sitting down is the Pfalzgraf restaurant, called Casa Palatina between 2015 and 2023; annual
pass holders get ten per cent off there. Next to it are the Palatine wine and beer garden, Pasta
Point, Koala Joe's Roadhouse in the Blinky Bill area, the Maya Burger in Majaland, a Super Wings
Fuel Station and a Dunkin' park café.

## A day in Haßloch, roughly planned

The park opens at 10:00 and, outside the Fright Nights, closes at 18:00.

1. **10:00** Straight to Expedition GeForce. It is empty for the first half hour, and two rides back to back are normal.
2. **10:45** On to Sky Scream next door. Both depend on the eight-degree rule, so it's worth
   getting them done early if the day starts cold.
3. **11:30** 100% Wolf. The ride is new and so it has the park's longest queue, which is still easy to live with.
4. **12:30** Lunch, then the water rides: DinoSplash and Wickie Splash sit close enough to do in
   one go. Both get you wet.
5. **14:30** Free Fall Tower and Sky Fly, plus the Lighthouse Tower for the view across the site.
6. **15:30** With children, Majaland and the indoor hall; without them, Expedition GeForce again.
   The afternoon is the day's second quiet stretch.
7. **17:00** The Smurfs' Adventure or Splash Battle last, then out.

On a Fright Night everything shifts four hours later, because the mazes don't open until the
evening.

## Practicalities: getting there, tickets, hours

The address is Holiday-Park-Str. 1–5, 67454 Haßloch.

**By car.** Via the A65 or the B9, with car parks right at the site. Dropping someone off or
picking them up is free for up to 45 minutes; the barrier opens on number-plate recognition.
The car park has more than 2,500 spaces for cars and 50 for coaches. Staying for the day needs a parking ticket, which you buy online or at the machine before you drive out. The Plopsa
Pass Europe Premium at 235 euros includes a parking subscription.

**By train.** To Haßloch station, from where a shuttle bus runs to the park several times a day
between 22 March and 1 November 2026. It is included in VRN tickets to Haßloch and in the
Deutschlandticket, so it costs nothing extra. Groups of ten or more are asked to book it ahead.

**Tickets.** A dated ticket costs from 36 euros online. Children under 85 centimetres get in
free, children up to 99 centimetres and guests aged 70 and over pay 22 euros. The family ticket
costs 139 euros for three people, 179 for four, 219 for five and 259 for six. An evening ticket
at 46 euros is valid from three hours before closing, and from 17:00 on Summernights and Fright
Nights. An undated ticket, valid for a year on one day of your choosing,
costs 58 euros. The daily price depends on the date: for Thursday 8 October 2026 the park's
calendar lists 42 euros.

**Annual passes.** The Pass Light costs 120 euros a year and has up to 35 blackout days. The
Plopsaland Deutschland Pass costs 150 euros, is valid on every operating day and brings
discounts on souvenirs, at the Pfalzgraf and at the other Plopsa parks. The Plopsa Pass Europe
Premium at 235 euros covers all Plopsa parks and the parking subscription. For children between
85 and 99 centimetres and for guests aged 70 and over, each of the three costs half.

**Opening hours.** In October 2026 daily from 10:00 to 18:00, and until 22:00 on the eight
Fright Night dates. The season ends on 1 November.

## Frequently asked questions

### Has Holiday Park been renamed?

Yes. Since 28 June 2025 it has been Plopsaland Deutschland. The operator, the site and the
attractions are the same, as is the address on Holiday-Park-Straße.

### How tall and how fast is Expedition GeForce?

The park gives 55 metres and 120 km/h, with 1,220 metres of track and 4.5 g. The ride lasts one
minute and 15 seconds.

### How tall does my child have to be?

For the two big coasters, Expedition GeForce and Sky Scream, a child needs 140 centimetres, and
for Sky Scream also a minimum age of 14. For the Free Fall Tower it's 120 centimetres. With an
adult, children can ride 100% Wolf and Wickie Splash from 100 centimetres and DinoSplash from 110. Riding alone needs 130 centimetres at
100% Wolf and DinoSplash, and 140 at Wickie Splash.

### When is Plopsaland Deutschland quietest?

On Fridays outside the Halloween season, and in September. On Fridays the median is below Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday, and in September even the upper end of a day stays lower than in any other main-season month. Saturday is the busiest day.

### How long is the average wait here?

Across all measured days the average wait in the park is well under half an hour, and at individual attractions usually only a few minutes.

### Is the Express Pass worth it?

More so on a July Saturday and at the Fright Nights than on a Friday in September. With the Classic
at 35 euros you get nine rides through the shortened entrance, with at least 15 minutes between
them.

### Which coasters does the park have?

Four: Expedition GeForce (2001), Sky Scream (2014), Tabaluga's Rollercoaster (2018) and
100% Wolf (2026). A fifth is under construction for 2028.

### Is one day enough for the whole park?

For the big attractions, comfortably. A large share of the 57 attractions are carousels and playgrounds in Majaland, the indoor hall and Super Wings that adults
without children skip. With children the same park fills two days, because you then ride the
small ones round after round.

### Can you get there without a car?

Yes. By train to Haßloch station, then the shuttle bus, which runs between 22 March and
1 November 2026 and is included in VRN tickets and the Deutschlandticket.

### What happened to the old attractions?

Superwirbel made way for Sky Scream in 2013. The Bounty Tower went to Wunderland Kalkar in 2015.
Holly's Wild Ride has been at Eifelpark since 2017. Donnerfluss has been DinoSplash since 2020,
Teufelsfässer has been Wickie Splash since 2013, and Burg Falkenstein reopened in 2025 as The
Smurfs' Adventure.
