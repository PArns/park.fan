## New

- The Orlando Crowd Calendar at park.fan/orlando shows which park has the shortest lines on which
  day: Walt Disney World, Universal with Epic Universe, SeaWorld, their water parks and the day
  trips, 16 parks in November 2026. The number for a park and a day is the big rides' wait at their
  busiest, in minutes. The top of the page names one day in Orlando time and the park with the
  shortest lines at each resort, with a note when a park closes early that day for an evening event.
  A tap on a day opens its opening hours, Early Entry, evening events, seasons, the drive from your
  hotel area, the five big rides and "Plan this day". Each of the next eleven months has a page of
  its own, and the More menu, the homepage and the pages of the Orlando parks link to it.
- The trip planner on the same page puts each of your parks on the day of your trip with the
  shortest lines, for trips of up to 21 days. It needs only your dates and your parks; the hotel,
  the hotel area, whether you want to see Magic Kingdom's fireworks, rest days and park hopping wait
  under "Fine-tune". The plan counts early closings and party nights, the early and late hours some
  hotels give their guests, and with a Park Hopper ticket two parks on one day. After a park that
  closes early it suggests an evening, in a second park with a hopper ticket or at Disney Springs or
  CityWalk. The answer starts with the minutes per ride the plan saves against an average day of
  your trip, or with the fact that the order barely matters.
- The plan comes as an agenda, a week or a month. The week view draws each day on the day planner's
  hour axis, so a park that closes early has a shorter block and a party night is hatched after it.
  A day can be pinned or swapped with another, and each swap shows its cost in minutes before you
  make it. Any day opens in the day planner, "Add all days to my planner" files the whole trip at
  once, "Add to calendar (.ics)" saves it in Orlando time, and "Copy link" copies the address of
  exactly this plan.
- With "Find the best dates" the planner picks the dates as well. Give it a trip of 3 to 21 days and
  a period, such as the next 12 months, a year or a season, and it names the three best stretches,
  if you like only those inside your region's school holidays. Each comes with its big-ride wait and
  how far that is below the period's average. Heat and the hurricane season show as short notes, and
  a stretch the hurricane season pushed down is marked "ranked lower".
- Three pages give the best time to visit Orlando, Disney World and Universal Orlando in 2027, and
  each September they move on to the next year. They open on the three quietest 10-day trips with
  "Plan these dates", then show a bar for every week and a row for every month: the big rides' wait,
  events, school holidays in Orlando and in Europe, the usual weather, opening hours, party nights
  and the hurricane season. The best and worst weeks follow, and links that open the planner inside
  German, Dutch and English school holidays.
- Parky plans Orlando trips in the chat: which park on which day, the best dates for a trip of a
  given length, the events and party nights, and the drive from your hotel to a park. Ask him to put
  the trip in your planner and every day of it lands in the day planner, two parks on a park-hopping
  day, and each day can be taken back on its own. He also opens the trip on the Orlando page with
  your dates and parks filled in.
- Planned maintenance shows in three more places, where it used to be a badge on the ride page
  alone. In the wait time calendar a day with rides in maintenance carries a wrench and their number
  (at Efteling four at once from 4 January 2027); hover names each ride and until when, and the
  day's panel lists them under "Closed for maintenance". The day planner names the rides in
  maintenance on the planned day and will not take one: the ride search greys it out, a drop on the
  timeline is refused with the reason, and the ride page's plan button turns into "Maintenance until
  …". A ride card shows "Maintenance until", "Maintenance from" or "Maintenance on" with the date
  and lists every coming window on hover or tap. The live status, "Refurbishment" in English until
  now, is called "Maintenance" too.
- The title card of a park page now carries the cover of the park's guide, the whole picture a link,
  or from 1024 px the park photo where there is no guide, and under it the park's newest news of the
  last 30 days. "Plan a day at …" is the card's one filled button; the website, tickets, Wikipedia
  and social media sit behind "More". The city and the pin open the route in Apple Maps on Apple
  devices and in Google Maps everywhere else. A ride page has the same top with the post about the
  ride, the park's guide or the ride photo, and "Add to plan" as its button. Its facts come in two
  rows: chips for your visit (minimum height, single rider, Quick Pass) and a bar with top speed,
  inversions, ride type, maker and opening year.
- Parky works with your day planner, alerts and favourites from the chat. He moves, changes and
  removes rides in your plan, adds a show, marks a ride as done, clears a day and shares the plan,
  and he takes back his own change as long as you have not touched that day since. He sets and
  removes alerts for a ride or a show and saves favourites. He also knows the rides you ticked off
  as ridden, so "Which headliners am I still missing?" gets an answer.
- His answers carry more cards: a crowd calendar of one to six weeks, tiles for one day (crowd
  level, average and peak wait, open rides, opening hours, weather), shows with their times and the
  next one marked, restaurants with a badge where you need a reservation, and up to three guides,
  news items or glossary terms. A ride card carries the badges of the ride page, from rope drop and
  single rider to maintenance and out of season.
- Parky dresses for the date and the weather: a pumpkin in October, a Christmas tree from 1 to 26
  December, a party hat on 31 December and 1 January, a wool hat and scarf until the end of
  February, and on a park page an umbrella, a thunder cloud, snowflakes, wind, fog, sun or a scarf
  to match that park's weather. "Try on an outfit" on the Parky page shows them all. New tricks
  include airtime, a churro, a balloon, a photo and applause, and he now rides a log flume and the
  teacups.
- On a park page his speech bubbles follow the live data: a walk-on of up to 10 minutes, a queue
  over 60, a full or a closed park, an outage, a wait that dropped by 20 minutes or more, a show
  starting in 15 to 30 minutes. In the hour before opening and the last half hour before closing he
  talks about the clock. After a minute without input he gets tired, twice as fast between 10 pm and
  5 am, and after a minute and a half he falls asleep (it used to be 75 seconds). He dreams in a
  cloud over his head of riding a loop, of fireworks or of a fairy-tale castle under a shooting
  star, and wakes up in one of four ways. In the chat window and in the homepage photo he only
  blinks, follows the pointer and sleeps.

## Improved

- A park's statistics and twelve-month outlook moved from the park page to its wait time calendar,
  under the grid, and the park's guides now come before the nearby parks.
- In the day planner a ride you tick off shows how far its wait was from the estimate (+10, −5, ±0),
  and the day's total names the minutes you walk next to the wait and the rides. The wizard plans
  today from the current time. "Optimise the day" shows "Sorting …" while it works; if it cannot
  run, a message says your plan is unchanged, next to a button to try again.
- On the homepage, the cards of parks near you say how soon a closed park opens, in hours or as
  tomorrow or a weekday, instead of giving the date, with the exact time in the tooltip. The cards
  of popular parks show the same as the nearby ones, the average wait and crowd level while open,
  where they used to say only "See wait times". The sentence under the heading now starts with what
  the site is for: "Find the park with the shortest queues".
- Before you type, the search list shows up to four of your saved parks after the parks near you or
  the popular ones, in the homepage search and in the search window. On the homepage the / key puts
  the cursor in the search field over the photo only while the whole field is in view and opens the
  search window otherwise, so letters no longer land in a field you scrolled past. Esc clears that
  field, closes its list and leaves it in one press.
- Search finds a glossary term by its other names too, and Parky also by a word from its definition:
  asked in German about a "Blockbremse", he now finds "Block Brake", where he found nothing before.
- In a park, the compass on the homepage names the next show of the day under its rides, with the
  time in park time, the distance and a link to the park's shows.
- On a phone, the share buttons start with "Share via app", which opens the phone's own share menu
  and reaches Instagram, Messenger, Telegram and any other app installed. The footer links park.fan
  on Instagram and Facebook.
- Parky's chats are deleted after 7 days instead of 90. The line under the chat's input field says
  Parky is an AI assistant whose answers may contain errors and come without warranty, and links to
  the Parky section of the privacy policy. That section now also lists the day plan, alerts and
  favourites that go with each question.
- The imprint has a section on using the data. What park.fan publishes is free for non-commercial
  use with a credit and a link to the park's or ride's page, texts and photos excepted. Forecasts
  come from api.park.fan only with a key, free on request for non-commercial use; commercial use
  needs a written agreement, and neither the pages nor the forecasts may be used to train models.

## Fixed

- On a 390 px phone the homepage was 1,026 px wide and a park page with nearby parks, such as
  Efteling's, 425 px, so both could be pushed sideways and the day planner looked cut off. Both fit
  the screen now.
- In or near a park, the homepage moved its search field once it had found you, by up to 142 px on
  an 800 px screen. The welcome line is gone, the park's block sits under the row of cards, and on
  screens under 1024 px the compass comes after the news, so it no longer pushes the news heading
  off the first screen.
- The homepage cards of rides near you kept the waits from page load when your location came from
  your connection rather than from GPS. While the tab is open they refresh every 5 minutes. The news
  under the photo no longer stays blank until you scroll.
- On park pages, the twelve-month outlook showed Phantasialand as closed from February to May 2027,
  a guess past the park's published season; such a month now reads "No forecast", and "Closed" only
  when every remaining day is closed. Official weather warnings for hours the park is shut, such as
  frost from 10 pm to 9 am, no longer appear. Sorting by shortest wait now sorts the headliners too,
  where the whole first screen used to stay as it was. The "As of" time above the ride list showed a
  closed park's closing time all night and could run up to 5 minutes ahead of the data; it now gives
  the time the data was read.
- The day planner put a show at midnight before the park's opening, which moved the last show of the
  day at Magic Kingdom and Disneyland Park in Anaheim to the start. It now stands at the end of the
  day.
- Saved shows and restaurants in the favourites menu and on the favourites page lead to the shows or
  restaurants of their park. Their links pointed nowhere before.
- Times on Parky's cards followed your device's clock, so a day in Orlando planned from London
  showed London times. They are in the park's time now, like everywhere else on the site. With his
  chat open on a phone, the page behind it no longer scrolls along, also with the keyboard up.
- Safari cut off the top of Parky's Ferris wheel hat on the check before a chat. Parky no longer
  flickers at the end of his entrance or dips away when search closes, his dream coaster stays
  steady through the loop, and with reduced motion his rides and dreams stand still even when you
  change the setting after the page has loaded. On the homepage he stands in the photo as soon as
  the page loads, instead of appearing in the corner and hopping up, and his hop never rises above
  the header.
- A shared link to the day planner or to the photo contribution page showed a preview with an empty
  heading over a count of 0 parks, and an author page's preview was titled "author". They carry
  their page titles now, and an author's preview shows the name and a short bio.
