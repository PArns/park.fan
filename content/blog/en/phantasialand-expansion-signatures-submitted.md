On Monday, 9 November, the Netzwerk NSG Ententeich handed 5,656 signatures for
its petition against the expansion of [Phantasialand](ref:phantasialand) to the
town administration in Brühl, RTL West and Radio Erft report. The petition needs
2,536 valid signatures from eligible voters in Brühl, and the network had been
collecting since 28 September. How the
[petition against the expansion](/news/phantasialand-expansion-petition) came
about and what the park wants to build at the Ententeich is in our post of 1
October.

Nothing changes for a visit to the park. Phantasialand can only build on the
land once a zoning plan is in force, and the town is still working on that.

## What happens now

First the town checks how many of the signatures are valid. Then the town
council decides whether the petition is admissible, and that isn’t certain.
Under North Rhine-Westphalia’s municipal code, drawing up, changing or revoking
zoning plans can’t be the subject of a petition. The one exception is the
decision to start such a procedure. The network wants to revoke the decisions of
9 July with which the planning committee started the procedure for zoning plan
06.01. Whether the exception covers that too, the network itself leaves open on
its website.

If the petition is admissible, the town can’t decide or carry out anything that
runs against it until the referendum. If the council adopts the demand, the
procedure ends. If it rejects it, Brühl has to vote within three months. In
July, the CDU, SPD and FDP/Volt voted for the procedure in committee, with the
Greens and the Left against. The town hasn’t announced yet when the council will
discuss the petition.

## What a referendum needs

A majority alone isn’t enough in the referendum. The yes votes also have to add
up to at least 20 percent of all eligible voters. With the 36,227 eligible
voters the network works with, that’s 7,246 votes, nearly 1,600 more than the
signatures it has now handed in.

If the referendum passes, it has the effect of a council decision. For the first
two years only a new referendum can change it, and the council has to call that
one. The procedure for the expansion would be over. The expert reports
Phantasialand is paying for haven’t been published yet. They cover seven fields,
from species protection and noise to the water balance, and the water report
also covers what would happen to the Ententeich. I’d have liked to see the
results before Brühl votes on the land.

## What the park and the supporters say

Phantasialand hasn’t published anything on the handover on its press page so
far. On its
["Standortsicherung" page](https://www.phantasialand.de/de/rechtliches/standortsicherung/)
it explains the expansion with short-break visitors. To keep more guests
overnight, the park says it needs attractions for longer stays, and there’s no
room for them on its roughly 28 hectares. With the roughly 15 hectares the
regional plan has set aside for it since 2013, Phantasialand would grow to just
over 40 hectares. [Movie Park Germany](ref:movie-park-germany) in Bottrop gives
its own size as about 45 hectares.

A [counter-petition](/news/phantasialand-expansion-online-petition) on
openPetition has been making the case for the expansion since May. It runs until
23 November. By early November, 211 people had signed, 52 of them from Brühl. If
740 signatures from Brühl come together, openPetition asks the council members
for a statement.
