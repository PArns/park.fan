# The header is 48 px, and its height is written down in four places (REQUIREMENT)

One standing rule. It is indexed from the repo's [`CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md), which carries the rule in one line and links here for the reasoning, the measurements and the counter-examples.

`h-12` on `<header>` (the inner container is `h-full`, never a second copy of the number), the `<Suspense>` fallback in `app/[locale]/layout.tsx` that reserves the bar while the client `Header` streams in, and `-mt-12` on the three heroes it floats over (homepage, blog post banner, editorial hero). Miss the fallback and every page opens with a shift of the difference. **One place outside those four reads the number without reserving it**, and it is listed here so a change to the bar finds it: the trip planner's bottom sheet rests at `calc(100svh-3rem)` on a portrait phone (`components/planner/planner-flyout.tsx`), i.e. it stops below the bar rather than under it. It was `92svh`, which is 736 px of 800 and leaves 64 — the bar plus 16 px of page nobody reads, and a 15-minute block of axis paid for them. Control heights come off the button scale in `components/ui/button.tsx` (`sm` 32 / default 36 / `lg` 40), not out of the air — the old bar put the **largest** of them, the 40 px `lg` search field, in the shortest row in the app at 71 % of a 56 px bar. That scale now has a **phone tier**: below `sm`, `sm`/`default`/`icon`/`icon-sm` all resolve to **44 px**, which is the number `app/admin/_ui/controls.tsx` and `globals.css`'s `.touch-target` had both already written down and neither had ever applied outside `/admin` — `.touch-target` was on **nothing at all**, and a grep for `min-h-11|h-11|touch-manipulation` over `components/` + `app/[locale]` returned five hits, none of them a control. The bar is the **one** documented exception, because 44 in 48 is the same mistake one size worse: `LocaleSwitcher` (`max-sm:h-8`), the `sm` branch of `search-bar.tsx` (`max-sm:h-9 max-sm:w-9`) and the burger (`max-sm:size-9`) cancel it at their own call site and name the requirement. Cancel it nowhere else without one. What stays open is about this bar and not about those classes — the 32 px locale switcher and the 28 px theme toggle sit 4 px apart in a row with no room to grow, so the answer there is the bar's height, i.e. the number in all four places, not the button's. **The width is written down nowhere and is 25 px**, which is what the third preference control cost: measured at 360 px the row carries 303 px in 328 (lockup 107, search 36, locale 56, theme 48, burger 36, 20 of gaps), the same to within 6 px in all six languages because nothing in it is prose. So the °C/°F control — moved out of the weather card, where it only ever reached park pages — is **one button showing the ACTIVE unit**, 28 px and 24 on a phone, not a 54 px `°C | °F` pill and not a 21.5 px bare text button either (both overflow once the gap is counted); it takes the theme switch's `h-7` and ring so the two read as a pair, and it looks the same above `sm` rather than growing a second markup. **Its width may not depend on which unit is active**, which is what sizing it by its label got wrong: `°F` is narrower than `°C`, so the button changed width under the finger that pressed it and slid its neighbours over with it, in a row where every pixel is spoken for. `min-w-7` / `max-sm:min-w-6` with `px-1` fixes the box at 28 and 24 — the label is ~13 px and irrelevant while it is under that, and the padding is what happens if a reader's font ever pushes past it. Those 28 px came from three places, none of them a control's height: the **locale switcher's country code** (22 px, `max-sm:hidden` — a flag that already says it, in front of a dropdown listing code and language name), the two **gaps between the action groups** (`max-sm:gap-1`, 8 px) and the button's own phone tier (`max-sm:min-w-6`, 4 px). Measured after at 320/360/390 px × six locales: 301 px used, 27 px slack at 360 — two pixels _less_ than before the button existed — identical in every language, and the button's box and its neighbours' positions unchanged to 0.0 px across a toggle in either direction. **320 px is the width that decides the last two savings**: the narrowest viewport in the logs, already 15 px over its box before any of this and invisible because the container's `px-4` swallowed it; the button alone took it to 26 over and scrolled the document to 330. It is 13 over now, inside the padding, document = viewport from 320 to 768. There is no room for a fourth preference: the next one is a question about the bar's height or about a preferences sheet. **And its breakpoints ask the BAR's width, not the window's** — `@container` on the `<header>`, `@min-[1024px]:` and `@min-[1280px]:` on the four switches inside it. The two numbers used to be the same thing and stopped being it when the trip planner's panel started insetting the page: with a 448 px panel on a 1440 px window the bar's box is 992 px while `lg:`/`xl:` still read 1440, so it kept laying out a desktop's worth of content in three quarters of the room — children summing 1087.9 px in 992, the °C/°F toggle and the theme switch 95.9 px **under** the panel in German and 171.1 in French, and worse the smaller the window (+255.9 at 1280, +389.7 at 1024). On the header and **not** on the `container mx-auto` row inside it: `container-type` makes an element the containing block for absolutely positioned descendants, and the corner logo is `absolute left-6` — against the row it would sit 80 px further right and break the very handoff described below. `container-type: inline-size` implies `contain: layout style inline-size` and **not** `contain: paint`, so it does not make the header a backdrop root; the menu band's blur is unchanged, measured. The thresholds are the old ones on purpose: with the planner shut the header spans the viewport, so the same two numbers are the same two switches at the same two window widths and every existing layout is untouched. The other half is a **cap on the panel** (`PAGE_MIN_PX = 360` in `lib/planner/panel-width.ts`, applied in `getSnapshot` so the panel, the page's inset and the edge tab cannot disagree): at 768 px the stored 448 becomes 408, because below 360 px of page the bar starts sliding under again whatever its breakpoints say. Measured after, at 1440/1280/1024/900/768 × de and fr: **0.0 px overhang in all ten**. And the logo is **one component rendered twice**, never two sets of classes: on a hero page the header parks a copy in the corner and keeps one in the flex flow, then cross-fades them while sliding one onto the other, which only reads as a single object if they are congruent. They were not — `h-7 md:h-9`+`h-5 md:h-6`+`gap-0.5` against `h-6`+`h-5`+`gap-1` made the handoff animate a 1.5× scale, and no single factor can reconcile a pin:wordmark ratio of 36:24 with 24:20, so the corner copy stayed 15–25 px wider than the bar copy the whole way across (measured at 1440). `BrandLockup` is the fix and the guarantee: `logoScale` resolves to 1.000, the handoff is a pure translate, and per-frame sampling puts the two boxes 0.0 px apart on all four edges. **And a height has to be the mark's height, not a box it sits somewhere inside**: all four lockup files were exports with the artboard's margin baked in — the pin filled 86.3 % of a 144×144 viewBox vertically and 62.5 % of it horizontally, the wordmark 78.1 % of a 2304×657 one — so `h-6` painted a **20.7 px** pin and `h-5` a **15.6 px** wordmark, a lockup on 43 % of a 48 px bar while every number in the component said 50 %, with a fifth of the device pixels the layout had already reserved going to transparent artwork. It reads as a raster on a 1× display and is invisible from the call site, which is how it survived a rewrite that measured everything else about this bar. The viewBox of all four is the **measured ink box** now (rendered at 6000 px, alpha walked: `fill = 100.00 %`, offset 0.00), the header is pin **26** / wordmark **19** / `gap-2` — the 1.37 between them is the ratio the padded files rendered — and the three other consumers (`theme-wipe`, whose `gap:0` existed _because_ the pin carried ~19 % of its own width as margin, the coaster watermark, the footer) carry the same numbers explicitly and keep the size they had. Cropping alone sharpens nothing: 20.7 px of ink is 20.7 px of ink whatever box is drawn around it. It is what lets the size be raised without a magic number at every call site. The **pin without the wordmark is `BrandPin`**, in the same file and used by the lockup itself, because the homepage hero draws the mark alone to the left of its headline (`components/home/hero-with-nearby.tsx`, 48 px — one line of its `text-5xl` — from `@min-[1304px]/page` up) and a second hand-written light/dark pair would be a second drawing to keep in step. It is the **pin** there and not the lockup for a measured reason: the wordmark is 4.21 : 1, so the smallest sensible hero lockup takes 122 px of a plate that is 608 px wide from 1280 up, and the headline needs ~536 of them for its second line in French. `logo-big*.svg` is **not** cropped and must not be: it is the lockup including the wordmark under the pin, which `generate-icons` finds by looking for the widest empty row band. `logo.svg`/`logo-dark.svg` — the detailed pin the footer draws — were the last raster in the set, a 1563×1116 PNG in an SVG wrapper at 77 KB each, and nothing was visibly wrong with them at the 32/48 px the footer asks for, which is precisely why they lasted: a raster with enough resolution for today's largest call site fails silently the first time somebody draws it bigger. They are **cut from the lockup** now by `generate-brand-pin.mjs` (6.2 KB each, −142 KB), and generated rather than hand-exported because the pin would otherwise be one drawing stored twice with nothing comparing them — `pnpm check:brand-pin` is what notices. The split is the exporter's own: Affinity names the halves `<g id="park.fan">` and `<g id="Icon">`, so the wordmark goes by id rather than by hunting an empty band, and `_Linear1` (its green dot) is dropped with it. Ink AR 0.8201 against the raster's 0.8202 is how you know it is the same drawing. The `.png` twins keep the old padded geometry, which is what `lib/og/brand-mark.tsx` and `lib/three/park-scene.ts` have hard-coded. **And the bar's two states may not decide whether the navigation exists**, which is what `isTransparent` did on all six hero pages: `opacity-0 pointer-events-none` on the nav row, the search trigger and the burger, `aria-hidden` on the `<nav>`, `tabIndex={-1}` on every link in it and a `disabled` that also refused to open the panels — so until 50 px of scroll the menu was unclickable, untabbable and out of the accessibility tree, with nothing on screen saying there was one (24 of 24 measured combinations). Two things come with the fix and both are contrast rather than taste. The **scrim is its own layer**: a `background/85` gradient flat over the bar's 48 px and fading out across 32 px below it, as a SECOND absolutely positioned layer rather than a second class list on the material — a gradient is `background-image` and a flat tint is `background-color`, so one layer would drop the scrim the instant the threshold is crossed while the solid colour fades in behind it over 500 ms; two layers cross-fade on `opacity`, which interpolates. Neither may become a `backdrop-filter` or a transform on the `<header>` itself. And the **ink switches with it**: `--muted-foreground` is 4.73 : 1 on `--background`, i.e. 0.23 over the line, so 15 % of a dark photo through the scrim reads 3.3 : 1 — the floating bar runs the nav at `foreground/90` (worst measured 13.31 : 1 against 1.01 : 1 for muted ink on an untinted hero) and it switches with the state and waits for nothing: the two layers' sum runs 85 % → ~65 % → 80 % across the cross-fade, i.e. never far from the 80 % the solid bar has always given muted ink while a hero is still behind it. A `delay-300` on the solid class list was the first fix and is the wrong one — `transition-delay` is one property and the hover rule shares it, so it delays the hover of all seven entries by 300 ms on every page in the app; a timer in state is a `setState` in an effect, which the lint rule refuses. The corner pill holding a second copy of the three preference controls is gone with it — visible search and burger draw exactly where it hung (360 px: pill 240–336 against burger 308–344) — and so is the header's GSAP stagger, whose `fromTo(y: -10 → 0)` is **reversed** on the way back up and only invisible while the row is `opacity-0`.
