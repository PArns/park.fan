# 2. Sentence shape: the tells that survive a vocabulary pass

Chapter 2 of the [writing rules](README.md), §2.1 to §2.19: the rhythms and constructions that
mark a text as generated after every banned word is gone. Read it before writing or reviewing any
running text. §2.20, the catalogue of patterns `check:prose` knows by name, is in
[2-20-check-patterns.md](2-20-check-patterns.md).

Swapping words is the easy half. Rhythm survives find-and-replace, and every item here can be
counted in a finished text.

## 2.1 Negative parallelism

The most recognisable cadence in every language:

- German: `nicht nur X, sondern auch Y` · `es geht nicht um X, sondern um Y` ·
  `X ist kein Y, sondern ein Z`
- English: `not just X, but Y` · `it isn't X, it's Y` · `Y rather than X`
- Dutch `niet alleen … maar ook`, French `non seulement … mais aussi` and `ce n'est pas …, c'est …`,
  Spanish `no solo … sino`, Italian `non solo … ma anche` and `non è …, è …`, and the tourism
  formula of every language: `Non è un hotel, è un'esperienza.`

In 2026 the English shape moved to the **contrast reframe**, `It isn't a queue, it's a waiting
room`, one of the six measured habits of the model this site is written with (§2.18). Same fix: say
the second half and drop the first.

Advice is not a reframe: `Donnerstag, Freitag oder Sonntag, nicht Samstag` and
`Nimm dir eine Stunde, nicht zwanzig Minuten` tell the reader what to do and stay.

Two or three in a long post is normal writing; eight is a machine. Budget: **at most one per 1,000
words**, and never as the opening or closing sentence of a section. `check:prose` warns above 1.5
per 1,000 words.

## 2.2 The rule of three

`kompakt, begehrt und anstrengend`: three adjectives, three bullets, three chapters, three
examples. Models reach for triads to make a thin analysis look complete. **One per section is
fine; two is a pattern.** A list with three items because reality has three items stays; the tell
is the triad used as rhythm.

## 2.3 Participial clauses

Instruction-tuned models use present participial clauses at 2 to 5 times the human rate (Reinhart
et al. 2025). The German Partizip I as an adverbial is rarer still and reads translated:
`…, hervorhebend, wie gut die Bahn ausgelastet ist.`, `…, ensuring visitors can plan ahead.` Write
two sentences, a `weil` or `sodass` clause, or nothing.

**The tail** is the commonest form: a clause hung on a finished sentence to say what the fact
_does_. `…, making it the tallest in Europe`, `…, creating a moment of weightlessness`; German
uses `was` (`…, was den Nervenkitzel des Manövers erheblich steigert`), and the other four have
their own (`, ce qui rend`, `, créant`, `, lo que convierte`, `, il che rende`, `, wat …
creëert`). It almost always carries an evaluation the sentence before it could not support, which
is why it reads as a sales pitch even in a definition. In September 2026 the English posts carried
1 in 95,000 words, the English glossary 39 in 38,000. Budget: one per 1,000 words, counted in six
languages. End the sentence, or give the effect its own sentence with a subject:
`Der Zug hängt dabei zwei Sekunden kopfüber.`

## 2.4 Nominal style

Models use nominalisations at 1.5 to 2 times the human rate, and German makes them easy:
`die Durchführung einer Optimierung der Wartezeitenberechnung`. Use verbs:
`Die Wartezeit wird jetzt anders berechnet.`

## 2.5 Copula avoidance

Models dodge plain `ist`/`is` for `stellt … dar`, `fungiert als`, `dient als`, `serves as`,
`boasts`, `features`, `holds the distinction of being`. Human prose uses the plain forms far more,
and so do we: `Troy ist eine Holzachterbahn.`, not `Troy stellt eine Holzachterbahn dar.`

## 2.6 Consistent terminology, not elegant variation

Models learned to rotate synonyms: `Wartezeit` → `Anstehzeit` → `Queue-Dauer` → `Wartedauer` in
four sentences. Pick the term the product uses and repeat it. Repeating a noun is not a style
error; rotating it is a tell.

## 2.7 Vary the shape

- **Paragraph length must be uneven.** A two-line paragraph next to an eight-line one is what
  real writing looks like.
- **Vary sentence openings.** Three paragraphs opening on `Und` or `Das ist` read like
  autocomplete.
- **Do not pivot every paragraph on a colon.** A colon that introduces a list is fine; the pivot
  that withholds is the tell: `Acht davon sind Überlebende: Piratenfiguren aus der alten Anlage`,
  `2027 geht es früher los: …`. The clause before the colon is a trailer for the sentence after
  it. Say the sentence: `Acht Piratenfiguren aus der alten Anlage von 1987 sind restauriert worden
und sitzen heute in einem Rettungsboot.` `check:prose` counts pivots in posts of 40 sentences or
  more and warns above 15 per 100 sentences (the busiest German posts sat at 16 to 21). A colon
  before a list, in a date stamp (`Stand:`) or inside a name (`Guardians of the Galaxy: Cosmic
Rewind`) does not count; French colons after a no-break space do.
- **Do not stack transitions.** `Darüber hinaus`, `Zusätzlich`, `Außerdem`, `Additionally`: one is
  normal, one per paragraph is mechanical.
- **Do not build every paragraph from the same parts.** Topic sentence, explanation, example,
  transition, four times in a row, is the template showing. Open one on the number, let one be a
  single sentence, let one stop without a bridge.
- **Do not introduce two widgets with the same line** (`Wie die Bahnen im Gebäude heute stehen:`
  above two widgets). A widget with a heading of its own needs no line in front of it.
  `check:prose` warns when one paragraph ending in a colon occurs twice in a post (a date stamp
  like `Stand 6. Oktober 2026:` is exempt); changing words for the same job is
  `widget-announcement` (§2.20).

## 2.8 The aphoristic closer

The tell that survives every other pass and has shipped here most often: a short, symmetrical,
abstract sentence at the end of a post, a section or a landing band, restating what was said as a
maxim. It carries no information; it exists to sound like an ending.

- `Sie ist nicht die Antwort. Sie ist die Frage.` (antithesis, and a queue turned into philosophy)
- `Such dir einen Park und lies eine Zahl.` (two imperatives on a symmetrical beat, nothing new)
- `Das ist die ganze Geschichte in einer Tabelle.` (restates the section)

**The test:** cover the last sentence and re-read. If only a feeling of closure is lost, it was
decoration. Endings that work: the concrete next action with its specifics, a fact not yet stated
(a caveat, a number, a date), or nothing at all. A section may just stop.

## 2.9 What still separates the two, now that the obvious tells are gone

The Economist compared 1.2 million words of its journalism rewritten by four models with its own
writers and other publications. The em dash came out even. What did not:

- **Latinate and scientific vocabulary** where a plain word exists (`signifikant`, `Parameter`,
  `Methodik`): `nutzen`, not `zur Anwendung bringen`.
- **Nominalisation and long words**, which §2.4 already bans, found again in a different corpus.
- **Sparse punctuation**: model prose has fewer commas, fewer semicolons and almost no
  parentheses. The fix is not "add commas" but "write the aside you would have written"; the
  parenthesis is missing because the thought is.
- **Long sentences in dense paragraphs**, with `und`/`and` doing the joining where a full stop
  belongs.
- **`nicht X, sondern Y`**, the construction of §2.1, found independently.

Two of these can be counted, and `check:prose` prints both for every post:

- **Sentence-length variance** (burstiness, `stdev(sentence lengths) / mean`): flat under 0.4.
  Our German posts read 0.57, the English 0.59 (September 2026).
- **Commas per 100 words**: thin under about 4. German 8.2, English 6.4.

Both are signals, never verdicts. A short reference text can be flat for good reasons, so the
check warns only on posts of 40 sentences or more; a long post under 0.4 has every sentence at the
same length and deserves a read-aloud pass.

## 2.10 Staccato

Three very short sentences in a row: `Die Bahn ist zu. Für immer. Das war's.` It reads like an
advert and is what a model writes when it wants punch. One short sentence after a long one is
rhythm; three in a row is a tic. Connect them with what relates them: `weil`, `aber`, `sodass`, a
comma. Not a semicolon in German or French, where the academic semicolon is a tell of its own.

`check:prose` flags three sentences of five words or fewer in a row inside one paragraph. Lists
and quotations are left out: a list item is not a sentence, and a quotation keeps its speaker's
rhythm.

## 2.11 The passive that hides who did it

The passive is often right: `Die Bahn wurde 2002 eröffnet` needs no actor. The tell is the
passive that leaves out an actor the text knows: `Es wurde entschieden, die Bahn zu schließen` when
Six Flags decided, `Es wird berichtet` when AP reported. Name who did it.

In anything legal the hidden actor turns one side's claim into a fact.
`Die Verletzungen wurden durch die Bahn verursacht` is a verdict nobody has reached;
`Die Kläger führen die Verletzungen auf die Bahn zurück` is the report
([a quote names its source](../rules/a-quote-names-its-source.md)).

## 2.12 The question set-up

`Das Ergebnis? Ein voller Park.`, `The reason? …`: a question the text answers one word later is a
slide transition. stop-slop bans every sentence that opens with a question word, which is too
blunt for German, where `Wann` and `Wie` open plenty of honest sentences; we ban the set-up. Ask a
question only where the reader would ask it, and three rhetorical questions in a row are a triad
(§2.2). `check:prose` flags `Das Ergebnis?`, `Der Grund?`, `Die Antwort?`, `Der Haken?` and their
English twins, and warns on a heading that asks and answers in one line
(`Sind 70 Minuten viel? Kommt drauf an, ob Dienstag ist`).

The set-up also comes folded into one sentence: `Offen bleibt, welche Bahnen an so einem Tag noch
lohnen, und das sind zuerst die im Gebäude.` Nothing was open. Write the answer:
`An so einem Tag lohnen sich in der Regel die überdachten Attraktionen.` `check:prose` flags
`Offen bleibt, welche …, und das …` and its five twins (`What's left open is which …, and`,
`Reste à savoir quelles …, et`); `Reste à savoir si la question est recevable.` stays open and is
not a hit.

## 2.13 Things that talk, and the product as protagonist

**A queue shows nothing, a number says nothing, and a calendar knows nothing.**
`Was die Warteschlangen gerade jetzt anzeigen`, `Welche Tage die ruhigsten sind, zeigt der
Kalender`, `the data says`, `the numbers tell a different story` hand a person's verb (sagen,
zeigen, verraten, wissen, verschweigen, lügen) to an object so the sentence sounds like a scene.
Write what is there and where: `Im Kalender sind die ruhigsten Tage grün.`,
`Nach unseren Messungen brauche ich ihn nicht.` A display that literally shows a figure is fine
(`Am Eingang stehen 70 Minuten`, §3.3), and so is a legend, one of our tables or widgets, or a UI
element that marks something on screen (`Der blaue Kegel zeigt, wohin du schaust`). Data and time
do it too: `Die Daten belegen das nicht`, `ein anderes Jahr verteilt das anders`. Say who cannot
prove it (`Belegen kann ich das mit diesen Zahlen nicht`) and what another year may bring.

The travel-guide version is **false agency**: `Der Park lädt zum Verweilen ein`,
`Die Bahn sorgt für Nervenkitzel`, `delivers thrills`. Say what a visitor does or gets.
`check:prose` warns on both families in six languages.

**The product as protagonist** is the same move with our software as the subject:
`Der Planer kennt die Öffnungszeit`, `Der Planer fragt zwei Dinge`, `der Planer erfindet keine`,
27 times on the planner page. A feature written as a character that knows, asks and refuses turns
an explanation into a portrait of the software. Say what the reader sees or does:
`Ein Block rastet auf fünf Minuten ein`, `Du gibst an, wie groß die kleinste Person ist`.
`check:prose` warns above 6 per 1,000 words (the planner page read 8.8).

**A document does no sums.** `Eines davon rechnet durch, was passiert, wenn der Ententeich
verschwindet` turned a report that examines the effects of filling in a pond into a cliffhanger.
The same with a page or a tool as subject: `Der Tagesplaner rechnet den Tag durch`,
`the planner works out whether the day adds up`. Say who did the sums (`habe ich nachgerechnet`)
or what the document examines and where the result is. `rechnet … durch` and the five translations
of _calculates what happens if_ (`works out what happens`, `rekent uit wat er`,
`calcule ce qui se passe`, `calcula qué pasa`, `calcola cosa succede`) are an error; a guide, page,
calendar, widget, report or the planner as the subject of `rechnen`, `calculate` or `work out` is a
warning. A forecast model computing a forecast is arithmetic and stays
(`Das Modell rechnet diesen Tag stundengenau`), and so does a person.

## 2.14 Saying what it does not do

A negation is information when the reader expected the opposite:
`Für den Hansa-Park kommt nie eine Zahl an`. A text that keeps answering objections nobody raised
(`Ein Verbot ist das nicht, und eine Freigabe auch nicht`) argues with a silent reader. Say what
happens instead. The German posts sit at a median of 1.2 negations per 100 words; `check:prose`
warns above 2.

The loudest form is the **pledge about a gap**: `Wir wissen dort nicht, welche Bahnen unter Dach
liegen, und füllen die Lücke nicht mit Annahmen.` Nobody asked us to guess, and the reader did not
need the gap (§2.19). Flagged in six languages.

## 2.15 The definition colon

`Typisch heißt: …`, `„Knapp“ bedeutet: …`. One definition is useful; four on one page are a
glossary written as prose. Put the meaning into the sentence that uses the word, or link the
glossary term. `check:prose` warns above two per text.

## 2.16 The teaser

A sentence that tells the reader how to feel about the next one: `Dann wurde es kurios.`,
`Interessanter ist, was rundherum steht.`, `Here's where it gets interesting.`,
`The best part?` It is the self-reference of §1.4 one level down: a paragraph announcing its own
payoff. A person with something odd to report reports it, and the reader decides whether it is odd.
Variants:

- **The set-up label**: `Das Beste:`, `Kurz gesagt:`, `Der Clou:`, `Pro-Tipp:`, `Spoiler:`,
  `Bottom line:`, `Here's why:`, a two-word heading with a colon in the middle of a paragraph.
- **The count announcement**: `Drei Einschränkungen gehören dazu.`,
  `There's a second reason.` A sentence that says how many caveats follow is a heading in
  disguise; start with the first caveat. `check:prose` warns on a sentence of up to seven words
  that counts caveats, notes or remarks, ordinals included; `Drei Dinge noch:` in front of a list
  is left to the review pass.
- **The expectation teaser**: `und keine davon ist die, die man erwartet`,
  `than you might think`, `plus long qu'on ne le pense`. Whether it surprises is the reader's
  call, and in the Alton Towers guide it was also untrue. Warned on in six languages.

Cut the teaser and start with the fact; if the fact is unremarkable without the announcement, the
announcement was covering for it. `check:prose` flags the common forms in German and English. The
quieter ones (`Die Schienen waren das Problem.`) are fine when the next sentence explains at once,
and a tell when every paragraph opens that way.

## 2.17 The wink

The aphoristic closer has a sibling that does not sound like a maxim and so passes its test: the
**wry last line** of a paragraph. `Ein Möbelhaus mit Probesitzen, nur eben für Achterbahnen.`,
`(Irgendwer muss die Churros ja frittieren.)` Each is fine alone; the tell is the rate. In
September 2026, 23 % of the German posts' paragraphs of three or more sentences ended on a short
sentence after a long one. A text that winks at the end of every paragraph is performing a
personality. Three shapes carry most of it:

- **The appended verdict**: `…, und genau das ist die Aussage.`, `…, and that's the point.`
- **No X, no Y, just Z**: `Kein Bildschirm, kein Projektor, seit 1952 einfach Mechanik.`
- **The verbless fragment**: `Elf Papierkörbe mit Sprachausgabe.`

Budget: **one wink per section, and never two paragraphs in a row**; a long catalogue section (a
dozen rides under one heading) may carry one per five paragraphs. Keep the funniest one or the one
that carries a fact; turn the rest into the fact they decorated, or stop a sentence earlier. A
spec fragment (`Mindestgröße 100 Zentimeter.`) is information and stays. `check:prose` flags the
first two shapes; the fragment and the rate are for the review pass (§7.2).

## 2.18 The register of the model that writes here

The word lists in §3 come from GPT-era studies; this site is written with Claude, and each model
has its own idiolect. Arize measured six habits in Claude's prose in September 2026. Four are
covered elsewhere: signposts are the teaser (§2.16), contrast reframes §2.1, gotcha framing the
question set-up (§2.12), verdict intensifiers (`the honest answer is`, `die ehrliche Antwort`) the
honesty family (§3.3). The other two, plus a pair of adverbs:

- **Salience flags**: telling the reader something matters instead of showing why. `This
matters.`, `Das ist wichtig, weil …`, `a fact worth internalising`, `deserves a moment`.
- **Stock metaphors**: `load-bearing`, `earns its keep`, `earns its place`, `der Knackpunkt`,
  `die Falle ist`, `verdient sich seinen Platz`, `tragende Rolle`.
- **Adverbs** fine alone and a tic in bulk: `quietly`, `genuinely`, `still`, `wirklich`
  (counted as `filler-adverb-pileup`, §2.20).

Commit messages and PR bodies follow the same rules (§5.9).

## 2.19 The database instead of the park

A post written from a query result keeps the query's words. The rain guide's excerpt (October 2026) read, and Patrick read it three times without understanding it:

> An Regentagen sind die Headliner in zwölf von zwölf ausgewerteten Parks kürzer besetzt als an
> trockenen Tagen gleicher Art, im Movie Park fast ein Drittel, im Disneyland Paris kaum.

Every phrase in it is a habit with a name:

- **Our schema as vocabulary**: `Einträge`, `38 von 39 Einträgen führen einen Wert`,
  `Jede Attraktion kann drei Werte tragen`. Write `Attraktionen`, `überdacht`, `im Freien`; a
  reader has never seen our table.
- **The field name read aloud**: `Bahnen im Gebäude`, 30 times, is the enum `indoor`. Write the
  word the reader searches and the UI shows: `überdacht`, `Indoor-Bahn`, `in der Halle`.
- **The gap reported instead of left out**: `Bei neun der 37 wissen wir es noch nicht: …`, a
  column „Noch ohne Angabe“, `Wo ein Strich steht, fehlt uns die Angabe.` Leave it out; name a
  gap only where the reader would otherwise draw a wrong conclusion, once.
- **A comparison against an unnamed bucket**: `trockene Tage gleicher Art`,
  `Tage derselben Gruppe`. Say what is compared, with an example: `Einen verregneten
Ferien-Samstag im Juli vergleichen wir mit trockenen Wochenend- und Feiertagen im Juli, die auch
in den Ferien lagen.`
- **A verb that does not fit its noun**: `die Headliner sind kürzer besetzt`,
  `Der Regen stammt aus den Tageswerten`. The wait is shorter; the rainfall figures come from
  Open-Meteo.
- **A genitive chain**: `der Durchschnitt der gemessenen Wartezeiten über die Headliner des
Parks` → `wie lange man an seinen Headlinern im Schnitt anstand`.
- **A circular definition**: `Als Headliner führen wir die Bahnen, die nach der Historie der
Wartezeiten dazugehören.` → `Headliner sind die Bahnen, an denen in einem Park am längsten
angestanden wird.`
- **A calque**: `Ein verregneter Samstag steht gegen trockene Wochenendtage` (_stands against_) →
  `vergleichen wir mit`.
- **The elliptical series**: `im Movie Park fast ein Drittel, im Disneyland Paris kaum` (shorter
  by what?) → `im Movie Park um fast ein Drittel kürzer`.
- **`X von X`**: `in zwölf von zwölf ausgewerteten Parks` → `in allen zwölf Parks` (§3.3 rule
  13).

The test is the reader's question. Somebody looking for a park on a rainy Saturday asks which rides
are under a roof and whether it is emptier; a sentence about our records answers neither and goes.
The same test settles SEO, because the reader's words are the search words: nobody types
„Freizeitpark Bahnen im Gebäude“, and „überdachte Attraktionen“ is what the park page's filter says.

`check:prose` counts the schema words (`führt … als` among them) per post in six languages and
warns above four per 1,000 words (the rain guide read 18 in German), warns on `gleicher Art`,
`derselben Gruppe`, `of the same kind`, `van hetzelfde soort` and on a sentence about our method
(`berücksichtigen wir`, `we take into account`), and fails on `kürzer besetzt` and on `X von X` in
six languages. The genitive chain, the circular definition, the calque and the needless gap are for
the review pass. §3.3 rule 23 is the next step: the query's rows read out, not only its words.
