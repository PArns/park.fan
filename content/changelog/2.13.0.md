## New

- Inside a park, the homepage opens on that park: its photo, today's opening hours, the weather
  and "Plan today", which starts the day planner for this park and this date. Under it is a
  compass with north at the top and each of the park's top rides as a dot in its direction,
  coloured by its current wait. The bar below the dial names the ride your phone is pointing at,
  and a tap pins one. On an iPhone it takes one press of "Turn on compass", because Safari asks
  before a page may read the motion sensor.
- "Shorter now than later" lists up to three rides whose live wait is at least 10 minutes below
  the forecast for the next two hours. You see it on the park page and on the homepage when you
  are in the park and have no plan for the day.
- News moved to /news, a stream with the age of every item and a filter by park. It has its own
  entry in the menu, the newest item appears as a chip on the homepage, and posts published since
  your last visit are announced once in a small toast. The old /blog/ addresses of news posts
  redirect.
- 32 parks have a page for visiting with children (as of 29 September): one card per height step
  the park itself uses, with how many rides that height allows ("23 of 40") and which ones it adds.
  Each step links to the park page filtered to that height and to a day plan for it.
- A crowd forecast on park pages colours twelve rows of days, one per month, by the predicted
  crowd level. The forecast reaches about six months ahead, so the later months stay grey.
- When the rain warning on a park page fires, it lists up to four covered rides nearby, and a
  "Covered" filter narrows the ride list to them. Both appear only where at least half of the
  park's rides in season say whether they are indoors.
- A plan can be shared by link. Whoever opens it gets a copy in their own browser, and only after
  pressing a button; the link carries the plan's id after the `#`, so it never reaches a server log.
- The ride list says when its live data is from ("As of 14:05") and switches to a warning with a
  retry button once it is more than ten minutes old.
- Every page links here from its footer, and the version number there opens that version's
  entry. The list at the top of this page jumps to any release by number and date.

## Improved

- A tap on a park page answers in 208 ms at the median instead of 712 ms, typing in the ride
  search in 88 ms instead of 892, and switching to the weather tab in 224 ms instead of 1,224.
  Measured on a phone-sized window at four times CPU slowdown. Search Console had flagged 361 park
  pages for slow responses on 23 September.
- Lists got shorter on phones. A ride card is one row of 72 px where it took 121 to 318, which
  brings the Phantasialand list from 6,503 px to 4,328 and Magic Kingdom's from 12,053 to 4,136.
  Park cards went from 146 px to 100, the footer from 1,102 px to 636, and the German homepage
  from 27,905 px to 22,558 with the park lists moved to the top.
- We ask for your location on the homepage and on park pages only, and on a first visit only
  after you tap our button. Saying no silences the banner for 30 days. A line under "Theme parks
  near you" lets you switch it back on, and explains how when the browser has blocked it.
- On a phone, the planner's sheet follows your finger and settles at three heights. Rides move in
  five-minute steps, and the action bar takes 105 px instead of 210.
- Picking a ride for a wait-time alert is a searchable list with photos and live waits, and the
  bell on a ride opens it with that ride already chosen.
- The tooltip on a ride card's crowd badge lists all six levels in that ride's own minutes, with
  the current one in bold.

## Fixed

- A failed request to our data API turned park, ride, calendar and statistics pages into "page
  not found".
- The city in the breadcrumb linked to a page that only redirected to the one park in it, in 103
  of 144 cities.
- The in-park line showed the raw status `OPERATING` instead of a translated word.
- Tapping into a planner field on an iPhone zoomed the page, because the inputs were 14 px.
- "Today at the park" had an empty band of 104 px (135 on a phone) in the middle, held free for
  a rain warning that 11 of 210 parks had on the day it was added. The warning is a line in the
  panel's title row now, 45 px tall whether it fires or not.
- On a 320 px phone, the cards of a country summary ran 38 px out of their box.
