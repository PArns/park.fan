# A ride's figure comes from the RCDB, and the park's own stands beside it (REQUIREMENT)

Height, length, drop and speed of a ride come from the [RCDB](https://rcdb.com). Where the park publishes a different figure, the post gives both, each with its owner, in the same sentence: „Silver Star ist laut RCDB 127 km/h schnell, der Europa-Park nennt 130.“ Where the two agree, the figure stands without attribution, like every other spec ([writing rules §1.2](../writing/1-substance.md#12-every-claim-carries-its-evidence-or-it-goes)), and the RCDB page goes into the source list.

A ride the RCDB does not list (a dark ride, a water ride, a show) takes the park's figure, stated plainly.

## Why

Parks round, and they measure from other points. Silver Star is 127 km/h in the RCDB and 130 km/h on the Europa-Park's page. Big One in Blackpool is 213 ft in the RCDB and 235 ft in the park's material, because the park counts from a different base. A post that takes whichever figure it found first contradicts the next post, the glossary and the park page, and a reader who knows the ride sees it at once.

The coaster-records guide and `rideProfile` carried both kinds of figure side by side until 2026-10-22 (PAR-993, PAR-994).

## How to apply

- Look the ride up in the RCDB before writing a figure. The fact list notes both values when they differ.
- Differ by rounding only (130 against 127)? Both still go into the sentence: the reader on the park's site sees 130.
- A table takes the RCDB figure; a footnote or the sentence under it names the park's figure where it differs.
