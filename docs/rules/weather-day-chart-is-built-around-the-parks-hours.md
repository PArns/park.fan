# Weather day chart is built around the park's hours (REQUIREMENT)

One standing rule. It is indexed from the repo's [`CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md), which carries the rule in one line and links here for the reasoning, the measurements and the counter-examples.

the hourly chart's time axis is **piecewise linear, not linear** — an open hour is drawn 4× as wide as a closed one, which takes the median park's opening hours from 42 % of the width to 74 % and is what makes room for hour-by-hour ticks and a few temperature readings inside the visit. Both kinks sit **exactly on the dashed borders of the opening-hours band**: a change of slope the eye cannot account for reads as weather, so it has to land on a line that is already there and is labelled with the opening time. Parks with no OPERATING row for today (about a quarter of the catalogue on any given day) keep the exact linear axis they had — `buildDayScale` returns `null` and every formula reduces to the old one, which the unit tests assert. Geometry is pure and lives in `lib/utils/weather-chart-axis.ts` (`pnpm test:weather-chart-axis`), never in the component; it works in a continuous **point index**, never in hours-since-midnight, because a DST day has 23 or 25 points and `points[i]` is then not the `i`-th hour. The chart owes the card **143 px** whatever it draws (`h-28` + `mt-1` + a fixed `h-[27px]` axis), so every tick and label is out of flow and `whitespace-nowrap` — as a flex row of 24 equal cells the German labels wrapped and it came out 154.5 px against a 143 px reservation on every phone. Tick density is a **container query** on the chart, not a `sm:` breakpoint: `/ui` renders this card three to a row, so a 355 px chart at a 1280 px viewport would otherwise get a desktop's worth of labels.
