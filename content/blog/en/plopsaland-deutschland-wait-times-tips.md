Anyone driving to Haßloch looking for Holiday Park will still find it. The road is still called
Holiday-Park-Straße, the house number has not moved, and the coaster most people come for has
stood in the same spot since 2001. Only the park has a different name: since 28 June 2025 it is
[Plopsaland Deutschland](ref:plopsaland-deutschland), named after the Belgian operator that has
owned it since November 2010.

Search for "Holiday Park" in 2026 and you also get pages from earlier years, with prices that no
longer apply. A dated ticket costs from 36 euros online in 2026. The operator dropped the old
brand with the rename; the street and the house number kept it.

The park sits in the Palatinate, between Neustadt an der Weinstraße and Ludwigshafen, on
40 hectares. It drew 804,218 visitors in 2025, which the operator calls the best year in the
park's history. That makes it smaller than Europa-Park or Phantasialand and bigger than almost
anything else in Rhineland-Palatinate. We have been measuring wait times here for two years,
currently across 57 attractions.

## Expedition GeForce, and why it has lasted 25 years

[bigFM Expedition GeForce](ref:plopsaland-deutschland/bigfm-expedition-geforce) is an Intamin
machine from 2001, and it's the reason people drive to the Palatinate from Hamburg. The park
gives 55 metres, 120 km/h, 1,220 metres of track, a ride time of 1:15 and up to 4.5 g. German
Wikipedia says 53 metres; that is the height of the track against the park's own figure, which
is taken from the highest point of the lift hill. Where they disagree, this guide uses the
park's number.

```glossary-widget slug=hyper-coaster

```

At opening it was the fastest coaster in Europe and, after The Big One at Pleasure Beach Resort
in England, the second tallest. It hasn't been either for a long time. What it has stayed is
harder to put in a table. It has won the Golden Ticket Award of the trade magazine _Amusement
Today_ as Europe's best coaster several times, and the _Internet Coaster Poll_ has voted it the
best in the world more than once. The park now counts 40 awards.

The reason sits behind the first drop. The ride has no inversion, no launch and no track
switch. It has three hills shaped so that the train is lighter at the crest than its passengers,
and a helix at the end where that effect flips the other way. If airtime is what you ride for,
that's the trip.

![A view from above of a red steel coaster whose track runs through wooded ground in long hills and one wide curve. | Expedition GeForce seen from the Free Fall Tower. The three hills after the first drop are where the ride earned its reputation. Foto: Hetamin1000, Wikimedia Commons (Public Domain) | right](/media/plopsaland-deutschland/expedition-geforce-airtime-huegel-4x3.jpg)

The practical part: 140 centimetres and up, nothing below that. It runs from 8 degrees Celsius,
which in March and November can decide whether you get a ride or a walk. The Express Pass is
valid.

## The other three coasters

**Sky Scream** by Premier Rides has stood since 2014 where Superwirbel stood from 1979 to 2013,
Germany's first coaster with inversions. Its successor is a Sky Rocket II: 45 metres tall,
100 km/h, 263 metres of track, one inversion. The train is launched out of the station three
times, forwards and backwards, before it clears the vertical top hat. Minimum height
140 centimetres, minimum age 14, and the same eight-degree rule applies, measured at the track.

![A red track climbs vertically up a grey support frame, tips over at the top and falls in a loop behind a brick building. | Sky Scream launches its trains three times before they clear the 45-metre top hat. Foto: Freddo, Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0) | left](/media/plopsaland-deutschland/sky-scream-top-hat-4x3.jpg)

**100% Wolf – The Family Roller Coaster** is the 2026 addition, opened on 22 March. Gerstlauer
built it: 17.5 metres tall, 760 metres long, 55 km/h, with a launch section, a track switch for
the backwards run and a spike as the closing element. It's why the park appears in this year's
new-ride lists at all, and it's a family machine: from 100 centimetres with an adult, from 130
alone, and it runs from 5 degrees.

In our measurements it sits top of the park's wait times, which says more about the park than
about the ride. More on that below.

**Tabaluga's Rollercoaster** by Zierer is the smallest of the four at 9 metres and 39 km/h, and
the only one under a roof, and it belongs to the Holiday Indoor hall, opened in 2018 across
5,000 square metres. On a wet day that is the part of the park which suddenly fills up.

## Water, height and the rest

The Free Fall Tower was Germany's first drop tower when it opened in 1997, an Intamin Giant
Drop, 70 metres tall, from 120 centimetres. It's the thing you see from the A65 before you take
the exit.

Two water rides keep the park busy through summer:

- **DinoSplash**, called Donnerfluss until 2019, dates from 1984 and was Germany's first rapid
  river ride. 500 metres, fed from groundwater, from 110 centimetres with an adult and from 130
  alone. During the Halloween Fright Nights it runs until 20:00 and no later.
- **Wickie Splash**, called Teufelsfässer until 2013, is a Mack log flume from 1992 with
  20 metres of height and 1,000 metres of track. It was the first log flume in Europe with a
  backwards shot. From 100 centimetres with an adult, from 140 alone.

Next to them sits **Splash Battle** in Wickieland, where boat crews and bystanders soak each
other, and **The Big Wave**, a Zamperla Disk'O. All three share the same themed area.

![Flat boats with dragon heads drift through a turquoise basin, with target discs beside them and a grey rock carrying a chute. | Splash Battle in Wickieland, with the track of Expedition GeForce behind it. Foto: Freak-Line-Community, Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0) | right](/media/plopsaland-deutschland/splash-battle-wikingerdorf-4x3.jpg)

**Sky Fly** by Gerstlauer, in place since 2015 where the sold-off Bounty Tower stood, is the one
for people who want to flip themselves over. The **Lighthouse Tower** by Funtime has stood since
2006 and is where you get a look at the whole site in one go.

For younger visitors the park is densely packed: Majaland from 2012 has eleven attractions, and
Blinky Bill, Super Wings, The Beach and the indoor hall add more. **The Smurfs' Adventure** is a
Mack dark ride that was called Burg Falkenstein until the end of 2022 and reopened for the 2025
season with new theming.

## The themed areas, and what is in each

The park is laid out as a star. The areas branch off the Square of Fountains behind the
entrance, and you cross the middle several times a day. That's less tiring than it sounds,
because 40 hectares stay walkable.

The **Square of Fountains** holds the Free Fall Tower and the dancing fountains that turn up in
every Plopsa park. From here it is a few minutes in any direction.

**Majaland** was built in 2012 on the site of the old entrance and exit and holds eleven
attractions for small children, most of them by Zamperla and Zierer: Maya's Flower Splash as a
Flying Fish, the Flower Tower as the Free Fall Tower's little brother, the Swinging Tree, the
Butterfly Flight, The Frogs, Mini-Cars, Lara's Ladybug Flight, Willy's Raft Circuit and the Bee
Whirl. Arrive with a three-year-old and you will spend half the day here.

**Holiday Indoor** is the 2018 hall, with Tabaluga's Rollercoaster, Mia's Elf Flight, the Farm
Carousel, a wave slide, a ball pool and a theatre seating over 500. It's the rain plan, and the
reason an October visit here carries less risk than at an open-air-only park.

The **Palatine Village** holds DinoSplash and The Smurfs' Adventure, plus the Pfalzgraf
restaurant and a giant wine barrel by Maurer Söhne. **Wickieland** carries Wickie Splash, Splash
Battle and The Big Wave. **Air Show 71** was finished in July 2016 on the plot of the sold Bounty
Tower and holds Sky Fly, Balloon Race, the Red Baron and the Wellenflug. Smaller areas round it
out: **The Beach** with the Lighthouse Tower and the fishing boats, **Blinky Bill** with the
Greenville Bus and Blinky's Soapboxes, and **Super Wings** with three slides and a water
playground.

### Shows and meet and greets

The park lists 16 programme items, and all but three are encounters with Studio 100 characters:
Maya and her friends, Vic and Halvar, Tabaluga, Heidi and Peter, the Smurfs, Blinky Bill, and
since 2026 Freddy Lupin from 100% Wolf. The Smurfs' Show, the Plopsa Heroes Show and a dance
session make up the rest.

Two things follow for planning. First, the times are only fixed in the morning and depend on the
daily programme the park publishes per date on its opening-hours page. Second, meet and greets
pull people away from the rides. While Maya is on the Majaland stage at 10:50, the Free Fall
Tower is emptier.

## How long you actually queue here

We have been collecting this park's wait times since 7 October 2024, by now across 225 operating
days. The figures below come out of that window rather than a single season.

```ride-waits-widget park=plopsaland-deutschland top=8 columns=land,peak,days highlight=bigfm-expedition-geforce

```

The figure that stands out on first reading is how low they all are. The median sits at 5 or
10 minutes at most attractions, and even at the busier end of a typical day it reaches 10 to 20.
The attraction with the park's longest measured queue is 100% Wolf, and it stands there with a
median of 10 minutes.

That's not a measurement error but the scale of this park: 804,218 visitors a year across
57 attractions spread out differently from 6 million across a hundred. Arrive from Europa-Park
or Phantasialand, where you budget 60 minutes for a headliner, and you'll plan this day wrong.

A planning rule follows that is specific to this park. The limiting factor is not the queue but
the opening hours. Eight hours, one of them spent on lunch and walking, leave room for twenty
rides and more at a median of ten minutes. Bring a list of eight attractions and you are through
it by early afternoon, deciding what to repeat. That's the difference from a large park, where
the same list fills the whole day.

It cuts the other way too. On a cold March day with both eight-degree coasters standing still,
not much is left for adults. A short queue is no cushion against a closed ride.

```hourly-profile-widget slug=plopsaland-deutschland top=8

```

Within the day the usual shape holds. The curve climbs through the morning, holds over midday
and drops off in the afternoon. Ride the three big coasters in the first hour after opening and
you walk onto all of them, and at a park this size that simply means being on time.

### The Express Pass

```glossary-widget slug=express-pass

```

The Express Pass costs 7 euros for one ride, 35 euros for nine rides and 95 euros for unlimited
rides, each per person per day. It is valid at eight attractions: 100% Wolf, DinoSplash, Wickie
Splash, The Big Wave, bigFM Expedition GeForce, Sky Scream, Free Fall Tower and Sky Fly. At
least 15 minutes have to pass between two rides, and the park sells only a limited number.

Doing the sums matters more here than elsewhere. With a median of 10 minutes at the busiest
ride, the 35-euro Classic buys you nine lots of roughly ten minutes, so around an hour and a
half on a day that ends at 18:00 anyway. A Saturday in July or a Halloween Fright Night changes
the arithmetic; a Friday in September doesn't support it. The price is the same all year, the
queue is not.

### The weekday

Saturday is the busiest day of the week by a clear margin: a median of 15 minutes, 20 at the
upper end, measured across 39 Saturdays. Every other day sits at a median of 10.

The quietest day is **Friday**, and it's the one result here nobody would have guessed. Across
38 measured Fridays the median is 5 minutes, below Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday. If you can
take a day off and are choosing between Friday and Wednesday, take the Friday.

One limit on that. From late September the advantage flips, because the Halloween Fright Nights
run on October Fridays and the park stays open until 22:00. Friday evening is then the busiest
part of the week.

### The month

```stats-widget slug=plopsaland-deutschland show=months

```

July is the busiest month, the only one besides October to pass the median of 10 that the rest
of the main season sits at, at 15. **September** is the quietest month with full operation: a
median of 10 minutes and 10 at the upper end of a day too, the lowest reading of any month
between April and October. On top of that the park is still open daily then, and September
weather in the Palatinate often beats June.

March and November are edge months. Expedition GeForce and Sky Scream only run once the track
reaches eight degrees, and a cold day costs you both of the rides you came for.

January and February sit at zero minutes in our data. That is not an empty queue but the winter
break: the park is shut, and the 2026 season runs from 22 March to 1 November. So a visit has
about seven and a half months to choose from, with the edge weeks hanging on the eight-degree
rule.

### Halloween 2026

The Halloween Fright Nights run every Friday and Saturday in October: the 9th and 10th, the 16th
and 17th, the 23rd and 24th, and the 30th and 31st. On those dates the park is open from 10:00
to 22:00 instead of 18:00. Every other October day stays at 10:00 to 18:00.

During the day there is the Plopsa Autumn Festival and a pumpkin display in the pumpkin village,
both of which work with small children. The mazes run only on the Fright Nights, and they show
up with us as attractions of their own as soon as they open: Skrämma, NEXUS AI, Murder District,
Academy of Freaks, Scarecrow, Titty Twister and LOST: Deep in the woods, plus Holly's Halloween
Party and a meeting with King Pumpkin for younger guests. On the remaining days they stand on the
park page as closed, and that's exactly what it means.

Two things run differently on a Fright Night. Some daytime attractions close before the park
does, DinoSplash at 20:00 for instance. And the eight hours you normally get are twelve, with
the last four belonging to the mazes. What the park has announced for this season is in our post
on the [Halloween Fright Nights 2026](/blog/plopsaland-deutschland-halloween-fright-nights-2026).

## From fairy-tale park to Plopsaland

The Schneider family bought the Märchenpark Haßloch in 1970 and opened it under their own
management in 1971, at first with a wooden wild mouse, a dolphin show and a village later known
as Liliputstadt. The family had run a circus of short-statured performers for generations, and
the park gave it a permanent home; that part existed until 1996. The name Holiday Park arrived
in 1973.

Then forty years of what happens at German parks this size: the Holiday Transit monorail in
1973, the wild mouse burning down in 1978, Superwirbel in 1979 as Germany's first coaster with
inversions, Donnerfluss in 1984, the Free Fall Tower in 1997, Expedition GeForce in 2001. The
70,000 square metres of the first season had grown past 400,000 by 2015.

One episode helped build Expedition GeForce's reputation: in 2002 and 2003 the American lecturer
Richard Rodriguez rode his coaster marathon here, days of continuous riding. A monument next to
the ride has stood there since, and his record attempts earned the park coverage far beyond the
region in both years.

The break came in November 2010, when the Belgian Plopsa group, a subsidiary of Studio 100, took
over. Since then it has been a themed park with connected areas, and its mascots are Maya, Vic,
Heidi, Mia and Tabaluga rather than Holly, the parrot who lived here before. Majaland went up in
2012 on the site of the old entrance, bringing today's main gate with it. The indoor hall
followed in 2018, the new coaster in 2026.

The rename of 28 June 2025 is the logical last step. The operator has long called its other
parks Plopsaland and Plopsa, and Haßloch was the last one still carrying a name from before. For
visitors it changes nothing except the search query.

## What comes next

For 2028 the park is building a coaster that has no name yet in the list of current ones: an
Xtreme Spinning Coaster by Mack Rides, due to get Tomorrowland theming of the sort The Ride to
Happiness already has at sister park Plopsaland Belgium. It's in the park's master plan and
listed as under construction at RCDB.

That would give the park its fifth coaster and the first since 100% Wolf aimed at adults. Until
then, Expedition GeForce is what you drive here for.

## Staying and eating

There's no park hotel. The park works with partner hotels in Haßloch, Speyer, Deidesheim,
Neustadt and Mannheim instead and sells ticket-and-room packages. If you are on holiday in the
Palatinate anyway you don't need them. The distances are short, and from Neustadt or Speyer the
car park is twenty minutes away.

For food the park lists 22 outlets, from snack stand to restaurant. The address for a hot meal
sitting down is the Pfalzgraf restaurant, called Casa Palatina between 2015 and 2023; annual
pass holders get ten per cent off there. Next to it are the Palatine wine and beer garden, Pasta
Point, Koala Joe's Roadhouse in the Blinky Bill area, the Maya Burger in Majaland, a Super Wings
Fuel Station and a Dunkin' park café.

The regional note is no accident. The park sits in the middle of a wine region, and a tarte
flambée or a glass of Riesling is part of the offer, which isn't what you expect at a park with
cartoon mascots.

## A day in Haßloch, roughly planned

The park opens at 10:00 and, outside the Fright Nights, closes at 18:00. Eight hours is tight
for 57 attractions and generous for the eight you actually want.

1. **10:00** Straight to Expedition GeForce. It is empty for the first half hour, and two rides
   back to back are normal here rather than a stroke of luck.
2. **10:45** On to Sky Scream next door. Both depend on the eight-degree rule, so it makes sense
   to get them done early if the day starts cold.
3. **11:30** 100% Wolf. The ride is new and so it has the park's longest queue, which at a
   median of ten minutes is a survivable statement.
4. **12:30** Lunch, then the water rides: DinoSplash and Wickie Splash sit close enough to do in
   one go. Both get you wet, and in October that's a decision rather than a side effect.
5. **14:30** Free Fall Tower and Sky Fly, plus the Lighthouse Tower for the view across the site.
6. **15:30** With children, Majaland and the indoor hall; without them, Expedition GeForce again.
   The afternoon is the day's second quiet stretch.
7. **17:00** The Smurfs' Adventure or Splash Battle last, then out.

On a Fright Night everything shifts four hours later: the mazes open in the evening, and some
daytime attractions run shorter than the park. DinoSplash, for one, stops at 20:00.

## Practicalities: getting there, tickets, hours

The address is Holiday-Park-Str. 1–5, 67454 Haßloch, and the street still carries the old park
name. Type it into a sat nav and you'll never need the new one.

**By car.** Via the A65 or the B9, with car parks right at the site. Dropping someone off or
picking them up is free for up to 45 minutes; the barrier opens on number-plate recognition.
Staying for the day needs a parking ticket, which the park sells alongside admission; the Plopsa
Pass Europe Premium at 235 euros includes a parking subscription.

**By train.** To Haßloch station, from where a shuttle bus runs to the park several times a day
between 22 March and 1 November 2026. It is included in VRN tickets to Haßloch and in the
Deutschlandticket, so it costs nothing extra. Groups of ten or more are asked to book it ahead.

**Tickets.** A dated ticket costs from 36 euros online. Family tickets for three to six people
start at 43 euros per person. An undated ticket, valid for a year on one day of your choosing,
costs 58 euros. The daily price depends on the date: for Thursday 8 October 2026 the park's
calendar shows 42 euros.

**Annual passes.** The Pass Light costs 120 euros a year and has up to 35 blackout days. The
Plopsaland Deutschland Pass costs 150 euros, is valid on every operating day and brings
discounts on souvenirs, at the Pfalzgraf and at the other Plopsa parks. The Plopsa Pass Europe
Premium at 235 euros covers all Plopsa parks and the parking subscription. For children between
85 and 99 centimetres and for guests aged 70 and over, each of the three costs half.

**Opening hours.** In October 2026 daily from 10:00 to 18:00, and until 22:00 on the eight
Fright Night dates. The season ends on 1 November.

## Frequently asked questions

### Has Holiday Park been renamed?

Yes. Since 28 June 2025 it has been Plopsaland Deutschland. The operator, the site and the
attractions are the same, as is the address on Holiday-Park-Straße.

### How tall and how fast is Expedition GeForce?

The park gives 55 metres and 120 km/h, with 1,220 metres of track and 4.5 g. The ride lasts one
minute and 15 seconds.

### How tall does my child have to be?

The two big coasters start at 140 centimetres, and Sky Scream additionally at a minimum age of 14. The Free Fall Tower starts at 120 centimetres. 100% Wolf and Wickie Splash take children
from 100 centimetres with an adult, DinoSplash from 110. Riding alone needs 130 centimetres at
100% Wolf and DinoSplash, and 140 at Wickie Splash.

### When is Plopsaland Deutschland quietest?

On Fridays outside the Halloween season, and in September. Across 38 measured Fridays the median
was 5 minutes, against 10 in the other main-season months. Saturday is the busiest day at 15.

### How long is the average wait here?

Shorter than you'll be used to from other parks. Across all measured days the park's typical
average sits at a good 18 minutes, and at individual attractions the median is usually 5 to
10 minutes.

### Is the Express Pass worth it?

More so on a July Saturday and at the Fright Nights than on a Friday in September. The Classic
at 35 euros buys nine rides through the shortened entrance, with at least 15 minutes between
them. At this park's measured waits that saves you an hour and a half rather than half a day.

### Which coasters does the park have?

Four: Expedition GeForce (2001), Sky Scream (2014), Tabaluga's Rollercoaster (2018) and
100% Wolf (2026). A fifth is under construction for 2028.

### Is one day enough for the whole park?

For the big attractions, comfortably. 57 attractions sound like more than they are: a large
share are carousels and playgrounds in Majaland, the indoor hall and Super Wings that adults
without children skip. With children the same park fills two days, because you then ride the
small ones round after round.

### Can you get there without a car?

Yes. By train to Haßloch station, then the shuttle bus, which runs between 22 March and
1 November 2026 and is included in VRN tickets and the Deutschlandticket.

### What happened to the old attractions?

Superwirbel made way for Sky Scream in 2013. The Bounty Tower went to Wunderland Kalkar in 2015.
Holly's Wild Ride has been at Eifelpark since 2017. Donnerfluss has been DinoSplash since 2020,
Teufelsfässer has been Wickie Splash since 2013, and Burg Falkenstein reopened in 2025 as The
Smurfs' Adventure.
