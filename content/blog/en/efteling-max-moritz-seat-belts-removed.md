In the first week of November, [Efteling](ref:efteling) took the seat belts back out of the cars on its family coaster [Max & Moritz](ref:efteling/max-and-moritz). Since then, each rider has been held by their own lap bar and nothing else. The Dutch theme park site Looopings reported the change. Efteling itself hasn't announced it. Efteling had fitted the belts in July 2025, meant at the time as a temporary fix. That fix lasted about 15 months.

## What changes when you board

Before each train leaves, the staff now only check the lap bars. Looopings reckons boarding goes faster as a result, and the ride gets more people through each hour.

Since the end of September there's also been a fixed order: the smallest person gets in first. There's a drawing of the rule in the queue, with text in Dutch, English, German and French. Whoever gets in first sits on the left, because the lap bars on the left and the right are set differently. A child riding next to an adult always ends up in the left seat. It's the same on both tracks, the blue one (Max) and the green one (Moritz).

## Who can ride

The minimum height is 0.90 m. Children between 0.90 m and 1.30 m have to ride with someone aged 16 or over. When the ride opened, Efteling said it was aimed at children from four to ten, and back then the minimum height was 1 metre.

Max & Moritz has its own single rider queue and offers Baby Switch.

## Max & Moritz during Winter Efteling

[Winter Efteling](/news/winter-efteling-2026-ice-rink-show) runs from 16 November 2026 to 31 January 2027. Max & Moritz isn't on the list of maintenance work the park keeps on its website. Other rides close for a while for maintenance, including the Droomvlucht until 4 December, De Oude Tufferbaan from 16 to 27 November and Joris en de Draak from 30 November to 18 December.

[Max & Moritz](ref:efteling/max-and-moritz?full)

## The ride and the one before it

Max & Moritz opened on Saturday, 20 June 2020. It took the place of the bobsled ride Efteling pulled down in 2019. Its old station building is still standing and is where Max & Moritz now boards. The theme comes from Wilhelm Busch's picture story, published in 1865. The restaurant Frau Boltes Küche is on the Max & Moritz square.

Mack Rides built the dual coaster as a Powered Coaster. Each train carries its own motors, so its speed can change along the way. Efteling had never had a ride of that type before. You pick your track at the station. The trains run in opposite directions and pass each other several times along the way. Each track is 300 metres long and no more than 6 metres high, and the cars lean up to 40 degrees into the turns. Part of the track runs inside a building. RCDB gives a top speed of 36 km/h, Mack Rides says 35. A ride lasts two and a half minutes, a train has 38 seats, and up to 1,800 people an hour can ride.

## Why there were belts at all

Older Powered Coasters have one lap bar for every two riders; on Max & Moritz each seat has its own. Five years after the opening, Efteling fitted the belts as a second restraint. At the time, insiders told Looopings that the bars had come open during the ride several times before then. Efteling itself only told Looopings about "een signaal over lichte speling op de beugel", a report of slight play in the lap bar.

> [!QUOTE]
> Of course we took that report seriously. […] The belts were added as a precaution, in consultation with the ride's supplier.
>
> [nl] Dat signaal hebben we uiteraard serieus genomen. […] De riemen zijn uit voorzorg toegevoegd in overleg met de leverancier van de attractie
>
> An Efteling spokeswoman to [Looopings in July 2025](https://www.looopings.nl/weblog/29769/Efteling-installeert-veiligheidsgordels-in-familieachtbaan-Max--Moritz--vijf-jaar-na-de-opening.html), translated from Dutch

The ride had always been safe and still is, the spokeswoman said at the time. Mack's other Powered Coasters in Europe don't have belts, for example the ones at Drievliet, [Plopsaland Belgium](ref:plopsaland-belgium) and [Bobbejaanland](ref:bobbejaanland). There's no rule there about who gets in first either.
