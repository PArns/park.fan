[Gröna Lund](ref:grona-lund) in Stockholm is opening its small Lilla Området area for "Barnens Jul", a new Christmas event for families with young kids and for grandparents bringing their grandchildren. The first day is 21 November, the last 30 December 2026.

The Tomte, Sweden's Father Christmas, sits among the fir trees in Lilla Området with Tomtemor and the Nissar, his elves. Gröna Lund has winterproofed the carousels and strung them with lights, and there are Christmas sweets too.

## Dates and opening hours

The area is open on 19 days, at weekends and during the Swedish Christmas holidays, from 10 am to 4 pm each time. It's closed on Christmas Eve.

| Month    | Days                                |
| -------- | ----------------------------------- |
| November | 21, 22, 28 and 29                   |
| December | 5, 6, 12, 13, 19 to 23 and 25 to 30 |

## Entry and tickets

Entry costs SEK 140 for everyone aged 4 to 64 and covers meeting the Tomte and every show. Children up to three and guests aged 65 and over get in free, so a grandmother of 65 pays less than her four-year-old grandchild. Rides and games cost extra, at prices Gröna Lund hasn't published yet. Tickets go on sale on 23 October at gronalund.com.

## The Tomte, wish lists and shows

Children write their wish lists in the Önskecafé, where the grown-ups sit over a fika, and then hand them to the Tomte in person. If that feels too scary, the list can go into the wish box in the café instead. The Nissar perform at the Tomte's stage.

The programme has the Gnuttarna's Christmas show, a choir of Christmas characters and an ice dance with the Ice Queen. Anyone who gets cold in between can head for the hot drinks or the grill, where children toast marshmallows. You can also look inside Pettson and Findus's house.

## Rides in Lilla Området

Of the 28 attractions that run in summer, the ones in Lilla Området open for Barnens Jul, among them the [Cirkuskarusellen](ref:grona-lund/cirkuskarusellen) and the [Tuff Tuff Tåget](ref:grona-lund/tuff-tuff-taget). Gröna Lund only runs them when the winter weather allows, and its announcement doesn't mention the big roller coasters.

## Getting there

Gröna Lund is on Djurgården, at Lilla Allmänna Gränd 9. Tram 7 leaves from T-Centralen at the central station and takes about ten minutes. Bus 67 comes from Karlaplan. Gröna Lund suggests staying on until the Skansen stop, one after Liljevalchs/Gröna Lund, because you'll get through the entrance faster from there.

The Djurgården ferry from Slussen takes about eight minutes, and an SL ticket for zone A is valid on board. While Slussen is being rebuilt, it leaves from Skeppsbrons kaj, about 130 metres further north. From Djurgårdsbron it's about a ten-minute walk.

The nearest car park, Apcoa's Garnisonen at Linnégatan 87, is a quarter of an hour's walk away, which is a long time when you're small and the Tomte is waiting. Gröna Lund itself recommends a park-and-ride outside the city centre.

## Gröna Lund in winter

Gröna Lund is Sweden's oldest amusement park and belongs to Parks and Resorts, the group that also owns Kolmården. The park has opened in winter before, but never with such a clear focus on Christmas for small children, says managing director Jan Eriksson, and he admits that doing something for the first time makes him a little nervous too ([press release](https://www.mynewsdesk.com/se/grona_lunds_tivoli/pressreleases/groena-lund-haaller-vinteroeppet-premiaer-foer-barnens-jul-21-november-3471962)).
