Six Flags is fitting seat belts to [Goliath](ref:six-flags-over-georgia/goliath?bare) at [Six Flags Over Georgia](ref:six-flags-over-georgia?bare) and to other roller coasters with a similar restraint. Chief executive John Reilly announced it on Friday evening, 23 October, in a letter to guests and pass holders. The fatal incident on Goliath had happened eleven days earlier. The ride stays closed until the safety checks and approvals it needs are in place. Eight more of the more than 220 coasters Reilly counts across all Six Flags parks are standing still at five other parks for now, although the parks themselves are open over the Halloween weekend.

> [!QUOTE]
> We are reinforcing safety precautions at our parks and taking additional measures, including adding seat belts on Goliath and other coasters with a similar restraint. […] We have engaged independent engineers and ride inspection specialists to comprehensively review current safety procedures across our entire portfolio of rides and attractions, across all of our parks.
>
> John Reilly, President and CEO of Six Flags Entertainment Corporation, in his [letter of 23 October 2026](https://www.sixflags.com/blog/update-from-john-reilly-six-flags)

## Which coasters are closed

Reilly doesn't say which eight he means or when they will run again. Shortly after the incident, a Six Flags representative gave the Atlanta station WSB-TV a list of nine closed coasters at six parks. One of them, [Goliath](ref:six-flags-magic-mountain/goliath?bare) at Six Flags Magic Mountain, comes from a different manufacturer and has a different restraint. According to AP, it was no longer on the list Six Flags updated on 15 October. KTLA reported that park president Brian Oerding had posted on the park's website that the ride was running again after an inspection.

Without the California Goliath, the list matches Reilly's count.

Six Flags Great America isn't open after 1 November, according to its calendar. That leaves Raging Bull only the days up to the Halloween weekend this year. The other four parks also have only a few days left before their winter events. Six Flags Great Adventure still opens from 5 to 8 November and on 14 and 15 November, Carowinds from 6 to 8 November and on 14, 15, 21 and 22 November, and Kings Island on 7 and 8 November. Holiday in the Park starts at Great Adventure on 20 November, and WinterFest at Carowinds and Kings Island on 27 November. Canada’s Wonderland is closed from 2 to 20 November and opens for WinterFest on 21 November.

## What's running at Six Flags Over Georgia

Six Flags Over Georgia reopened on Friday, 16 October, at first from 6 p.m., with Fright Fest in the evening, and for the whole day on the Saturday after. Every other ride that's scheduled to operate is open to guests, park president Richard Pretlow wrote on 13 October. According to The National Desk, Lisa Cupid, chair of the Cobb County Commission, said the decision on Goliath lies with the park and with Georgia's Insurance Commissioner, whose office inspects rides in the state.

Fright Fest runs on all three days of the Halloween weekend. The park opens on Friday, 30 October, from 6 to 11 p.m., on Saturday from noon to 11 p.m. and on Sunday, 1 November, from noon to 10 p.m. It opens again on 7 November, and Holiday in the Park starts on 21 November.

## Goliath's lap bar

Goliath in Georgia has the kind of lap bar known in the US as a "clamshell". It sits in front of each seat on a vertical post between the rider's knees and rests, padded, across the lap and thighs. AP reports that the ride has no second restraint such as a seat belt, and that photos and videos of most of the closed coasters show the same type of bar but no belt. Bolliger & Mabillard (B&M), Goliath's manufacturer, declined to comment on the closures, according to AP. Six Flags doesn't say whether any of the coasters already have a belt, when the belts will be in, or whether the coasters will stay shut until then. Atlanta News First, however, writes that some of the B&M Hyper Coasters at Six Flags are already fitted with one.

## Hersheypark, SeaWorld and La Ronde

[Candymonium](ref:hersheypark/candymonium?bare) at [Hersheypark](ref:hersheypark?bare) is a B&M Hyper Coaster too, but it has a seat belt at every seat as well as the lap bar, and the park isn't part of Six Flags. Hersheypark announced that the ride would run from 16 October, after the park's own inspection team, which includes state-certified inspectors, and an independent outside inspector had checked it. Hersheypark told the Pennsylvania Department of Agriculture, which oversees amusement parks in the state, about the decision. Hersheypark says that, from what's known so far, the manufacturer sees no technical reason to shut similar rides because of the Georgia incident alone.

[SeaWorld Orlando](ref:seaworld-orlando?bare) went the other way and closed [Mako](ref:seaworld-orlando/mako?bare), with no date for reopening, the park told AP. [La Ronde](ref:la-ronde?bare) in Montreal also took its own Goliath out of service as a precaution on 13 October. Time Out Montréal reports that it stays closed until the park hears more from the manufacturer and is sure the ride can be run safely.

## The incident on 12 October

On 12 October a 42-year-old rider was thrown from his seat on Goliath and died. According to AP, he struck other riders before he hit the ground beneath the ride. Four more riders were hurt, WSB-TV and The National Desk reported. Our post [on the rider's death on Goliath](/news/goliath-six-flags-over-georgia-fatal-incident) covers what was known on the day.

According to Atlanta News First, Cobb County Police and the Office of the Insurance and Safety Fire Commissioner are investigating together; AP names the police and a state agency. Neither the investigators nor Six Flags have given a cause.
