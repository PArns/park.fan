# A cell is gated on its content, and a component that fills one may not return `null` — nor one line (REQUIREMENT)

One standing rule. It is indexed from the repo's [`CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md), which carries the rule in one line and links here for the reasoning, the measurements and the counter-examples.

the ride page's „Beste Besuchszeit planen" grid counts its columns from `attraction.ropeDrop && typicalWaits?.displayable` — one level above `RopeDropCard`, which answered `null` for its negative case (not worth a rope drop, not an evening ride, and no neighbour in the park carrying a recommendation either, so the muted „no need to rush" note had nothing to contrast against). The cell, its hairline and a second column were drawn around nothing: measured over all 213 parks, **183 ride pages with a visibly empty half** plus 159 more showing an empty box under the chapter heading, because **60 parks carry no rope-drop recommendation at all** — every Efteling ride that has one is in that set, which is how it was reported. The fix is not a third condition at the call site, which is a second copy of the card's own logic free to drift from it, but making the component **total**: `ropeDropCardVariant()` resolves every recommendation to one of three panels and `RopeDropCard` returns `React.ReactElement`, so the next `return null` is a compiler error. Where a component genuinely may render nothing, export the predicate it uses itself and let the count and the cell ask the same question. **An element is not the bar — filling the cell with one line is the same bug one size smaller**, and it was the larger half: where the park DID carry recommendations, that note rendered alone in half a card beside a full chart, on **470 ride pages in 87 parks** (measured 2026-09-15) against the 183 the `null` cost. The note stays, because the contrast to a recommended neighbour is real information, but it is a **footer inside the stand-in panel** rather than a panel of its own, under the same single hairline as the low-confidence hint — so `parkHasRecommendations` is a footer detail and never a branch, and the ride gets the readings it always had (312 of those 470 print the quietest-weekday sentence, 66 %, against 209 of 377 for the rides that reach the panel on their own). **And moving a sentence changes what it claims, because its neighbours change**: it read „die Wartezeiten bleiben über den Tag überschaubar (ca. {openWait} Min. zur Öffnung)", which survives alone in half a card and not above three tiles — the bracket is a third copy of the wait at opening, and „überschaubar" is contradicted outright on **65 of the 470** (peak ≥ 60 min, 7 of them ≥ 90), up to Tokyo DisneySeas Soaring at 140 to 180. The footer keeps only what the branch knows, `worth === false` plus the contrast, and leaves the size of the queue to the tiles that measured it; the contrast may name the park's other rides because **zero** parks holding one of those 470 recommend only evenings. Rewritten in all six locales. The stand-in panel then has to earn the chapter's heading, and **what it may say is a measurement rather than a design**: over those 183 rides the quietest hour is defensible on **15** (146 carry a trough equal to the wait at opening), the quietest weekday on **119** (`quietestWeekdays` drops a thinly measured day rather than refusing and names both days of a tie, like the best-travel-time hub one grain coarser; 61 more read as a flat week, and 3 stay silent rather than name a day while a dropped thin day draws the shorter bar right beside the sentence — „no weekday stands out“ is a measurement and may only be printed where the week was measured), and `busyPeak − openWait` on all 183, median 25 minutes. It carries no sentence explaining the missing recommendation: that threshold lives in the backend, this repo cannot cite it, and an invented reason reads as measured to whoever finds it next. Same card, one more rule: **one colour rank, in every place a number appears** — the summary tiles said blue = Voll while the bars and legend said blue = Normal. The tiles carry the rank as the legend's own **swatch** rather than by tinting the digits, because `text-primary` is 3.47 : 1 on the light card and `text-lg font-semibold` is 18 px at 600, i.e. not WCAG „large text" and owing 4.5 — a swatch owes 3, which the same colour clears, and both figures keep a text-grade contrast. And **a wash is not a boundary**: `bg-primary/25` over the `bg-muted/40` track computes to **1.32 : 1** in light and 1.40 : 1 in dark, which is why the bar looked like it ended at the Normal value with the Voll number floating above it, and no opacity fixes it — the light track is rgb(251,251,251) and `bg-primary` itself only reaches 3.36 : 1 against it, so no third tone clears 3 : 1 from both the track and the solid segment. A boundary that must be seen is a **line**: a solid `bg-primary` top rule (3.36 : 1 light, 5.24 : 1 dark). Printing „typical–busy" above each bar instead was rejected on a count, not a hunch — 217 of the catalogue's 5,942 ride-days round to seven characters, ~40 px in a ~36 px column at 360 px, and the seven columns are `flex-1` so the row runs past the card rather than wrapping. **And the rank cannot name that number**, which the code claimed it did („the Voll value, in the Voll tone"): `text-muted-foreground` is `lab(66.13 0 0)` dark and `lab(48.50 0 0)` light, i.e. **achromatic**, so it sits outside a rank whose two members are a solid accent and a washed one — and the `title` carrying the pair predates the change and is unreachable on a phone. The row gets a caption of its own instead (the legend's Voll swatch plus „Zahlen: Voll"), with the swatch rather than the word alone because a lone label at the left edge of a seven-column row reads as the first column's. `pnpm test:typical-waits`.
