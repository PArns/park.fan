**[Der Schwur des Kärnan](ref:hansa-park/the-oath-of-kaernan)** stands **73 metres** tall and runs at
**127 km/h**. It has shared both figures with [Silver Star](ref:europa-park/silver-star?bare) at Europa-Park since **2015**, and
together the two are the tallest and fastest coasters in Germany.

Sierksdorf sits 24 kilometres north of Lübeck as the crow flies. The park calls itself "Germany's
only theme park by the sea". The holiday houses of the resort next door
stand right on the Baltic, the regional train from Lübeck to Neustadt runs between them and the
park, and from the top of the Highlander you look out over the Bay of Lübeck.

## A park on 46 hectares

Hansa-Park makes do with **46 hectares**. Heide Park has 85. Two coasters are rarely more than a few minutes' walk apart here, and the eleven themed
areas run into one another instead of being separated by lawns.

The [Highlander](ref:hansa-park/highlander?bare) is the tallest gyro drop tower in the world.
Alongside it there's a canal boat ride left over from the Legoland years and four family and kids'
coasters.

Four new coasters came between 2009 and 2017, Kärnan among them, then none
for nine years until the Cornwall Coaster opened in 2026. In the gap came the Highlander and a few
smaller themed areas, and since 2008 the park has been building one big area, "Hanse in Europa",
city by city.

## The eight coasters

**[Der Schwur des Kärnan](ref:hansa-park/the-oath-of-kaernan?bare)** opened in 2015 and cost around €26 million, the biggest investment the park had made up to then. Gerstlauer
built it as an Infinity Coaster: **1,235 metres** of track, minimum height 1.30 metres. The lift hill
sits entirely inside a **79-metre tower** that pulls the train straight up, and the first drop falls
67 metres.

Before that drop, inside the tower, lying on your back in the dark, the train falls backwards for a stretch at more than
60 metres up, before the lift carries it on to the first drop. The park calls that a world first.
In 2017 it more than doubled the drop speed, from around 5 to around 11 metres per second.

The second big one has been running since **2009**, these days in the Peterhof von Novgorod area.
**[Flucht von Novgorod](ref:hansa-park/escape-of-novgorod)** is another Gerstlauer, 40 metres tall,
**100 km/h**, and it has an LSM launch that takes you to 100 km/h in 1.4 seconds, in the dark, and a vertical
chain lift as well. The lift sits in the
second dark section of the ride, and the drop that follows goes **97 degrees**, past vertical. Until
2020 it was called Fluch von Novgorod, the curse of Novgorod, and since 2021 Flucht von Novgorod,
the escape from Novgorod.

Anton Schwarzkopf built **[Nessie](ref:hansa-park/nessie)** in **1980**: 26 metres tall, 741 metres long, 80 km/h, one
vertical loop, and at the time the largest looping coaster in Europe. It's been running for 46
years and got a rethemed area in 2019.

Of the four smaller ones, only **[Crazy Mine](ref:hansa-park/crazy-mine)** isn't a family ride. It's a Maurer and has run
as a wild mouse in mine-shaft dressing since 1997, 15 metres tall and over in 70 seconds. The
**[Royal Scotsman](ref:hansa-park/royal-scotsman)** is a Vekoma junior coaster from **1993** that was
called Rasender Roland until 2019 and runs straight through Nessie's loop. The **[Schlange von Midgard](ref:hansa-park/midgard-serpent)** has
stood in Wikingerland since 2011, **[Der kleine
Zar](ref:hansa-park/the-little-tsar)** in the Alter Jahrmarkt since 2017, three metres tall. Both take children from 90 centimetres.

The **[Cornwall
Coaster](ref:hansa-park/new-2026-cornwall-coaster)** opened in the Beautiful Britain area on **23 May 2026**, Gerstlauer again, 18
metres tall, roughly 570 metres long, 50 km/h, from 90 centimetres. The park counts **seven launches** on one
circuit, forwards and backwards. A ride lasts a good minute and a
half, half a minute longer than [Crazy Mine](ref:hansa-park/crazy-mine?bare) or the [Royal Scotsman](ref:hansa-park/royal-scotsman?bare).

## The Highlander

The **[Highlander](ref:hansa-park/highlander)** opened in **April 2019** as the tallest gyro drop
tower in the world. **120 metres** of structure, **103
metres** of free fall, 120 km/h, seats tilted 30 degrees forward, built by Funtime. The title had
belonged to [Scream](ref:heide-park/scream?bare) at [Heide Park](/blog/heide-park-wait-times-tips), 103 metres tall. That is exactly the [Highlander](ref:hansa-park/highlander?bare)'s drop height.

At the end of 2021 the 131-metre Orlando FreeFall opened
in Florida, another Funtime tower, and it was taller. After a fatal accident in March 2022 it was closed and then
taken down in 2023, and since then the Highlander has been the tallest again. After the accident,
Hansa-Park added extra belts to the Highlander's restraints.

The Highlander has the highest minimum height in the park, 1.40 metres.

## A calendar with no closing day

The 2026 season runs from **26 March to 25 October**. Between those two dates the park's calendar has **not one closed day**, as of 25 September. That
makes 214 opening days.

Europa-Park and Phantasialand do the same. Heide Park, on the other hand, regularly takes Monday out
of the calendar in spring and autumn, Movie Park mostly closes Monday to Wednesday outside the high
season, and even Legoland Deutschland in Günzburg has a handful of closed days in May, June and
September 2026. So a [quiet weekday](/best-time-to-visit) in Sierksdorf is always an opening day.

Until **9 October** the park opens daily at **10:00** and the rides
close at **18:00**. On Good Friday,
3 April, the attractions didn't start until 11:00. On 11 August the grounds stayed open until 19:00
and on 12 August until 18:30, but the rides still stopped at 18:00 on both evenings. If you come
before the end of September, expect a changed show line-up on weekdays while the park gets
Herbstzauber ready.

For 2027 the park currently plans its season from **25 March to 24 October**.

## Herbstzauber instead of Halloween

From **10 to 25 October 2026** the park opens **11:00 to 21:00**, and what runs in that window is
called "Herbstzauber am Meer", the autumn magic by the sea. There are no mazes, no scare zones and
no minimum age of 16. The park used to run a scare event on autumn weekends, "Zeit der
Schattenwesen"; it no longer does. Instead it hangs up around **2,000 illuminated figures** and runs
two set pieces:

- The **light parade** starts at about 19:30 in dry weather. It has run since **2002**, introduced
  for the park's 25th anniversary.
- The evening show "Adrienne & der Regenbogen" follows at about 20:30 on the Plaza del Mar and ends
  with fireworks out of the arena walls.

On the [Blumenmeerbootsfahrt](ref:hansa-park/flower-magic-boat-tour), close to 1,000 illuminated
figures dance to classical music once it gets dark. In October this canal boat ride from the Legoland days is the one I'd queue for first.

The
[Wildwasserfahrt](ref:hansa-park/wild-water-ride-the-great-pike) and
[Super Splash](ref:hansa-park/super-splash) close as early as **16:00**. Super Splash doesn't run again after 25 October. [Kärnan](ref:hansa-park/the-oath-of-kaernan?bare), [Störtebekers Kaperfahrt](ref:hansa-park/stoertebekers-sea-raid?bare), [Awildas Abenteuerfahrt](ref:hansa-park/awildas-adventure-ride?bare) and [Awildas Ausguck](ref:hansa-park/awildas-lookout?bare) take their **last ride at 19:30**, because the fireworks
need setting up. And during the light parade eleven further attractions stand still, among them [Highlander](ref:hansa-park/highlander?bare),
[Nessie](ref:hansa-park/nessie?bare) and the [Royal Scotsman](ref:hansa-park/royal-scotsman?bare).

If you want those four rides, join the queue well before half past seven, because the queues close
early. The rest of the park keeps running until 20:30, with the pause during the parade.

The [Halloween guide 2026](/blog/halloween-theme-parks-2026) covers ten parks. Eight of them run
mazes or scare zones through these October weeks, two skip Halloween entirely. Hansa-Park opens
the same evenings with a full programme. With children under ten you're
better off here than at a maze night. The evening show is loud, though, and the park itself warns that it can startle the youngest visitors.

## Height limits and age limits

At Hansa-Park many ride entrances give an age as well as a minimum height, and the age decides whether your child may ride alone or only with an adult alongside.

Only the big three set a hard minimum: [Highlander](ref:hansa-park/highlander?bare) 1.40 metres, [Kärnan](ref:hansa-park/the-oath-of-kaernan?bare) 1.30, [Flucht von Novgorod](ref:hansa-park/escape-of-novgorod?bare) 1.25. On the other coasters less will do, as long as an adult sits next to the child. [Crazy Mine](ref:hansa-park/crazy-mine?bare) takes children from 1.10 metres, but alone only from **age 9**. [Nessie](ref:hansa-park/nessie?bare) and the [Royal Scotsman](ref:hansa-park/royal-scotsman?bare) start at one metre, alone only **from
age 10**. The [Cornwall Coaster](ref:hansa-park/new-2026-cornwall-coaster?bare) starts at 90 centimetres and alone at age 8, the [Schlange von Midgard](ref:hansa-park/midgard-serpent?bare) at 90 centimetres and alone at 6. [Der kleine Zar](ref:hansa-park/the-little-tsar?bare)
takes children from 90 centimetres on a child seat next to an adult; to ride alone you need 1.20 metres.

A seven-year-old at 1.25 metres therefore gets onto seven of the eight coasters, but onto four of
them only with an adult alongside. Our park page lists **82 attractions** (as of 25 September 2026); the park itself advertises more
than 125.

## From Legoland to Hansa-Park

From **1973** this site held Germany's first Legoland. The building contractor Hans Peter Rüster put
it up out of 28 million plastic bricks, with everything from Rüdesheim am Rhein to the New York
skyline rebuilt in miniature. Landesbank Schleswig-Holstein had co-financed the project with around
**30 million marks**. It lasted four seasons. The end came late in 1976, because the licence fees to
the Lego group were too high and the visitor numbers too low; Rüster's company was wound up, and the
bank went on administering the site as trustee.

Rudolf Erich Leicht bought it in the autumn of 1976 together with two investors. _Der Spiegel_ put
their stake at about 10 million marks in 1977. With Horst Hamelberg as first managing director,
Leicht drew up a concept around the theme of the maritime Hanseatic League. At Legoland every ride
had cost separately; at Hansaland an all-inclusive ticket was phased in.

Plenty of Legoland attractions simply stayed where they were and got a new look. The
[Blumenmeerbootsfahrt](ref:hansa-park/flower-magic-boat-tour), the one that turns into a channel of
lights in October, is one of them. Hansaland opened on **15 May 1977**, and on 21 July of the same
year Germany's first genuinely American log flume joined it. That one is still running too.

In **1978** the largest and most modern domed
dolphinarium in Europe opened here, with 1,400 seats. Since **1994** the park has done without
dolphin shows. The show theatre opened in 1984 with Frank Elstner's "Wetten Spaß": contestants
who had lost their bet on "Wetten, dass..?" got a second chance in Sierksdorf. Wim Thoelke and Michael Schanze stood on the
same stage in the years after.

The ride side grew alongside it: **1980** [Nessie](ref:hansa-park/nessie?bare), **1986** the [Super Splash](ref:hansa-park/super-splash?bare), **1987** the renaming to
Hansa-Park for the tenth anniversary, along with the 100-metre Holsteinturm and its revolving cabin.
That one has been gone since **January 2025**, taken down over technical problems that couldn't be
repaired. The Metroliner of 1989 went a similar way, an electric train on a figure of eight folded
into itself, later running as El Paso Express, closed after the 2015 season.

The park's biggest building project is **Hanse in Europa**. It has been replacing the old
Landungsbrücken since 2008 and is going up façade by façade, in eight documented phases: the
Holstentor as an entrance in 2008; Bruges, Visby, Lübeck, Hamburg and Rostock in 2010; Groningen and
Paris in 2011; London, Ribe and Bergen in 2012; six more from Copenhagen to Nuremberg in 2013; Gdańsk
and Kraków in 2018; and in 2021 the largest phase so far, 14 façades from Kraków, Stockholm,
Stralsund, Helsinki, Riga and Venice. Twenty years was the estimate. The last phase, the Palacio de
Braga, isn't finished.

Since 2021 the park has added Awildas Welt, the Peterhof von Novgorod in place of the old logging
camp, New Lübeck in place of the western town Bonanza City, the Carrousel Baltique, the Lost Trails
of Roanoke and Einars Fjordfahrt.

## Where to stay

The **HANSA-PARK Resort am Meer** sits at the edge of Sierksdorf, with the railway line running
between the holiday park and the theme park. Booking goes through Landal or Ostseezeit, the minimum
stay is **three nights**, and two nights are on request only. You stay in a holiday house; the park hotels at Rust and Brühl are themed hotels. Three nights suit a Baltic week with two park days better than a
single park day.

## Practicalities: getting there, tickets, parking

**Getting there.** Am Fahrenkrog 1, 23730 Sierksdorf. Take the regional train from Lübeck towards
Neustadt to Sierksdorf, then it's ten minutes on foot and signposted; with a pushchair or a
wheelchair, take the waymarked route towards the beach at the station and you avoid the steps.

**Parking.** **€7** per day for a car in the main car park, maximum entry height 2.30 metres.
Motorbikes park free, motorhomes pay €15 per twelve hours started. Electric cars get their own EV
car park with ten charging points. The electricity is free there, but the day costs **€16**, more
than double the main car park, and the park asks you to move your car once it's charged. The
drop-off zone is free for 30 minutes.

**Tickets 2026.** **€53** from age 12, **€43** for children aged 4 to 11, free under 4, €43 from age 70. Come on your birthday up to and including your twelfth and you get in free, but only at the gate.
The two-day ticket costs €85 or €65 and is personalised by facial recognition.

The online price is the same as the gate price. At Soltau the gate costs up to €27 more than
booking online. Here booking ahead only saves you the queue at the till.

**Season pass.** The 2026 pass cost €135 once the season had started. The 2027 pass costs **€120**
until 24 March 2027 and €140 after that. Adults who buy it by 25 October 2026 park free in the main
car park all through 2027; buy it by 24 March and parking is €1 a day.

**Included in the ticket.** Every ride, show and hands-on attraction that's running. A few extras,
such as the gold panning, cost separately.

## What comes in 2027

For the park's 50th birthday, the **Buddenbrook-Turm** goes up in the Hansa-Garten: an 86-metre
observation tower by Funtime that takes 60 guests up at once, two of them in wheelchairs. The name
comes from Thomas Mann's "Buddenbrooks", in which Hanno spends his summer holidays in Travemünde on
the Bay of Lübeck. Construction is due to start in the 2026/27 off-season, with the opening set for
some point during the 2027 season. That gives the park an observation tower again, a little over two
years after the Holsteinturm came down.

The
[Super Splash](ref:hansa-park/super-splash?bare) from 1986 comes down in the 2026/27 off-season
because the park is replanning its corner of the grounds. Hansa-Park hasn't said what goes there
instead.

## Frequently asked questions about Hansa-Park

### How tall and how fast is Der Schwur des Kärnan?

73 metres of ride height, 127 km/h, 1,235 metres of track. That makes it, together with [Silver Star](ref:europa-park/silver-star?bare)
at Europa-Park, the tallest and fastest coaster in Germany. Gerstlauer built it and it opened in 2015.

### When is Hansa-Park open in 2026?

From 26 March to 25 October 2026, without a single closed day in between. Until 9 October from 10:00
with rides until 18:00, from 10 to 25 October from 11:00 to 21:00. The 2027 season is planned from
25 March to 24 October. The published calendar is on the [park page](ref:hansa-park), updated daily.

### How many coasters does Hansa-Park have?

Eight, since the [Cornwall Coaster](ref:hansa-park/new-2026-cornwall-coaster?bare) opened on 23 May 2026. Four of them are junior, family or kids' coasters:
[Royal Scotsman](ref:hansa-park/royal-scotsman?bare), [Schlange von Midgard](ref:hansa-park/midgard-serpent?bare), [Der kleine Zar](ref:hansa-park/the-little-tsar?bare) and the Cornwall Coaster.

### What does entry to Hansa-Park cost?

€53 from age 12, €43 for children aged 4 to 11, free under 4. Online costs the same as the gate.
Parking is €7 per day, €16 in the EV car park, where charging is free.

### How tall does my child have to be?

[Highlander](ref:hansa-park/highlander?bare) from 1.40 m, [Der Schwur des Kärnan](ref:hansa-park/the-oath-of-kaernan?bare) from 1.30 m, [Flucht von Novgorod](ref:hansa-park/escape-of-novgorod?bare) from 1.25 m. With an adult alongside: [Crazy Mine](ref:hansa-park/crazy-mine?bare) from 1.10 m (alone from age 9), [Nessie](ref:hansa-park/nessie?bare) and [Royal Scotsman](ref:hansa-park/royal-scotsman?bare) from 1 m (alone from 10), [Cornwall Coaster](ref:hansa-park/new-2026-cornwall-coaster?bare) from 90 cm (alone from 8), [Schlange von Midgard](ref:hansa-park/midgard-serpent?bare) from 90 cm (alone from 6), [Der kleine Zar](ref:hansa-park/the-little-tsar?bare) from 90 cm on a child seat (alone from 1.20 m).

### What is Herbstzauber am Meer?

The evening programme of the season's last 16 days, from 10 to 25 October 2026. Around 2,000
illuminated figures across the park, a light parade at about 19:30 and an evening show with fireworks
at about 20:30. It has nothing to do with Halloween.

### Is Hansa-Park worth it for coaster fans?

For Kärnan and Flucht von Novgorod definitely, and for Highlander. Coming from southern Germany, build the park day
into a Baltic holiday.
