Noël Gaulois, the winter season at [Parc Astérix](ref:parc-asterix) near Plailly, runs from 19 December 2026 to 3 January 2027. This year the park adds an evening show on the front of [OzIris](ref:parc-asterix/oziris?bare), "Les Merveilles de Cléopâtre". The park announced its Christmas programme on 16 November. It opens on all 16 days, Christmas Day and New Year's Day included.

## Cleopatra throws a party at the temple of OzIris

Once it's dark, the park projects images onto the coaster's Egyptian temple, with music, fountains and pyrotechnics. In the story, Cleopatra invites Gauls, Hispanians, Vikings, Romans and Egyptians to a celebration, and each people dances a routine to impress her. The Egyptians are caught off guard and have to turn their palace into a stage in a hurry.

The show plays in the Egypt area, which opened in 2026. You watch standing up and in the open air, and late December is hat weather. The park hasn't published a showtime or a running time yet.

## Ice rink, tube slide and a trail of lights

In the Viking area you'll find the [Patinoire Viking](ref:parc-asterix/patinoire-viking), a 500-square-metre outdoor rink with a roof over it. Skates are free to borrow. Up on the Mont Gaulois, [La Glissade d'Obélix](ref:parc-asterix/la-glissade-dobelix) is back, a slide you ride down on a rubber tube.

[Les Jardins Merveilleux du Père Noël](ref:parc-asterix/les-jardins-merveilleux-du-pere-noel) is a trail of lanterns and glowing animals through Le Bois Enchanté, La Vallée Glacée and Le Village du Père Noël. With a bit of luck you'll run into the Père Noël Gaulois on the way. At the Marché de Noël, wooden huts sell seasonal sweets and goods from French craftspeople, and next to them the Chantons Noël cast sings and acts for half an hour. Christmas decorations hang in four areas, from the Village Gaulois to the Empire Romain.

## Shows and the parade

On the ice, "Qui veut la peau du Père Noël ?" puts 25 minutes of acrobatics and dance into a toy factory. "Quel Cirque !" is a 20-minute acrobatics show, and "C'est du Délire !" a musical. As night falls, the Défilé Gaulois de Noël winds through the park with five floats. Its song is "Le Père Noël rêve de Potion", sung by Gwendal Marimoutou. In Asterix country it makes perfect sense that even Santa wants a sip of the magic potion. The day closes over the lake with L'Île aux Vœux, an eight-minute night show with pyrotechnics.

## Opening times

The park is open from 11 a.m. to 8 p.m., closing at 7 p.m. on Christmas Eve and New Year's Eve. Where else you can ride this winter is in our [overview of theme parks open in winter 2026/27](/blog/winter-theme-parks-2026).

## Tickets and food

The Billet Noël costs €57, the same for adults and children. It has an open date, is valid at weekends too and can be bought up to the day before. It covers every Noël Gaulois attraction and show; food and the Christmas and New Year's Eve evenings cost extra.

For the first time there's an afternoon Goûter du Père Noël, and the Buffet de Noël turns into a secret toy factory for it. The elves and Saperlipopette sort out a few mishaps before the big round, then there's hot chocolate and sweets, and at the end the Père Noël Gaulois, Astérix, Obélix and the wild boar come by. It costs €18.90 per person, children included, so a parent who only watches still pays for a full cup of cocoa. There are two sittings, 4:30 to 5:30 p.m. and 5:30 to 6:30 p.m. You can book in advance, on the day in the app, or at the park. At lunchtime the Buffet de Noël in the Egypt area serves a buffet of seasonal produce, €37.90 for adults and €12.90 for children.

On New Year's Eve the Comptoirs d'Épidemaïs, also decorated for Christmas, put on a festive buffet. On 31 December the Buffet de Noël hosts a gala evening from 8 p.m. to 2 a.m., a dinner show first and dancing after, for €179, or €69 for children. The three hotels serve evening buffets on Christmas Eve and New Year's Eve for €110, or €49 for children, with the party running until 2 a.m.

## Which rides are running

According to the park, the coasters usually run, except in bad weather. [Romus et Rapidus](ref:parc-asterix/romus-et-rapidus?bare), [La Revanche des Pirates – Le Grand Splatch](ref:parc-asterix/la-revanche-des-pirates-le-grand-splatch?bare), [Menhir Express](ref:parc-asterix/menhir-express?bare), [La Rivière d'Élis](ref:parc-asterix/la-riviere-delis?bare), [Hydrolix](ref:parc-asterix/hydrolix?bare) and [Les Chaises volantes](ref:parc-asterix/les-chaises-volantes?bare) stay closed. The [Petit Train](ref:parc-asterix/le-petit-train?bare) and [Enigmatix](ref:parc-asterix/enigmatix?bare) split the day between them: the train runs from 11 a.m. to 3:30 p.m., Enigmatix from 3:30 p.m. until the park closes. In a frost, the park may close individual attractions as well.

## Hotels with early entry

Guests at the park's three hotels get into some attractions half an hour before opening every morning and meet characters from the comics. During Noël Gaulois that means 10:30 a.m., about as merciful as early entry gets. The hotels sit in the woods within walking distance: Les Quais de Lutèce (four stars, 150 rooms), La Cité Suspendue (three stars, 150 rooms) and Les Trois Hiboux (three stars).

## Getting there

Plailly is 35 kilometres north of Porte de la Chapelle in Paris. By car, take the A1 towards Lille; the park has its own exit between junctions 7 and 8, and the drive takes about half an hour. Parking costs €25 a day.

By train, take RER B to Aéroport Charles de Gaulle and change to the park's shuttle there. It runs every 15 to 30 minutes, takes about 25 minutes and costs €14 return or €8 one way, for a fixed date. The last one leaves the park an hour after closing, so at 8 p.m. on Christmas Eve and New Year's Eve. You can also come by long-distance coach or on the shuttle from the Eiffel Tower; both routes are in our [Parc Astérix guide](/blog/parc-asterix-wait-times-tips), along with the rides with the longest queues outside the Christmas season.
