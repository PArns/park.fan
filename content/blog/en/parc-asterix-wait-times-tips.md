On opening day, a Sunday, 20,000 people came through the gates of a park built
for 15,000 a day. Over 1989 as a whole, Parc Astérix turned around 150,000
visitors away at the gate, because it ran short of parking, of restaurant seats
and of ride capacity. The park had expected two million visitors that year and
got 1.34 million, and the guests who left unhappy did the rest.

Thirty-seven years on, the park in Plailly, in the Oise département about 30
kilometres north of Paris, counted 2.9 million visitors for 2025, according to
Wikipedia. By attendance that makes it France's third-biggest theme park,
behind the two parks of
[Disneyland Paris](ref:/parks/europe/france/paris/disneyland-park). Its
coasters now run from Goudurix, which opened with the park, to
[Toutatis](ref:parc-asterix/toutatis), the tallest and fastest coaster in
France.

## The park that nearly didn't survive Euro Disney

[Parc Astérix](ref:parc-asterix) opened to the public on **30 April 1989**,
three days after culture minister Jack Lang inaugurated it. Albert Uderzo had
been carrying the idea around since 1981, after a visit to Disneyland in
California, and the park cost 850 million francs to build. The Astérix statue,
seven metres tall and four tonnes heavy, had been standing on its rock since
20 February.

The hardest year came with Euro Disney. When the resort in Marne-la-Vallée
opened on 12 April 1992, Parc Astérix dropped to 990,000 visitors, 30 per cent
fewer than the year before, and the American press was predicting it would
close. The park recast itself as the French alternative to Disney. It turned a
profit for the first time in 1994, and in 1997 it reached 1.9 million visitors
on the back of a wooden coaster called Tonnerre de Zeus.

It's run by the **Compagnie des Alpes**, which also owns
[Walibi Belgium](ref:walibi-belgium) and [Walibi Holland](ref:walibi-holland).
The park in Wavre has its own [guide](/blog/walibi-belgium-wait-times-tips).

## The coasters, oldest to newest

**[Goudurix](ref:parc-asterix/goudurix)** (Vekoma, 1989) is the park's oldest
coaster. With seven inversions it held the European record for years, until
Dragon Khan opened at PortAventura in 1995.

**[Tonnerre 2 Zeus](ref:parc-asterix/tonnerre-2-zeus)** is a wooden coaster.
It opened in 1997 as Tonnerre de Zeus, built by Custom Coasters International,
and has carried its current name since 2021, with The Gravity Group credited as
the manufacturer. Of the ten headliners in the table, it's also the only one
where our analysis says arriving at opening is worth it.

**[OzIris](ref:parc-asterix/oziris)** (Bolliger & Mabillard, 2012) is an
inverted coaster with five inversions and a 40-metre lift hill, and at one
point the train passes underneath a pool. Together with the Egyptian area
around it, OzIris cost 20 million euros, at the time the biggest investment in
the park's history.

**[Pégase Express](ref:parc-asterix/pegase-express)** (Gerstlauer, 2017,
16 million euros) is a family launch coaster that carries on backwards halfway
through. **[La Trace du Hourra](ref:parc-asterix/la-trace-du-hourra)** (Mack
Rides, 2001) is a bobsled coaster with a 900-metre run at close to 60 km/h.
**[Discobélix](ref:parc-asterix/discobelix)** (Zamperla, 2016) is a Disk’O
Coaster.

**[Toutatis](ref:parc-asterix/toutatis)** (Intamin, 2023) is a launch coaster
with 51 metres of height, 107 km/h and a 101-degree drop. It was announced in
2018 for 2021, then pushed back twice.
**[Cétautomatix](ref:parc-asterix/cetautomatix)** (Gerstlauer, 2025) is a
spinning coaster that opened on 18 August 2025, several months late because of
manufacturing and delivery problems.

## The yearly records fall in Christmas week

Over the last twelve months we've measured, five of the park's ten headliners
had their longest wait of the year between 26 and 30 December 2025: Toutatis,
OzIris, Cétautomatix, Discobélix and La Trace du Hourra. The park opens again
for **Noël Gaulois** from 19 December 2026 to 3 January 2027, sixteen days in
all. For the other five headliners, the worst day of the year falls between
April and August.

We don't know why. Crowds squeezed into just sixteen opening days would be one
explanation, fewer trains running in the cold another, and nothing in our data
settles it either way.

The season runs from Easter to All Saints' Day. Since 2007 the park has stayed
open until after the French autumn holidays around All Saints', and it only
opens in winter in some years: from 2007 to 2010, and again since 2019.
Halloween here is called **Peur sur le Parc**, and in 2026 it runs from
3 October to 8 November, with twelve evening openings that need a separate
ticket. The details are in our
[Halloween guide 2026](/blog/halloween-theme-parks-2026), and the Christmas
opening is in the [winter guide](/blog/winter-theme-parks-2026).

## What's closing and what's coming

The Rue de Paris, the area that recreated Paris from the Middle Ages to the
1889 World's Fair, has been almost entirely closed since November 2025 and is
being demolished, according to Wikipedia. A district called Londinium is due to
take its place from 2028. The park has moved two rides into its Egypt area:
L’Envol d’Ibis, and La Descente du Nil, which was called L’Oxygénarium until
the end of the 2025 season. For 2027, Wikipedia mentions an extension of the
Greek district modelled on the island of Symi, with a restaurant, two small
drop towers for children and a ride of the Go Go Bounce type.

## Frequently asked questions about Parc Astérix

### What's the best ride at Parc Astérix?

On the numbers, Toutatis, with 51 metres, 107 km/h and a 101-degree drop. For
time saved at opening, Tonnerre 2 Zeus. Current wait times are on
[Toutatis's page](ref:parc-asterix/toutatis).

### What time should you get to Parc Astérix?

At opening for Tonnerre 2 Zeus, a little later for Toutatis. For the other
headliners your arrival time makes little difference. The day decides, and
the calendar further up has the quiet ones.

### Is Parc Astérix busier at Christmas than in summer?

For five of the ten headliners, yes: their worst day of the last twelve months
fell between 26 and 30 December 2025. The other five had theirs between April
and August.

### Is Parc Astérix open all year?

No. It opens from Easter to All Saints' Day, plus a window at Christmas, this
year from 19 December to 3 January. The rest of the year it's closed.

### Who owns Parc Astérix?

The Compagnie des Alpes, which has run it since 2002. The group also owns
[Walibi Belgium](ref:walibi-belgium) and [Walibi Holland](ref:walibi-holland).
