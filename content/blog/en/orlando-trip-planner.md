On 8 December 2026 you'll have to leave [Magic Kingdom](ref:magic-kingdom-park) at 6 pm if you hold an ordinary day ticket. Mickey's Very Merry Christmas Party starts after that, and it needs a ticket of its own. The day before, the park stays open until 10 pm. We expect about the same crowds at the big rides on both days, which is what makes the 8th a trap: in a crowd calendar it looks as good as the 7th, it just has four hours fewer, and they're the ones with the fireworks.

For days like that, [park.fan/orlando](/orlando) now has a trip planner. You tell it when you're in Orlando and which parks you want to see, and each park lands on the day of your trip when its big rides are least busy. For ten days from 5 to 14 December, Magic Kingdom gets Monday the 7th, one of the five days without a party. Orlando is the planner's first region.

## Every Orlando park in one calendar

The same page puts Walt Disney World, Universal with Epic Universe, SeaWorld, their water parks and three day trips into one calendar, 16 parks in November 2026. Each square is one park on one day, and the first column is today in Orlando. The number in it is the minutes we expect at the park's big rides at the busiest time of day, and that's what you compare parks by. The colour is set another way, by comparing the day with a typical day at the same park, which is why a park with longer queues can still be green.

A folded corner means the park closes early for day guests, at Magic Kingdom for Mickey's Very Merry Christmas Party and at Hollywood Studios for Disney Jollywood Nights. Hatched squares are parks closed for the season, in winter 2026/27 Typhoon Lagoon and Volcano Bay among them.

![The Orlando Crowd Calendar with 16 rows for the Disney, Universal and SeaWorld parks and the day trips, one square per day with the expected minutes. | The second half of November 2026. Where Magic Kingdom's square has a folded corner, the park closes at 6 pm for the party. | wide](/media/reiseplaner/orlando-kalender-en.webp)

At the very top there's a single day in Orlando time, with the park that has the shortest queues at each resort and a note when one of them closes early that day for an evening event. Tap a day and you get the opening hours, Early Entry, evening events and seasons, the drive from your hotel area and that day's five big rides. "Plan this day" opens the [day planner](/trip-planner) with the park and the date filled in.

Without traffic, EPCOT to Universal Studios is a 25-minute drive, LEGOLAND in Winter Haven just under an hour, and Busch Gardens and Adventure Island in Tampa about an hour and a half. Strictly speaking you don't drive to Magic Kingdom at all. You drive to its car park at the Transportation and Ticket Center, and from there it's another quarter of an hour by monorail or ferry, which the plan counts on every trip there.

Each of the next eleven months has a page of its own. Three more pages cover the best time to visit [Orlando](/orlando/best-time-to-visit), [Disney World](/orlando/best-time-to-visit/disney-world) and [Universal Orlando](/orlando/best-time-to-visit/universal-orlando), each for the coming year, and from September for the one after. They open on the three quietest ten-day trips. After that come a bar for every week, a row for every month with its events, school holidays and usual weather, and links that open the trip planner inside German, Dutch or English school holidays.

## What the plan takes into account

You enter your dates, up to 21 days, and the parks. If you leave the selection alone, it's the big seven: Magic Kingdom, EPCOT, Hollywood Studios and Animal Kingdom at Disney, and Universal Studios, Islands of Adventure and Epic Universe at Universal. With "Split my days for me" on, a park gets a second day where your trip has room for one. Hotel, hotel area, rest days and park hopping sit under "Fine-tune".

Every day counts with the hours you really get as a day guest. A party night that ends at 6 pm, when the park otherwise stays open until 11 pm, weighs up to one and a half times as much in the plan as a full day with the same waits. It only gets picked when its waits are short enough to make up for the missing hours. Disney After Hours starts after the regular close, so the day loses no hours to it. Answer "Do you want to see Magic Kingdom's fireworks?" with yes, and the plan only uses a party night when nothing else fits.

The hotel counts too. A Disney hotel brings Early Entry, a Disney deluxe hotel Extended Evening Hours on top, and a Universal hotel Early Park Admission. The Universal Premier hotels, which are the Royal Pacific, the Hard Rock and the Portofino Bay, include Express at Universal Studios and Islands of Adventure, and on those days the queues there count for a lot less.

With a Park Hopper the plan may put two parks on one day, when your trip has fewer days than park visits and the two are under 30 minutes apart door to door. When a park closes early, you get an idea for the evening: a second park if you have a hopper, otherwise Disney Springs or CityWalk, which are free to enter. Rest days go on the busiest days of your trip, and you won't get more than four park days in a row unless your parks don't fit any other way. On a rest day you don't queue anywhere, or at least nowhere we measure.

The answer opens with minutes. You see how long you'll queue for a big ride on your days on average, and how long at the same parks on an average day of your trip. Below that is how far a forecast for one ride on one day can be off, so you can hold the saving up against that error. If the saving is under three minutes a ride, the answer says the order barely matters on these dates.

You can view the plan as an agenda, a week or a month. You can pin a day or swap it with another one, and before you swap, you see how many minutes it costs you. Single days or the whole trip go into the day planner, into a calendar file in Orlando time, or out as a link to exactly this plan.

## When your dates are still open

Under "Find the best dates" you pick a trip length of 3 to 21 days and a period: the next 12 months, a year, a spring, summer or autumn, or dates of your own. Back come the three best stretches, each with the wait at the big rides and how far that is below the period's average. With "Only in our school holidays" ticked, only stretches that lie entirely inside your region's school holidays are left, and the planner tells you how far the holiday dates we have reach. "Plan these dates" turns a stretch into a plan.

Heat and the hurricane season show up as short notes on each stretch. A day counts as hot from a forecast of 35 °C, and further ahead, where there's no forecast yet, from a long-term average of 31.5 °C. The hurricane season runs from 1 June to 30 November, with its peak from mid-August to mid-October. A hurricane doesn't show up in any wait time, so during the season we add up to eight minutes per park day, for the ranking only. A stretch in September therefore ends up behind an equally busy one in May and is marked "ranked lower", while its minutes stay as they are.

## Tickets you already have

The planner's first step asks "Do you already have tickets?". If you do, you pick them by name for Disney, Universal, SeaWorld or LEGOLAND. US tickets include Disney's date-based ticket for 1 to 10 days and Universal's for 1 to 5 days, both with or without the hopper, and at Universal, Epic Universe only comes in from three days. If you're not sure which one you have, the plan goes with the strictest version of that length.

The 14-day tickets sold outside the US are in the list too, tagged "Sold outside the US". Disney's 14-Day Magic Ticket is good for 14 park days within 18 days, park hopping and water parks included. Universal's 14-day ticket is valid every day for 14 days in a row, with park-to-park and Epic Universe. So the Disney ticket leaves you four days off, and the Universal one none at all: it keeps running whether you're in the park or by the pool.

The Disney and Universal Combo Ticket, as Attraction Tickets sells it, fills in both at once, and one first day counts for both. If you arrive between 20 October 2026 and 31 March 2027, the Universal half is the Three Park Adventure Ticket, without Volcano Bay, because the water park is closed until the end of March.

![The trip planner's ticket step with two tickets of your own, Disney's 14-Day Magic Ticket and Universal's 14-day ticket, both tagged "Sold outside the US", each with its first day and dates. | A Combo Ticket starting Saturday 6 February 2027. Leave the first day open and the plan picks the start that fits best. | wide](/media/reiseplaner/tickets-uk-en.webp)

With 6 February as the first day, the Disney ticket runs to 23 February and the Universal one to the 19th. Both windows sit as bars under the days in the calendar, and every park day stays inside its ticket's window. With the big seven, the plan uses eight of the 14 Disney days in those 18 days, two for each of the four parks, and under "Your tickets" it says why six are left over: your parks need fewer. It only mixes Disney and Universal days where that saves at least three minutes a ride.

On days when a park hasn't published its opening hours yet, the trip planner plans without them, and the block says "no hours yet". For a trip that's still months away, that's often every day. Open a saved link again later and it plans with whatever hours have come out by then.

Where a ticket's terms leave a rule open, the plan takes the cautious side. The 7-Day Magic Ticket's terms don't give a number of water-park visits, so the plan assumes seven.

## No tickets yet

If you answer "Not yet, just plan", the plan comes with a suggestion of which tickets you need. For ten days from 5 to 14 December with the big seven, it reads: 4 days Disney from Sunday 6 December, then 3 days Universal from Friday the 11th.

"Ticket details" shows what each ticket allows. A four-day Disney ticket is good on any four of the seven days from its first, a three-day Universal ticket on any three of five. Next to that is what a hopper would add, and on which date. "Worth adding" only appears where it opens up an evening in a second park, fits in a park that otherwise wouldn't fit, or saves at least three minutes a ride. In December it's a single evening. On the 10th, Animal Kingdom closes at 6 pm, and with a Park Hopper you can head over to Hollywood Studios afterwards. For the three Universal days, park-to-park adds nothing, apart from a note that you need it for the Hogwarts Express between Universal Studios and Islands of Adventure.

You'll have to work out yourself whether one evening at Hollywood Studios is worth the extra cost. The planner shows no prices. Instead, each ticket comes with links to where you buy it: the cheapest authorised seller we found, Attraction Tickets and the park's own shop, with the pages in your language first. If one of them is a partner link, it says so next to it, and if you buy through it, park.fan earns a commission and your price stays the same. Because the trip is ten days long, there's also a line about the 14-day tickets sold outside the US, including the Combo.

## "Disney day 2 of 4" in the week view

Take both suggestions as your tickets, add the Park Hopper on the Disney one, and the week view lays the plan on the same hour axis as the day planner. A block runs from opening to the time you have to leave as a day guest, so a short day is also drawn shorter. Above each column you see which day of your ticket it is.

Monday at Magic Kingdom is "Disney day 2 of 4" and runs from 9 am to 10 pm. On Thursday, Animal Kingdom is followed after a 17-minute drive by the evening at Hollywood Studios. On Friday, Universal starts with Islands of Adventure, and the evening at CityWalk is dashed because it isn't a park. If one of your parks fell on a party night, the time after the block would be hatched, with the party's name and "separate ticket". This week, none of them does.
