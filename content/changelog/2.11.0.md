## New

- The blog went online on 7 July, the German welcome post first and the other five languages the
  same morning. By 15 August it held seven articles in six languages. Park and ride pages show up
  to three posts about them.
- Posts that name a park or a ride show its live status, not the status from the day the post was
  built. The Phantasialand guide had shown all twelve coasters it names as closed in the middle
  of an operating day.
- A ride page's header lists its facts with labels (manufacturer, ride type, number of track
  elements) and its measurements in your units: switch to °F and a speed reads in mph, a height in
  feet, and a minimum height of 140 cm as 55 in.
- Rides and the glossary link both ways. A ride's profile lists its track elements in the order
  you ride them, each linked to its glossary term, and a term page lists the rides that have it.
  The glossary grew to 262 terms.
- Click a day in the crowd calendar and it opens with that day's hours, the forecast, the waits of
  the headliners, an hourly chart, the weather and the holidays around it (20 July). "Forecast
  today" in the park header opens the same view for today, and the arrow keys step through the
  days.
- School holidays in neighbouring regions are marked in the calendar.
- A banner on the homepage names the three hottest parks in Germany, France, Italy, the
  Netherlands and Belgium on days of 35 °C and more (26 June).
- Official severe-weather warnings appear on the park page, and the weather card has an hour-by-hour
  view with rain bars.
- Rides that are worth heading to first thing in the morning carry a rope-drop badge.
- New pages: Fancast, where the prediction model reports its live accuracy (21 July), a hub for
  the best time to visit, in six languages (22 July), and a page to send us your own photos
  (30 June).
- Queue overviews show the prices of Lightning Lane and Express passes, and a card lists the
  day's skip-the-line prices.
- The top-ten table of longest waits has a column with what each ride shows right now (15 August).

## Improved

- A park's crowd level measures the park instead of its busiest ride. Phantasialand read "high"
  while Taron and F.L.Y. sat at 20 minutes against typical waits of 45 and 40; it reads "low" for
  such a day now.
- The homepage photo carries the live number of open parks, a search that works in place, and on
  wide screens a world map (6 August).
- Park, ride and geo pages say how far away you are.
- Park pages stopped jumping as their late sections arrived: the median layout shift went from
  0.245 to 0.0005. The live update every five minutes shrank from 90 KB to 40 KB.

## Fixed

- The weather chart disappeared after midnight.
- Without location permission, "Parks near you" placed you at the data centre your request went
  through, so a visitor in Geilenkirchen got the parks around Frankfurt or London.
- Searching for "fly" did not put F.L.Y. first.
- Hansa-Park looked like a park with every ride open and no queue anywhere. It says that it
  publishes no wait times now.
