## New

- The trip planner arrived on 4 September, in all six languages. Four questions lead in: the park,
  the day (picked on a month grid coloured by that park's crowd forecast), who is coming, and which
  headliners. The day then opens as a timeline where each ride is a block as tall as its predicted
  queue, with the walk to the next one in between and the weather down the edge. You can drag
  blocks around, pull rides in from the park page and add blocks of your own, such as lunch.
- On a day that does not fit, "plan all headliners" opens a three-step assistant: what would make
  room without dropping anything, the rides in the order the optimiser gives them up, and the
  result with the dropped rides by name (7 September). Before, the answer was an 11 px grey line
  under the buttons, and the ride that fell out was the one with the most minutes of queueing,
  usually the one people came for. Each suggestion is a measured difference between two plans:
  across 35 park days at six parks, five did not fit, and two of those were solved by shortening a
  break.
- "Today at this ride" on a ride page is the park page's panel one level down: the live wait and
  its trend, today's range and peak, the recommended slots, the typical and busy pair, and the
  park's own status and hours, which a ride page never stated before (5 September). The 30-day
  history became a chapter, drawn in the crowd calendar's colours with the queue curve along the
  floor of each tile.
- A ride that is down carries a line under its status saying since when the park has reported it,
  with the weekday and the time (6 September).

## Improved

- A planned day ends when the park closes (7 September). The grid used to read a closing hour of 18
  as "open until 18:59" and would plan a ride at 18:15 in a park that shuts at 18:00. The hour after
  closing is drawn as slack you can drag into, and nothing is planned there. What counts is when you
  join the queue, so a 40-minute queue at 17:45 is still a slot you can take on purpose.
